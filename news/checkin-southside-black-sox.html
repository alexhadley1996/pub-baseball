The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Southside Has the Best Record in the ABL, and Jake Westbrook Hasn't Lost a Start

Picked second in the preseason consensus, the Black Sox are 26-14, allow the fewest runs in the league, are 8-0 when Westbrook starts, and have Jorge Posada playing second base.

Southside Black Sox · 26-14 · 218 runs scored, 177 allowed

The preseason consensus put Southside second in the ABL, on the strength of a rotation that could be excellent and critical to the season. The projections were cooler, at 77-74. Forty games in, the Black Sox are 26-14, the best record in the league, three games clear in the Western Conference. The site gives them an 83.4% chance at the playoffs and a 48.0% chance at a bye.

Without many frills

The rotation came in described as competent and experienced, without many frills, a bit old and without much upside. It has turned out to be exactly that, and it's enough. Southside leads the ABL in ERA, at 4.18, and allows the fewest runs per game. Brad Radke, the leader of the staff, has walked six batters in 59.2 innings. Tim Wakefield has allowed two home runs all year, threw 7.2 shutout innings against Minnesota and on April 30 in Portland added eight more scoreless innings in a 1-0 win. The oldest of them, David Wells, 38, has struggled, with a 6.27 ERA.

The best results have come from the youngest. Jake Westbrook, 24, has a 4.09 ERA and a 2-0 record, and Southside has won all eight of his starts, including a 1-0 win in New York on April 15.

Then the bullpen finishes the job. Closer Terry Adams has 12 saves and a 1.45 ERA and hasn't allowed a home run, and Todd Stottlemyre and Albie Lopez have been nearly as good. That's why Southside is 4-0 in extra innings and 9-5 in one-run games. On April 25 and 26 it beat Cincinnati on back-to-back walk-offs, the second in 12 innings.

The catchers

The lineup's calling card was the best catching pair in all three leagues, Jorge Posada and Ivan Rodriguez on the same team. Both are hitting. Rodriguez is batting .386. Posada has nine homers and a 147 OPS+, and to get both bats in the lineup he's been playing second base. On May 1 he went 3-for-3 with two homers as Southside came back from six down to beat Cleveland 9-7.

Of the younger hitters, Nick Johnson has been the best, with 10 homers and a 133 OPS+. Jason Bay has an 87 OPS+ but is 17-for-18 stealing, part of the reason Southside leads the ABL in steals. Jerry Hairston is at 65. Joe Randa's hot start came with a big assist from his batting average on balls in play, and he's settled at .309. As a team the offense ranks only 12th in runs per game. The pitching has carried it.

Next

The Black Sox went 9-1 from April 15 to 25, sweeping New York and Minnesota, and they're 15-7 on the road. Their clubhouse pranksters came into the year as one of the league's morale X-factors, and whatever is going on in there is working. Next come three in Baltimore, then the biggest series of the week: San Diego, three games back with the league's best run differential, comes to Southside. After that the Black Sox go to Austin.
