40-Game Check-In · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Black Sox Will Take the Next Base, Thanks

Southside Black Sox, 26-14 through 40 games

The Black Sox have stolen 59 bases in 40 games. They have been caught 22 times. You can learn a lot about a team from whether it believes the next base is worth asking for, and this one asks constantly.

Through 40 games they were 26-14, scoring 218 runs and allowing 177 (+41). As of the May 13 export they are in first place in the Western Conference, and 1st of 20 in the ABL. That run differential would usually produce a record around 24-16. They are 9-5 in one-run games and 4-0 in extra innings.

What is driving it

Their most productive hitter is Jorge Posada (2B, 30), at .326/.416/.594 with 9 HR. Nick Johnson leads the club with 10 home runs. Jason Bay has 17 steals (caught 1 time). As a team they are hitting .297/.366/.439, 13th of 20 in the ABL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 45 home runs.

Brad Radke leads the starters with a 3.77 ERA over 59.2 innings (38 K, 6 BB). Tim Wakefield (3.93 ERA in 50.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.18 ERA, 1st of 20 in the ABL; starters 4.42, relievers 3.71. Terry Adams has 12 saves and 1 blown.

That 1.010 OPS is also the surprise. In 2001, per the export, Jorge Posada managed .838 over 557 plate appearances, which is a long way from here. In the rotation, David Wells has a 6.27 ERA over 47.1 innings, after a 4.47 ERA in 2001.

Close games are where baseball goes to flip coins, and the Black Sox have flipped a lot of them: 14 one-run games in their first 40, and they are 9-5 in them.

Some details worth knowing. They once scored 16 runs in a game (Apr 4, against the Athletics); that one game is 7% of their season's runs.

The stakes

The ABL is the middle rung, so it is the only league here where you can fall and climb in the same breath: each conference winner gets a bye, second and third play in, both finalists of the American Championship are promoted to MLB, and the bottom three are relegated to the FBL. The Black Sox lead the Western Conference by 3 games, which is bye territory for now. The drop is 8 games below them, where the Violets is the best of the clubs on relegation pace.

The best night was Apr 16, an 11-1 win over the Knights. The worst was May 9, an 11-2 loss to the Stars. Their best 10-game stretch went 9-1 (Apr 15 to Apr 25); their worst, 5-5 (Apr 1 to Apr 10).

Looking ahead

The next stretch is 10 games (4 at home) against the Orioles, the Padres, the Violets, the Royeaux. Those clubs are 197-203 combined, a .492 winning percentage, so it will mostly be up to them. The longer-range question is whether the 2 or so wins they have banked beyond their run differential stay banked. History says to expect the runs to win that argument eventually; history is not always right in May. In six weeks the Black Sox have shown us who they might be. The next six will show us who they are willing to keep being.
