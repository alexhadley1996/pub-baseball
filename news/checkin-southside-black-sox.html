The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Southside Has the Best Record in the ABL, and Jake Westbrook Hasn't Lost a Start

Alexander ranked the Black Sox second but predicted only 77-74. They're 26-14, they allow the fewest runs in the league, they're 8-0 when Westbrook starts, and Jorge Posada is playing second base.

Southside Black Sox · 26-14 · 218 runs scored, 177 allowed

Alexander's two preseason looks at Southside didn't quite agree. The power rankings put the Black Sox second and praised a rotation that "could be excellent and critical this season." The predicted standings had them at 77-74. Forty games in, the power rankings look closer. Southside is 26-14, the best record in the ABL, and leads the Western Conference by three games. The site gives it an 83.4% chance of the playoffs and a 48.0% chance of a bye.

Without many frills

He described the rotation as "a competent, experienced rotation without many frills. A bit old, and without much upside." Southside leads the ABL in ERA (4.18) and allows the fewest runs per game. Brad Radke has walked six batters in 59.2 innings and has a 3.77 ERA. The trends piece called him the leader of the staff. Tim Wakefield has a 3.84 FIP and has allowed two home runs. The trends piece highlighted his 7.2 shutout innings against Minnesota. On April 30 in Portland he threw eight more scoreless innings in a 1-0 win.

The oldest of the three, David Wells, 38, has the worst numbers, with a 6.27 ERA. The best results have come from a starter Alexander didn't mention. Jake Westbrook, 24, has a 4.09 ERA and a 2-0 record, and Southside has won all eight of his starts, including a 1-0 win in New York on April 15.

The bullpen has been even better, with a 3.71 ERA. Closer Terry Adams has 12 saves, a 1.45 ERA and has allowed no home runs. Todd Stottlemyre (3.29) and Albie Lopez (2.01) have been nearly as good. That bullpen is why Southside is 4-0 in extra innings and 9-5 in one-run games. On April 25 and 26 it beat Cincinnati on back-to-back walk-offs, the second in 12 innings.

The catchers

The lineup headline was "the best catcher combo in all three leagues: Posada and Pudge on the same team." Both are hitting. Ivan Rodriguez is batting .386/.411/.571. Jorge Posada is at .326/.416/.594 with nine home runs and a 147 OPS+, and he has been playing second base. On May 1 he went 3-for-3 with two homers as Southside came back from six runs down to beat Cleveland 9-7. Of the "rising talents," Nick Johnson has been the best, with 10 home runs and a 133 OPS+. Jason Bay has an 87 OPS+ but is 17-for-18 stealing bases. Jerry Hairston is at 65. Southside leads the ABL in steals. The trends piece credited Joe Randa's start to "a big BABIP assist." He's now at .309 with a 104 OPS+. As a team, the offense ranks only 12th in runs per game.

Next

The Black Sox went 9-1 from April 15 to 25. That run included sweeps of New York and Minnesota, which put them on the trends piece's "great weeks" list. They are 15-7 on the road. The personalities report named their "pranksters" as one of the ABL's morale X-factors, and whatever is going on in that clubhouse is working. Next come three in Baltimore, then the biggest series of the week: San Diego, three games back with the league's best run differential, comes to Southside. After that the Black Sox go to Austin.
