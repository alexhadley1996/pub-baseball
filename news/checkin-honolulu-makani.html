The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Honolulu Pitches Well Enough to Win 1-0 Games. It Has to.

The Makani came in with an A- for pitching and a D for hitting. Honolulu has the FBL's second-best ERA and the league's fewest runs, and it has scored three or fewer in 21 of its 40 games.

Honolulu Makani · 18-22 · 144 runs scored, 167 allowed

Honolulu's preseason grades were about as far apart as grades get. The pitching got an A-, with Rich Harden and Kelvim Escobar at the top of the rotation and one of the strongest bullpens in the FBL, four quality arms deep. The offense got a D. There were no stars, and not even an up-and-coming player, except maybe Chris Woodward.

Forty games in, both grades look right. Honolulu has allowed 167 runs, second-fewest in the FBL, with the second-best ERA, 3.89. It has scored 144, the fewest in the league, and it's last in OPS. The record is 18-22, seventh in the West Group and eight games behind Monterrey.

Three or fewer

The Makani have scored three runs or fewer in 21 of their 40 games and gone 5-16 in them. Winning that way requires the pitching to be close to perfect, and sometimes it has been. On April 23 Escobar threw eight shutout innings with 10 strikeouts and no walks in a 1-0 win over Inland Empire. On Monday, with Harden starting, Honolulu beat Eugene 1-0 on a walk-off.

Sometimes perfect isn't enough. On April 9 in Sacramento, Jesse Foppert, 21, gave up four hits in eight shutout innings, an incredible start. The game was scoreless until Olmedo Saenz homered off Brad Lidge in the ninth, and Honolulu lost 1-0. Foppert has 48 strikeouts in 48 innings and a 1-2 record.

Harden, 20, has a 3.47 ERA and is 2-4, with 48 strikeouts and 25 walks. Escobar is at 4.03. The mish-mash at the back of the rotation has been fine, with Eric Knott at 4.02 and Carlton Loewer at 4.50. The bullpen has a 3.63 ERA and only two blown saves all season. Satoru Komiyama, 36, has a 1.59 ERA, and on April 5 he and closer Mike Nakamura pulled off some bullpen heroics in a 5-4 win in San Antonio. Nakamura has 13 saves.

The lineup

Tim Salmon is the one regular hitting well, with a .401 on-base percentage, 23 walks and a 125 OPS+. Charles Johnson leads the team with eight home runs and drove in five against Memphis on May 4. After that, it gets thin. B.J. Surhoff, 37, plays first base with a .283 on-base percentage, a 52 OPS+ and -1.1 WAR. Shortstop Justin Baughman is at 32. Woodward, the one maybe, has an 85. Kelly Johnson, 20, has a 67 in center.

Baughman and Kelly Johnson are only playing because of injuries. The injury list has hit Honolulu repeatedly, leaving the middle infield and center field unsettled at the same time. Shortstop Rey Sanchez played five games before a high ankle sprain that will cost him six to seven weeks, and center fielder Damon Buford hasn't played since April 12 because of a fractured wrist, with five to six weeks still expected.

Streaky

Honolulu started 1-9, won 10 of its next 12, including six straight from April 18 to 23, and then lost seven straight from April 27 to May 3, three of them on walk-offs, two in Louisville. It's 6-3 since. The site gives the Makani a 7.7% chance at the playoffs and projects about 71 wins, a little short of the preseason projection of 73-78. Next is a trip to first-place Monterrey, then Santo Domingo and Savannah at home.
