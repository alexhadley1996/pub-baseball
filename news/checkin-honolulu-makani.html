40-Game Check-In · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

Makani at 40: Life After 7 Straight Losses

Honolulu Makani, 18-22 through 40 games

There was a stretch, Apr 27 to May 3, when the Makani lost seven straight. Seasons recover from that sort of thing all the time, but they do not forget it, and neither does the standings page.

The FBL is the bottom rung and nobody gets relegated from it; the whole point is the way up. The top seven make a ladder playoff, seeds one and two are promoted outright, and the winner of the Federal Promotion round makes three. They are 15th of 20 in the table, 3½ games behind the Larks for the last ladder place.

Through 40 games they were 18-22, scoring 144 runs and allowing 167 (-23). As of the May 13 export they are 7th in the West Group, 8 games back, and 15th of 20 in the FBL. By the old Pythagorean arithmetic, that is a 17-win team so far. They are 9-7 in one-run games and 0-1 in extra innings.

The hitters

Tim Salmon has been the Makani hitter opponents least want to see: .292/.401/.467, 5 homers, 15 driven in. Charles Johnson leads the club with 8 home runs. Justin Baughman has 8 steals (caught 1 time). As a team they are hitting .250/.310/.373, 20th of 20 in the FBL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 31 home runs. And it is not the line anyone would have written for him: his 2001 OPS was .748 in 581 plate appearances. The weakest regular bat has been Justin Baughman, at .221/.250/.254 in 128 plate appearances.

The pitchers

Rich Harden leads the starters with a 3.47 ERA over 57.0 innings (48 K, 25 BB). Eric Knott (4.02 ERA in 47.0 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 3.89 ERA, 2nd of 20 in the FBL; starters 4.00, relievers 3.63. Mike Nakamura has 13 saves and 1 blown.

The best and worst of it

The best night was Apr 19, an 8-3 win over the Knights. The worst was May 8, a 7-0 loss to the Spirit. Their best 10-game stretch went 8-2 (Apr 12 to Apr 21); their worst, 1-9 (Apr 1 to Apr 10).

Some details worth knowing. When they score three runs or fewer they are 5-16; when they score six or more, 6-3. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

What next

The next stretch is 10 games (6 at home) against the Sultanes, the Gallos, the Bananas, the Flying Squirrels. Those clubs are 223-171 combined, a .566 winning percentage, so the schedule is not doing them any favors. There is no luck to give back and none to collect: 18 wins, about 17 expected. What changes the picture from here is health and Tim Salmon, not arithmetic.
