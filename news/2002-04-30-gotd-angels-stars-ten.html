ABL · 2002-04-30 · By Sam Holloway, Sporting Weekly

Grissom Doesn't Get Tired

Nashville led Anaheim 6-1 going into the seventh. Marquis Grissom, 35 and hitting .409, doubled and singled home runs in the eighth and ninth, and Cliff Brumbaugh singled in the winner in the 10th for a 7-6 Angels win.

ANAHEIM, Calif. — Marquis Grissom turned 35 in the spring and plays left field like a man who has decided that nobody informed him. He came into Tuesday hitting .409. He went 4-for-5. And when Anaheim needed someone to start a comeback from five runs down, and then someone to finish it, it was Grissom both times.

The Angels beat the Nashville Stars 7-6 in 10 innings. They trailed 6-1 in the seventh.

“Grizz is the best hitter in this league right now, and it’s not close,” Angels manager Mike Scioscia said. “He’s the guy you want up there. Twice, apparently.”

How it got to 6-1

For six innings this was Nashville’s game, and specifically Miguel Batista’s. The Stars’ right-hander came in with a 2.59 ERA and pitched like it, allowing a first-inning run on Austin Kearns’ double and nothing else through six.

His team built him a lead. Bobby Kielty homered off Heath Murray in the third. Milton Bradley grounded in a run in the fourth. And in the sixth, Kevin Tolar, the Angels’ reliever with a 9.00 ERA, gave up three straight two-out doubles: Chris Gomez for two runs, Aaron Guiel for one and Jason Grabowski for another. It was 6-1.

Matt Laney, Nashville’s manager, is known around the ABL as the man with the clipboard full of numbers. His numbers liked the Stars’ chances.

Chipping

The Angels got two back in the seventh on Cliff Brumbaugh’s single and James Lofton’s sacrifice fly. Josh Hancock, carrying a 13.97 ERA into the game, threw two scoreless innings in the eighth and ninth, and kept it close enough to matter.

In the eighth, Scott Podsednik singled off Batista, and Laney brought in Rheal Cormier, who came in with a 1.46 ERA. Grissom doubled to make it 6-4.

In the ninth, Jason Lane doubled and Lofton singled him home. It was 6-5, two outs, a man on first. Podsednik singled. Grissom singled, and it was tied.

“I wasn’t thinking about the four hits,” Grissom said. “I was thinking about the fastball. He’d thrown me a fastball in the eighth. I figured he’d try to come back with it. He did.”

The 10th

LaTroy Hawkins struck out two in a clean top of the 10th. Laney sent Cormier back out, which may have been the decision he thinks about tonight. Jason Phillips and Rich Becker singled. Mike Trombley came on and got Lane on a fly ball. Brumbaugh, who has been hitting near .220 all season, singled, and Phillips scored.

“That’s on me,” Laney said. “Rheal had been great. I trusted him one inning too long. That’s baseball.”

Hawkins got the win. Cormier took the loss after allowing seven hits and three runs in two innings. The Angels are 15-13. The Stars are 12-16, and Grissom is still hitting .400-something, still not tired.
