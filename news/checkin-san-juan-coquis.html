The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

San Juan's Bullpen Was Supposed to Be a Strength. It Has the Worst ERA in the ABL

The Coquis came in with an A- pitching staff built around Mike Mussina and closer Steve Karsay. The bullpen ERA is 7.35, and San Juan has been outscored by 31 runs. It's still 20-20.

San Juan Coquis · 20-20 · 214 runs scored, 245 allowed

San Juan's case was all pitching. The preseason consensus ranked the Coquis fifth despite a C- offense that looked decidedly average, because the staff got an A-: Mike Mussina at the top, Steve Karsay closing and Jay Witasick right behind him in a very solid bullpen.

Instead the bullpen has been the worst in the ABL. Its 7.35 ERA is more than a run worse than anyone else's. Karsay, a 2.35 ERA pitcher in 2001, has six saves, four blown saves, three losses and a 7.50 ERA. Witasick is at 7.15, Roberto Hernandez at 7.71 and Jim Parque at 11.02. As a staff, San Juan ranks 18th of 20 in ERA.

The rotation

The starters have mostly held up their end, with a 5.08 ERA. Mussina is 5-2 with a 3.70 ERA and 10 walks in 65.2 innings. He threw a complete game against Pittsburgh on April 27 and struck out 10 in Nashville on Monday, though he's also allowed 11 home runs. Adam Eaton has the best FIP on the staff, 4.11. On April 8 he struck out nine without a walk against Southside, and five days later he threw a 10-strikeout, three-hit shutout in Tampa Bay. Felix Diaz, 21, has the best ERA among the starters, 3.50. The back end, which looked merely respectable in March, has been worse than that: Sidney Ponson is at 7.87 and Mark Redman at 6.75.

Decidedly average

The offense has been about what was expected. It ranks 14th in runs per game and 16th in OPS. J.D. Drew, Marty Cordova and Todd Walker have been three of the team's four best hitters, along with center fielder Alex Escobar, who has eight homers. Drew has a .384 on-base percentage and a 119 OPS+. Cordova has nine home runs, three of them in one game, an all-time slugfest in Anaheim on April 2 that San Juan won 15-12 in 13 innings. Walker is hitting .327, though he missed 12 days starting April 27. The weak spots are up the middle. Shortstops Brent Butler and Osvaldo Fernando have OPS+ marks of 86 and 48, and third baseman Michael Cuddyer is at 67.

The record

A -31 run differential usually buys 17 or 18 wins in 40 games. San Juan has 20, helped by a 5-4 record in one-run games. Most of the damage came in early May, when the Coquis went 2-8 from May 1 to 11 and were outscored 88-49 for the month, including a sweep in Cleveland in which they gave up 36 runs and scored 10. They came in as one of the league's motivation risks, and that stretch didn't help. They won the last two in Nashville and sit fifth in the East, three games out. Next come three in Salt Lake, Baltimore at home and three in San Diego.
