The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

San Juan's "Very Solid" Bullpen Has the Worst ERA in the ABL

Alexander gave the Coquis an A- on pitching, built around Mike Mussina and closer Steve Karsay. The bullpen ERA is 7.35, and San Juan has been outscored by 31 runs. It's still 20-20.

San Juan Coquis · 20-20 · 214 runs scored, 245 allowed

Alexander's case for San Juan was all pitching. He ranked the Coquis fifth even though he graded the offense C- ("the overall effect is decidedly average"), because the staff got an A-. "It's a top of the line pitching staff here, with the big headliners as Mussina and Steve Karsay closing." The bullpen, "with Karsay as the closer, and Jay Witasick right behind him, is very solid."

The bullpen has been the worst in the ABL. Its ERA is 7.35, more than a run worse than any other team's. Karsay has six saves, four blown saves, three losses and a 7.50 ERA. His ERA in 2001 was 2.35. Witasick has a 7.15 ERA. Roberto Hernandez is at 7.71 and Jim Parque at 11.02. The team ERA ranks 18th of 20.

The rotation

The starters, as a group, have held up their end. Their ERA is 5.08. Mike Mussina is 5-2 with a 3.70 ERA and 10 walks in 65.2 innings. He threw a complete game against Pittsburgh on April 27, and on Monday in Nashville he struck out 10. He has also allowed 11 home runs. Adam Eaton has the best FIP on the staff (4.11). On April 8 he struck out nine without a walk against Southside, according to the roundup. Five days later he threw a 10-strikeout, three-hit shutout in Tampa Bay, which the trends piece also highlighted. Felix Diaz, 21, wasn't in Alexander's preview. He has a 3.50 ERA, the best among the starters. Alexander called the rest of the rotation "merely respectable." Sidney Ponson (7.87) and Mark Redman (6.75) have been worse than that.

Decidedly average

The offense has been about what he said. It ranks 14th in runs per game and 16th in OPS. The three players he named have been three of the team's four best hitters, along with center fielder Alex Escobar, who has eight home runs. J.D. Drew has a .384 on-base percentage and a 119 OPS+. Marty Cordova has nine home runs. Todd Walker is hitting .327, though he missed 12 days starting April 27. The wildest game came on April 2 in Anaheim: San Juan won 15-12 in 13 innings, and Cordova hit three home runs. The roundup called it "an all-time slugfest." The weak spots are where Alexander expected, "up the middle." Shortstops Brent Butler and Osvaldo Fernando have OPS+ marks of 86 and 48, and third baseman Michael Cuddyer is at 67.

The record

A -31 run differential usually produces about 17 or 18 wins in 40 games. San Juan has 20, thanks in part to a 5-4 record in one-run games. Most of the damage came in early May. The Coquis went 2-8 from May 1 to 11 and were outscored 88-49 for the month. That included a three-game sweep in Cleveland in which they were outscored 36-10. The personalities report listed San Juan among its "motivation risks." The Coquis won the last two in Nashville and are fifth in the East, three games out. Next come three in Salt Lake, three at home against Baltimore and three in San Diego.
