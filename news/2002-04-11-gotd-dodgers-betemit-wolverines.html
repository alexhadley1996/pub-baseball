MLB · 2002-04-11 · By Hal Brennan, Diamond Daily

Nine Zeroes, Then Everybody Wakes Up

Mickey Callaway and Denny Neagle traded zeroes for nine innings. Detroit scored in the 10th and 11th, the Dodgers answered both times with two outs, and Wilson Betemit's sacrifice fly in the 12th made it 3-2 and five straight wins for Los Angeles.

LOS ANGELES — The Dodgers have Pedro Martinez, and everybody knows it. They also have Mickey Callaway, and on Thursday night 32,328 people at Dodger Stadium found out.

Callaway, a 27-year-old right-hander who spent most of his career shuttling between Triple-A and the back of somebody’s bullpen, held the Detroit Wolverines to two hits over 7⅓ scoreless innings. Denny Neagle matched him most of the way for Detroit. Nobody scored for nine innings. Then everybody did, a little, and the Dodgers won 3-2 in 12 on Wilson Betemit’s sacrifice fly.

“I’m not Pedro,” Callaway said. “Nobody’s Pedro. I just try to be the guy after him who doesn’t screw it up.”

The duel

The Wolverines lineup Prior faced on Opening Day, the one with Carlos Delgado, Troy Glaus and the 42-year-old Tim Raines, never figured Callaway out. He struck out six and walked two.

Neagle was nearly as good. He went 6⅓ innings, gave up six hits, struck out five and didn’t allow a run.

“Two guys who know how to pitch,” Jim Tracy said. “Nobody threw 98. Nobody had to.”

The 10th and the 11th

With two outs in the top of the 10th, pinch-hitter Todd Sears, hitting .091, tripled into the gap in right-center off Brandon Villafuerte to bring home Detroit’s first run.

The Dodgers answered in the bottom half. Three straight singles off Mike Jackson loaded the bases with nobody out. Jim Mecir came on, struck out David Segui and got Juan Gonzalez on a fly ball too shallow to score anyone, and then Chris Basak, a 23-year-old who’d come into the game at third, singled up the middle. 1-1.

In the 11th it happened again. Raines doubled with two outs to give Detroit a 2-1 lead, and in the bottom half, with two outs and runners on second and third, Tomas Perez singled off Jeremy Affeldt to tie it.

“You get two outs and you think, OK, one more,” Detroit manager Phil Garner said. “Then they get a hit. Then you get two outs again. I’d rather they’d just hit a home run in the first and get it over with.”

Betemit

Affeldt stayed in for the 12th and didn’t get out of it. Basak singled with one out and Darren Bragg walked. Garner brought in Jimmy Anderson, who walked Mike Hubbard to load the bases, and Betemit, the 20-year-old shortstop who homered on Opening Day and came in hitting .161, lifted a fly ball to left. Basak tagged and scored easily.

“Not my best night with the bat,” Betemit said. “But I’ll take that one.”

Justin Kaye got the win with a scoreless 12th. The Dodgers are 9-3 and have won five straight. Pedro’s ERA is 1.48. Callaway’s is now 1.21.

“Don’t tell Pedro,” Callaway said.
