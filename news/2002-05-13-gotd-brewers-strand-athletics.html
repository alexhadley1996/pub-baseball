MLB · 2002-05-13 · By Rob Lindqvist, Diamond Daily

Bases Loaded, Nobody Home

Oakland loaded the bases three times against Milwaukee, scored twice on walks, and lost 3-2 when Tyler Houston singled home the winner with two outs in the ninth. The A's have lost four straight and now share first place with San Francisco.

MILWAUKEE — There’s a version of Monday night’s box score that tells you Oakland should have won by five. The A’s got eight hits and eight walks. They had the bases loaded with nobody out in the third inning and with one out in the ninth. Tom Glavine gave them seven innings and allowed one run.

The other version is the one that counts. Milwaukee 3, Oakland 2, on Tyler Houston’s two-out single in the bottom of the ninth, and the best team in the MLB West has now lost four in a row.

“You can’t keep giving a team like that extra outs and expect to get away with it,” Brewers manager Davey Lopes said. “We gave them a lot. They gave a few back.”

The walks

Both Oakland runs came on bases-loaded walks from Aaron Harang, which tells you most of what you need to know about the 24-year-old’s evening. Corey Koskie drew one with two outs in the second to tie the game 1-1, after Milwaukee had scratched out a run in the first on Marcus Giles’ groundout. Orlando Merced drew another in the fourth to put Oakland ahead 2-1.

Harang walked six in five innings and threw 109 pitches. He also struck out eight, and in the third inning he did the thing that kept the Brewers alive. Albert Pujols doubled, Brian Giles walked and Merced singled, and Oakland had the bases loaded with nobody out. Keith Lockhart grounded out without a run scoring. Ramon Castro struck out. Shawon Dunston struck out.

“That’s the inning,” Lopes said. “If they get three there, it’s a different night.”

Glavine and the bullpen

Glavine, who came in 5-1 with a 2.86 ERA, was the steadier starter but not a perfect one. He loaded the bases in the first on a single and two walks, and the Brewers got only one run out of it. After that only one Milwaukee runner reached third against him until the seventh, when Jason Bartlett singled and Julio Franco doubled. Glavine got Manny Ramirez on a fly ball and Marcus Giles on a grounder to leave the score 2-1.

The Milwaukee bullpen kept it there. Alan Embree threw two hitless innings and John Bale one more, and the Brewers came to bat in the eighth still down a run.

John Franco, 41, struck out Houston and Rob Mackowiak to start the inning. Then Jason LaRue singled, his third hit of the night, and Oakland manager Art Howe went to Bob Wickman. Craig Paquette doubled, and LaRue came all the way around from first to tie it at 2.

The ninth

Oakland had one more chance, and it looked a lot like the third inning. Joe Beimel got Koskie on a fly ball, then gave up a single to Denny Hocking, who finished 3-for-5, and a double to Pujols, his second of the game. With runners on second and third, Brian Giles walked to load the bases. Merced grounded out without the run scoring, and Sal Fasano, pinch-hitting, flied out.

Wickman stayed in for the bottom half and got two quick outs before Ramirez singled. Howe brought in Ryan Franklin. Marcus Giles singled Ramirez to third, and Houston, who had struck out twice, singled to end it.

“I’d been terrible all night,” Houston said. “You just want one more shot. That’s the whole game, getting one more shot.”

Where it leaves them

The A’s are 26-15, still tied for first with the Seals, but the four-game skid has erased the two-game lead they held on Friday. Howe didn’t dwell on the walks, which were Harang’s problem, or the loaded bases, which were his.

“We had our chances,” he said. “Two of them with the bases loaded and nobody out or one out. You don’t get those every night. We’ll get them again, and we’d better do something with them.”

Milwaukee is 19-22. Beimel, who faced six batters and got three outs, picked up the win.
