The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Austin Has the ABL's Highest Relegation Odds, and the Pitching Isn't Why

The worry about the Violets was the pitching after Billy Wagner. The two innings eaters have been the best part of the team, Wagner has been almost perfect, and the offense ranks 19th in the ABL.

Austin Violets · 18-22 · 169 runs scored, 209 allowed

Austin came into the season getting old, and it looked like a team approaching a real cliff. The preseason consensus ranked the Violets 13th, and the projections had them at 75-76. The top of the lineup looked strong, with Mike Piazza starring alongside Mark Grace, Jay Payton and Jose Cruz. The pitching after Billy Wagner and Jeff Weaver looked ready to take a real nose dive.

The cliff might be here. Austin is 18-22, ninth in the Western Conference and eight games out, with a -40 run differential, and the site gives it a 55.5% chance of relegation, the highest in the ABL. It just hasn't come from where anyone expected.

The pitching held up

The nose dive hasn't happened. Shane Reynolds and Jason Bere, the two innings eaters who were supposed to provide average value, have been the best part of the team. Reynolds, 34, has a 2.65 ERA and has walked eight batters in 51 innings. Bere has a 3.32 ERA and 49 strikeouts. Three weeks in, the staff was a mess outside Reynolds. Since then Bere and Jason Beverlin have joined him. Beverlin allowed one run over 8.2 innings in Salt Lake on April 25, then threw eight shutout innings with nine strikeouts against Baltimore five days later. The team ERA ranks seventh in the ABL. And Wagner has been nearly perfect: seven saves in seven chances, a 1.06 ERA, 22 strikeouts and no walks.

The soft spots are Weaver and the middle innings. Weaver has a 5.70 ERA, a season that includes 7.1 scoreless innings with 10 strikeouts against Nashville and a start in which Salt Lake scored seven runs in three innings. The bullpen in front of Wagner has a 5.54 ERA, and Nate Teut's is 11.09.

The lineup didn't

The offense is the problem. Austin ranks 19th in the ABL in runs per game and 17th in home runs, and it's been shut out five times. Piazza, a .957 OPS hitter in 2001, has five homers and a 95 OPS+. He's had his moments, a two-homer game early on and a home run in the 12-inning win over Cincinnati on April 9, but not enough of them. Payton has an 84 OPS+. Grace has kept up his end: he's 37, hitting .381, and has struck out seven times in 168 plate appearances. Cruz, at 112, has been the other productive regular, with a 4-for-5, one-homer game against Cincinnati on April 8.

The middle infield was completely bereft of talent coming into the year, and Delino DeShields has a 61 OPS+, -0.7 WAR and seven caught stealings. Luis Alicea, 36, has been a surprise, hitting .341. Third base hasn't helped: Brandon Inge and Dean Palmer are at 64 and 80.

The slide

The Violets opened 3-0 with two extra-inning wins. On April 6 Pittsburgh beat them 13-0 on 17 hits, with the bottom of the order going berserk. From April 13 to 20 they lost eight straight, then answered with five straight wins and went 8-2 over a 10-game stretch. Since then they're 4-7, including four straight losses that ended with Sunday's 9-0 defeat, the game in which Anaheim's Austin Kearns hit for the cycle. Payton had four hits in Monday's 6-4 win in Anaheim. Next come three with Kansas City, three in New Orleans and a home series against conference-leading Southside.
