40-Game Check-In · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Violets, After the 8-Game Skid

Austin Violets, 18-22 through 40 games

Eight losses in a row is a long time. It is long enough for a team to try everything, and the Violets had from Apr 13 to Apr 20 to do so. They are 18-22 now.

Through 40 games they were 18-22, scoring 169 runs and allowing 209 (-40). As of the May 13 export they are 9th in the Western Conference, 8 games back, and 15th of 20 in the ABL. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 16 wins in 40 games. They are 5-5 in one-run games and 3-0 in extra innings.

What is driving it

The best bat has belonged to Mark Grace, 37, who is hitting .381/.452/.558 with 4 home runs and 25 RBI in 168 plate appearances. Jose Cruz Jr. leads the club with 7 home runs. On the bases, Delino DeShields is 12-for-19 stealing. As a team they are hitting .279/.351/.409, 18th of 20 in the ABL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 32 home runs.

Shane Reynolds leads the starters with a 2.65 ERA over 51.0 innings (26 K, 8 BB). Jason Bere (3.32 ERA in 57.0 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.76 ERA, 7th of 20 in the ABL; starters 4.38, relievers 5.54. Closing has mostly meant Billy Wagner: 7 saves, 1.06 ERA.

Then there is Brandon Inge, whose OPS has gone from .453 in 2001 to .660 now. Some of that is the sample. The question is how much. On the other side of the ledger, Mike Piazza is at .291/.352/.441, a long way from the .957 OPS he managed in 2001. In the rotation, Jason Middlebrook has a 6.55 ERA over 33.0 innings.

The fine print is fun here. Mark Grace has walked 19 times and struck out 7, a ratio that belongs to a different, more patient century. Home crowds have averaged 22,454 over 20 dates in a park that holds 54,816, about 41% full. There is room to be convinced.

The stakes

The ABL is the middle rung, so it is the only league here where you can fall and climb in the same breath: each conference winner gets a bye, second and third play in, both finalists of the American Championship are promoted to MLB, and the bottom three are relegated to the FBL. They sit 9th in the Western Conference, 8 games back of the Black Sox. The less fun line: the site has them on relegation pace, 2 games behind the Stars, the nearest club above the drop.

The best night was May 8, an 8-2 win over the Royeaux. The worst was Apr 6, a 13-0 loss to the Pirates. Their best 10-game stretch went 8-2 (Apr 21 to May 1); their worst, 1-9 (Apr 10 to Apr 20).

Looking ahead

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 4 of them at home, against the Monarchs, the Athletics, the Black Sox, the Coquis, opponents with a combined 227-173 record (.568). That is a real test. One more thing to watch: Mark Grace is on the milestone watch, closing on 2,500 hits. A quarter of a season tells you what a team is capable of. The other three quarters tell you what it is. For the Violets, the interesting part starts now.
