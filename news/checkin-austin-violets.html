The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Austin Has the ABL's Highest Relegation Odds, and the Pitching Isn't Why

Alexander worried about the Violets' pitching after Billy Wagner. The two "innings eaters" he called average have been the best part of the team, Wagner has been almost perfect, and the offense ranks 19th in the ABL.

Austin Violets · 18-22 · 169 runs scored, 209 allowed

Alexander's March write-up on Austin had a warning in it: "they're getting old and Austin looks like they're approaching a real cliff." He ranked the Violets 13th in the ABL and predicted 75-76. He liked the top of the lineup, "Mike Piazza starring alongside co-stars Mark Grace, Jay Payton, and Jose Cruz," and thought the pitching would "take a real nose dive" after Billy Wagner and Jeff Weaver.

The cliff might be coming. Austin is 18-22, ninth in the Western Conference and eight games out, with a -40 run differential. The site gives the Violets a 55.5% chance of relegation, the highest in the ABL.

The pitching held up

The nose dive hasn't happened. Shane Reynolds and Jason Bere, the two "innings eaters" Alexander expected to provide "average value," have been the best part of the team. Reynolds, 34, has a 2.65 ERA and has walked eight batters in 51 innings. Bere has a 3.32 ERA and 49 strikeouts. At three weeks Alexander wrote, "Outside of Shane Reynolds, the pitching staff has been a mess." Since then, Bere and Jason Beverlin have joined him. Beverlin allowed one run over 8.2 innings in Salt Lake on April 25 and threw eight shutout innings with nine strikeouts against Baltimore five days later. The rotation's ERA is 4.38, and the team ERA ranks seventh in the ABL. Wagner has seven saves in seven chances, a 1.06 ERA, 22 strikeouts and no walks.

Weaver, the other pitcher Alexander named, has a 5.70 ERA. His season includes one start of 7.1 scoreless innings with 10 strikeouts against Nashville and another in which Salt Lake scored seven runs in three innings. The bullpen in front of Wagner has a 5.54 ERA. Nate Teut has an 11.09 ERA.

The lineup didn't

Austin ranks 19th in the ABL in runs per game and 17th in home runs, and it's been shut out five times. Piazza, the star, is hitting .291/.352/.441 with five home runs and a 95 OPS+. In 2001 he had a .957 OPS. The week-three notes mentioned "a two-homer game," and he homered in the 12-inning win over Cincinnati on April 9. Payton has an 84 OPS+. Grace has kept up his end of the deal: he's 37, hitting .381/.452/.558, and has seven strikeouts in 168 plate appearances. Cruz, at 112, has been the other productive regular. He went 4-for-5 with a homer against Cincinnati on April 8.

Alexander said the middle infield was "completely bereft of any kind of talent." Delino DeShields has a 61 OPS+ and -0.7 WAR and has been caught stealing seven times. Luis Alicea, 36, has been a surprise at .341/.421/.407. Third base hasn't helped either: Brandon Inge and Dean Palmer are at 64 and 80.

The slide

The Violets opened 3-0 with two extra-inning wins. On April 6 Pittsburgh beat them 13-0, a game the roundup said came with "17 hits — bottom of the order went berserk." From April 13 to 20 they lost eight straight. They answered with five straight wins and went 8-2 over a 10-game stretch. Since then they're 4-7. That includes four straight losses, the last a 9-0 loss on Sunday in which Anaheim's Austin Kearns hit for the cycle, before Monday's 6-4 win in Anaheim, when Payton had four hits. Next come three games with Kansas City, then three in New Orleans and a home series against conference-leading Southside.
