April 14-21: Who Is Trending Up, Who Is Fraying

The Dodgers can’t get on base, Detroit and Washington steady themselves, and a league-by-league look at the third week.

Week three is when early luck starts to separate from early quality, and this week it separated in both directions. Detroit and Washington found their footing, Dallas and Milwaukee started to look competent, and the Dodgers, Diamondbacks and Blue Jays began to fray, each in its own particular way. League by league, here is who is trending which way, and why.

MLB

Detroit and Washington have steadied themselves after sluggish starts, and Dallas and Milwaukee have looked more competent week-to-week. At the top, New York and Miami have largely sustained their early runs, with San Francisco and Oakland continuing to play like true contenders. On the other end, the Dodgers, Diamondbacks, and Blue Jays have begun to fray—each for slightly different reasons.

Teams trending down

Los Angeles Dodgers: empty OBP, survival pitching

The Dodgers simply can’t get on base. They’re sitting on an 80 wRC+, and most nights the offense feels like it’s trying to win 3–2 games without ever putting pressure on the pitcher.

The pitching, weirdly, hasn’t been the main culprit—it’s been okay overall—but it has wobbled during key series (notably against Miami, New York, and Seattle). Mickey Callaway has already surrendered five homers in two starts, and Dave Mlicki has had a couple rough outings as well. The rotation has still gotten real value from Pedro Martínez and José Lima, but the lineup is thin: among regulars, Juan González and Jason Varitek have been the only consistently above-average bats. The bottom has been a sinkhole, with rookies Shane Victorino and Wilson Betemit looking overmatched by big-league pitching.

Arizona Diamondbacks: pitching is the issue, not talent

For Arizona, the problem has been run prevention. Pitching has been the main drag, even if the underlying indicators hint at a better future: a 91 FIP- suggests they should be closer to average (or better) going forward. The defense has been roughly average overall (you’ve got them around +30), and the offense has enough pop to stay afloat.

The issue is that nothing is connecting on the bases—Arizona hasn’t consistently turned baserunners into innings where the lineup can cash in. They can hit, but they haven’t been able to sustain rallies.

Toronto Blue Jays: rough staff, strong lineup

Toronto’s struggles have mostly been on the mound. Virtually every starter has been below average so far, though Brandon Webb and Jeremy Bonderman have flashed promising peripherals thanks to strikeout ability. Kevin Olson, however, has been a major problem—walking more hitters than he strikes out, and trending in the wrong direction as the season progresses.

That’s made it easy to miss the good news: the lineup is real. Dmitri Young and Jim Edmonds have both started hot, and Edmonds just signed a six-year extension, which is the kind of move that says, “We think this core is worth building around.”

Defense-driven success stories

Philadelphia Phillies and San Francisco Seals: turning contact into outs

Philadelphia and San Francisco have each leaned heavily on defense. Both clubs have been surviving with roughly average FIP-ish pitching profiles, yet sitting around ~110 ERA+ territory because they keep converting balls in play into outs.

For San Francisco, the defense has shown up in the “hard-to-get” zones: Jacque Jones, Bengie Molina, and Jimmy Rollins have been especially effective at tracking down remote balls—your note about them converting 17 remote chances (nearly twice what the rest of the league is doing) is the kind of weird, specific early-season stat that screams “this team is locked in.” Add that to a legitimately strong offense, and it’s why they keep stacking wins.

Philadelphia’s defense has been consistently excellent across the board—likely, even, unlikely, and remote balls—led by Mike Cameron in center, Roger Cedeño in left, and Joe Crede at third. It’s not just highlight-reel range; it’s quiet, constant competence.

Weird offenses and worse ones

Dallas Griffins: two hitters and vibes

Dallas has had a strange offensive season. Lew Ford has been legitimately good (.867 OPS), and Jeremy Reed has contributed in his opportunities, but the bottom half of the lineup has been brutal. In your words, they’ve basically canceled themselves out—about as much positive WAR as negative WAR on offense, which is a tough way to live for six months.

Detroit Wolverines: walks, strikeouts, and a defense-shaped hole

Detroit might have the league’s worst lineup in terms of overall production. Even their “power” hasn’t arrived yet: Carlos Delgado has only three homers, and the overall hit tool has been undermined by miserable BABIP luck.

What’s especially frustrating is that the Wolverines are doing one valuable offensive thing: they’re walking a ton—second-best walk rate in the league. But they’re also striking out 23% of the time, about four percentage points higher than anyone else, and you can’t take walks if you keep ending plate appearances without the ball in play.

And while the offense hasn’t been good, the bigger anchor has been the defense: Detroit’s league-worst defense has turned a tolerable pitching situation into a painful one. You’ve got multiple culprits: outfield range issues (including Mark Kotsay/Brzezinski and Tim Raines), plus infield struggles from Felipe López and Terry Shumpert—with Shumpert especially “playing his way” into negative value.

Teams mashing (and not always running well)

San Francisco: scoring without walks

The Seals have generated more offense than almost anyone despite poor baserunning. They don’t walk much, but they’ve put up a ton of runs anyway because they’re hitting everything: Rondell White, Tony Bautista, Bengie Molina, Adrian Brown, and Andrés Galarraga have all mashed, and the team’s batting averages have done the heavy lifting.

Houston Astros: walks + contact + Sammy’s share of the bombs

Houston has scored the second-most runs in the league, built on a rare combo: low strikeouts and high walks, which gives them enough traffic to survive cold spells and bad BABIP stretches. And then there’s the sledgehammer: they’ve hit 30 home runs, best in the league—and, yes, as you note, Sammy Sosa has hit about a third of them.

Baserunning snapshot

The baserunning metrics have been funny early: it feels like everyone is below average on UBR. Still, a few teams have separated themselves:

  • Seattle, Milwaukee, and Oakland have been “less bad” (Seattle especially, with one of the league’s fastest lineups).
  • Arizona has been the slowest team by impact, with Robin Ventura in particular costing them runs, plus Matt Lawton.
  • Colorado has extracted the most value from steals, powered by Johnny Gomes (7 SB) and José Canseco (5 SB).

Pitching: the good, the bad, the Sox

Best starts: Chicago Cubs and New York Yankees

The Cubs and Yankees have been the strongest pitching staffs early—about 25% better than league average, while Boston has been about 25% worse.

Chicago’s rotation has been steady, but the bullpen has been the real story. Tim Worrell and Oded Vega have both been excellent (sub-3 ERAs in meaningful innings), and Brandon Kolb has been nails as well (1.2 FIP, per your note). Odalis Pérez has taken some lumps lately, but you’re framing it correctly: a lot of it looks like sequencing/defense, and the overall line is still strong (ERA down around 3.03).

New York’s staff has been led by Brian Toberg and Chris Benson, with Chuck Smith and Greg Maddux doing their jobs up top. Toberg’s early-season statline is pristine: three walks, one homer allowed, 29 innings. In the bullpen, Cliff Lee, Mike DeJean, and Chad Bradford have stabilized things—even without big strikeout totals. Bradford, in particular: 12.2 IP, 0 HR allowed, and a 2.84 RA.

St. Louis: run prevention via strand rate

The Cardinals’ starters have posted sparkling ERAs despite middling FIPs, largely because they’ve stranded runners at an unusually high rate. Zack Greinke has been homer-prone early, but a very low BABIP against has kept the damage contained.

Boston Red Sox: strikeouts, and then everything else

Boston has been the disaster staff so far: 5.47 team ERA, roughly 0.6 runs worse than the next-worst team. The frustrating part is they’ve actually struck guys out—but the walk rate and homer rate have been catastrophic. The defense hasn’t had much range, but they have turned a lot of double plays, which is about the only thing keeping the wheels from coming fully off.

Randy Johnson has been solid overall, but even he’s been vulnerable to the long ball. The deeper problem is the rest of the staff: Oswaldo Verdugo, Brian Sikorsky, and Colby Lewis have been crushed—12 homers allowed in 11 starts, roughly 2 HR/9 territory. In the bullpen, Edwin Jackson has been the lone bright spot as a true HR-prevention arm; otherwise it’s been too many walks, too many mistakes in the zone, and not enough margin.

Miami Marlins: best record, best timing

Miami having the best record in baseball is still a little mysterious on pure “how good are they?” indicators, but your explanation is compelling: Win Probability Added. They lead the league in WPA for both hitters and pitchers—meaning the timing has been immaculate, even if their total WAR trails teams like the Seals.

The cleanest signal is their high-leverage hitting. League-wide, you’ve got hitters around a .760 OPS in those spots. Miami is at 1.143, with an absurd .417 batting average in high leverage. That’s not sustainable forever, but it explains exactly how they’re banking wins now: they’re getting the big hit, not just the small edges.

FBL

Richmond Flying Squirrels (1–7)
 Richmond had a brutal week, going 1–7 and getting swept by both Hoboken and Sacramento. The core issue has been a lack of offensive production. A late bright spot came in an 8–5 win over Carolina, which could be a sign of life going forward.

One consistent bright spot has been C Greg Colbrunn, who still owns an average over .400 and a slugging percentage over .800, making him one of the league’s most productive hitters so far. Eric Hinske, on the other hand, has been struggling badly.

Santo Domingo Gallos (5–3)
 Santo Domingo stayed on track with a 5–3 stretch, but nearly every game has felt tight. They haven’t shown much “blowout gear” yet, and the lineup has been top-heavy. Luis Polonia has been excellent (OPS over 1.000), but they’ve also had four regulars sitting below a 90 OPS+, which has capped their scoring.

Carolina Disco Turkeys & Orlando Juicers
 Both clubs have been stuck in a rough patch, with pitching at the center of it.

  • Orlando has had a particularly ugly run, allowing 28 runs in their last three games. There are real building blocks — Matt Williams has been a legitimate middle-of-the-order force (OPS .914), Alfonso Soriano has looked strong, and Ichiro Suzuki has nudged his average up to .286 — but the offense still hasn’t been consistently threatening, and the rotation is thin beyond Brian Lawrence.
  • Carolina has been even shakier on the mound. John Burkett is the only starter with an ERA+ above 100; the rest of the rotation is living in the 6–7 ERA neighborhood. Offensively, Chad Tracy, Bubba Trammell, and Pablo Sánchez have been good, but Terrence Long, Ken Griffey Jr., and Mike Lieberthal have all been major disappointments.

Hoboken Zephyrs (6–2)
 Hoboken went 6–2 and rattled off six straight wins, driven by a lineup that has been both deep and hot. C Mike Kincaid is still hitting over .400, and Michael Young and Vernon Wells have also been central to the team’s offensive surge.

Savannah Bananas (6–2, now .500)
 Savannah also went 6–2 to climb back to .500, including a sweep of Carolina. The club has gotten steady work from Bill Mueller and Jose Vidro, while the pitching has been quietly strong:

  • Steve Kline still hasn’t allowed a run in his first nine innings.
  • Barry Zito is off to a solid start (3.51 ERA).

Louisville Bats (12–8)
 Louisville continues to look like a legitimate promotion contender. The pitching has been stable:

  • Roger Clemens has been excellent.
  • Paul Quantrill has been reliable in the closer role.

But the real story is the top of the lineup: Wendell Magee has been on fire (193 OPS+) — and that’s with Barry Larkin and Andruw Jones both still playing below their standards.

Monterrey Sultanas vs. Vancouver Mounties: The heavyweight pairing
 Monterrey has come back to earth a bit, but they’re still 15–4. Vancouver has surged into the same tier, sitting 13–7 and matching Monterrey for one of the best Pythagorean profiles in the league.

Vancouver is in the middle of a massive series against Monterrey and just took a statement game 17–5, highlighted by a huge two-run single from Todd Hollandsworth, who has been one of the league’s best all-around players so far. Vancouver’s rebound comes after a shocking sweep at the hands of Albuquerque, where they scored only five runs total — but they responded by dominating Eugene and then jumping right back into the Monterrey series looking like themselves.

Monterrey’s offense has still been powered by a clear early-season MVP: Jeff Bagwell, who has been absurd through 20 games: 8 HR, 9 doubles, 13 walks, and a 263 OPS+. Alex Ochoa has been nearly as valuable, and Rob Quinlan has also been excellent. On the mound, Bronson Arroyo has been rough, but the rest of the rotation has held together better — especially Don Lemon and Chris Capuano.

Albuquerque Spirit
 Albuquerque had a very strong week. Darren Erstad has climbed to a 131 OPS+, but the real identity of the team is run prevention. The rotation has been excellent at the top (notably Víctor Santos and Alan Benes), and the bullpen has been dominant: Damaso Marte, Doug Brocail, Ian Snell, and John Ennis have allowed just one run combined in 35 innings.

Sacramento Solons (6–2)
 Sacramento went 6–2 and jumped to second in the West and second in the FBL in Pythagorean record, continuing a quietly excellent start.

ABL

Week theme

  • Bad weeks: Austin, Minnesota, New York, Tampa
  • Great weeks: Las Vegas, Portland, Southside, Nashville, New Orleans

Austin Violets
 Outside of Shane Reynolds, the pitching staff has been a mess. The offense has been decent up the middle, and Mike Piazza had a two-homer game, but overall the team has looked shaky and inconsistent.

Minnesota Loons
 Minnesota’s offense has been thin outside of Shay Hillenbrand, David Ortiz, and Brian Simmons. Rickey Henderson is hitting .152 with one HR, and Chipper Jones has zero homers and a 50 wRC+ start. The club did extend Darryl Kile on a four-year deal; the rotation has been solid, but the bullpen has been uneven outside of Mike Koplove.

New York Knights
 The Knights have struggled to score since the chaos of their 12–11 loss to New Orleans. They beat San Juan 11–2, but otherwise they’ve scored more than three runs only once in nine games. Lance Berkman and Carlos Guillén have been excellent, but the rest of the offense has collapsed — especially Ramon E. Martinez and 19-year-old Jhonny Peralta, who together have only three extra-base hits and are sitting at -1.4 WAR.

Tampa Bay Rays
 Tampa won the Caribbean series against San Juan last weekend, then immediately lost three straight one-run games to Salt Lake. After a blowout win over Las Vegas (11–4), the offense completely froze, scoring only two runs total in three games against Las Vegas and Baltimore. J.J. Davis and Juan Pierre have been strong, but 18-year-old 1B Hanley Ramírez has a 29 OPS+ start and CF Alex Sanchez is at 44 OPS+. The rotation (especially Ben Sheets and Dave Bush) has been excellent; the bullpen has been the weak link.

Las Vegas Gamblers
 Las Vegas has rolled behind steady pitching and competent offense. Jeff Conine has struggled, but Kevin Millar, Desi Relaford, and Mike Bordick have all hit well, and Millar has brought real power. Kevin Brown has a strong “looks-better-than-the-ERA” profile (excellent peripherals) and has been among the league’s most valuable pitchers. Brandon Duckworth and Josh Towers have been solid as well, and the bullpen has held up, especially Mike Stanton and Solomon Torres.

Portland Beavers
 Portland’s bullpen has been the headline: Jim Brower and Troy Percival combined for 24 innings, allowing only three runs, and Eric Gagné has also been excellent despite occasional control issues. The rotation outside of Steve Paris has been shaky. Offensively, the outfield has been strong (Ross Gload, Geoff Jenkins, Marlon Byrd), but overall the lineup has been more average than explosive. A big series with Kansas City and New Orleans is looming.

Southside Black Sox
 Southside swept both the Knights and the Loons. Pitching has been the foundation, led by Brad Radke and a strong relief group (notably Terry Adams and Todd Stottlemyre). Offensively, Nick Johnson and Joe Randa have been excellent, though Randa has gotten a big BABIP assist, and CF Jason Ellison has benefited from that same wave. Pudge Rodríguez and Jorge Posada have been solid. The glaring issue: LF Jayson Bay has struck out 28 times in 20 games.

Nashville Stars
 After an awful start, Nashville has stabilized with nine wins in 12 games, built on a staff that can still implode. Francisco Campos finally posted a scoreless outing and trimmed his ERA to 11.70; Tim Redding still has a WHIP close to 3.00. The steadier arms have been Rheal Cormier and Miguel Bautista. Offensively, Bobby Kielty, Michael Tucker, and Milton Bradley have driven the rebound; Russell Branyan has been the weak spot.

New Orleans Athletics
 New Orleans has played much better since their wild 19–8 win over Las Vegas on April 10, climbing to 11–9. The pitching staff has been the defining strength, led by Kerry Wood and Casey Fossum. The offense isn’t built for fireworks, but Raúl Mondesí, Nick Swisher, and Brian Roberts have been steady contributors.
