The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Salt Lake's Rotation Has a 7.25 ERA, and the Bees Are 9-3 in One-Run Games

Salt Lake's pitching got an F in the preseason rankings, and its starters have the worst ERA in the ABL. Brad Fullmer, Jason Michaels and a 14-7 home record are keeping the Bees close.

Salt Lake Bees · 19-21 · 223 runs scored, 245 allowed

Salt Lake's preseason case was short and clear. The offense was extremely talented and young. The pitching got an F: very few innings last year from this group, and closer Ramiro Mendoza was the only one with half-decent stuff. The preseason projections spelled out what that would look like: a league-high 979 runs scored, 926 allowed and a 77-74 finish.

The pitching has been as bad as expected. Salt Lake's 5.97 team ERA is the worst in the ABL. The record has been better than it should be. The Bees are 19-21 with a -22 run differential, which usually means about 18 wins. They're eighth in the Western Conference, seven games back, and the site gives them a 33.0% chance of relegation.

Very few innings

The rotation's ERA is 7.25. All five starters are 24 or younger, and none has an ERA below 6.75. John Patterson is 2-6 and has walked 33 in 50.1 innings. Dan Wright is at 7.68, Luis de los Santos at 7.59, Steve Bechler at 7.31 and Pat Strange, 21, at 6.75. The starters have thrown only 195 innings, which has left the bullpen to cover 150.

It has handled the load well, with a 4.32 ERA. Mendoza has eight saves and a 1.80 ERA, as advertised. Scott Schoeneweis, who threw over 200 innings and struggled in 2001, has moved to relief and has a 2.16 ERA in 25 innings. That bullpen is a big reason Salt Lake is 9-3 in one-run games. From April 15 to 17 Tampa Bay lost three straight one-run games to the Bees, and two days later Salt Lake beat Baltimore on walk-offs on back-to-back days.

Extremely talented

The hitters have held up their end. Salt Lake ranks fourth in the ABL in OPS. Brad Fullmer is hitting .379 with 11 homers and a 179 OPS+, fourth-best among ABL regulars, and he homered twice in the April 20 walk-off over Baltimore. Jason Michaels is hitting .370, and on Friday he went 5-for-6 in a 17-9 win over New Orleans. Scott Rolen has a 120 OPS+, and his grand slam won the team's first game, an 8-6 win over Portland on April 5 after an 0-4 start. A.J. Pierzynski, a leader of the lineup coming in, has a .286 on-base percentage and hasn't drawn a walk in 140 plate appearances. Alex Cintron, 23, is hitting .361.

Home and away

Salt Lake, like Minnesota, came into the year with a reputation as a selfish clubhouse. Whatever it's like in there, the Bees are much better at home, 14-7, than on the road, 5-14. They've been swept in San Diego, outscored 30-8, and in Nashville. At home they swept Mexico City, scoring 29 runs, including a seven-homer game on April 9. Fittingly, the next six games are at home, three against San Juan and three against Las Vegas, before a trip to New York.
