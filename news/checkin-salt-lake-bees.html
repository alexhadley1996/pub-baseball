The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Salt Lake's Rotation Has a 7.25 ERA, and the Bees Are 9-3 in One-Run Games

Alexander gave Salt Lake's pitching an F, and its starters have the worst ERA in the ABL. Brad Fullmer, Jason Michaels and a 14-7 home record are keeping the Bees close.

Salt Lake Bees · 19-21 · 223 runs scored, 245 allowed

Alexander's Salt Lake preview was a short one with a clear thesis. The offense was "extremely talented and young." The pitching got an F: "very few innings last year from this group," and closer Ramiro Mendoza "is the only one with half decent stuff." His predicted standings explained what that combination would look like. "Salt Lake scores a league-high 979 runs in my numbers and still only finishes 77-74, because it allows 926."

The pitching has been as bad as he expected. Salt Lake's team ERA is 5.97, the worst in the ABL. Its record is better than its run differential. The Bees are 19-21 with a -22 run differential, which usually works out to about 18 wins. They're eighth in the Western Conference, seven games back, and the site gives them a 33.0% chance of relegation.

Very few innings

The rotation's ERA is 7.25. All five starters are 24 or younger, and none of them has an ERA below 6.75. John Patterson is 2-6 and has walked 33 batters in 50.1 innings. Dan Wright is at 7.68, Luis de los Santos at 7.59, Steve Bechler at 7.31 and Pat Strange, who is 21, at 6.75. The starters have thrown only 195 innings, so the bullpen has had to cover 150.

The bullpen has handled it well. Its ERA is 4.32. Mendoza has eight saves and a 1.80 ERA, as advertised. Scott Schoeneweis is the pitcher Alexander said "pitched over 200 but struggled" in 2001. He has moved to relief and has a 2.16 ERA in 25 innings. That bullpen is a big part of why Salt Lake is 9-3 in one-run games. The trends piece pointed to a stretch from April 15 to 17 in which Tampa Bay "lost three straight one-run games to Salt Lake." Two days later, the Bees beat Baltimore with walk-offs on back-to-back days.

Extremely talented

The hitters have done their part. Salt Lake ranks fourth in the ABL in OPS. Brad Fullmer, whom Alexander listed as one of the "big parts," is hitting .379/.458/.683 with 11 home runs and a 179 OPS+, the fourth-best OPS+ among ABL regulars. He hit two homers in the April 20 walk-off over Baltimore. Jason Michaels is hitting .370/.458/.571. On Friday he went 5-for-6 in a 17-9 win over New Orleans. Scott Rolen has a 120 OPS+. His grand slam won the team's first game of the season, an 8-6 win over Portland on April 5, after the Bees started 0-4. A.J. Pierzynski, whom Alexander named with Rolen as the leaders of the lineup, has a .286 on-base percentage and has drawn no walks in 140 plate appearances. Alex Cintron, 23, is hitting .361.

Home and away

The personalities report gave Salt Lake, like Minnesota, a "selfish" tag. Whatever the clubhouse is like, the Bees play much better at home, where they're 14-7. On the road they're 5-14. That includes three-game sweeps in San Diego, where they were outscored 30-8, and in Nashville. At home they swept Mexico City, scoring 29 runs. In the April 9 win, according to the roundup, they hit "seven HR total." Fittingly, the next six games are at home, three against San Juan and three against Las Vegas, before a trip to New York.
