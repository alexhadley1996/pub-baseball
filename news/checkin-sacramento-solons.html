The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Aubrey Huff Is Hitting .410 in Sacramento's "Pretty Mediocre Lineup"

Alexander ranked the Solons 10th and gave both the hitting and the pitching a C-. They're 25-15, one game out of first, with a +48 run differential and a rotation that has carried a shaky bullpen.

Sacramento Solons · 25-15 · 216 runs scored, 168 allowed

Alexander gave Sacramento matching C-minus grades in March. "Mark Loretta, Aubrey Huff, Kenny Lofton lead this pretty mediocre lineup," he wrote, and on the mound, "Dontrelle Willis shows tremendous potential and Octavio Dotel is a great closer, but there's not much there after that." He ranked the Solons 10th and predicted 75-76.

Sacramento is 25-15, one game behind Monterrey in the West Group, with the second-best record in the FBL. The week-three notes called it "a quietly excellent start." It hasn't been quiet since. The Solons' +48 run differential is second in the league, and the site gives them a 90.1% chance at the playoffs and a 59.3% chance of promotion.

Huff

Of Alexander's three lineup leaders, one has been great. Huff is hitting .410/.450/.660 with eight home runs, 32 RBI and a 184 OPS+. That's the highest average among FBL regulars, ahead of Richmond's Greg Colbrunn at .385. In consecutive games against Carolina on May 9 and 10, he went 3-for-4 with two homers and then 4-for-4. Sacramento lost both. Loretta is hitting .292 with a 102 OPS+. Lofton, 34, has a 74 OPS+ and a .218 average, though he's drawn 24 walks.

The rest of the lineup has been better than mediocre. Olmedo Saenz, David Dellucci and Hiram Bocachica each have an OPS+ of 117 or 118, and Saenz and Dellucci have eight home runs each. Saenz hit the ninth-inning solo homer that beat Honolulu 1-0 on April 9. The roundup said it "spoiled an incredible Jesse Foppert start." The Solons have hit 45 home runs, tied for fifth in the FBL, and haven't been shut out all season.

"Not much there after that"

Alexander was wrong about the rotation and right about the bullpen. Sacramento's starters have a 3.46 ERA, second in the FBL to Alaska's. Willis, the one starter he named, has the worst ERA of the five at 4.96. Brandon Knight is 6-2 with a 1.96 ERA, though he has 27 walks and a 3.93 FIP. On Monday he allowed one run over 8.1 innings in a 14-1 win in Charlotte. Terry Mulholland, 39, has a 2.98 ERA. The week-three notes said he "nearly threw a shutout against Richmond." On April 16 he went 8.1 scoreless innings. Darren Oliver hasn't allowed a home run in 43 innings. Kyle Lohse is at 3.96. The staff has five shutouts.

Dotel, the "great closer," has 13 saves. He also has three blown saves, a 5.89 ERA, six home runs allowed in 18.1 innings and a 7.23 FIP. On April 6 in Monterrey, Sacramento led 8-0 by the third inning. Alex Ochoa hit "a tying 3-run HR in the 9th off Octavio Dotel," and the Solons lost 10-9 in 10 innings. Their bullpen ERA is 4.94, and they're 1-3 in extra-inning games.

Lately

Sacramento's best stretch was a six-game winning streak from April 25 to 30, including a three-game sweep of Alaska at home. The Solons are 6-6 in May. They lost two of three at home to Carolina, then finished the weekend by winning the last two games in Charlotte by a combined 25-8. They're 14-6 at home. Next, Inland Empire visits for three games. Then the Solons play three in Vancouver against the West's third-place team before Oklahoma City comes to Sacramento.
