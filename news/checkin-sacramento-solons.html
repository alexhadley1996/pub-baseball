The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Aubrey Huff Is Hitting .410 in Sacramento's Pretty Mediocre Lineup

The preseason consensus ranked the Solons 10th, with a C- for both the hitting and the pitching. They're 25-15, a game out of first, with a +48 run differential and a rotation that has carried a shaky bullpen.

Sacramento Solons · 25-15 · 216 runs scored, 168 allowed

Sacramento came into the season with matching C-minus grades. Mark Loretta, Aubrey Huff and Kenny Lofton led a pretty mediocre lineup. Dontrelle Willis showed tremendous potential, and Octavio Dotel was a great closer, but there wasn't much there after that. The consensus ranked the Solons 10th, and the projections had them at 75-76.

Sacramento is 25-15, a game behind Monterrey in the West Group, with the second-best record in the FBL. Three weeks in it was a quietly excellent start. It hasn't been quiet since. The +48 run differential is second in the league, and the site gives the Solons a 90.1% chance at the playoffs and a 59.3% chance of promotion.

Huff

Of the three lineup leaders, one has been great. Huff is hitting .410 with eight homers, 32 RBI and a 184 OPS+, the highest average among FBL regulars, ahead of Richmond's Greg Colbrunn at .385. On back-to-back days against Carolina, May 9 and 10, he went 3-for-4 with two homers and then 4-for-4, and Sacramento lost both. Loretta has been about average, at a 102 OPS+. Lofton, 34, is hitting .218, though 24 walks have kept him useful.

The rest of the lineup has been better than mediocre. Olmedo Saenz, David Dellucci and Hiram Bocachica all have OPS+ marks of 117 or 118, and Saenz and Dellucci have eight homers each. Saenz's ninth-inning solo shot beat Honolulu 1-0 on April 9 and spoiled an incredible start by Jesse Foppert. The Solons have 45 home runs, tied for fifth in the FBL, and haven't been shut out all season.

More than expected

The real surprise is the rotation. Sacramento's starters have a 3.46 ERA, second in the FBL to Alaska's, and the one starter everybody knew about, Willis, has the worst ERA of the five, at 4.96. Brandon Knight is 6-2 with a 1.96 ERA, though 27 walks and a 3.93 FIP suggest some luck, and on Monday he allowed one run over 8.1 innings in a 14-1 win in Charlotte. Terry Mulholland, 39, has a 2.98 ERA and nearly threw a shutout against Richmond on April 16, going 8.1 scoreless innings. Darren Oliver hasn't allowed a home run in 43 innings, and Kyle Lohse is at 3.96. The staff has five shutouts.

The bullpen is where the trouble is. Dotel has 13 saves, three blown saves, a 5.89 ERA and six homers allowed in 18.1 innings. On April 6 in Monterrey, Sacramento led 8-0 in the third. Alex Ochoa tied it with a three-run homer off Dotel in the ninth, and the Solons lost 10-9 in 10. The bullpen ERA is 4.94, and they're 1-3 in extra innings.

Lately

Sacramento's best stretch was a six-game winning streak from April 25 to 30 that included a home sweep of Alaska. The Solons are 6-6 in May. They lost two of three at home to Carolina, then won the last two in Charlotte by a combined 25-8. They're 14-6 at home. Inland Empire visits next, then come three in Vancouver against the West's third-place team and Oklahoma City at home. Monterrey has been slipping. A good week puts Sacramento in first.
