40-Game Check-In · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Rebeldes Keep Playing Extra Baseball

San Antonio Rebeldes, 17-23 through 40 games

The Rebeldes have played six extra-inning games already, going 2-4. Baseball keeps giving them more baseball, and they keep having to decide what to do with it.

40 games, by the numbers

  • 17-23 record
  • -41 run differential (160 scored, 201 allowed)
  • 7-5 in one-run games
  • 7-11 at home
  • 10-12 on the road
  • 4.54 / 4.95 starter / reliever ERA
  • 43 home runs
  • 41 stolen bases

Through 40 games they were 17-23, scoring 160 runs and allowing 201 (-41). As of the May 13 export they are 9th in the West Group, 9 games back, and 17th of 20 in the FBL. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 16 wins in 40 games. They are 7-5 in one-run games and 2-4 in extra innings.

Julio Lugo has been the Rebeldes hitter opponents least want to see: .291/.367/.503, 6 homers, 23 driven in. Justin Morneau leads the club with 11 home runs. On the bases, Brian Jordan is 9-for-9 stealing. As a team they are hitting .264/.315/.413, 16th of 20 in the FBL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 43 home runs.

In the rotation, Josh Beckett has been the steadiest: 5-1 with a 2.24 ERA, 55 strikeouts and 20 walks in 56.1 innings. Carlos E. Hernandez (3.61 ERA in 42.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.69 ERA, 10th of 20 in the FBL; starters 4.54, relievers 4.95. Closing has mostly meant Juan Rincon: 12 saves, 3.06 ERA.

And it is not the line anyone would have written for him: his 2001 OPS was .698 in 586 plate appearances. The weakest regular bat has been Wilton Guerrero, at .240/.283/.295 in 144 plate appearances. In the rotation, Jeff Fassero has a 6.05 ERA over 38.2 innings, after a 3.42 ERA in 2001.

Some details worth knowing. When they score three runs or fewer they are 1-19; when they score six or more, 12-1. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

The best night was May 10, a 6-0 win over the 66ers. The worst was Apr 2, a 16-1 loss to the Sultanes. Their best 10-game stretch went 5-5 (Apr 3 to Apr 13); their worst, 3-7 (Apr 17 to Apr 27).

The FBL is the bottom rung and nobody gets relegated from it; the whole point is the way up. The top seven make a ladder playoff, seeds one and two are promoted outright, and the winner of the Federal Promotion round makes three. They are 17th of 20 in the table, 4½ games behind the Larks for the last ladder place.

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 4 of them at home, against the Twisters, the Bats, the Pharoahs, the Spirit, opponents with a combined 203-193 record (.513). Neither a gift nor an ambush. Their record is about what their runs say it should be, which is reassuring or alarming depending on which side of .500 you are standing on. Watch Julio Lugo: the offense has run through him. In six weeks the Rebeldes have shown us who they might be. The next six will show us who they are willing to keep being.
