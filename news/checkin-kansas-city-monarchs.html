40-Game Check-In · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Monarchs and the Runs That Went Somewhere Else

Kansas City Monarchs, 23-17 through 40 games

There is a version of the Monarchs season that the scoreboard has seen and the box scores have not. The scoreboard says 23-17. The box scores, which only know that the Monarchs scored 245 runs and allowed 242, would have guessed something closer to 20-20. Somewhere between those two numbers is either a skill nobody can measure yet or a bill that has not arrived.

The ABL is the middle rung, so it is the only league here where you can fall and climb in the same breath: each conference winner gets a bye, second and third play in, both finalists of the American Championship are promoted to MLB, and the bottom three are relegated to the FBL. They sit 2nd in the Western Conference, 3 games back of the Black Sox, inside the play-in places. The drop is 5 games below them, where the Violets is the best of the clubs on relegation pace.

Through 40 games they were 23-17, scoring 245 runs and allowing 242 (+3). As of the May 13 export they are 2nd in the Western Conference, 3 games back, and 3rd of 20 in the ABL. By the old Pythagorean arithmetic, that is a 20-win team so far. They are 3-3 in one-run games and 2-0 in extra innings.

The hitters

Their most productive hitter is Luis Gonzalez (LF, 34), at .342/.429/.610 with 9 HR. As a team they are hitting .303/.372/.486, 1st of 20 in the ABL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 51 home runs. Then there is Geoff Blum, whose OPS has gone from .664 in 2001 to .979 now. Some of that is the sample. The question is how much.

The pitchers

In the rotation, Randy Wolf has been the steadiest: 2-0 with a 4.20 ERA, 51 strikeouts and 22 walks in 55.2 innings. Rick Ankiel (4.79 ERA in 47.0 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 5.66 ERA, 15th of 20 in the ABL; starters 5.78, relievers 5.47. Mark Guthrie has 12 saves and 1 blown. In the rotation, James Baldwin has a 10.74 ERA over 31.0 innings, after a 4.42 ERA in 2001.

The best and worst of it

The best night was Apr 14, an 18-1 win over the Loons. The worst was May 13, a 16-2 loss to the Padres. Their best 10-game stretch went 7-3 (Apr 3 to Apr 13); their worst, 4-6 (Apr 21 to May 1).

A couple of small, true things. When they score three runs or fewer they are 1-8; when they score six or more, 16-5. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks. Geoff Blum has walked 19 times and struck out 15, a ratio that belongs to a different, more patient century.

What next

The next stretch is 10 games (6 at home) against the Violets, the Royeaux, the Angels, the Rays. Those clubs are 203-197 combined, a .507 winning percentage, so it will mostly be up to them. The longer-range question is whether the 3 or so wins they have banked beyond their run differential stay banked. History says to expect the runs to win that argument eventually; history is not always right in May.
