The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Kansas City Has the Best Lineup in the ABL, as Promised, and an Ordinary Record

Alexander gave the Monarchs an A on offense and a D+ on pitching. Both grades have held up, and KC is 23-17 with a +3 run differential.

Kansas City Monarchs · 23-17 · 245 runs scored, 242 allowed

Alexander said Kansas City's veteran lineup would "terrorize the sub-par pitching of this league for sure." He gave the offense an A and ranked the Monarchs third. He gave the pitching a D+: "Age is again their defining trait but the pitching is much less effective." Those two grades are hard to fit into one prediction, and his predicted standings had them at 77-74.

So far it has played out about the way he described. The Monarchs are 23-17, three games behind Southside in the Western Conference. They lead the ABL in OPS and rank third in runs per game. They're 15th in runs allowed per game, and their run differential is +3.

Terrorizing

The two players he named first have both been excellent. Luis Gonzalez, 34, is hitting .342/.429/.610 with nine home runs, 37 RBI and a 154 OPS+. Larry Walker, 35, is hitting .346/.444/.566. Walker's three-run homer drove the opening-day comeback over Southside, according to the April 1 notes. Fernando Vina is hitting .346, and Scott Spiezio .329. The best surprise is shortstop, which Alexander thought was a weak spot ("a bit weak in the infield"). Geoff Blum has a .424 on-base percentage and a 141 OPS+. Erick Aybar, who is 18, is hitting .340/.396/.660 in 53 plate appearances.

The two players Alexander called "talented young left handed bats" haven't hit. Travis Lee has a 78 OPS+, and Paul Konerko has an 80 OPS+ with one home run. The May 12 injury report noted that Konerko "had 32 homers in 2001" and now has a separated shoulder. Ivan Cruz is hitting .135, but six of his nine RBI came in one game, the 18-1 win over Minnesota on April 14. The trends piece noted that game.

Much less effective

Alexander picked out Rick Ankiel, and the yips, as the staff's "only upside." Both parts have shown up. On April 3 in San Juan, Ankiel allowed one hit and struck out 11 in 7.2 innings. The roundup called it "a masterpiece." On April 18 against Cincinnati, he allowed nine runs in three innings. He has walked 29 batters in 47 innings, and his ERA is 4.79. Randy Wolf has been the most reliable starter, with a 4.20 ERA and 51 strikeouts. James Baldwin's ERA is 10.74. In the bullpen, Jose Silva has allowed 11 home runs in 27 innings and has a 10.00 ERA. Mark Guthrie has 12 saves and a 5.62 ERA. Jeff Nelson, the other pitcher Alexander called good, has a 5.28 ERA.

The result is a lot of lopsided games in both directions. The Monarchs won 18-1 and 15-4. They lost 16-2 to San Diego on Monday and 15-1 in Baltimore on May 8. They lost two of three to the Padres over the weekend and were outscored 31-23 in the series.

Next

Alexander's original case for this roster was about promotion. He said the lineup "bodes well if they win promotion to the MLB next year." The site gives them an 11.1% chance of promotion and a 40.2% chance of making the playoffs. Next up are three games with Austin (two at home), three at home against Montreal, and three in Anaheim.
