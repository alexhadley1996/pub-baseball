The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Kansas City Has the Best Lineup in the ABL, as Promised, and an Ordinary Record

The Monarchs came in with an A on offense and a D+ on pitching. Both grades have held up, and KC is 23-17 with a +3 run differential.

Kansas City Monarchs · 23-17 · 245 runs scored, 242 allowed

Kansas City's veteran lineup was supposed to terrorize the sub-par pitching in this league, and it has. The preseason consensus ranked the Monarchs third, with an A for the offense and a D+ for a pitching staff that was just as old and much less effective. Those two grades don't add up to an obvious record, and the projections split the difference at 77-74.

The season has played out almost exactly as described. The Monarchs are 23-17, three games behind Southside in the Western Conference. They lead the ABL in OPS and rank third in runs per game, and they're 15th in runs allowed. The run differential is +3.

Terrorizing

The two names at the top have been excellent. Luis Gonzalez, 34, is hitting .342 with nine homers, 37 RBI and a 154 OPS+. Larry Walker, 35, is hitting .346 with a .444 on-base percentage, and his three-run homer powered the Opening Day comeback over Southside. Fernando Vina is hitting .346, and Scott Spiezio .329. The best surprise has come at short, which looked like a weak spot in March. Geoff Blum has a .424 on-base percentage and a 141 OPS+, and Erick Aybar, all of 18, is slugging .660 in 53 plate appearances.

The two talented young left-handed bats haven't come around. Travis Lee has a 78 OPS+, and Paul Konerko, who hit 32 homers in 2001, has an 80 OPS+, one home run and now a separated shoulder. Ivan Cruz is hitting .135, though six of his nine RBI came in one game, an 18-1 rout of Minnesota on April 14.

Much less effective

The staff's only real upside was Rick Ankiel, and the yips came along with it. Both have shown up. On April 3 in San Juan, Ankiel allowed one hit and struck out 11 in 7.2 innings, a masterpiece. On April 18 against Cincinnati he gave up nine runs in three innings. He has walked 29 batters in 47 innings, and his ERA is 4.79. Randy Wolf has been the most reliable starter, with a 4.20 ERA and 51 strikeouts. James Baldwin's ERA is 10.74.

The bullpen has been worse. Jose Silva has allowed 11 home runs in 27 innings, with a 10.00 ERA. Mark Guthrie has 12 saves and a 5.62 ERA, and Jeff Nelson, the other good arm coming in, is at 5.28.

Put a great lineup in front of that staff and you get a lot of lopsided games in both directions. The Monarchs have won 18-1 and 15-4. They lost 15-1 in Baltimore on May 8 and 16-2 to San Diego on Monday, the finale of a series in which the Padres took two of three and outscored them 31-23.

Next

The point of this roster was always promotion: a lineup this good bodes well for MLB next year, if the Monarchs can get there. The site gives them an 11.1% chance of promotion and a 40.2% chance at the playoffs. Next up are three with Austin, two of them at home, then Montreal at home and three in Anaheim.
