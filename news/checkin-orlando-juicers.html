The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Ichiro Has Hit .420 Since Week Three. Orlando's Pitching Is Still Ugly.

Alexander ranked the Juicers 19th, called the staff "pretty horrible" outside of Byung-Hyun Kim, and still listed Ichiro as an MVP candidate. After an 0-7 start, Orlando is 16-24.

Orlando Juicers · 16-24 · 174 runs scored, 206 allowed

In March, Alexander ranked Orlando 19th of 20 in the FBL, though his predicted standings were kinder at 77-74. The pitching review was one sentence long: "This is a pretty horrible staff except for Byun Hyun Kim at closer." He also listed Ichiro Suzuki among his three FBL MVP candidates, and his personalities report said Orlando was the "only team with multiple true fan favorites; crowd energy matters here."

The fans haven't had a lot to cheer about. The Juicers lost their first seven games. By April 5, according to that day's roundup, "Orlando fell to 0-5, with 11 runs in five games." They lost 1-0 to Richmond in a seven-inning game that day. The first win came on April 8 in Hartford, with "a big game from Ryan Ludwick" and the "first save for Byung-Hyun Kim." Orlando is 16-24, ninth in the East Group and 8.5 games back.

Ichiro

At three weeks, Alexander noted that Ichiro had "nudged his average up to .286." Since then he's hit .420, going 34-for-81. He's now at .354/.386/.443 with 16 steals and a team-best 1.5 WAR. The MVP case has a problem: one home run and eight walks keep his OPS+ at 115. The week-three notes also named Matt Williams as "a legitimate middle-of-the-order force." Williams, 36, has a 120 OPS+ and seven home runs. Alfonso Soriano leads the team with eight home runs, and on April 7 the roundup credited him with "a 3-run HR + three hits" in a 10-6 loss in Santo Domingo. Sean Casey, the third of the infield pieces Alexander liked, has a 95 OPS+.

The outfield is as weak as he said. Geronimo Berroa, 37, is hitting .188 with a 70 OPS+. Ludwick, in center, has no home runs and an 89 OPS+. Jared Sandberg has played some second base and hit .155 with 32 strikeouts in 91 plate appearances. Catcher Johnny Estrada has a 60 OPS+.

The staff

Orlando's 5.15 ERA ranks 18th of 20. The week-three notes said "the rotation is thin beyond Brian Lawrence," after the Juicers had allowed 28 runs in three games. Lawrence has a 3.69 ERA and 43 strikeouts against 15 walks in 63.1 innings. He's 3-5, partly because of games like his April 17 complete game in Alaska, which Orlando lost 2-1. On April 2 in Savannah the roundup noted that "rookie Joe Kennedy got shelled." Kennedy is now at 4.85. Mark Hendrickson has a 6.00 ERA and has been knocked out before the third inning was over twice. Clay Condrey, in relief, has a 10.26 ERA.

Kim, the one exception Alexander named, has nine saves and no blown saves. He's pitched only 9.1 innings, though. The bullpen ERA is 5.66, and it lost a five-run lead in Hoboken on April 23.

Lately

Orlando went 7-3 from April 8 to 18 and has gone 9-14 since. It finished the weekend by beating Louisville, the East leader, in two of three games, including Monday's 12-4 win with Lawrence on the mound. The site gives the Juicers a 2.6% chance at the playoffs and projects about 68 wins. Next come three games in Memphis, a home series against Albuquerque and three in Eugene.
