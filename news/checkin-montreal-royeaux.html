The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Montreal Is Winning, and the Pitching Is Doing More of the Work Than Expected

The preseason No. 1 was built around its lineup, which ranks 17th in scoring. The rotation ranks fourth in ERA, and Montreal is 11-5 in one-run games.

Montreal Royeaux · 23-17 · 203 runs scored, 197 allowed

The preseason consensus ranked Montreal first in the ABL, and the reason was the lineup. It got an A-, a solid procession of experienced big leaguers from Rafael Palmeiro to Richard Hidalgo to Craig Counsell, with the only real gap at catcher, and the small matter that they're all quite old. The pitching got a C+ for a rotation that looked satisfyingly decent. The clubhouse was the case to watch, with a disruptive Hidalgo in an outspoken room. Talent usually wins that argument.

So far it has. Montreal is 23-17, tied with Cleveland for first in the Eastern Conference, and has won five straight. The site gives it a 74.4% chance at the playoffs. It's just winning in a different way than anyone expected.

The procession

The three names at the top of the list have been terrific. Palmeiro, 37, is hitting .380 with 11 home runs and a 182 OPS+, two of them in one game in Cincinnati on April 12. Counsell is hitting .389 and leads the team with 2.5 WAR. Hidalgo has a 106 OPS+, and he's had big days: two two-run homers in New York on April 3 and four hits in a 2-1 win at Austin on May 9. Even the catcher has hit. Bobby Estalella's eight home runs are second on the team.

The rest of the procession has stalled. Greg Vaughn, 36, is hitting .192 with 42 strikeouts. Jeromy Burnitz is hitting .230 with a 72 OPS+, and first baseman Phil Hiatt is at 59. Montreal ranks 17th of 20 in runs per game.

Satisfyingly decent

That has left the pitching to carry the team, and it has. Montreal is fourth in the ABL in ERA and sixth in fewest runs allowed, and its starters have thrown 261.2 innings, more than anyone else's. Derek Lowe, who came in with the potential for stardom, has a 2.47 ERA in 65.2 innings and two complete games, one of them a five-hit shutout of Mexico City on April 22. Rick Helling has two complete games of his own, nine innings and one earned run each time. Chan Ho Park and Glendon Rusch, both better by FIP than by ERA, have held the back end. Park has 50 strikeouts, and Rusch, whose ERA is 5.44, allowed one run in eight innings at Austin on May 9.

Billy Koch, the closer, has 10 saves and a 4.60 ERA. Will Cunnane has allowed two earned runs in 16.1 innings.

The one-run games

A team that doesn't score much and doesn't allow much plays a lot of close games, and Montreal has won most of them. It's 11-5 in one-run games, with a +6 run differential that would usually mean about 20 or 21 wins. The early weeks fit the volatile billing. The Royeaux went 3-7 from April 8 to 18, then won five straight, including a sweep of Mexico City in which they outscored the Golden Eagles 23-2. Now they've won five more, two at Austin and three at home against Tampa Bay, outscoring the Rays 31-9. Next come three at home against Anaheim, three in Kansas City and three at home against New Orleans.
