The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Montreal Is Winning, but the Pitching Is Doing More Than Alexander Expected

Alexander ranked the Royeaux first for their lineup. The lineup ranks 17th in scoring. The starters he called "satisfyingly decent" rank fourth in ERA, and Montreal is 11-5 in one-run games.

Montreal Royeaux · 23-17 · 203 runs scored, 197 allowed

Alexander ranked Montreal first in the ABL before the season, mostly because of the lineup. It got an A-: "a solid procession of experienced major leaguers from Rafael Palmeiro to Richard Hidalgo to Craig Counsell," and "the only real gap is at catcher (and at the fact that they're all quite old...)." He gave the pitching a C+ and called the rotation "satisfyingly decent." The personalities report called the Royeaux "the case to watch": they have "a disruptive Hidalgo... in an outspoken clubhouse." He added: "Talent usually wins that argument."

So far it has. Montreal is 23-17, tied with Cleveland for first in the Eastern Conference, and has won five straight. The site gives it a 74.4% chance of the playoffs. The Royeaux are winning in a different way than Alexander expected, though.

The procession

The three hitters at the top of his list have been terrific. Palmeiro, 37, is hitting .380/.470/.683 with 11 home runs and a 182 OPS+. He hit two of those homers in Cincinnati on April 12. Counsell is hitting .389/.465/.550 and leads the team with 2.5 WAR. Hidalgo has an OPS+ of 106. On April 3 in New York he hit two two-run homers, and on May 9 in Austin he had four hits in a 2-1 win. The catching spot Alexander worried about has been fine. Bobby Estalella has eight home runs, second on the team.

The rest of the lineup has been the problem. Alexander's note listed "Mo Vaughn" as a supporting bat. The Vaughn on this roster is Greg Vaughn, 36, and he is hitting .192/.292/.295 with 42 strikeouts. Jeromy Burnitz is at .230 with a 72 OPS+. First baseman Phil Hiatt has a 59 OPS+. Montreal ranks 17th of 20 in runs per game.

Satisfyingly decent

The pitching has carried the team. Montreal ranks fourth in the ABL in ERA and sixth in fewest runs allowed per game. The rotation has thrown 261.2 innings. Derek Lowe, whom Alexander said had "the potential for stardom," has a 2.47 ERA in 65.2 innings and two complete games. One was a five-hit shutout of Mexico City on April 22. Rick Helling has two complete games of his own, both nine innings with one earned run allowed. Chan Ho Park has 50 strikeouts. Alexander said Park and Glendon Rusch were "liked by FIP," and they have FIPs of 4.59 and 4.34. Rusch's ERA is higher, at 5.44. On May 9 at Austin he allowed one run in eight innings.

Billy Koch, the "solid closer," has 10 saves and a 4.60 ERA. Will Cunnane has allowed two earned runs in 16.1 innings.

The one-run games

A team that doesn't score much and doesn't allow much plays a lot of close games, and Montreal has won most of them. It is 11-5 in one-run games. Its run differential is +6, which would usually mean about 20 or 21 wins. The personalities report also listed Montreal among the "most volatile" teams, and the early weeks fit that description. The Royeaux went 3-7 from April 8 to 18. Then they won five straight, including a three-game sweep of Mexico City in which they outscored the Golden Eagles 23-2. They have now won five straight again, two at Austin and three at home against Tampa Bay, outscoring the Rays 31-9. Next come three at home against Anaheim, three in Kansas City and three at home against New Orleans.
