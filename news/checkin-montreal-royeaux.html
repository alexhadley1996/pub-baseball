40-Game Check-In · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

Royeaux at 40: Coin Flips, Counted

Montreal Royeaux, 23-17 through 40 games

If you want to understand the Royeaux at 23-17, do not look at the blowouts. Look at the 16 games decided by a single run, in which they have gone 11-5. That is either a bullpen doing its job or a coin landing on its edge. Possibly both.

Through 40 games they were 23-17, scoring 203 runs and allowing 197 (+6). As of the May 13 export they are 2nd in the Eastern Conference, tied for first, and 4th of 20 in the ABL. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 21 wins in 40 games. They are 11-5 in one-run games and 1-1 in extra innings.

What is driving it

The best bat has belonged to Rafael Palmeiro, 37, who is hitting .380/.470/.683 with 11 home runs and 30 RBI in 166 plate appearances. On the bases, Craig Counsell is 10-for-14 stealing. As a team they are hitting .284/.364/.451, 12th of 20 in the ABL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 50 home runs.

In the rotation, Derek Lowe has been the steadiest: 5-2 with a 2.47 ERA, 35 strikeouts and 18 walks in 65.2 innings. Rick Helling (3.81 ERA in 54.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.54 ERA, 4th of 20 in the ABL; starters 4.33, relievers 5.12. Closing has mostly meant Billy Koch: 10 saves, 4.60 ERA.

Nobody should have expected Craig Counsell to be hitting .389/.465/.550. He posted a .721 OPS in 533 plate appearances in 2001. Forty games is enough to notice and not enough to be sure. The disappointment, at least so far, is Greg Vaughn: a .766 OPS in 2001, .587 through 178 plate appearances this year. In the rotation, Paul Wilson has a 6.64 ERA over 39.1 innings, after a 4.88 ERA in 2001.

A couple of small, true things. When they score three runs or fewer they are 7-7, which is either excellent pitching or a clerical error. Rafael Palmeiro has walked 23 times and struck out 13, a ratio that belongs to a different, more patient century.

The stakes

The ABL is the middle rung, so it is the only league here where you can fall and climb in the same breath: each conference winner gets a bye, second and third play in, both finalists of the American Championship are promoted to MLB, and the bottom three are relegated to the FBL. They are tied for the Eastern Conference lead with the Spiders, inside the play-in places. The drop is 5 games below them, where the Violets is the best of the clubs on relegation pace.

The best night was Apr 21, a 14-1 win over the Golden Eagles. The worst was Apr 26, a 17-7 loss to the Stars. Their best 10-game stretch went 7-3 (Apr 30 to May 10); their worst, 3-7 (Apr 8 to Apr 18).

Looking ahead

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 6 of them at home, against the Angels, the Monarchs, the Athletics, the Black Sox, opponents with a combined 218-182 record (.545). That is a real test. The longer-range question is whether the 2 or so wins they have banked beyond their run differential stay banked. History says to expect the runs to win that argument eventually; history is not always right in May. In six weeks the Royeaux have shown us who they might be. The next six will show us who they are willing to keep being.
