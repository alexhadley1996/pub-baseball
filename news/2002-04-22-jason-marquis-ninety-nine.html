MLB · 2002-04-22 · By Sam Holloway, Sporting Weekly

Ninety-Nine Pitches, Thirteen Ground Balls, One Hit

Jason Marquis doesn't have an overpowering pitch, and he struck out only four Blue Jays on Monday. He also faced 28 batters, walked none, gave up one hit, got 13 ground-ball outs and finished a 5-0 shutout in 99 pitches. A different kind of gem.

TORONTO — When Jason Marquis was in high school on Staten Island, a scout told his father that the boy would never pitch in the majors because nothing he threw was good enough. The fastball was ordinary. The curve was ordinary. Everything was ordinary.

“He was right, sort of,” Marquis said Monday night. “Nothing I throw is great. But I throw six things. Most hitters can’t handle six ordinary things.”

On Monday at Rogers Centre the Toronto Blue Jays couldn’t handle any of them. Marquis threw a one-hitter, walked nobody, struck out four and beat Toronto 5-0. He needed 99 pitches. The only Toronto hit was Brandon Phillips’ single in the third inning. Marquis retired the last 20 batters in order.

“That’s the most efficient game I’ve ever seen,” Mariners manager Lou Piniella said. “Not the most dominating. The most efficient. They put the ball in play, and we caught it.”

Six pitches

Marquis throws a sinker, a cutter, a changeup, a slider, a curveball and a four-seam fastball, and he isn’t shy about any of them. Scouts grade his changeup as his best pitch, and the cutter and sinker close behind. None of them would make a highlight reel. Thrown together, in any count, they produce a lot of weak contact.

On Monday they produced 13 ground-ball outs and 10 fly balls, most of them harmless. He averaged 11 pitches an inning.

“I wasn’t trying to strike anybody out,” he said. “When I try to strike people out, I throw 30 pitches an inning. I was trying to get them to hit it on the ground in three pitches.”

The lineup

Toronto’s lineup has some names in it. Jim Edmonds came in hitting .310. Dmitri Young was at .324. Preston Wilson, who leads the team with five homers, batted leadoff. Between them they went 0-for-10, and Wilson grounded out to end the game.

“He changes speeds on every pitch,” Young said. “You go up there thinking sinker, you get the cutter. You think cutter, you get the change. By the time you figure it out, you’re 0-for-3.”

The hit

Phillips’ single, with one out in the third, was a ground ball into center field on a sinker that didn’t sink. Phillips, 20, is a rookie and hadn’t been playing much.

“I was trying to get on base,” he said. “That’s it. I didn’t know it was going to be the only one.”

Marquis got Joe Girardi and Wilson on fly balls to end the inning, and that was the last time a Blue Jay reached base.

Enough runs

Aaron Sele was good too, for a while. The Mariners scratched out a run in the fifth on Marty Malloy’s sacrifice fly and another in the sixth on Rocco Baldelli’s single. In the eighth, Saul Soto doubled and Jose Offerman hit a two-run homer. Malloy added another sacrifice fly in the ninth. It was 5-0, which on this night was about four more than Marquis needed.

Afterward

Marquis, 23, has heard the knocks on his stuff for years.

“People look at my stuff and they’re not impressed,” Marquis said. “That’s fine. I’m not trying to impress anybody. I’m trying to get to the ninth inning with a lead.”

He’s 2-1 with a 2.16 ERA. Seattle is 11-11.

Asked whether he’d ever thrown a one-hitter before, Marquis thought about it.

“In Little League,” he said. “I threw about 140 pitches. I’ve gotten better at it.”
