MLB · 2002-05-06 · By Marty Kessler, Diamond Daily

Raines Gave Detroit the Lead. Petrick Took It Back.

Tim Raines, 42, drove in three runs and put Detroit ahead in the ninth. Ben Petrick tied it with a two-out homer in the bottom half, and Fernando Tatis won it for Brooklyn in the 11th, 6-5, ending the Wolverines' four-game winning streak.

BROOKLYN, N.Y. — Tim Raines is 42 years old, and on Monday night he did almost everything a Detroit Wolverine could do to win a baseball game.

With two outs in the seventh, Detroit down 4-2 and runners on second and third, Raines singled both of them home to tie it. In the ninth, with the bases loaded and one out, he lifted a sacrifice fly that put the Wolverines ahead 5-4.

It wasn’t enough. Ben Petrick homered with two outs in the bottom of the ninth, and Fernando Tatis singled home the winning run in the 11th. Brooklyn won 6-5.

“I’ll take three RBI every night,” Raines said. “I’d rather have the win. Petrick had a heck of a night.”

Petrick’s night

Petrick, the Gothams’ 25-year-old center fielder, came in hitting .246. He doubled home two runs in the first inning off Detroit starter Luke Prokopec. He added another double later. And with two outs and nobody on in the ninth, against 37-year-old Mike Jackson, he hit the home run that sent the game to extra innings.

He finished 4-for-6 with two doubles, a homer and three RBI.

“The one in the ninth is the one I’ll remember,” Petrick said. “Two outs, nobody on. You’re just trying not to make the last out, and then it goes.”

Getting to the ninth

Brooklyn led most of the night. After Petrick’s double in the first, B.J. Waszgis homered for Detroit in the third, but Moises Alou answered with a homer to start the fifth, and another run scored on Jack Wilson’s single later that inning. Gothams starter Mike Smith left with two outs in the sixth and a 4-1 lead, having allowed one run.

The seventh came apart for the Gothams’ bullpen. Grant Roberts, who came in with a 0.47 ERA, walked Troy Glaus, and manager Matt Swanson went to closer Jason Isringhausen early. A run scored while Isringhausen walked Hee-seop Choi, and after a fly out, Raines tied it.

In the ninth, James Mouton doubled off Chris George, Glaus walked, and Felipe Lopez singled to load the bases. Ken Pumphrey came on, and Raines’ sacrifice fly put Detroit ahead.

Then Petrick.

The 11th

Elmer Dessens and Jayson Durocher got Brooklyn through the top of the 11th. In the bottom half, Detroit reliever Bob Howry got a ground ball from Craig Wilson, then gave up a single to Bobby Higginson and a double to Derrek Lee. Alou was walked to load the bases. Detroit manager Phil Garner brought in Jimmy Anderson, who got Petrick to ground out without a run scoring.

Tatis, who came in hitting .288, singled, and Higginson came home.

Durocher, who faced one batter, got the win. Howry, who came in 2-0 with a 1.89 ERA, took the loss.

Two teams going the same way

The loss ended Detroit’s four-game winning streak and dropped the Wolverines to 19-16. Brooklyn, which has won nine of its last 11, is 20-15.

“That’s two teams that are playing well,” Garner said. “One of them had to lose. I’d have liked it to be them.”
