MLB · 2002-05-05 · By Marty Kessler, Diamond Daily

Five Down in the Ninth, and Brooklyn Never Lost

Brooklyn trailed Boston 7-2 going to the bottom of the ninth, tied it, fell behind in the 13th, tied it again, fell behind in the 16th, tied it again on a two-out homer by Koyie Hill, and won 11-10 when Ben Petrick walked with the bases loaded.

BROOKLYN, N.Y. — At 8:40 on Sunday night, with the Red Sox up 7-2 and the bottom of the ninth about to start, a good part of the crowd was already on the subway. Steve Cohen was not. The Gothams’ owner was still in his box behind home plate, and so were the people sitting around him, because nobody leaves before the owner does, and nobody, it turned out, should have left at all.

Four hours and 16 innings after the first pitch, Ben Petrick took ball four with the bases loaded, Julio Ramirez jogged home, and the Gothams beat Boston 11-10. They had trailed by five in the ninth, by one in the 13th and by two in the 16th, and they never lost.

“I’ve been to a lot of baseball games,” manager Matt Swanson said, sounding like a man who wasn’t sure he’d ever been to one quite like this. “That one I’m going to tell people about.”

The ninth

For most of eight innings this was a pitchers’ afternoon that turned into a Red Sox rout. Derrek Lee hit a two-run homer in the first, Boston got one back in the third, and then Kevin Millwood and Pedro Astacio settled in. Boston didn’t pull ahead until the seventh. Then the Brooklyn bullpen came apart: Wes Obermueller couldn’t get out of the eighth, four more runs scored, and Shawn Gilbert, a 37-year-old shortstop who had 17 at-bats coming in, singled two of them home to make it 7-2.

Boston manager Grady Little handed the bottom of the ninth to Oswaldo Verdugo, a 21-year-old with a 7.77 ERA, figuring five runs was plenty of room. It wasn’t. Verdugo loaded the bases with nobody out. Moises Alou, pinch-hitting, walked to force in a run. Julio Ramirez, hitting .179, singled home two more. Little brought in Matt Belisle, his best young reliever, and with two outs Petrick doubled into the gap to score two and tie the game 7-7.

“When Julio got that hit, the whole dugout stood up,” Petrick said. “It was like, wait, we’re actually going to do this.”

The long middle

Then nothing happened for three innings, which in this game felt like a breather. Boston’s Armando Almanza struck out four in 1⅔ innings and allowed one hit. Brooklyn’s Grant Roberts and Jayson Durocher held the Red Sox down.

Gilbert broke the quiet in the 13th, hitting a homer off Jaime Cerda to put Boston back ahead 8-7. In the bottom half, with two outs, Petrick singled and Fernando Tatis doubled him home, one of Tatis’ three doubles on the day, and it was tied again.

“Fernando had three doubles and I don’t think he got credit for any of them,” Swanson said. “Every one of them meant something.”

The 16th

Elmer Dessens, Brooklyn’s sixth pitcher, had gotten through the 14th and 15th. With two outs in the 16th, Chone Figgins singled, and Dessens gave up a home run to Pat Burrell, the former Phillie, who had come off the bench and was 1-for-2 on the day. Boston led 10-8, and for the third time the Gothams were down to their last few outs.

It didn’t look good. Angel Echevarria doubled and Jack Wilson singled against Mike Lincoln, Boston’s eighth pitcher, but Alex Cora’s fly ball turned into a double play when Echevarria was cut down trying to score from third. Two outs, one on. Then Koyie Hill, the 23-year-old catcher who came in with one home run in 32 at-bats, hit a two-run homer to tie it 10-10.

“I don’t hit home runs,” Hill said. “I mean, I hit one before. Now two. I don’t know what I’m supposed to say. I’m just glad it went.”

Ramirez doubled. Lincoln walked Higginson, then walked Lee on purpose to load the bases. Against Petrick, he couldn’t find the strike zone at all.

Afterward

Ken Pumphrey, who got one out in the top of the 16th, got the win. Lincoln took the loss. The two teams combined to use 15 pitchers.

The Gothams are 19-15 and have won eight of their last 11, the hottest stretch of Cohen’s young franchise. The Red Sox are 14-20, and Little, who has spent the spring watching his bullpen give away leads behind Randy Johnson, didn’t have much to say.

“We had a five-run lead in the ninth,” he said. “There isn’t a lot to explain.”
