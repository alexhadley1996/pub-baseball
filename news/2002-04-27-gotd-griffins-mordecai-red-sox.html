MLB · 2002-04-27 · By Ken Albrecht, The Baseball Courier

The .169 Hitter Had Four Hits

Mike Mordecai came in hitting .169. He went 4-for-6 and singled home the winning run in the 13th as Dallas beat Boston 8-7, after Fred McGriff's two-out homer in the ninth had tied it and Lew Ford's grand slam had given the Griffins a lead Boston erased.

DALLAS — Mike Mordecai has spent most of his career as the guy who plays wherever there’s an opening. Third base this year, mostly, for a Dallas team that values versatility and needed a veteran glove. He is 34, he was hitting .169 on Saturday morning, and the rumors around the Griffins this week were that he was about to lose his job to someone younger.

On Saturday night he had four hits, the last of them a two-out single in the bottom of the 13th that beat the Boston Red Sox 8-7.

“Somebody showed me that number this morning,” Mordecai said, meaning .169. “I said, I know. I was there for all of them. Now I’ve been there for four more.”

A game that kept turning

The game had at least four lives. Dallas led early. Boston’s four-run third, built on a Pat Burrell double and a lot of patience against Chris Haney, put the Red Sox ahead, and a sacrifice fly in the fifth made it 5-3. Haney came in with a 1.65 ERA and left in the fifth with five runs charged to him, only one of them earned.

In the sixth, Lew Ford gave Dallas the lead back the loudest way possible. Boston reliever Javier Cruz, carrying a 12.00 ERA, didn’t record an out, and Ford, the 25-year-old who has quietly become the Griffins’ best hitter, hit a grand slam. 7-5.

Pedro Valdes homered in the eighth. And with two outs and nobody on in the ninth, Fred McGriff, 38 and as patient as ever, hit a homer off Dallas closer David Riske to tie it.

“Fred’s done that to a lot of people,” Red Sox manager Grady Little said. “It’s nice when he does it for us.”

Boston’s bullpen

Then three innings of nothing, and the long, odd night of Boston’s bullpen. Oswaldo Verdugo, 21, who came in with a 10.47 ERA, threw three scoreless innings. Matt Belisle threw two more. Eddie Oropesa got the 13th and couldn’t finish it.

It’s been that kind of spring for the Red Sox. They’re 11-15. Randy Johnson has been fine, Pedro Astacio has been good, and nobody else has pitched like the club expected. The bullpen in particular has become a source of daily second-guessing in New England.

“We’ll get it right,” Little said. “We have to. That’s not a choice.”

Dallas

The Griffins are 13-13, and they have been a strange team to watch: through 25 games they had scored exactly as many runs as they allowed, 92 each. Most nights are close. Most nights come down to a hit like Mordecai’s.

Dallas manager Jerry Narron was asked whether Mordecai’s night changed anything about his playing time.

“It changes tomorrow,” Narron said. “Ask me about Tuesday on Tuesday.”

Nate Bump, who threw a scoreless 13th, got the win. Oropesa took the loss. Burrell had two doubles and two RBI for Boston, and McGriff was 3-for-5 with a homer.
