MLB · 2002-05-05 · By Owen Kirby, Diamond Daily

Flash Gordon's Best Day in Years

Three weeks after the Yankees knocked him out in the fourth inning, Tom Gordon threw a one-hitter at Busch Stadium: no walks, 11 strikeouts, 99 pitches. The only St. Louis hit was a two-out single in the third. He retired the last 19 Cardinals he faced.

ST. LOUIS — Tom Gordon has been throwing the same curveball since he was a teenager in Avon Park, Fla., and for most of the last 15 years it has been one of the best in baseball. It starts at a hitter’s eyes and finishes at his shoelaces. Catchers have described catching it as “like trying to catch a bird.”

On Sunday afternoon at Busch Stadium, the Cardinals didn’t catch up to it either. Gordon threw a one-hitter, walked nobody, struck out 11, and beat St. Louis 5-0 on 99 pitches. The only Cardinal to reach base was Carlos Rodriguez, who singled with two outs in the third inning. Nobody reached after that.

“That’s as good as I’ve felt in a long time,” Gordon said. “Everything was working. When everything’s working, I don’t have to think. I just throw it.”

April 15

Three weeks ago, Gordon couldn’t get out of the fifth inning against the Yankees. They scored eight runs off him. He came into Sunday 2-4 with a 4.63 ERA, and in Phoenix there had been some talk about whether the Diamondbacks’ highest-paid player, 34 and in the first year of a three-year, $30 million contract, was starting to wear down.

Gordon heard the talk. He didn’t say much about it.

“You give up eight to the Yankees, people are going to talk,” he said. “The Yankees do that to people. I wasn’t worried about my arm. I was worried about my location. I fixed it.”

What he fixed, according to pitching coach Nick Moore, was his front shoulder. Gordon had been flying open, and his fastball was running back over the middle of the plate. They spent two bullpen sessions on nothing else.

“He’s 34 and he’s still willing to work on things,” Moore said. “Not everybody is.”

The arsenal

Gordon throws four pitches, and on Sunday he threw all of them for strikes. The fastball sat in the low 90s and touched 94 in the eighth. The slider was hard and late. The cutter broke bats. And the curveball, the one that made him famous, was the out pitch. Of his 11 strikeouts, catcher Tom Prince said, at least seven came on the curve.

“You know it’s coming,” said Kevin Mench, who struck out twice. “Everybody in the league knows it’s coming. It doesn’t matter. It still drops off the table.”

He piled up strikeouts in pairs. In the second he struck out Ken Harvey and Luis Matos. In the fifth he got Pat Borders and Barry Wesson. In the eighth, Matos and Wesson again.

The hit

Rodriguez’s single was a ground ball through the right side, on a 1-2 fastball that Gordon said afterward he wanted back.

“I threw him a fastball because I’d thrown him two curves,” Gordon said. “I outthought myself. That’s the only bad pitch I threw all day, and he hit it.”

Rodriguez, 34, has been one of the quiet stories of the Cardinals’ season, hitting .336 in a lineup that has been looking for anybody to get on base. He was asked whether he knew he’d had the only hit.

“Not until the ninth,” he said. “Then I knew. Then I kind of wished I didn’t.”

The offense

Paul Byrd, still looking for his first win, matched Gordon through six innings. Then in the seventh Chris Stynes and Shane Spencer led off with doubles, and Enrique Wilson doubled in two more with two outs. In the eighth, Michael Restovich singled in two. That was more than enough.

The ninth

Gordon went out for the ninth at 88 pitches. He struck out Noe Munoz, got Rodriguez on a ground ball, and got Jermaine Dye, the Cardinals’ home run leader, to fly to center on the 99th pitch.

“I asked Bob [Brenly] in the eighth if I was going back out,” Gordon said. “He looked at me like I was crazy. He said, ‘You’re going back out until they hit it.’ They never really did.”

Arizona is 15-19 and has won seven of its last 11. Gordon is 3-4, and his ERA, in one afternoon, dropped from 4.63 to 3.86.

“It’s one game,” he said. “But it’s a good one to have.”
