ABL · 2002-04-02 · By Ned Calloway, Diamond Daily

Eleven Home Runs, 13 Innings, and Two Very Tired Bullpens

Marty Cordova hit three home runs, Anaheim answered with five of its own, including three solo shots in one inning and a four-run ninth to tie it, and San Juan won its first game ever 15-12 in 13 innings on Scott Sheldon's three-run homer.

ANAHEIM, Calif. — The San Juan Coquis, the ABL’s newest franchise, played the second game in their history on Tuesday night. It lasted 13 innings and nearly five hours, featured 11 home runs, used 12 pitchers, and ended with the Coquis’ first win, 15-12 over the Angels.

“I don’t know if every game is going to be like this,” Coquis manager Tim Maurer said, slumped in a chair in the visitors’ office around 11:45 p.m. “I hope not. I’m too old to do this every night.”

Cordova

The Coquis were built, in part, around the idea of bringing Puerto Rican players home. Marty Cordova isn’t one of them; he’s from Las Vegas. But on Tuesday he played like the star of somebody’s homecoming. He homered in the first inning off Sterling Hitchcock, hit a three-run homer in the sixth that put San Juan back ahead 11-8 after Anaheim had tied it, and added a solo homer in the eighth.

Three home runs, five RBI, four runs scored. It was the first three-homer game in Coquis history, which is admittedly not a long history.

“I don’t usually do that,” Cordova said. “I’ve hit two before. Three is new. It’s a nice feeling. I’d like it to happen again sometime, when I’m not this tired.”

Anaheim’s answer

San Juan led 5-0 after half an inning. Matt LeCroy hit a three-run homer in the first, J.D. Drew had a double and a triple on his way to three hits, and by the third it was 8-2.

Then Anaheim started hitting home runs. Mike Sweeney homered twice. In the fourth inning alone, Sweeney, Jose Valentin and Dante Powell hit solo homers off Mark Redman. In the fifth, Jason Lane hit a three-run homer to tie the game 8-8.

The Angels trailed 12-8 going into the ninth, which, by that point in the evening, seemed like a perfectly manageable deficit. Scott Podsednik doubled in a run. Austin Kearns doubled in another. With two outs, Valentin singled in two more off Steve Karsay, and it was 12-12.

“At some point you stop being surprised,” Angels manager Mike Scioscia said. “I stopped being surprised around the fifth inning.”

Quiet, finally

From the 10th through the 12th, nobody scored, mostly because Roberto Hernandez, the Coquis’ 37-year-old reliever, threw three scoreless innings in a game where scoring had been the only consistent thing. In the 13th, with two on and nobody out against Matt Mantei, Scott Sheldon, the 33-year-old shortstop who had been hitless in his first game, hit a three-run homer.

Peter Munro pitched the bottom of the 13th for the save, despite giving up three hits.

Both teams are 1-1. Both teams’ bullpens will need the day off. Neither will get it.
