ABL · 2002-04-16 · By Nate Fischer, Ball Theory

Two Riveras, Two Foxes, Thirteen Innings

New Orleans, winners of five straight, scored three in the 12th and one more in the 13th at Minnesota. The Loons came back both times with two outs, and Juan Rivera's single in the 13th won it 8-7 and ended a four-game losing streak.

MINNEAPOLIS — My colleague Dave Okonkwo, who handles our ABL coverage on most nights, wanted it noted that he watched every inning of this one from the press box at the Loons’ ballpark and that he is not going to do it again.

Here is what he saw. The New Orleans Athletics, winners of five straight, scored three runs in the top of the 12th and one in the top of the 13th. The Minnesota Loons, who had lost four in a row, answered both times with two outs. Juan Rivera’s single with two outs in the 13th won it 8-7.

“Two outs, both times,” Loons manager Ron Gardenhire said. “You don’t plan that. You don’t even hope for it. It just happens, and you try not to fall over.”

A slow nine innings

For a long time this was a pitchers’ night. Salvador Rodriguez went 6⅔ innings for the Loons and allowed only an unearned run. Pablo Ortega went 5⅔ for New Orleans and allowed one, a Shea Hillenbrand homer in the second. It was 1-1 into the eighth.

Ruben Rivera, the New Orleans center fielder, doubled in two runs in the top of the eighth. Hillenbrand, catching, singled in a run in the bottom half, and Brian Simmons, the hero of last week’s 14-inning win over Anaheim, homered leading off the ninth to tie it 3-3.

The 12th

In the 12th, New Orleans loaded the bases off Aaron Fultz with nobody out, and Andy Fox, the New Orleans third baseman, cleared them with a double off Brad Voyles. 6-3.

The Loons got two outs in the bottom half with only a runner on second. Then Juan Rivera doubled in a run, pinch-hitter Aaron Miles doubled in another, and Simmons singled to tie it again.

“It’s the same thing every time,” Simmons said. “I don’t know why. I keep getting up there with the game on the line. I’m not complaining.”

The 13th

Voyles walked Eric Young to start the 13th. Brian Roberts singled, and Young came around to score when the throw got away. New Orleans led 7-6.

This is the part where it gets confusing, so stay with me. In the bottom of the 13th, Keith Ginter doubled, and after a fly ball New Orleans brought in Chad Fox, no relation to Andy, to protect the lead. He walked David Ortiz and struck out Hillenbrand. Then David DeJesus singled in the tying run and Juan Rivera, no relation to Ruben, singled in the winning run.

So: one Fox drove in three, and the other Fox took the loss. Each Rivera drove in two. Only one of them drove in the last run.

“I’ve been asked about Ruben a lot,” Juan Rivera said. “I’ve never met him. I’d like to. We’d have something to talk about now.”

The numbers

Voyles gave up the lead in the 12th, gave up the go-ahead run in the 13th, and got the win. Since I write for a numbers site, I’m obligated to point out that this is why we don’t put much stock in pitcher wins.

Hillenbrand finished with four hits. Simmons had four. New Orleans is 8-7 and its streak is over. Minnesota is 7-8 and its streak is over too.

“Good,” said Gardenhire, about the second part.
