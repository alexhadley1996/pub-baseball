MLB · 2002-04-29 · By Frank DeLuca, New York Evening Star

Jeter Did His Part. Bonds Did More.

In the rubber game of Derek Jeter's first series against the Yankees, Philadelphia led 10-3 in the fifth. The Yankees came back, Jeter put the Phillies ahead again in the eighth, and Barry Bonds won it with his second homer of the night in the ninth, 14-12.

PHILADELPHIA — For three days, Derek Jeter said the same thing to everybody who asked him about playing the Yankees. He was focused on Philadelphia. It was just another series. He didn’t look at the other dugout.

On Monday night, in the bottom of the eighth inning, with the Phillies down 11-10 and two men on, Jeter lined a single into center field that scored both of them, stole second base on the next pitch, and stood there with his hands on his hips while 37,797 people screamed his name. He looked at the other dugout then. Everybody saw him do it.

Then Barry Bonds came up in the ninth.

“I’ll say it,” Jeter said afterward, which for Jeter is practically a press conference. “That one hurt. That’s a team I know. I wanted that one.”

10-3

It shouldn’t have come down to that. The Phillies chased Yankees starter Brian Tollberg, who came in 4-1 with a 1.67 ERA, in the fifth inning of what was, for a while, a very satisfying night in South Philadelphia. Miguel Cabrera, 19, homered in the second. Juan Carlos Diaz hit a two-run homer in the third. In the fifth, Cabrera singled in two more off Tollberg, Jeff Tam hit Trenidad Hubbard with the bases loaded, Dennis Anderson grounded in a run and Diaz doubled in two more. Six runs in the inning. 10-3, Phillies.

The crowd sang. Somebody in the upper deck had a bedsheet that said 39-5 WAS LAST YEAR, which nobody in the press box fully understood but everybody agreed was the right spirit.

10-8

The Yankees got five back in the sixth. Adam Kennedy doubled in a run off Phillies starter Horacio Estrada. Ron Villone came on and walked Bonds with the bases loaded, gave up an RBI grounder to Torii Hunter, an RBI single to Fernando Seguignol and a sacrifice fly to Lenny Harris. Suddenly it was 10-8 and nobody in South Philadelphia was singing.

In the eighth, Jonathan Johnson walked Bonds and gave up a single to Hunter, who finished 4-for-6. Carlos Baerga hit a three-run homer. The Yankees led 11-10.

Jeter’s turn

In the bottom half, Cliff Politte walked Anderson, gave up a pinch single to Felix Jose and got two outs. Jeter came up with men on second and third.

“Everybody in the building knew what was going to happen,” Phillies manager Larry Bowa said. “Derek knew. Their dugout knew. Politte knew.”

Jeter singled. 12-11 Phillies.

Bonds’ turn

Chris Hammond, the 36-year-old who had un-retired over the winter to pitch for the Phillies, came on in the ninth after Mark Grudzielanek reached base. Bonds, who had homered in the first inning and walked three times since, hit Hammond’s second pitch into the right-field seats. 13-12 Yankees. Harris grounded in one more run, and Mike DeJean struck out the side around a single in the bottom of the ninth.

Bonds finished 2-for-3 with two homers, three walks and four RBI. Baerga was 3-for-4 with three RBI. Chad Bradford, who got one out in the eighth, got the win. Hammond took the loss.

“Same as always,” Bonds said when asked about the homer. “He threw it, I hit it.”

Joe Torre was asked about Jeter.

“I watched him do that for six years,” Torre said. “I wasn’t surprised. I’d have been surprised if he didn’t.”

The Yankees took two of three in the series and are 14-14. The Phillies are 15-13. The teams meet again June 27 at Yankee Stadium, Jeter’s first trip back to the Bronx. He has already been asked about it. He is focused on Philadelphia.
