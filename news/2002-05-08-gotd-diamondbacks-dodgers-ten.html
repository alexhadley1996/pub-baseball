MLB · 2002-05-08 · By Hal Brennan, Diamond Daily

Bases Loaded, Nobody Out, and Anthony Telford

Erubiel Durazo hit a grand slam, Wilson Betemit hit a homer to bring the Dodgers back, and Arizona won 9-8 in 10 innings on Raul Gonzalez's fourth hit, after 36-year-old Anthony Telford escaped a bases-loaded, nobody-out ninth.

PHOENIX — The Arizona Diamondbacks won 9-8 in 10 innings on Wednesday night on a two-out single by Raul Gonzalez, his fourth hit of the game. That’s how it ended. How it got there needs a longer explanation, and most of that explanation is the ninth inning.

The game was tied 8-8. Jason Varitek singled to lead off the top of the ninth for Los Angeles. Juan Gonzalez walked. Kit Pellow singled, and the bases were loaded with nobody out.

Anthony Telford, 36, who had already blown a lead in the eighth, got Darren Bragg on a ground ball that didn’t score a run. He struck out Wilson Betemit. He struck out Ben Candelaria.

“That’s the game,” Arizona manager Bob Brenly said. “Everything else was noise. That’s the game.”

Five runs, then a grand slam

For a while it looked like a Dodgers night. Varitek doubled in a run in the first. Betemit, the 20-year-old shortstop who came in hitting .202, doubled in another in the fourth. And in the fifth, with two out and two on, Juan Gonzalez hit a three-run homer off Arizona starter Shawn Estes for a 5-3 lead.

Arizona had kept close on a Robin Ventura homer in the first, a bases-loaded walk to Erubiel Durazo in the second and a run on a groundout in the third. In the sixth it got all of it back at once. The Diamondbacks loaded the bases against Dave Mlicki and Russ Springer, and with two outs Durazo, hitting .216, hit Springer’s pitch for a grand slam. Arizona led 7-5. Gonzalez added an RBI single in the seventh, and it was 8-5.

Durazo finished 2-for-4 with two walks and five RBI. Ventura was 3-for-4 with a homer and two walks.

The Dodgers come back

In the eighth, Pellow walked against Jose Paniagua, and Betemit hit a two-run homer to make it 8-7. Betemit finished 3-for-5 with a double, a homer and three RBI.

“He’s 20,” Dodgers manager Jim Tracy said. “He’s going to have a lot of nights where he looks overmatched, and then he’s going to have nights like this. This is the one we keep.”

Paniagua walked pinch-hitter David Segui and struck out Chris Saunders. Brenly went to Telford, who walked Shane Victorino. Tomas Perez singled to tie the game 8-8.

The 10th

After the ninth-inning escape, Telford retired the Dodgers in order in the 10th, three groundouts.

Calvin Maduro, pitching his second inning for Los Angeles, gave up a single to Durazo, got Matt Lawton on a groundout, walked Ventura and got Chris Stynes to fly out. Tracy brought in Hector Carrasco. Todd Hundley pinch-hit and singled to load the bases, and Raul Gonzalez singled home Durazo.

Gonzalez finished 4-for-6 with two RBI and two stolen bases.

“I wasn’t thinking about the four hits,” Gonzalez said. “I was thinking, don’t make the last out with the bases loaded. Anthony did all that work.”

Telford got the win, his first, with 2⅓ innings in which he allowed no runs of his own. The Dodgers, who have lost nine of their last 11, fell to 14-23. Arizona is 17-20.
