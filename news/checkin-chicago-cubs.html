The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Cubs Have a Cycle, a 1-0 One-Hitter and a 20-21 Record

Alexander's preseason No. 3 has a 2.88 bullpen ERA, a lineup with almost no power, and a 2-8 stretch it still hasn't made up.

Chicago Cubs · 20-21 · 183 runs scored, 176 allowed

On consecutive nights at the end of April, the Cubs beat the Yankees 1-0 and 15-1. In the first game Odalis Perez allowed one hit in 8.2 innings, and the Cubs won it on a walk-off in the ninth. In the second Shawn Green went 5-for-5 with six RBI and hit for the cycle. Two nights later the Cubs had a five-run lead on the same Yankees and lost 8-7 in 10 innings. Eddie Guardado got one out and gave up four runs.

That is the Cubs' season in three games. They are 20-21, fifth in the West, six games behind Oakland and San Francisco. Alexander picked them to go 88-63, the best record in his MLB predictions, and ranked them third in his preseason power rankings.

Name brands, no home runs

His March pitch for the offense was a list: "Todd Helton, Shawn Green, Melvin Mora, Johnny Damon, Omar Vizquel...the list goes on and on." He gave it an A-/A grade and wrote that "the Cubs could slug their way to the pennant." Six weeks in, they've walked plenty, 164 times, and slugged very little. Chicago has 34 home runs, 19th of 20 in MLB, and is 10th in runs per game.

Helton has done his part: .318/.424/.534, 159 OPS+, seven homers. His first big moment came on April 1 in Philadelphia, when the Cubs trailed 5-1 and he hit a one-out, three-run homer off Chris Hammond in the top of the ninth to win it. Damon (118 OPS+) and Green (116, with nine homers and 30 RBI) have been above average. The names on the bottom of the list have not. Vizquel, 35, has an 82 OPS+. Jose Molina, the regular catcher, is hitting .212/.262/.280. Orlando Palmeiro and Mora have combined for three home runs in 351 plate appearances.

The pitching was the worry

Alexander's March grade for the staff was a C-: "pretty uninspiring, but not baaaaad." It has turned into the reason the Cubs are not further back. At the three-week mark he reported that the Cubs and Yankees had "the strongest pitching staffs early—about 25% better than league average." The Cubs' team ERA is still 3.83, seventh in MLB.

Perez, one of the two starters he said showed "potential," has a 2.71 ERA and 2.94 FIP. His record is 2-5 anyway. Besides the one-hitter, he threw a four-hit shutout of Atlanta on Friday. The bullpen ERA is 2.88. Andy Pratt, 22, has thrown 17 innings without allowing a run, Ricky Bottalico has a 1.33 ERA, and Brandon Kolb has 19 strikeouts, three walks and no home runs allowed in 16 innings. The one reliever who has struggled is the closer. Guardado, one of the two bullpen names Alexander was counting on, has a 4.80 ERA and has allowed five home runs in 15 innings. The rotation behind Perez also has some trouble. Mike Thurman and Darrell May both have ERAs over 5.30, and May has allowed 10 home runs in 40.1 innings.

Where it went wrong

Chicago was 14-9 on April 23 after Shane Andrews hit two home runs in a comeback from six runs down against Miami. The next 11 games went 2-9: losses in the last two games of the Marlins series, a sweep in Seattle that included a walk-off home run by Keith McDonald, the 8-7 collapse against the Yankees, and a home sweep by Oakland in which two of the three games went to extra innings. The Cubs are 2-6 in extra-inning games and 6-12 on the road. Since then they've gone 4-3, including a three-game sweep of Atlanta, before losing 2-0 in San Francisco on Monday.

That loss started a week Alexander had already flagged. His Seals notes say this is the week San Francisco faces "both the Astros and Cubs, the preseason favorites who are hanging around .500." The Cubs play two more in San Francisco, then host Arizona and visit St. Louis. Their run differential is +7, and the site's simulations give them a 1.2% chance at the division and a 4.5% chance of relegation. If the home runs don't come, the pitching that was supposed to be the weakness will have to carry the season.
