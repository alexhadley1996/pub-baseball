The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Cubs Have a Cycle, a 1-0 One-Hitter and a 20-21 Record

The preseason No. 3 has a 2.88 bullpen ERA, a lineup with almost no power and a 2-8 stretch it still hasn't made up.

Chicago Cubs · 20-21 · 183 runs scored, 176 allowed

On back-to-back nights at the end of April, the Cubs beat the Yankees 1-0 and 15-1. In the first game Odalis Perez allowed one hit in 8.2 innings and the Cubs won it on a walk-off in the ninth. In the second Shawn Green went 5-for-5 with six RBI and hit for the cycle. Two nights later they took a five-run lead on the same Yankees and lost 8-7 in 10 innings, with Eddie Guardado getting one out and giving up four runs.

That's the Cubs' season in three games: brilliant, then more brilliant, then a mess. They're 20-21, fifth in the West and six games behind Oakland and San Francisco. The preseason consensus ranked them third in MLB, and the preseason projections had them at 88-63, the best record in baseball.

Name brands, no home runs

The case for the Cubs in March was a list of names: Todd Helton, Shawn Green, Melvin Mora, Johnny Damon, Omar Vizquel. The offense got an A-/A, and the idea was that Chicago could slug its way to the pennant. Six weeks in, it has walked plenty, 164 times, and slugged very little. The Cubs have 34 home runs, 19th of 20 in MLB, and are 10th in runs per game.

Helton has held up his end, with a .424 on-base percentage, seven homers and a 159 OPS+. His first big moment came on Opening Day in Philadelphia, when the Cubs trailed 5-1 and he hit a one-out, three-run homer off Chris Hammond in the top of the ninth to win it. Damon and Green, who has nine homers and 30 RBI, have been above average. The bottom of the list has not. Vizquel, 35, has an 82 OPS+, and the catcher, Jose Molina, is hitting .212 with almost no power. Orlando Palmeiro and Mora have three home runs between them in 351 plate appearances.

The pitching was supposed to be the worry

The staff got a C- in March, uninspiring but not bad. It has turned into the reason the Cubs aren't further back. Three weeks in, the Cubs and Yankees had the strongest pitching staffs in the league, about 25% better than average, and Chicago's 3.83 team ERA is still seventh in MLB.

Perez has been the best of it. He has a 2.71 ERA and a 2-5 record, and besides the one-hitter he threw a four-hit shutout of Atlanta on Friday. The bullpen has a 2.88 ERA. Andy Pratt, 22, hasn't allowed a run in 17 innings, Ricky Bottalico is at 1.33, and Brandon Kolb has 19 strikeouts, three walks and no home runs allowed in 16 innings. The one reliever who's struggled is the closer. Guardado has a 4.80 ERA and has given up five homers in 15 innings. Behind Perez, Mike Thurman and Darrell May both have ERAs over 5.30, and May has allowed 10 home runs in 40.1 innings.

Where it went wrong

Chicago was 14-9 on April 23, after Shane Andrews hit two homers in a comeback from six runs down against Miami. Then it went 2-9. The Cubs dropped the last two games of that Marlins series and were swept in Seattle, the finale ending on Keith McDonald's walk-off homer. Then came the collapse against the Yankees and a home sweep by Oakland in which two of the three games went to extra innings. The Cubs are 2-6 in extras and 6-12 on the road. Since then they've gone 4-3, including a sweep of Atlanta, before losing 2-0 in San Francisco on Monday.

The rest of the week doesn't get easier: two more in San Francisco, then Arizona at home and a trip to St. Louis. Chicago's run differential is +7, and the site gives it a 1.2% chance at the division and a 4.5% chance of relegation. If the home runs don't come, the pitching that was supposed to be the weakness will have to carry the season.
