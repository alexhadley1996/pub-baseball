40-Game Check-In · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Cubs Keep Playing Extra Baseball

Chicago Cubs, 20-20 through 40 games

The Cubs have played eight extra-inning games already, going 2-6. Baseball keeps giving them more baseball, and they keep having to decide what to do with it.

The best bat has belonged to Todd Helton, 28, who is hitting .326/.434/.549 with 7 home runs and 25 RBI in 173 plate appearances. Shawn Green leads the club with 9 home runs. As a team they are hitting .264/.346/.403, 7th of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 34 home runs.

Odalis Perez leads the starters with a 2.71 ERA over 63.0 innings (41 K, 14 BB). Mike Fyhrie (4.47 ERA in 54.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 3.87 ERA, 9th of 20 in MLB; starters 4.37, relievers 2.90. Closing has mostly meant Eddie Guardado: 6 saves, 4.80 ERA.

Through 40 games they were 20-20, scoring 183 runs and allowing 174 (+9). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 20-21, 5th in the West Division, 6 games back, and 9th of 20 in MLB. By the old Pythagorean arithmetic, that is a 21-win team so far. They are 9-7 in one-run games and 2-6 in extra innings.

The surprise is Johnny Damon. His 2001, by the export's count, was a .687 OPS over 719 plate appearances; this year it is .815. Players do not usually get 128 points better by accident, though they do sometimes get there by April. The weakest regular bat has been Jose Molina, at .217/.268/.287 in 124 plate appearances.

The fine print is fun here. They once scored 15 runs in a game (May 1, against the Yankees); that one game is 8% of their season's runs. When they score three runs or fewer they are 3-12; when they score six or more, 11-3. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

The best night was May 1, a 15-1 win over the Yankees. The worst was May 8, a 10-1 loss to the Cherry Blossoms. Their best 10-game stretch went 7-3 (Apr 1 to Apr 10); their worst, 2-8 (Apr 24 to May 4).

MLB has no wild card and no consolation prize: division winners play for the title, the last-place club in each division goes down to the ABL. The Cubs are 6 games behind the Athletics in the West Division. The floor is 3 games below them, where the Dodgers currently stand on relegation pace.

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 5 of them at home, against the Seals, the Diamondbacks, the Cardinals, the Dodgers, opponents with a combined 194-216 record (.473). Neither a gift nor an ambush. Their record is about what their runs say it should be, which is reassuring or alarming depending on which side of .500 you are standing on. Watch Todd Helton: the offense has run through him.

None of this is settled, which is the point of May. But the Cubs have given us enough to argue about, and that is all you can ask of 40 games.
