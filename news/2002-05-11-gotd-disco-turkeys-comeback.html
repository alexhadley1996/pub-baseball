FBL · 2002-05-11 · By Paul Ricci, Sporting Weekly

Six Down, Then Nothing

Hoboken led Carolina 6-1 after three innings. Carolina's bullpen allowed one run the rest of the way, Mike Mohler threw 3⅓ hitless innings, and Orlando Cabrera's double in the 10th finished an 8-7 comeback.

For three innings on Saturday night, the Hoboken Zephyrs hit Blake Stein hard. Vernon Wells homered with two men on in the first. Rusty Greer singled in a run in the second. Randall Simon homered in the third. Carolina was down 6-1, and Stein was in the dugout after 2⅓ innings.

Then the Zephyrs got one more run in seven innings, and the Disco Turkeys won 8-7 in the 10th on Orlando Cabrera’s double.

“You look up and it’s 6-1 and you think, OK, it’s one of those,” Carolina manager Tatsuhito Tamura said. “Then the bullpen makes it not one of those.”

Wood, then Mohler

Mike Wood, 22, took over for Stein in the third and gave Carolina 4⅓ innings, allowing one run. That run, an RBI single by Simon in the seventh, put Hoboken back up 7-5. Then Mike Mohler came in and nobody got another hit off him. He retired all 10 batters he faced over 3⅓ innings.

“He’s been doing that all year,” Tamura said of Mohler, who came in with a 1.46 ERA. “Quiet guy. Throws strikes. Gets outs.”

The chase

Carolina got most of the way back against Hoboken starter Travis Smith. Ken Griffey Jr. drove in a run with a sacrifice fly in the first and another with a double in the third. In the fourth a run scored on Cabrera’s groundout and Tom Goodwin singled in another. Mike Lieberthal homered to start the fifth. By then it was 6-5, and Smith was done after four innings.

Simon’s single in the seventh pushed Hoboken’s lead back to two, and Mike Drumright, who had relieved Smith, carried it into the eighth. Bubba Trammell singled and Terrence Long doubled. Chad Tracy, the 21-year-old third baseman, hit a sacrifice fly to make it 7-6. Cabrera singled to put runners on the corners, and Long, who had moved to third, scored the tying run while Goodwin was striking out.

Drumright struck out six in 4⅔ innings and was charged with two runs. Brian Schmack came on in the ninth with two out and a runner on second, walked Trammell, struck out Long, and went back out for the 10th.

The 10th

Tracy led off the bottom of the 10th with a single, his third hit of the night. Cabrera, who came in hitting .238 with no home runs, hit Schmack’s pitch for a double, and Tracy ran home from first with the winning run.

“I didn’t see him score,” Cabrera said. “I saw everybody running at me.”

Two teams in the middle

The win was Carolina’s third straight and lifted the Disco Turkeys to 18-19, five games behind Louisville in the FBL East Group. Hoboken fell to 17-20, six back.

Zephyrs manager Evan Griffith was blunt about the bullpen’s work in the late innings, and less blunt about his own lineup’s.

“We scored six in three innings and then we stopped,” Griffith said. “That’s on the hitters. Credit their bullpen. Mohler was really good. But we stopped.”
