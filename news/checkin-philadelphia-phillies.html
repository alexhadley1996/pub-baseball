The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Phillies' Bullpen Has Four Saves and Seven Blown Ones

Miguel Cabrera reached base in 34 straight games, Philadelphia leads MLB in steals, and the late innings have cost the Phillies the top of the East. Alexander is "not buying them as a whole."

Philadelphia Phillies · 20-21 · 199 runs scored, 189 allowed

Philadelphia has four saves this season and seven blown saves. Jonathan Johnson has blown four and Chris Hammond three. Alexander's 40-game notes say that pair took "the Phillies from the top of the east to mid-table singlehandedly," and that is close to literal. The Phillies are 20-21 with a +10 run differential, fifth in the East, five games behind Miami.

Hammond was the story he chose to open his March preview with. Hammond had retired "about five years ago," came back at 36 throwing "soft stuff and junk with surprising effectiveness," and Alexander called it "compelling, and also kind of terrifying if you're trying to win games." Both halves have come true. Hammond has a 2.38 ERA and a 2.80 FIP. And by Alexander's count, opponents are hitting ".333/.429/.778 in high leverage against him — wowza." On April 1, Todd Helton hit a one-out, three-run homer off him in the ninth for a 7-6 Cubs win. On April 29 the Yankees came back from seven runs down to win 14-12, and Hammond gave up two runs in his inning of work.

Cabrera

"If Cabrera hits immediately," Alexander wrote in March, "it changes the entire shape of the Phillies' season." He has. Miguel Cabrera, now 19, is hitting .347/.425/.510 with a 153 OPS+, the best on the team. His 4-for-5, four-RBI night in Milwaukee on April 8 was the first big one. He then reached base in 34 consecutive games, the league's longest streak until Jake Peavy and the Seals ended it on Sunday in a 6-0 loss. Alexander's caution is about the .417 BABIP ("a bit foreboding, but hey, no one's perfect"), and his outlook is that "No way Cabrera can keep this up."

Joe Crede has nine home runs and a 137 OPS+, and he went 5-for-5 in an 11-inning walk-off win over Detroit on April 19. Derek Jeter is at .301/.401/.448. The rest of the "contact-and-athleticism spine" Alexander described in March has been all athleticism. Mike Cameron has 11 steals and a .306 OBP, and Roberto Alomar, who had a .956 OPS last year, is at .221/.294/.344. Philadelphia leads MLB with 49 steals, and Alexander rates the outfield defense of Cameron, Roger Cedeno and Trenidad Hubbard among the best in the league. That helps explain how the team is fifth in runs scored while sitting 14th in OPS.

The rotation

The March plan rested on Brad Penny, "the only arm that looks like a true difference-maker." Penny is 1-6 with a 6.26 ERA, 21 walks and 13 home runs in 50.1 innings. Alexander's word for it is "an unmitigated disaster." Matt Kinney is 0-4 with a 7.36 ERA. Runelvys Hernandez, who got no mention in March, is 6-1 with a 2.79 ERA and threw a complete game in Arizona on Monday. Esteban Loaiza has 52 strikeouts and a 3.09 FIP, and Alexander calls him "promising despite a high ERA." Loaiza also gave the team 7.2 innings and 10 strikeouts in the 17-inning win in Atlanta on May 7, in which Cameron hit two home runs.

Two predictions

Alexander made two forecasts for this team in March, and they disagree. His predicted standings had Philadelphia at 86-65, tied with Washington for the East. His Teams to Watch preview said 76-75 and warned that the pitching "is likely to be the limiting factor over a full season." The clubhouse, rated among the healthiest in the league in his personalities report, hasn't been tested yet. The worst run so far was a four-game losing streak at the turn of the month.

At 40 games he chose the second forecast: "The preseason projection systems loved them, but honestly I'm not seeing it." He expects them to "stick around .500 for sure with a pretty high floor," and the site's 1.9% relegation chance agrees with the floor part. He named Andy Van Hekken and Miguel Gomez as possible minor-league reinforcements for the rotation. The next stretch is one more game in Arizona, three at home against St. Louis, and then the Dodgers, one game in Philadelphia and two in Los Angeles.
