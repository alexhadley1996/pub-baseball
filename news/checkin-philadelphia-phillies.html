Phillies at 40: Ten Innings Minimum

Philadelphia Phillies, 19-21 through 40 games

Nine innings have not been enough for the Phillies, five times. They are 3-2 in those games and 19-21 overall.

Through 40 games they were 19-21, scoring 193 runs and allowing 188 (+5). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 20-21, 5th in the East Division, 5 games back, and 11th of 20 in MLB. That run differential would usually produce a record around 20-20. They are 3-5 in one-run games and 3-2 in extra innings.

What is driving it

Miguel Cabrera has been the Phillies hitter opponents least want to see: .350/.429/.517, 5 homers, 26 driven in. Joe Crede leads the club with 9 home runs. Mike Cameron has 11 steals (caught 2 times). As a team they are hitting .255/.328/.397, 15th of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 45 home runs.

In the rotation, Runelvys Hernandez has been the steadiest: 5-1 with a 3.10 ERA, 30 strikeouts and 18 walks in 52.1 innings. Esteban Loaiza (4.50 ERA in 56.0 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.34 ERA, 17th of 20 in MLB; starters 4.91, relievers 3.31.

The surprise might be Miguel Cabrera, who arrived without a 2001 top-tier line worth the name (under 150 plate appearances) and is hitting .350/.429/.517. Roberto Alomar has not looked like himself, or at least like his 2001 self: .956 OPS then, .659 now. In the rotation, Matt Kinney has a 7.36 ERA over 25.2 innings.

The fine print is fun here. When they score three runs or fewer they are 1-14; when they score six or more, 13-3. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

The stakes

MLB has no wild card and no consolation prize: division winners play for the title, the last-place club in each division goes down to the ABL. The Phillies are 5 games behind the Marlins in the East Division. The floor is 6 games below them, where the Braves currently stand on relegation pace.

The best night was Apr 8, an 11-3 win over the Brewers. The worst was Apr 27, a 12-3 loss to the Yankees. Their best 10-game stretch went 6-4 (Mar 31 to Apr 9); their worst, 3-7 (Apr 22 to May 2).

Looking ahead

The next stretch is 10 games (6 at home) against the Diamondbacks, the Cardinals, the Dodgers, the Blue Jays. Those clubs are 180-230 combined, a .439 winning percentage, so this is where a record can get fatter. The record matches the runs, so there is no regression bill coming and no hidden credit either. The next 122 games will mostly be about whether Miguel Cabrera keeps doing this. A quarter of a season tells you what a team is capable of. The other three quarters tell you what it is. For the Phillies, the interesting part starts now.
