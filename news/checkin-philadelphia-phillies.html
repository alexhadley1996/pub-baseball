The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Phillies' Bullpen Has Four Saves and Seven Blown Ones

Miguel Cabrera reached base in 34 straight games, Philadelphia leads MLB in steals, and the late innings have cost the Phillies the top of the East.

Philadelphia Phillies · 20-21 · 199 runs scored, 189 allowed

Philadelphia has four saves this season and seven blown saves. Jonathan Johnson has blown four and Chris Hammond three, and between them they've taken the Phillies from the top of the East to mid-table almost singlehandedly. Philadelphia is 20-21 with a +10 run differential, fifth in the East and five games behind Miami.

Hammond was the most compelling story of the Phillies' spring. He had retired about five years ago and came back at 36 throwing soft stuff and junk with surprising effectiveness, which was also kind of terrifying if you were trying to win games. Both halves have come true. His 2.38 ERA is excellent, and in high-leverage spots opponents are hitting .333 and slugging .778 against him. On Opening Day, Todd Helton hit a one-out, three-run homer off him in the ninth for a 7-6 Cubs win. On April 29 the Yankees came back from seven down to win 14-12, and Hammond gave up two runs in his inning.

Cabrera

The other big question in March was whether Miguel Cabrera would hit right away, because if he did, it would change the shape of the Phillies' season. He has. Cabrera, now 19, is hitting .347 with a .425 on-base percentage and a 153 OPS+, the best on the team. His first big night was a 4-for-5, four-RBI game in Milwaukee on April 8. He then reached base in 34 straight games, the longest streak in the league, until Jake Peavy and the Seals stopped him on Sunday in a 6-0 loss. A .417 batting average on balls in play is a bit foreboding. Nobody can keep this up. But no one's perfect.

Joe Crede has nine homers and a 137 OPS+, and he went 5-for-5 in an 11-inning walk-off win over Detroit on April 19. Derek Jeter has a .401 on-base percentage. The rest of the lineup has been more athleticism than contact. Mike Cameron has 11 steals and a .306 on-base percentage, and Roberto Alomar, a .956 OPS hitter last year, is hitting .221. Philadelphia leads MLB with 49 steals, and Cameron, Roger Cedeno and Trenidad Hubbard make up one of the league's best defensive outfields. That's how a team that's 14th in OPS is fifth in runs.

The rotation

The pitching was supposed to start with Brad Penny, the one arm on the staff that looked like a real difference-maker. Penny has been an unmitigated disaster: 1-6 with a 6.26 ERA, 21 walks and 13 home runs in 50.1 innings. Matt Kinney is 0-4 at 7.36. The man holding it together is Runelvys Hernandez, who came into the year with no fanfare and is 6-1 with a 2.79 ERA after a complete game in Arizona on Monday. Esteban Loaiza has a high ERA but 52 strikeouts and a 3.09 FIP, and he gave the Phillies 7.2 innings and 10 strikeouts in the 17-inning win in Atlanta on May 7, a game Cameron won with two home runs.

What's next

The preseason projections loved Philadelphia, putting it at 86-65, tied with Washington for the East. The March preview was more cautious: the offense would keep them afloat, but the pitching would be the limiting factor over a full season, and if Hammond faded and the young arms wobbled, they'd drift back toward .500. That's about where they are. The clubhouse, one of the healthiest in the league, hasn't been tested much; the worst run so far was a four-game losing streak at the turn of the month.

The floor looks high. This team should stick around .500, and the site gives it only a 1.9% chance of relegation. Andy Van Hekken and Miguel Gomez could help the rotation from the minors. Next up are one more in Arizona, three at home against St. Louis, and then the Dodgers, one in Philadelphia and two in Los Angeles.
