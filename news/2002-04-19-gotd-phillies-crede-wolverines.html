MLB · 2002-04-19 · By Ken Albrecht, The Baseball Courier

Joe Crede Went 5-for-5

Joe Crede had five hits, Detroit pinch-hitter Roberto Kelly tied it in the seventh, and Midre Cummings singled home the winner in the 11th as Philadelphia beat Detroit 6-5. Crede's night: two doubles, a homer and two RBI.

PHILADELPHIA — Joe Crede has spent most of his first month in Philadelphia as the answer to a trivia question: who’s the third baseman on Derek Jeter’s team?

On Friday night, he was the answer to a better one. Crede went 5-for-5 with two doubles and a home run, and the Phillies beat the Detroit Wolverines 6-5 in 11 innings.

“I don’t think I’ve ever done that,” Crede said. “Not in high school, not anywhere. You get three, you think about four. You get four, you try not to think about anything.”

Early hole

The night started badly. Hee-seop Choi, Detroit’s 23-year-old DH, came into the game hitting .186 and hit a three-run homer off Horacio Estrada in the first. Choi has been one of the bigger disappointments in Detroit’s young lineup, and for one swing he looked like the hitter the Wolverines thought they were getting.

The Phillies chipped away. Jeter drove in two runs with a sacrifice fly and a single. Crede homered in the second. Ben Davis grounded in the go-ahead run in the fourth. In the sixth, Crede doubled in another run, and it was 5-3.

The seventh

Then Phillies manager Larry Bowa made a decision he’ll be asked about for a while. Estrada had pitched well after the first, and Bowa gave him the seventh. When Estrada tired, Bowa went to Chris Hammond, the 36-year-old who came out of retirement over the winter, with two runners on.

Detroit manager Phil Garner sent up Roberto Kelly, 37, to pinch-hit. Kelly doubled them both home. 5-5.

“Roberto’s been in a lot of big spots,” Garner said. “He knows what they feel like. That’s why he’s on the team.”

Four quiet innings, then Cummings

From there, the bullpens took over. Hammond, Jonathan Johnson and Ron Villone allowed Detroit only a couple of hits the rest of the way. Detroit’s Jeremy Affeldt, Bob Howry and Mike Jackson matched them.

In the 11th, Luke Prokopec, Detroit’s long reliever, put four men on in two-thirds of an inning, and with two outs and runners on second and third, Midre Cummings singled home the winner. Villone got the win.

Crede, by then, had already done his part. He was 5-for-5. He’d been asked about the trivia question before.

“I’m fine being the other guy,” he said. “Derek’s the guy. I’m the guy who plays next to the guy. Tonight I was a guy too.”

The Phillies are 11-8. Detroit is 9-10. James Mouton, who came in hitting .131, had three hits for the Wolverines, which is its own kind of story, for a different day.
