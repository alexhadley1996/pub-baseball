MLB · 2002-04-21 · By Nate Fischer, Ball Theory

The One St. Louis Threw Back Came Back

Albert Pujols, released by the Cardinals before the strike, returned to St. Louis and hit the go-ahead sacrifice fly in the 10th. Jeff D'Amico pitched nine innings, Ramon Castro homered twice off 18-year-old Zack Greinke, and Oakland won 4-2 for its fifth straight.

Let’s get the awkward part out of the way. Before the strike, the St. Louis Cardinals had a 20-year-old infielder in their system named Albert Pujols. They looked at him and saw, in the language of the scouting report everybody has now read, an inconsequential minor leaguer. They let him go. Oakland picked him up.

This weekend, Pujols played his first series at Busch Stadium as a visitor. He went 0-for-4 over nine innings. Then, in the 10th, with the bases loaded and nobody out, he lifted a fly ball to center that scored the go-ahead run, and the A’s beat the Cardinals 4-2.

I want to be careful here, because a sacrifice fly is not a home run, and the narrative wants it to be a home run. But I’m also a person who has watched this franchise make the same kind of mistake more than once, and I’m allowed one moment of enjoyment.

“I don’t think about that,” Pujols said, which is what he has said every time anyone has asked him about the Cardinals. “I think about the next pitch. Tonight the next pitch was a fly ball.”

D’Amico and Greinke

The game we were promised, before Pujols showed up in the 10th, was a pitchers’ duel, and the pitchers delivered.

Jeff D’Amico, who came in 0-2 despite a 2.90 ERA, which tells you most of what you need to know about Oakland’s run support in his starts, went nine innings on 100 pitches. He walked nobody, struck out six and allowed two runs, a second-inning RBI single by Pat Borders and a seventh-inning homer by Raul Ibanez, the converted catcher who came in hitting .429 and has become the best story in St. Louis.

Zack Greinke, 18, matched him. His only mistakes were two pitches to Ramon Castro, the A’s catcher, who came in hitting .164 with one home run. Castro hit both of them out, leading off the sixth and leading off the eighth. Otherwise Greinke allowed three hits over seven innings, struck out three and walked one.

He is 18. I need you to keep remembering that he is 18.

The 10th

The Cardinals brought in Francisco Cordero, their closer, for the 10th. He loaded the bases with nobody out. Pujols hit his fly ball. Orlando Merced added another sacrifice fly off Darren Holmes, and it was 4-2.

Mariano Rivera came on for the bottom half. Rivera has been, to put it gently, not himself since arriving from Alaska in the A’s network’s last-minute shuffle; he came in with an 8.10 ERA. On Sunday he gave up a single, walked a man and got the save anyway. Our model has no opinion about this. I have several.

Where it leaves them

Oakland has won five straight and is 12-9, which is better than most projections thought it would be at this point. The Cardinals have lost four straight and are 11-10, and somewhere in their front office, someone who signed off on the Pujols decision is having a rough week.

“He’s a good player,” Cardinals manager Tony La Russa said, choosing each word. “He was a good player then too. Not everybody saw it. That’s baseball.”
