The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Santo Domingo Has Five Walk-Off Wins and a +1 Run Differential

The Gallos were supposed to win with Javier Vazquez's rotation and lose late with a weak bullpen. The rotation has a 5.45 ERA, the bullpen has been better, and close games have put Santo Domingo at 21-19.

Santo Domingo Gallos · 21-19 · 208 runs scored, 207 allowed

Santo Domingo came into the year ranked ninth in the FBL, projected at 73-78, with a lineup brimming with young, undeveloped talent like Victor Martinez and a rotation led by Javier Vazquez that looked solid by FBL standards. The catch was the bullpen, or the lack of one, and a clubhouse listed among the league's motivation risks.

Motivation hasn't been a problem late in games. Santo Domingo is 21-19, fourth in the East Group and 3.5 games behind Louisville. The Gallos have scored 208 runs and allowed 207, the profile of a .500 team, and they're a game better than that because of five walk-off wins, a 5-2 record in one-run games and a 2-0 mark in extras. The site gives them a 41.2% chance at the playoffs.

Close games

The pattern showed up right away. They won their first four, starting with an 11-inning walk-off on Opening Day. On April 2 they beat Richmond with five runs in the eighth, the key blows coming from Ryan Freel and Orlando Hudson. On April 6 Vazquez went 8.1 dominant innings against Orlando, and a sacrifice fly won it, 2-1 on a walk-off. On April 14 Hartford's Ryan Vogelsong struck out 10 and left with a lead, and the Gallos scored four in the ninth for another walk-off.

Nearly every game has been tight, because the Gallos don't have much blowout gear. The lineup has been top-heavy, and three regulars are still well below average: Hudson at an 84 OPS+, Brian Hunter at 74 and third baseman Kevin Orie at 68. Martinez, 23, has a .356 on-base percentage and a 93 OPS+. Freel has been the most dependable hitter, with a .400 on-base percentage, 15 steals in 16 tries and 35 runs scored. Luis Polonia, 38, had an OPS over 1.000 three weeks in and has cooled only a little in part-time duty, hitting .361. On April 8 in Oklahoma City he had four hits and three RBI.

The rotation and the bullpen

Vazquez has held up his end. He has 64 strikeouts and 12 walks in 66.1 innings and a 3.93 ERA, with 10 strikeouts in each of two recent gems, a complete game in Albuquerque on April 28 and 8.1 scoreless innings against Hoboken on May 8. The rest of the rotation hasn't been solid. Cal Eldred, 34, is 0-3 with an 8.49 ERA and 10 homers allowed in 41.1 innings. Kevin Jarvis has a 6.03 ERA and Darren Dreifort a 5.74, though Dreifort is 4-1. The rotation's ERA is 5.45, and the staff has allowed 52 home runs, more than any in the FBL.

The bullpen, meanwhile, has been the better half. Its ERA is 4.29, more than a run lower than the rotation's. Kyle Farnsworth is 3-0 with six saves and a 1.54 ERA, and Heath Bell is at 2.66. Craig Dingman, at 8.16, has been the exception.

Next

The Gallos are 12-7 at home and 9-12 on the road. They're coming off a series in Monterrey where they lost a five-run lead on Sunday and then lost 9-8 on a walk-off, for once on the wrong end of one. Carolina visits next, then three in Honolulu and last-place Charlotte at home.
