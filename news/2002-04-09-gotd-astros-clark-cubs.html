MLB · 2002-04-09 · By Owen Kirby, Diamond Daily

Tony Clark Doesn't Care What You Think

Tony Clark crossed the line during the strike and has been hearing about it ever since. On Tuesday he had four hits, drove in Houston's second run and its last, and ended a 12-inning, 6-5 win over the Cubs with a walk-off single. Houston snapped a three-game slide.

HOUSTON — Tony Clark has been booed in four ballparks this season. Most of them were road games. One of them, on Opening Day, was in a park where he was booed even while Pedro Martinez was striking him out to end the game.

On Tuesday night, at Minute Maid Park, he got a standing ovation. Clark went 4-for-6 with two doubles, and with nobody out in the bottom of the 12th, he singled home the winning run in a 6-5 Astros win over the Chicago Cubs.

“I’m not going to sit here and tell you I didn’t notice,” Clark said. “I noticed. It’s nice. It’s not why I play. But it’s nice.”

The man who crossed

Some background, for those who’ve been lucky enough to miss it. Clark was among the first established players to break ranks during the strike, back when the whole sport was falling apart. Houston signed him when the new league formed. Half the players in the game have an opinion about that. Most of them are not polite.

“I had a family and a career,” Clark said in March. “I made a choice. People don’t have to like it.”

Houston, the most expensive team in baseball, came in 4-5 and had lost three straight. The lineup that cost so much has been ordinary. Alex Rodriguez went 0-for-6 on Tuesday. Jeff Kent came in hitting .121. Clark, meanwhile, has been the one guy who has hit from the first day.

Back and forth

The Cubs, at 6-3 one of the surprises in the MLB, led most of the night. Shawn Green led off the second with a homer, and Chicago was up 3-1 in the fourth on Brant Brown’s single. Clark doubled in a run in the bottom of the fourth, Rodriguez grounded in the tying run in the fifth, and Kent singled Houston ahead 4-3 in the sixth.

The Cubs got it back in the seventh against Julian Tavarez, with two outs: Miguel Cairo singled in a run, and Jarrod Patterson, playing second base, doubled in another. In the eighth Shawn Wooten singled in the tying run for Houston. It was 5-5.

The quiet part

John Wasdin, who hasn’t had many nice days this season, threw 3⅓ hitless innings of relief for Houston, and Scott Eyre added two more. For Chicago, Tim Worrell and Ricky Bottalico kept it tied through the 11th. There was a stretch in the late innings when you could hear individual conversations in the seats.

“That’s my favorite part of the game, honestly,” manager Jimy Williams said. “When it gets quiet like that, somebody’s about to do something.”

Clark

Sergio Atondo, 23, came in for the Cubs in the 12th. The first two Astros he faced got hits, and with runners on second and third and no one out, Clark slapped a single through the right side.

His teammates met him in shallow right field. Sammy Sosa got there first.

“Sammy and I haven’t always agreed on things,” Clark said. “Tonight we agreed.”

Houston is 5-5. Chicago is 6-4. Clark is hitting .409.
