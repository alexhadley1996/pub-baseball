MLB · 2002-05-02 · By Rob Lindqvist, Diamond Daily

Barry Bonds, Twice

Down 6-1 at Wrigley, the Yankees got two-run homers from Barry Bonds in the eighth and ninth, lost the lead on Todd Helton's homer, and won 8-7 in 10 innings on Lenny Harris' single. Bonds drove in five.

CHICAGO — For seven innings at Wrigley Field on Thursday, the Yankees looked exactly like what their critics said they’d be in March: Barry Bonds, Greg Maddux and a lot of guys who catch the ball well. The Cubs led 6-1. Bonds had singled in the first run of the game and otherwise been pitched around, the way every team has pitched around him since Opening Day.

Then the Cubs stopped pitching around him.

Bonds hit a two-run homer off Tim Worrell in the eighth. He hit another off Eddie Guardado in the ninth to give the Yankees a 7-6 lead. Todd Helton tied it back up with a homer in the bottom of the ninth, and Lenny Harris singled home the winning run in the 10th. The Yankees won 8-7.

“I don’t know why you’d pitch to him there,” Cubs manager Don Baylor said, and then answered his own question. “Well. I know why. Because the alternative was putting the tying run on. And then he hits it out anyway.”

The third inning

The Cubs got all the runs they’d need, or thought they would, in the third. Chuck Smith loaded the bases with nobody out, and it came apart: Helton singled in two, Omar Vizquel grounded in a run, Orlando Palmeiro singled in another and Jarrod Patterson added a sacrifice fly. Only one of the six runs charged to Smith was earned, a detail that will make his ERA look better than his afternoon was. Jose Molina singled in a sixth run in the sixth.

Mike Fyhrie, the Cubs’ starter, held the Yankees to one run over 5⅓ innings.

Eighth and ninth

In the eighth, with a man on first, Bonds hit Worrell’s pitch onto Waveland Avenue. It was 6-3.

Guardado came on for the ninth with a 0.84 ERA. Harris singled. Chan Perry, the first baseman, homered to make it 6-5. Carlos Baerga doubled. Jose Vizcaino flied out. And Bonds, with first base open and the tying run on second, got a pitch he could hit.

“The first one, I was just trying to put a good swing on it,” Bonds said. “The second one, I knew he had to come to me. That’s all you want. Somebody has to come to you.”

It was his 13th home run of the season. He finished 3-for-5 with five RBI.

Helton, then Harris

Mike DeJean, the Yankees’ closer, gave it back on his first batter: Helton homered to tie the game. DeJean walked two more and got out of it.

In the 10th, Obed Vega gave up three straight singles to start the inning, to Mark Grudzielanek, Bengie Molina and Harris, and Harris’ single scored Grudzielanek. Vega then got three fly balls with two men on, but the run was in. Chad Bradford, the submariner, closed out the Cubs in the bottom half for his second save. DeJean got the win.

The Yankees, who had lost two straight, are 15-16. They are not running away with anything. But on a day when the plan looked foolish for seven innings, the plan’s whole premise, that Bonds by himself can win you games other teams think are over, looked pretty good for two.

“That’s what we built this team around,” Joe Torre said. “That’s why he’s here.”
