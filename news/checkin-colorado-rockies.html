The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Colorado Was Ranked Dead Last. It Leads MLB in Home Runs.

The preseason No. 20 is 20-21, has won four straight, and has a second baseman with 13 homers. Alexander is not sure what to make of the Rockies, and neither is anyone else.

Colorado Rockies · 20-21 · 205 runs scored, 186 allowed

Here is what Alexander wrote about the Rockies' pitching in March, in full: "The worst pitching staff in the league, led by a weak Tony Armas. However, they have a couple of young prospects (Oliver Perez and Pat Neshek) that could develop, but as it is, this club is built to get hammered in Colorado." He graded it an F, ranked the club 20th of 20, and predicted a 63-88 record.

Six weeks in, Colorado is 20-21 with a +19 run differential. The Rockies just swept Washington at home and opened a road trip with a 4-0 win in Atlanta on Monday, their fourth win in a row. Their ERA is 4.14, 12th in MLB. Armas has a 3.63 ERA.

The home runs are real. Probably.

The Rockies lead MLB with 64 home runs, are second in runs per game and are second in stolen bases. Matt Stairs, a 34-year-old second baseman, has 13 of those homers and a 146 OPS+. Jose Canseco, 37, has 12, and Jon Nunnally has a 143 OPS+ in right. Alexander's March assessment of the lineup was a D+, "not much experience and less ability," with "a bit of pop" from Canseco and Stairs. At 40 games he still had doubts: "their 60 home runs are definitely a bit of an illusion; middling walk and strikeout numbers and the Coors effect reveal the true problems inherent in this lineup."

The road record backs him up. Colorado is 14-10 at home and 6-11 away. Still, some of the production doesn't look like a ballpark effect. Brad Ausmus, who had a .625 OPS last year, is at .302/.352/.473, and Alexander also likes his framing. Alex Gonzalez is hitting .313. Stairs, in Alexander's words, "has been excellent defensively at 2nd." On the other side, Jonny Gomes (.162) and Corey Hart (.218) "have been terrible at the plate," but the two of them have 16 steals in 18 tries.

A rotation that wasn't supposed to work

The fear was getting "hammered." Armas and Brian Moehler both have ERAs under 3.75. Alexander says the two "have actually been great despite their around 4 ERAs" once the park is accounted for. Moehler threw 8.1 shutout innings at Brooklyn on April 21. Paul Rigdon, with a 4.62 ERA, followed the sweep with seven scoreless innings in Atlanta on Monday. Chris Bootcheck started the club's first shutout, a 7-0 win over Philadelphia on April 4 that the roundup that day called "surprising." The bullpen depends on who's pitching. Gary Glover has a 1.53 ERA and Perez has five saves, while Neshek (9.82 ERA, three home runs in 7.1 innings) and Gus Gandarillas have struggled.

Some long weeks

The record hides a lot of close losses. Colorado is 5-9 in one-run games and 1-4 in extra innings. The bad stretch came in mid-April. On April 9, Boston rallied from four down in the late innings to win 10-9 in a game the roundup described as a bullpen collapse. Starting on April 11, they lost six straight: two to Houston at home, then three in Milwaukee by a combined four runs and one to Dallas. They have lost twice on walk-offs in Brooklyn and once in 11 innings at Yankee Stadium. The Yankees swept them in early May.

The good stretch was the week before that. Colorado swept Seattle at home with two walk-off wins. On April 30, Canseco went 2-for-3 with a homer and four RBI in a 5-4 win, and on May 2 they won 4-3 in 10. That was a five-game winning streak. Then on May 7, a day after losing 1-0 to Oakland, the Rockies beat the A's 14-3.

What it all means

"It's pretty hard to know what to make of this team at this point," Alexander wrote, "definitely better than expected." The relegation math agrees with him. The site's simulations give Colorado a 3.2% chance of finishing last in the West, a long way from the 63-88 he predicted. There are two more in Atlanta, then the Seals come to Colorado for three before the Rockies visit Arizona. Arizona and the Dodgers are tied for last in the West, three games behind the Rockies.
