The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Colorado Was Ranked Dead Last. It Leads MLB in Home Runs.

The preseason No. 20 is 20-21, has won four straight and has a second baseman with 13 homers. Nobody is quite sure what to make of the Rockies.

Colorado Rockies · 20-21 · 205 runs scored, 186 allowed

The Rockies came into the season ranked 20th of 20. The preseason rankings called their staff the worst in the league, led by a weak Tony Armas, with a couple of young prospects, Oliver Perez and Pat Neshek, who might develop. As it was, the club looked built to get hammered at Coors Field. The pitching got an F, and the projections had Colorado at 63-88.

Six weeks in, the Rockies are 20-21 with a +19 run differential. They just swept Washington at home and opened a road trip with a 4-0 win in Atlanta on Monday, their fourth straight. Their ERA is 4.14, 12th in MLB. Armas's is 3.63.

The home runs are real. Probably.

Colorado leads MLB with 64 home runs and is second in both runs per game and stolen bases. Matt Stairs, a 34-year-old second baseman, has 13 of those homers and a 146 OPS+. Jose Canseco, 37, has 12, and Jon Nunnally has a 143 OPS+ in right. The lineup got a D+ in March for not much experience and less ability, with a bit of pop from Canseco and Stairs, and there's still reason to be skeptical. With middling walk and strikeout rates, a lot of the power looks like Coors.

The road record makes that case: 14-10 at home, 6-11 away. Some of it doesn't look like the park, though. Brad Ausmus, a .625 OPS hitter last year, is hitting .302 and has framed well. Alex Gonzalez is hitting .313. Stairs has been excellent with the glove at second. At the other end, Jonny Gomes is hitting .162 and Corey Hart .218, though the two of them are 16-for-18 stealing bases.

A rotation that wasn't supposed to work

The pitching hasn't been hammered. Armas and Brian Moehler both have ERAs under 3.75, which in that park makes them great. Moehler threw 8.1 shutout innings in Brooklyn on April 21. Paul Rigdon, at 4.62, followed the Washington sweep with seven scoreless innings in Atlanta on Monday. Chris Bootcheck started the club's surprising first shutout, a 7-0 win over Philadelphia on April 4. The bullpen depends on who's pitching. Gary Glover has a 1.53 ERA and Perez has five saves, while Neshek, with a 9.82 ERA and three homers allowed in 7.1 innings, and Gus Gandarillas have struggled.

Close losses

A .500 record hides a lot of near misses. Colorado is 5-9 in one-run games and 1-4 in extra innings. The worst of it came in mid-April. On April 9 the bullpen collapsed as Boston rallied from four down late to win 10-9, and starting April 11 the Rockies lost six straight: two at home to Houston, three in Milwaukee by a combined four runs and one to Dallas. They've lost twice on walk-offs in Brooklyn and once in 11 innings at Yankee Stadium, and the Yankees swept them in early May.

The best of it came just before that. Colorado swept Seattle at home with two walk-off wins. Canseco had a homer and four RBI in a 5-4 win on April 30, and two days later the Rockies won 4-3 in 10 to finish a five-game winning streak. Then on May 7, a day after losing 1-0 to Oakland, they beat the A's 14-3.

What it all means

It's hard to know what to make of this team, except that it's better than anyone expected. The site gives Colorado a 3.2% chance of finishing last in the West, a long way from 63-88. Two more in Atlanta come next, then the Seals visit before the Rockies go to Arizona. The Diamondbacks and Dodgers, tied for last, are three games behind them.
