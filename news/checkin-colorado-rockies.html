Rockies at 40: 64 Home Runs, No Apologies

Colorado Rockies, 19-21 through 40 games

Count the home runs first: 64. Then everything else about the Rockies season (19-21, 201 runs scored) starts to make a kind of loud sense.

MLB has no wild card and no consolation prize: division winners play for the title, the last-place club in each division goes down to the ABL. The Rockies are 6 games behind the Athletics in the West Division. The floor is 3 games below them, where the Dodgers currently stand on relegation pace.

Through 40 games they were 19-21, scoring 201 runs and allowing 186 (+15). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 20-21, 6th in the West Division, 6 games back, and 10th of 20 in MLB. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 21 wins in 40 games. They are 5-9 in one-run games and 1-4 in extra innings.

The hitters

Their most productive hitter is Matt Stairs (2B, 34), at .254/.338/.599 with 13 HR. Jonny Gomes has 8 steals (caught 1 time). As a team they are hitting .252/.321/.463, 3rd of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 64 home runs. Then there is Brad Ausmus, whose OPS has gone from .625 in 2001 to .825 now. Some of that is the sample. The question is how much.

The pitchers

Tony Armas leads the starters with a 3.63 ERA over 52.0 innings (43 K, 23 BB). Brian Moehler (3.74 ERA in 55.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.24 ERA, 13th of 20 in MLB; starters 4.38, relievers 4.01. Oliver Perez has 5 saves and 1 blown.

If you want to understand the Rockies at 19-21, do not look at the blowouts. Look at the 14 games decided by a single run, in which they have gone 5-9. That is the difference between a good start and this one.

The best and worst of it

The best night was May 7, a 14-3 win over the Athletics. The worst was Apr 23, a 12-3 loss to the Wolverines. Their best 10-game stretch went 6-4 (Apr 21 to May 1); their worst, 3-7 (Apr 7 to Apr 17).

A couple of small, true things. They once scored 14 runs in a game (Apr 24, against the Wolverines); that one game is 7% of their season's runs.

What next

The next stretch is 10 games (5 at home) against the Braves, the Seals, the Diamondbacks, the Cardinals. Those clubs are 195-215 combined, a .476 winning percentage, so it will mostly be up to them. The hopeful read is the run differential: if runs keep coming at this rate, the wins tend to follow, and the Rockies are about 2 wins short of where that differential usually lands.
