40-Game Check-In · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

One Run at a Time in Hoboken

Hoboken Zephyrs, 18-21 through 39 games

If you want to understand the Zephyrs at 18-21, do not look at the blowouts. Look at the 8 games decided by a single run, in which they have gone 0-8. That is the difference between a good start and this one.

Their most productive hitter is Mike Kinkade (C, 29), at .325/.406/.600 with 8 HR. Reggie Sanders leads the club with 10 home runs. As a team they are hitting .287/.344/.491, 3rd of 20 in the FBL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 62 home runs.

The rotation's anchor is Travis Smith: 8 starts, 44.0 innings, 3.68 ERA. Roy Halladay (3.80 ERA in 64.0 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.79 ERA, 14th of 20 in the FBL; starters 4.51, relievers 5.34. Closing has mostly meant Rafael Soriano: 10 saves, 1.69 ERA.

Through 39 games, all they had played when the export was taken on May 13, they were 18-21, with 196 runs scored and 200 allowed (-4). As of the May 13 export they are 8th in the East Group, 6 games back, and 14th of 20 in the FBL. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 19 wins in 39 games. They are 0-8 in one-run games and 1-3 in extra innings.

That 1.006 OPS is also the surprise. In 2001, per the export, Mike Kinkade managed .726 over 177 plate appearances, which is a long way from here. In the rotation, Claudio Vargas has a 6.75 ERA over 29.1 innings.

Odds and ends: When they score three runs or fewer they are 3-13; when they score six or more, 12-4. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks. Rusty Greer has walked 17 times and struck out 12, a ratio that belongs to a different, more patient century.

The best night was Apr 18, a 14-5 win over the Flying Squirrels. The worst was May 13, a 12-0 loss to the Disco Turkeys. Their best 10-game stretch went 8-2 (Apr 14 to Apr 23); their worst, 2-8 (Apr 3 to Apr 13).

There are subtle teams and there are the Zephyrs, who have hit 62 home runs in 39 games and seem unbothered by subtlety.

The FBL is the bottom rung and nobody gets relegated from it; the whole point is the way up. The top seven make a ladder playoff, seeds one and two are promoted outright, and the winner of the Federal Promotion round makes three. They are 14th of 20 in the table, 3 games behind the Larks for the last ladder place.

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 6 of them at home, against the Knights, the 66ers, the Mounties, the Twisters, opponents with a combined 174-226 record (.435). That is a chance to bank wins. Their record is about what their runs say it should be, which is reassuring or alarming depending on which side of .500 you are standing on. Watch Mike Kinkade: the offense has run through him.
