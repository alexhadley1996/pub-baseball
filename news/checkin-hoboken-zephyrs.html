The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Hoboken Leads the FBL in Home Runs and Is 0-8 in One-Run Games

Alexander expected the Zephyrs to win with young arms and a weak lineup. They've hit more home runs than anyone, lost every close game, and are 18-21.

Hoboken Zephyrs · 18-21 · 196 runs scored, 200 allowed

In March, Alexander ranked Hoboken 16th in the FBL. He called it "one of the weaker lineups; almost no stars," and said "this team will rely more on their pitching than their hitting." The pitching was "a three-headed pitching monster of youth: Roy Halladay, Livian Hernandez, and Carlos Silva." The bullpen was "extremely weak; a young Rafael Soriano looks promising but after him....nothing."

Instead, the Zephyrs lead the FBL with 62 home runs and rank third in OPS. Their staff ERA is 4.79, 14th in the league. And the record matches neither preseason description: Hoboken is 18-21, eighth in the East Group and six games back. It's 0-8 in one-run games.

The lineup that wasn't supposed to hit

Michael Young, the "exciting player" in the March review, is hitting .344/.393/.500. He's also 2-for-7 stealing bases. Vernon Wells has 10 home runs. On April 16 he drove in five with two homers in a 13-5 win over Savannah, which the week-three notes singled out. Wily Mo Peña, the other corner outfielder Alexander named, played two games for Hoboken in early April and has spent the rest of the season with Staten Island.

The two best hitters weren't in the preview. Reggie Sanders, 34, is hitting .304/.373/.615 with 10 home runs and a 151 OPS+. On April 28 he hit two home runs and drove in four in a 13-inning walk-off win over San Antonio. Catcher Mike Kinkade is at .325/.406/.600 with eight home runs and a 157 OPS+, the best on the team. He's the "red-hot Mike Kincaid" in the April 12 roundup and the catcher "still hitting over .400" in the week-three notes. Tom Wilson (116) and Randall Simon (104) have also been above average. The lineup's weak spots are Juan Castro at second (65 OPS+) and Rusty Greer at first (88).

The monster

Halladay has been good, not dominant. He's 5-3 with a 3.80 ERA. On April 6 he threw eight shutout innings against Eugene, which the roundup noted as "8 shutout, 8 K, 1 BB." He has also walked 26 batters, the most on the staff. Hernandez has a 4.08 ERA. Silva is 2-3 with a 5.49 ERA and has struck out 13 in 41 innings. On April 3 in Albuquerque he lasted 1.2 innings. Travis Smith, a 29-year-old who wasn't part of the "monster," has the best ERA in the rotation at 3.68.

Alexander was right about the bullpen. Soriano has 10 saves, a 1.69 ERA and no home runs allowed, but he's 0-4. All four losses came on walk-offs or in extra innings, and three were by one run. Gabe Molina has a 9.00 ERA, Brett Jodie 6.41 and Rodney Myers 6.32. As a group, the bullpen has a 5.34 ERA. In Carolina on Saturday, Hoboken lost a five-run lead and then lost 8-7 on a walk-off in the 10th.

Streaks

Hoboken's season has come in long streaks. It lost seven straight from April 8 to 15, then won eight straight from April 16 to 23, including a three-game sweep in Richmond during the stretch the week-three notes described as "a lineup that has been both deep and hot." Since then the Zephyrs are 6-11. They're 12-7 at home and 6-14 on the road. They finished a road trip on Monday with a 12-0 loss in Carolina. Halladay started that game.

A team with a -4 run differential and no one-run wins is probably due for some better luck. The site projects 73 wins and gives Hoboken a 14.6% chance at the playoffs, close to Alexander's 75-76 prediction, though the team got there by a very different route. Next, last-place Charlotte visits for three games. Then the Zephyrs play three in Inland Empire before returning home for Vancouver.
