The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Hoboken Leads the FBL in Home Runs and Is 0-8 in One-Run Games

The Zephyrs were supposed to win with young arms and a weak lineup. They've hit more home runs than anyone, lost every close game and are 18-21.

Hoboken Zephyrs · 18-21 · 196 runs scored, 200 allowed

Hoboken was supposed to be a pitching team. The preseason consensus ranked the Zephyrs 16th in the FBL, with one of the weaker lineups in the league and almost no stars, so the team would lean on a three-headed monster of young arms: Roy Halladay, Livan Hernandez and Carlos Silva. The bullpen looked extremely weak, with a promising young Rafael Soriano and nothing after him.

Instead, the Zephyrs lead the FBL with 62 home runs and rank third in OPS, and their staff ERA is 4.79, 14th in the league. The record fits neither picture. Hoboken is 18-21, eighth in the East Group and six games back, and it's 0-8 in one-run games.

The lineup that wasn't supposed to hit

Michael Young, the most exciting player on the roster coming in, is hitting .344 with a .393 on-base percentage, though he's only 2-for-7 stealing. Vernon Wells has 10 home runs, two of them in a five-RBI night in a 13-5 win over Savannah on April 16. Wily Mo Pena, the other young corner outfielder, played two games for Hoboken in early April and has spent the rest of the season with Staten Island.

The two best hitters have come out of nowhere. Reggie Sanders, 34, is slugging .615 with 10 homers and a 151 OPS+, and on April 28 he hit two homers and drove in four in a 13-inning walk-off win over San Antonio. Catcher Mike Kinkade has been even better, hitting .325 with eight homers and a team-best 157 OPS+; he was still over .400 three weeks into the season. Tom Wilson and Randall Simon have also been above average. The soft spots are Juan Castro at second, with a 65 OPS+, and Rusty Greer at first, at 88.

The monster

Halladay has been good rather than dominant. He's 5-3 with a 3.80 ERA, and on April 6 he threw eight shutout innings against Eugene with eight strikeouts and one walk. He has also walked 26, the most on the staff. Hernandez has a 4.08 ERA. Silva is 2-3 with a 5.49 ERA and 13 strikeouts in 41 innings, and on April 3 in Albuquerque he lasted 1.2 innings. The best ERA in the rotation belongs to Travis Smith, a 29-year-old, at 3.68.

The bullpen has been as thin as feared. Soriano has 10 saves, a 1.69 ERA and no home runs allowed, and he's somehow 0-4. All four losses came on walk-offs or in extra innings, three of them by a run. Behind him, Gabe Molina has a 9.00 ERA, Brett Jodie 6.41 and Rodney Myers 6.32, and the bullpen as a whole is at 5.34. On Saturday in Carolina, Hoboken blew a five-run lead and lost 8-7 on a walk-off in the 10th.

Streaks

The season has come in long runs. Hoboken lost seven straight from April 8 to 15, then won eight straight from April 16 to 23, including a sweep in Richmond, when the lineup was both deep and hot. Since then the Zephyrs are 6-11. They're 12-7 at home and 6-14 on the road, and they finished the trip Monday with a 12-0 loss in Carolina, with Halladay on the mound.

A team with a -4 run differential and no one-run wins is probably due for some better luck. The site projects 73 wins and gives Hoboken a 14.6% chance at the playoffs, close to the preseason projection of 75-76, reached by a very different route. Last-place Charlotte visits next, then come three in Inland Empire and Vancouver at home.
