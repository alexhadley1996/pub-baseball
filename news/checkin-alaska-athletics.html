The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Alaska Was the FBL's Surest Thing. The Pitching Held Up. The Hitting Didn't.

Alexander's preseason No. 1 is 20-20. The Athletics have the best ERA in the FBL and the second-fewest runs, and the "remarkably hole-free" lineup has several holes.

Alaska Athletics · 20-20 · 151 runs scored, 155 allowed

In March Alaska was supposed to be the easy call. Alexander ranked the Athletics first in the FBL, and his predicted standings had them at 94-57, "the most lopsided favorite of the three tiers... 14 games clear of anyone in the West Group, built on a projected 634 runs allowed."

The runs-allowed part is going fine. Alaska has allowed 155 runs, the fewest in the FBL, and its 3.77 ERA leads the league. The rest of the projection isn't. The Athletics are 20-20, sixth in the West Group and six games behind Monterrey. They've scored 151 runs, and only one FBL team has scored fewer per game. They've hit 27 home runs, the fewest in the league. The site's projection now has them at about 75 wins, with a 26.1% chance at the playoffs.

Matt Morris, as advertised

The power rankings named "three legit starting pitchers in Jeff Suppan, Kevin Appier and Matt Morris." Two of the three have been better than legit. Morris opened the season with a complete-game shutout at Eugene. Five days later he held Honolulu to one hit over eight innings and struck out 13, which the April 6 roundup called a "masterpiece." He has a 1.83 ERA over 68.2 innings. Suppan is 4-1 with a 2.11 ERA and has allowed two home runs. Ryan Jensen, who wasn't mentioned in March, has a 2.68 ERA. Appier is the exception at 1-5 with a 4.82 ERA. As a group the rotation has a 3.24 ERA.

The bullpen has been a problem, and part of the reason is a name missing from the box scores. "Mariano Rivera closing games is terrifying," the rankings said. Rivera has pitched in 18 games this season, all for Oakland in MLB. Rod Beck took the job in Alaska. He has 10 saves and a 6.28 ERA, and on April 28 in Sacramento he gave up five runs in two-thirds of an inning as the Athletics lost a four-run lead, 8-6. Ten days earlier they lost a five-run lead in Hartford. Jamie Walker has blown four saves and has a 9.22 ERA. The bullpen ERA is 5.23.

The holes

The lineup was "remarkably hole-free," Alexander wrote, with "Bernie Williams and Junior Spivey" at the top. Williams has played all of his 31 games for New Orleans in the ABL. Spivey is hitting .300/.387/.393. He had four hits in a 2-1 win over Savannah on April 9 and went 5-for-5 at Sacramento on April 28. Rontrez Johnson has been the best hitter, with a 121 OPS+ and 11 steals in 11 tries. In Hartford on April 18 and 19 he went 8-for-10 with three home runs and eight RBI. After that come the holes. Third baseman John McDonald has a 40 OPS+. Shortstop Mike Benjamin, 36, has a 30 OPS+ and three walks in 134 plate appearances. Dave Hansen, at first base, has a 63. Mark Little, in left, has a 67.

The result is a team that has to pitch its way to wins. Alaska has scored two or fewer runs 14 times and still won five of those games, including 2-0 wins at Richmond and in Inland Empire and a 10-inning 2-1 win over Hoboken. The Athletics have also been shut out twice.

Lately

Alaska is 8-4 in May and won six of eight before Monday's 3-2 walk-off loss at Inland Empire. Its run differential is -4, which says a .500 team is about right. Vancouver, third in the West, comes in for three, followed by three games in Oklahoma City and a series with Louisville, the East leader. At six games back, the March favorite would need the lineup to start hitting for the pitching to matter.
