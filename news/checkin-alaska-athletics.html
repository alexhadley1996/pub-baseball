The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Alaska Was the FBL's Surest Thing. The Pitching Held Up. The Hitting Didn't.

The preseason No. 1 is 20-20. The Athletics have the best ERA in the FBL and the second-fewest runs, and a lineup that looked remarkably hole-free has several holes.

Alaska Athletics · 20-20 · 151 runs scored, 155 allowed

In March Alaska was supposed to be the easy call. The preseason consensus ranked the Athletics first in the FBL, and the projections had them at 94-57, the most lopsided favorite in any of the three leagues, 14 games clear of anyone in the West Group, built on just 634 projected runs allowed.

The runs-allowed part is going fine. Alaska has given up 155 runs, the fewest in the FBL, and its 3.77 ERA leads the league. The rest hasn't followed. The Athletics are 20-20, sixth in the West Group and six games behind Monterrey. They've scored 151 runs, and only one FBL team scores fewer per game. Their 27 home runs are the fewest in the league. The site now projects about 75 wins and gives them a 26.1% chance at the playoffs.

Matt Morris, as advertised

The rotation came in with three legitimate starters in Jeff Suppan, Kevin Appier and Matt Morris, and two of them have been better than that. Morris opened the season with a complete-game shutout at Eugene, and five days later he held Honolulu to one hit over eight innings with 13 strikeouts, a masterpiece. He has a 1.83 ERA in 68.2 innings. Suppan is 4-1 with a 2.11 ERA and has allowed two home runs. Ryan Jensen has added a 2.68 ERA. Appier is the exception, at 1-5 with a 4.82 ERA. As a group the rotation is at 3.24.

The bullpen has been a problem, and part of the reason is a name missing from the box scores. Mariano Rivera was supposed to be closing games here. He's pitched in 18 games this season, all of them for Oakland in MLB. Rod Beck took the job and has 10 saves and a 6.28 ERA, and on April 28 in Sacramento he gave up five runs while getting two outs as the Athletics blew a four-run lead and lost 8-6. Ten days earlier they'd blown a five-run lead in Hartford. Jamie Walker has four blown saves and a 9.22 ERA, and the bullpen ERA is 5.23.

The holes

The lineup, built around Bernie Williams and Junior Spivey, looked remarkably hole-free. Williams has played all 31 of his games for New Orleans in the ABL. Spivey is hitting .300 with a .387 on-base percentage, and he had four hits in a 2-1 win over Savannah on April 9 and five at Sacramento on April 28. Rontrez Johnson has been the best hitter, with a 121 OPS+ and 11 steals in 11 tries, and in Hartford on April 18 and 19 he went 8-for-10 with three homers and eight RBI.

After that come the holes. Third baseman John McDonald has a 40 OPS+. Shortstop Mike Benjamin, 36, has a 30 OPS+ and three walks in 134 plate appearances. First baseman Dave Hansen is at 63 and left fielder Mark Little at 67. So Alaska has to pitch its way to wins. It has scored two or fewer runs 14 times and still won five of those games, including 2-0 wins at Richmond and Inland Empire and a 10-inning 2-1 win over Hoboken. It's also been shut out twice.

Lately

Alaska is 8-4 in May and had won six of eight before Monday's 3-2 walk-off loss at Inland Empire. A -4 run differential says .500 is about right. Vancouver, third in the West, comes in for three, then the Athletics go to Oklahoma City and play a series with Louisville, the East leader. Six games back, the March favorite still has the pitching to make a run. It needs the lineup to give it a reason.
