ABL · 2002-05-09 · By Ned Calloway, Diamond Daily

Mulder Deserved Better, and Fick Made Sure the Padres Got It

Mark Mulder pitched 8⅔ innings against Anaheim with eight strikeouts and no walks and didn't get a decision. Robert Fick drove in three of San Diego's four runs, including the winner in the ninth, and Joey Dawley won on one pitch.

SAN DIEGO — Mark Mulder threw 116 pitches on Thursday night. He struck out eight Anaheim Angels, walked none, and got within one out of a complete game. He also left with nothing to show for it, which is how this game goes sometimes.

Joey Dawley threw one pitch. He got the win.

In between, Robert Fick drove in three runs, the last one with two outs in the bottom of the ninth, and the San Diego Padres beat the Angels 4-3 in front of 34,156.

“I’m happy for Joey, I guess,” Mulder said, grinning. “One pitch. I should’ve thought of that.”

A duel

For five innings, nobody scored. Mulder and Anaheim’s Ryan Dempster traded zeros, and the first run of the night came from a 35-year-old: Marquis Grissom, hitting .385, homered with two outs in the sixth.

The Padres answered in the bottom half. With runners on first and second and one out, Fick doubled them both home off Dempster for a 2-1 lead. Dempster lasted 5⅓ innings, walking three.

Anaheim took it right back. Cliff Brumbaugh led off the seventh with a homer, his third hit of the night, to tie it. Three batters later, Jose Valentin singled home the go-ahead run, and the Angels led 3-2. That was the last run Mulder allowed.

Walking it in

San Diego tied it with a walk. In the eighth, Angels reliever Kevin Tolar came on with nobody out and a runner on first. Fick singled. Dustan Mohr walked to load the bases, and Damon Minor walked to force in the tying run. Tolar got out of it from there, but the game was 3-3.

Mulder took the mound again for the ninth, retired two, and gave up a two-out single to Scott Podsednik, who had three hits. Padres manager Bruce Bochy came to get him.

“He was at 116,” Bochy said. “He was terrific. I’m not going to let him lose that game in the ninth inning.”

Dawley got Valentin on a fly ball, and his evening was over.

The ninth

Tolar stayed in for the bottom of the ninth and walked Brad Wilkerson to start it. After a fly out, Brandon Larson, pinch-hitting, singled to put two on. Angels manager Mike Scioscia brought in Matt Mantei, who got Phil Nevin on a fly ball. Fick, who came in hitting .250, singled to end it.

He finished 3-for-5 with three RBI.

“I just didn’t want to be the guy who left Mulder out there with nothing,” Fick said. “He still has nothing. But at least we won.”

The Padres are 20-16, fourth in the ABL’s Western Conference, three games behind the Southside Black Sox. Anaheim fell to 19-17. Mulder, who came in 3-3 with a 3.62 ERA, is still 3-3.
