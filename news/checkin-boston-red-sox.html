The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Red Sox Are 4-17 on the Road, and Randy Johnson Can Only Pitch Every Fifth Day

Boston has walked more batters than any team in MLB, has a bullpen with nine blown saves and a 38-year-old ace with 81 strikeouts. It's two games clear of Atlanta for the East's relegation spot.

Boston Red Sox · 16-25 · 164 runs scored, 205 allowed

The best week of Boston's season was the first one. On April 5 the Red Sox trailed Oakland 6-1, still trailed 8-5 going into the ninth against Mariano Rivera, and won 10-9 in 14 innings, with Kevin Young and David Bell driving in three apiece. The next afternoon Rich Aurilia won it again with a walk-off single. Boston was 4-3.

The Red Sox are 16-25 now, ninth in the East, nine games out of first and two games ahead of Atlanta for the relegation spot. They have looked simply dreadful, and the site's simulations give them a 28.1% chance of going down.

Johnson and the walks

The plan in March was simple: the rotation was extremely weak behind Randy Johnson, so everything depended on Johnson dominating and pitching a ton of innings. Johnson, 38, has done his part. He has 81 strikeouts and 16 walks in 62.1 innings and a 3.18 ERA, and he has struck out 10 or more five times. He's 2-2. Pedro Astacio has been solid, too, with a 3.33 ERA and a complete game against Arizona on April 30.

Everyone else has been the problem. Colby Lewis has been an utter disaster, with an 8.08 ERA and 26 walks in 39 innings. Oswaldo Verdugo had an 8.10 ERA when the Red Sox moved 18-year-old Edwin Jackson out of the bullpen to stop the bleeding, and Jackson has a 3.06 ERA since. Three weeks in, Boston's 5.47 team ERA was about 0.6 runs worse than the next-worst staff. It has improved to 4.53, 18th in MLB, but the Red Sox have still walked 181 batters, the most in baseball, and allowed a league-high .309 batting average on balls in play. Javier Cruz has a 13.50 ERA in 11.1 innings.

In the bullpen, the numbers that matter most are five saves and nine blown saves. Matt Belisle, 21, has three of the blown saves, and on May 5 in Brooklyn the Red Sox gave back a five-run lead and lost 11-10 in 16 innings. To be fair to the late-inning arms, Belisle and Dan Serafini have mostly pitched very well. The mop-up men have been terrible.

The offense that never showed

The lineup got a B in March, with some of the strongest per-plate-appearance numbers in the league. It has never materialized. Boston is 16th in runs, 15th in OPS and 15th in home runs. Pat Burrell, with a .503 slugging percentage, and Fred McGriff, at a 120 OPS+, have hit. Aurilia, a .941 OPS hitter last year, has a .297 on-base percentage. Israel Alcantara, the regular in right, has no home runs and a .264 slugging percentage. The one fun line belongs to Shawn Gilbert, 37, who has three homers in 28 plate appearances. One was a three-run shot in his only at-bat on May 1 that beat Arizona.

Home 12-8, road 4-17

At Fenway, Boston is a perfectly respectable team. On the road it has been swept in Houston, Dallas and, last weekend, Detroit, and lost two of three in Chicago, Colorado, Milwaukee and Brooklyn. The ugliest loss came at home, though: 15-1 to the Braves on April 19, the team Boston is now trying to stay ahead of. Brian Sikorski beat the Dodgers 7-2 on Monday, and there's one more with Los Angeles before three in Miami and three at home with Toronto. The preseason projections had the Red Sox at 75-76. To get there they'd have to go 59-51 the rest of the way.
