The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Red Sox Are 4-17 on the Road, and Randy Johnson Can Only Pitch Every Fifth Day

Boston has walked more batters than any team in MLB, a closer corps with nine blown saves, and a 38-year-old ace with 81 strikeouts. It is two games clear of Atlanta for the East's relegation spot.

Boston Red Sox · 16-25 · 164 runs scored, 205 allowed

The first week of April was the best of Boston's season. On April 5 the Red Sox trailed Oakland 6-1, then trailed 8-5 entering the ninth against Mariano Rivera, and won 10-9 in 14 innings. Kevin Young and David Bell drove in three runs each. The next afternoon Rich Aurilia won it again with a walk-off single. Boston was 4-3.

The Red Sox are now 16-25, ninth in the East, nine games out of first and two games above Atlanta and the relegation spot. Alexander's 40-game entry begins: "The Boston Red Sox look simply dreadful so far this year." The site's simulations put their relegation chance at 28.1%.

Johnson and the walks

The March preview put it plainly: "The rotation is extremely weak behind Randy Johnson... it all depends on how much they can rely on Johnson to dominate and pitch a ton of innings." Johnson, 38, has done his part. He has 81 strikeouts and 16 walks in 62.1 innings, a 3.18 ERA and a 2.83 FIP, and he's struck out 10 or more in five starts. He's 2-2. Pedro Astacio has a 3.33 ERA and threw a complete game against Arizona on April 30.

Everyone else has been the problem. Colby Lewis, in Alexander's words "an utter disaster," has an 8.08 ERA and 26 walks in 39 innings. Oswaldo Verdugo had an 8.10 ERA when he was replaced by 18-year-old Edwin Jackson, who was moved from the bullpen "to stop bleeding." Jackson has a 3.06 ERA. At three weeks, Alexander called Boston "the disaster staff," with a 5.47 team ERA, "roughly 0.6 runs worse than the next-worst team." That has improved to 4.53, 18th in MLB. But the Red Sox have walked 181 batters, the most in MLB, and by Alexander's count have allowed the league's highest BABIP, .309. Javier Cruz has a 13.50 ERA in 11.1 innings.

The bullpen numbers that matter most are five saves and nine blown saves. Matt Belisle, 21, has three of the blown saves, and on May 5 in Brooklyn the Red Sox gave back a five-run lead and lost 11-10 in 16 innings. Alexander does separate the high-leverage arms, Belisle and Dan Serafini, who have been "pitching very well in the late innings," from the mop-up men who "have been terrible."

The offense that didn't show up

Alexander's March grade for the offense was a B: "Per plate appearance though, they have some of the strongest numbers in the league." The lineup, he wrote at 40 games, "has completely failed to materialize as they rank around 15th in pretty much every category." The numbers agree: 16th in runs, 15th in OPS, 15th in home runs. Pat Burrell (.297/.366/.503) and Fred McGriff (120 OPS+) have hit. Aurilia, who had a .941 OPS last year, has a .297 OBP. Israel Alcantara, the regular in right field, has no home runs and a .264 slugging percentage. Shawn Gilbert, 37, has three home runs in 28 plate appearances. One of them was a three-run shot in his only at-bat on May 1 to beat Arizona.

Home 12-8, road 4-17

Boston is a perfectly respectable team at home. On the road it has lost three straight in Houston, three in Dallas, and three in Detroit last weekend, plus two of three in Chicago, Colorado, Milwaukee and Brooklyn. The ugliest loss, though, came at home: 15-1 to the Braves on April 19, the team Boston is now trying to stay ahead of. The Red Sox beat the Dodgers 7-2 on Monday, behind Brian Sikorski, and have one more game against Los Angeles at home before three in Miami and then three at home against Toronto. Alexander's March table had them at 75-76. To get there they would have to go 59-51 from here.
