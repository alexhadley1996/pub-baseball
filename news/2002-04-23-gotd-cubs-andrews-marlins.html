MLB · 2002-04-23 · By Hal Brennan, Diamond Daily

Shane Andrews, of All People

Miami, the best team in MLB, led the Cubs 6-0. Chicago tied it in the seventh on Todd Helton's homer, and Shane Andrews, who had one home run all season, hit his second of the night in the eighth to win it 7-6. The Marlins have lost three straight.

CHICAGO — The Miami Marlins have spent April looking like the team everyone said they’d be when John Henry started writing checks during the strike. They came into Wrigley Field on Tuesday at 16-6, the best record in MLB, with the best rotation in the league and a lineup that doesn’t seem to have a soft spot.

For two innings they looked like that team again. Jason Giambi and Wes Helms hit two-run homers in the first. By the fifth it was 6-0, and the Cubs’ Darrell May had given up 11 hits.

Then Shane Andrews happened, twice.

“I’m not a guy who hits two homers,” Andrews said afterward, still sounding mildly surprised. “I’m a guy who hits one, every once in a while, and then people forget about it. Tonight was different. I don’t know why. I’ll take it.”

The middle

Andrews’ first homer, a two-run shot off Ruben Quevedo in the fifth, didn’t seem like much at the time. 6-2. Quevedo, Miami’s 23-year-old fill-in starter, was pitching well and would go into the seventh.

But the seventh is where the Marlins’ season-long weakness, a bullpen that is mostly a collection of guys trying to get to Kazuhiro Sasaki, finally caught up with them. Danny Graves came on with two runners aboard and couldn’t get anybody out. Melvin Mora doubled in two. Todd Helton, the Cubs’ best hitter, homered to tie it.

“That’s the inning,” Marlins manager Frank Duffy said. “We had it, and then we gave it to them in about five minutes.”

Wrigley got loud in a way it hadn’t been all April. The Cubs, who have spent most of the season a few games over .500 and invisible, suddenly looked like a team that might matter.

The eighth

Mark Wohlers came on for the eighth with a 1.12 ERA and got two outs. Andrews, who came into the night with one home run in 69 at-bats and a reputation as a glove-first third baseman, hit his second homer of the game into the left-field bleachers.

“I knew it was gone,” Andrews said. “I didn’t know what to do. I haven’t practiced that.”

Andy Pratt, a 22-year-old left-hander, got the win with 1⅔ hitless innings. Eddie Guardado pitched the ninth for the save, his ERA still at 0.00.

The Marlins

Miami has now lost three straight, the first real stumble of a season that had looked like a march. They are still 16-7 and still in first. Giambi went 4-for-5 with a homer and is hitting over .350. Cory Lidle, the ace, is 4-0. Nobody in Miami is panicking.

Duffy, though, didn’t sound thrilled.

“We’ll be fine,” he said. “But we’ve got to figure out the seventh and eighth innings. Every good team I’ve been around had that figured out by now.”

The Cubs are 14-9. Andrews finished 2-for-4 with three RBI. Wrigley Field, by the end, was singing his name, which may be a first.
