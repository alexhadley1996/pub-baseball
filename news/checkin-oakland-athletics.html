13 Straight and Then the Rest: the Athletics at 40

Oakland Athletics, 26-14 through 40 games

Thirteen in a row. That is the headline, and it should be: from Apr 23 through May 6 the Athletics won every game they played, and that run is most of the reason they are 26-14.

In MLB the stakes are blunt: win the division and you are in the World Series, a best-of-nine; finish last and you are relegated to the ABL. The Athletics are tied atop the West Division with the Seals, which is the most precarious way to be in first.

Through 40 games they were 26-14, scoring 192 runs and allowing 162 (+30). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 26-15, in first place in the West Division, and 1st of 20 in MLB. By the old Pythagorean arithmetic, that is a 23-win team so far. They are 10-4 in one-run games and 6-1 in extra innings.

The hitters

Albert Pujols has been the Athletics hitter opponents least want to see: .349/.411/.651, 11 homers, 34 driven in. As a team they are hitting .272/.333/.460, 2nd of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 54 home runs. Nobody should have expected Orlando Merced to be hitting .348/.381/.546. He posted a .786 OPS in 153 plate appearances in 2001. Forty games is enough to notice and not enough to be sure. The disappointment, at least so far, is Brian Giles: a .994 OPS in 2001, .783 through 162 plate appearances this year.

The pitchers

Tom Glavine leads the starters with a 2.86 ERA over 63.0 innings (32 K, 14 BB). Jeff D'Amico (2.98 ERA in 63.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 3.77 ERA, 5th of 20 in MLB; starters 3.48, relievers 4.70. Mariano Rivera has 12 saves and 1 blown.

If you want to understand the Athletics at 26-14, do not look at the blowouts. Look at the 14 games decided by a single run, in which they have gone 10-4. That is either a bullpen doing its job or a coin landing on its edge. Possibly both.

The best and worst of it

The best night was Apr 23, a 13-2 win over the Dodgers. The worst was May 11, a 20-3 loss to the Astros. Their best 10-game stretch went 10-0 (Apr 23 to May 3); their worst, 4-6 (Mar 31 to Apr 9).

Odds and ends: Brian Giles has walked 25 times and struck out 22, a ratio that belongs to a different, more patient century.

What next

The next stretch is 10 games (5 at home) against the Brewers, the Griffins, the Gothams, the Wolverines. Those clubs are 209-201 combined, a .510 winning percentage, so it will mostly be up to them. The longer-range question is whether the 3 or so wins they have banked beyond their run differential stay banked. History says to expect the runs to win that argument eventually; history is not always right in May.
