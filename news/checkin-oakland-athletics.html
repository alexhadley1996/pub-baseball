The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Thirteen Straight, Then Twenty Runs: The A's Are Tied for First and Still Explaining May

Oakland won 13 in a row, then dropped four straight, one of them 20-3 to Houston. The A's are tied with the Seals, and the cushion from late April is doing a lot of the work.

Oakland Athletics · 26-15 · 194 runs scored, 165 allowed

For two weeks this spring the Oakland Athletics did not lose. The streak started April 23 with a 13-2 win over the Dodgers, when Corey Koskie went 5-for-5 with four doubles and a homer, and it ended 13 games later, on May 6, with Jeff D'Amico throwing eight shutout innings in a 1-0 win at Coors Field. Along the way Oakland swept the Dodgers, the Blue Jays, the Phillies and the Cubs. Six of the wins were by one run and three went to extra innings, but the A's also outscored people 78-31. It wasn't all luck. The timing was just very good.

Then came the hangover. Colorado beat them 14-3 the next night. Houston came to the Coliseum and swept three games by a combined 32-9, including a 20-3 game in which D'Amico lasted 2.1 innings and gave up seven earned runs. On Monday the Brewers walked them off, 3-2. A run differential that stood at +53 before the Houston series is +29 now. The streak still pays the bills: at 26-15, Oakland is tied with San Francisco for first in the West and for the best record in MLB.

A rotation that outran its billing

Nobody saw this coming in March. The preseason power rankings had Oakland 11th of 20, with a C offense "built for the future," a rotation that looked "solid, though low-upside," and a bullpen "led by (and only by) popular closer Bob Wickman." The projections had the A's at 80-71, fifth in the West.

The rotation is the reason they're in first. Oakland's starters have a 3.43 ERA over 286 innings. Tom Glavine is 5-1 with a 2.70 ERA and three complete games, including a shutout of Arizona. Freddy Garcia has the highest ERA of the three, 3.68, and still leads the staff with 1.7 WAR on 48 strikeouts and 11 walks. D'Amico, who had a 6.08 ERA a year ago, is at 2.98 even after the Houston start.

The bullpen has been the shakier unit, with a 4.81 ERA and nine blown saves, and Wickman no longer closes. Mariano Rivera does. He has 12 saves and a 3.94 ERA that is mostly a .370 batting average on balls in play; his FIP is 2.69.

Where to put Pujols

The lineup's best hitter is also its biggest puzzle. Albert Pujols is 22 and hitting .351 with 11 home runs and a 184 OPS+, and his defense in left field has been terrible. A move to first base would seem to fix two problems at once, since Oakland's first basemen, Gene Schall and Tom Evans, are hitting .204 and .196. Manager Art Howe has kept Pujols in left anyway.

Koskie has helped cover for it. He came over from New Orleans, Oakland's ABL affiliate, on April 8 in the trade for Brian Roberts, and he's hitting .309 with a .528 slugging percentage and 1.6 WAR. Orlando Merced, 35, has a .930 OPS and went 5-for-5 at Wrigley on May 3. Backup catcher Sal Fasano homered twice in that game and has five home runs in 41 plate appearances, a barrage nobody expected. Second base has been the sore spot since Roberts left. Denny Hocking has been about league average, and Keith Lockhart has a 68 OPS+.

From here

MLB has no wild card. Each division winner goes to the World Series, a best-of-nine, and each last-place club drops to the ABL. Oakland is nine games clear of the bottom, so the only race that matters is the one across the bay. The Seals took two of three from the A's in April, all of them close, and the rematches come in June and August. The site gives Oakland a 36.1% chance to win the West and projects about 86 wins.

The question for the next month is which version is real, the team that went 13-0 or the one that has gone 1-5 since. The run totals point somewhere in between: they look like a team that should have about 23 or 24 wins, not 26. The A's are 10-5 in one-run games and 6-1 in extra innings. That kind of record can carry a team into June. It also tends to even out.
