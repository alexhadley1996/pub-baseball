40-Game Check-In · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

Larks at 40: Life After 6 Straight Losses

Eugene Larks, 21-18 through 39 games

The Larks season has a hole in it, six games long, running from Apr 17 to Apr 22. Around the hole is a team that has gone 21-12 otherwise, which is the more useful number.

The FBL is the bottom rung and nobody gets relegated from it; the whole point is the way up. The top seven make a ladder playoff, seeds one and two are promoted outright, and the winner of the Federal Promotion round makes three. They are the 7th seed today: in the ladder, 3½ games out of an automatic promotion spot and ½ games clear of the first team outside.

Through 39 games, all they had played when the export was taken on May 13, they were 21-18, with 197 runs scored and 164 allowed (+33). As of the May 13 export they are 4th in the West Group, 4½ games back, and 7th of 20 in the FBL. That run differential would usually produce a record around 23-16. They are 3-3 in one-run games and 1-0 in extra innings.

The hitters

John Olerud has been the Larks hitter opponents least want to see: .321/.423/.550, 7 homers, 23 driven in. As a team they are hitting .278/.348/.418, 12th of 20 in the FBL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 30 home runs. The surprise is Mark DeRosa. His 2001, by the export's count, was a .740 OPS over 184 plate appearances; this year it is .970. Players do not usually get 230 points better by accident, though they do sometimes get there by April. On the other side of the ledger, Craig Wilson is at .263/.381/.386, a long way from the .979 OPS he managed in 2001.

The pitchers

Jae-weong Seo leads the starters with a 2.24 ERA over 56.1 innings (25 K, 10 BB). Carlos Pulido (3.26 ERA in 49.2 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.02 ERA, 4th of 20 in the FBL; starters 4.00, relievers 4.06. Closing has mostly meant Ugueth Urbina: 10 saves, 0.77 ERA.

The best and worst of it

The best night was May 2, an 11-0 win over the Spirit. The worst was Apr 5, a 12-1 loss to the Solons. Their best 10-game stretch went 8-2 (Apr 23 to May 4); their worst, 3-7 (Apr 13 to Apr 22).

Some details worth knowing. When they score three runs or fewer they are 2-13; when they score six or more, 14-3. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks. John Olerud has walked 25 times and struck out 19, a ratio that belongs to a different, more patient century.

What next

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 4 of them at home, against the Bananas, the Flying Squirrels, the Juicers, the Yard Goats, opponents with a combined 193-206 record (.484). Neither a gift nor an ambush. One more thing to watch: John Olerud is on the milestone watch, closing on 1,000 rbi.
