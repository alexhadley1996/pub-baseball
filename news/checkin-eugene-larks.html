The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Eugene's Smorgasbord Has Outpitched Andy Pettitte

The preseason consensus ranked the Larks 15th, with a staff of Pettitte, Urbina and little else. Pettitte is 0-5, Eugene has the FBL's fourth-best ERA, and the Larks have a +33 run differential.

Eugene Larks · 21-18 · 197 runs scored, 164 allowed

The preseason consensus put Eugene 15th of 20 in the FBL, and the pitching got a D+. Andy Pettitte and Ugueth Urbina were in their primes, but after them came a smorgasbord of not-very-young career minor leaguers, plus reliever Kane Davis. The projections had the Larks at 71-80.

Eugene is 21-18, fourth in the West Group and 4.5 games behind Monterrey. It has the FBL's fourth-best ERA, 4.02, and a +33 run differential that suggests it should be about two games better than it is. The site gives the Larks a 56.5% chance at the playoffs and projects about 79 wins. The smorgasbord has been delicious.

The smorgasbord

Jae-weong Seo, 24, is 6-2 with a 2.24 ERA and has allowed two home runs in 56.1 innings. Three times he's gone at least eight innings and allowed one run or fewer, most recently eight scoreless against Santo Domingo on May 4. Carlos Pulido, 30, is 5-1 with a 3.26 ERA. On April 8 he went 8.1 innings against Carolina, allowing five baserunners and striking out nine, and on May 5 he threw eight more shutout innings against Santo Domingo. Jeriome Robertson is 3-1 with a 3.66 ERA. And Urbina has been exactly what was promised: 10 saves, no blown saves and one earned run in 11.2 innings.

Then there's Pettitte. He's 0-5 with a 5.40 ERA, though a 4.31 FIP says he deserves better, and the Larks have certainly not helped. Eugene has been shut out five times this season, and four of them came in his nine starts: April 1 against Alaska, April 6 in Hoboken, April 17 against Inland Empire and Monday in Honolulu. On April 6 he threw an eight-inning complete game and lost 2-0. On Monday Eugene lost 1-0 on a walk-off. The Larks are 2-7 when he starts.

Not much thump

The lineup came in as OK, aging and without much thump, and that's held. Eugene has 30 home runs, third-fewest in the FBL, but a .348 on-base percentage keeps it in the middle of the league in runs. John Olerud, 33, has done most of the work. He's hitting .321 with 25 walks and a 151 OPS+. Mark DeRosa is hitting .368 with a 149 OPS+ and leads the team with 1.9 WAR. Tsuyoshi Shinjo is at 118 and drove in five in an 11-0 win in Albuquerque on May 2. Chuck Knoblauch's best night came April 12 in Charlotte, a 4-for-5, one-homer game in a 10-inning win, but he's at 83 for the season. Brady Anderson, 38, is at 75. Craig Wilson, the future behind the plate, has a .381 on-base percentage as the regular catcher.

The run

The season turned in late April. Eugene lost six straight from April 17 to 22 to fall to 9-11, then went 8-2, including a five-game winning streak and a sweep of Memphis. The Larks are 12-7 since and 13-7 at home. They came into the year as one of the league's morale X-factors, the one club with both fan favorites and pranksters, and whatever the mix is, it's working. Next come three with Savannah, three in Richmond and Orlando at home.
