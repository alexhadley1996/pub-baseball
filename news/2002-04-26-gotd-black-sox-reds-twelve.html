ABL · 2002-04-26 · By Marty Kessler, Diamond Daily

The Black Sox Are Winning, and Jerry Reinsdorf Would Like You to Notice

Southside's bullpen threw 6⅓ hitless innings, Ivan Rodriguez tied it in the seventh, and Nick Johnson's double in the 12th beat Cincinnati 6-5. The team Jerry Reinsdorf renamed in a fit of spite is 16-8 and has won 10 of its last 11.

CHICAGO — In January, when the new league’s founders left the White Sox out of the top tier, Jerry Reinsdorf renamed his team the Black Sox. People took it as a joke, then as a tantrum, then as a dare. Three months later, it’s starting to look like a promise.

The Southside Black Sox beat the Cincinnati Reds 6-5 in 12 innings on Friday night, on Nick Johnson’s two-out double off Trevor Hoffman. They are 16-8, the best record in the ABL. They have won four straight and 10 of their last 11. And their bullpen has started doing things like Friday’s, when Albie Lopez and Terry Adams combined for 6⅓ innings without allowing a hit.

“Everybody wanted to talk about the name,” manager Jerry Manuel said. “I’d like them to talk about the bullpen. Those guys are why we’re here.”

Down early, again

The Black Sox have made a habit of falling behind and not panicking about it. Cincinnati scored three in the first off Jake Westbrook, all with two outs, and kept answering every time Southside caught up. Jason Bay’s single and triple brought home four runs. Adrian Beltre, who came in with 10 home runs at 23, singled in the go-ahead run in the sixth to make it 5-4.

Ivan Rodriguez tied it in the seventh with a two-out homer off Kevin Ohme. Rodriguez, hitting .395 coming in, is one of several Black Sox hitters putting up numbers that look like typos: Joe Randa was at .418 before the game, Jason Ellison at .383.

“You look at that lineup card and it’s a lot of guys nobody wanted in January,” Rodriguez said. “That’s how Jerry likes it. That’s how we like it.”

Hitless

Then came the part of the night that made the Reds’ dugout go quiet. Lopez came on in the seventh and got 10 outs without giving up a hit. Adams, the closer, came on in the 10th and got nine more. Cincinnati’s hitters, who came in averaging 5.4 runs a game, went 0-for-their-last-20-something, depending on who in the press box was counting.

Vladimir Guerrero went 0-for-6.

“We didn’t have it after the seventh,” Reds manager Bob Boone said. “Credit to them. Their bullpen threw strikes and got outs, and ours almost did.”

Hoffman

Boone gave the 10th, 11th and 12th to Hoffman, the 34-year-old who has been one of the best relievers in baseball for most of a decade. He came in with a 1.86 ERA and pitched well for most of his 2⅔ innings. With two outs in the 12th and a man on first, Johnson, the Black Sox’s 23-year-old first baseman and their home run leader, doubled into the corner, and the run came around.

“He’s a great one,” Johnson said of Hoffman. “I don’t even know what he threw me. I just know it was low enough.”

Adams got the win. Hoffman took the loss. Cincinnati is 11-13.

Reinsdorf watched from his suite, as he does most nights. He didn’t come down to the clubhouse. A team spokesman said he was “pleased.” Somebody who sat near him said he was a little more than that.
