MLB · 2002-05-07 · By Frank DeLuca, New York Evening Star

Seventeen Innings, and Then Seven Runs

Ernie Young's two-run homer in the ninth sent Philadelphia and Atlanta into extra innings, where nobody scored for seven innings. In the 17th, Mike Cameron, Miguel Cabrera and Felix Jose homered and the Phillies scored seven to win 11-4.

ATLANTA — By the 17th inning on Tuesday night, the score between the Phillies and Braves was the same 4-4 it had been since the bottom of the ninth. Then Philadelphia sent 12 men to the plate.

Midre Cummings walked. Mike Cameron homered. Roberto Alomar singled and was thrown out stealing. Derek Jeter singled. Miguel Cabrera, 19 years old, homered. Felix Jose homered on the next batter. By the time Alomar flied out to end the inning, the Phillies had seven runs, and they won 11-4.

“I don’t know what happened,” Phillies manager Larry Bowa said. “For seven innings we couldn’t hit anything. Then we hit everything.”

Loaiza’s night

It should have been a different story, and for most of eight innings it was Esteban Loaiza’s. The Phillies’ starter struck out 10, walked one and allowed four hits in 7⅔ innings. Philadelphia gave him a 4-0 lead: Dennis Anderson singled in a run in the second, Cameron homered and another run scored on a Cabrera single in the third, and Trenidad Hubbard homered to lead off the fourth.

The Braves got two back in the fourth on Steve Finley’s leadoff homer and Ernie Young’s sacrifice fly. Then Loaiza shut the door. He retired the side in order in the seventh, and he struck out Kevin Nicholson and Mo Vaughn in the eighth before Bowa went to Jonathan Johnson, who struck out Finley.

Atlanta’s bullpen had matched him. Jose Rijo, the 37-year-old who came back last season after five years away, threw 3⅓ scoreless innings and allowed one hit.

The ninth

Johnson stayed in for the ninth with a 4-2 lead. Hideki Matsui singled. Ben Broussard struck out. Ernie Young hit a two-run homer, his 11th, to tie it.

“That’s on me,” Johnson said. “Esteban pitched the game of his life, and I gave it away in one pitch.”

Johnson struck out the next two batters, but the game was going to extra innings.

Seven innings of nothing

From the 10th through the 16th, nobody scored. Atlanta’s Keith Foulke threw two hitless innings and Carlos Marmol, 19, threw two more. Philadelphia’s bullpen was just as good: Oscar Villarreal, Chris Hammond, Ron Villone, Pedro Borbon and Ignacio Flores combined to allow one hit in eight innings. The Phillies stranded two in the 12th and two more in the 13th. The Braves stranded runners in the 10th, 11th and 15th.

Juan Dominguez, a 21-year-old, came on for the Braves in the 15th and got out of that inning and the 16th. In the 17th he gave up two homers to the first five batters he faced. Baudel Zambrano relieved him and gave up Jose’s homer and two more runs.

The rest

Cameron finished 4-for-8 with two homers and four RBI. Jeter was 3-for-8. Alomar went 1-for-9 with four strikeouts. Flores, who pitched the 16th and 17th, got the win.

The Phillies, who had lost two straight, are 18-18. The Braves, last in the MLB East, are 13-23.

“Seventeen innings,” Bobby Cox said. “Sixteen of them were good.”
