FBL · 2002-04-28 · By Paul Ricci, Sporting Weekly

Reggie Sanders Was Due

Roy Halladay gave Hoboken eight good innings and still trailed. Reggie Sanders tied it with a two-run homer in the eighth and won it with another in the 13th, and San Antonio, the preseason darling of the FBL, lost its third straight.

HOBOKEN, N.J. — Reggie Sanders has played for seven big-league teams, and he will tell you, if you ask him, that he has never once been the guy people come to the ballpark to see. That’s fine with him. He’s 34. He has a routine, a lot of bats, and a theory that a veteran hitter who keeps showing up eventually gets the pitch he’s been waiting for.

On Sunday he got two of them. Sanders hit a game-tying, two-run homer with two outs in the eighth inning and a game-winning, two-run homer with two outs in the 13th, and the Hoboken Zephyrs beat the San Antonio Rebeldes 5-3.

“People say I was due,” Sanders said. “I’m always due. That’s the whole job. You stay due.”

Halladay, again

The afternoon started as another chapter in the Roy Halladay story, and for a while it looked like the wrong kind. Justin Morneau, San Antonio’s 20-year-old first baseman, hit a two-run homer off him in the first inning, his 10th of the season. The Rebeldes added a run in the sixth.

But that was it. Halladay went eight innings, struck out five and walked two, and the 3-0 deficit he left behind was more about his teammates than about him. Carlos E. Hernandez, the Rebeldes’ 22-year-old starter, held Hoboken to one run into the seventh. Stacy Anastasia, who runs the Zephyrs’ baseball side, watched the whole thing from her usual seat by the dugout and, according to the people around her, didn’t say a word until the eighth.

“Roy was fine,” manager Evan Griffith said. “Roy’s been fine for a year. We just weren’t hitting.”

The eighth

Then Sanders came up against Nate Robertson with a man on and two outs, and the ball landed in the seats in left. 3-3.

The game went quiet after that, five scoreless innings in which the most interesting development was Rafael Soriano, Hoboken’s 22-year-old closer, allowing one hit over three innings of relief with the kind of fastball that makes scouts lean forward. Brian Schmack followed with two more scoreless innings.

In the bottom of the 13th, with two outs and a runner on first, San Antonio brought in Dennis Cook, the 39-year-old left-hander. Sanders hit his first pitch out.

“I’ve faced him before,” Sanders said. “A long time ago. He doesn’t remember. I remember.”

San Antonio’s spring

For the Rebeldes, it was the third straight loss and the latest disappointment in a season that was supposed to look very different. One preseason ranking put them second in the FBL. They are 10-16, averaging 3.7 runs a game, and their best player so far, Josh Beckett, is 3-0 with a 2.02 ERA for a team that can’t seem to win when he isn’t pitching.

Morneau, who went 2-for-6 with a homer and a stolen base, has been the bright spot. He’s hitting .316 with 10 homers at 20. The rest of the lineup has been the problem.

“We’re better than this,” Rebeldes manager Mike Hamilton said. “I keep saying it. At some point we have to show it.”

Hoboken is 13-12. Sanders went 3-for-6 with four RBI and is hitting close to .300. Schmack got the win.
