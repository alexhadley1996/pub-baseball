ABL · 2002-04-20 · By Colin Mercer, Ball Theory

About That Bullpen

Baltimore led Salt Lake 5-2 in the eighth behind seven strong innings from Brian Sweeney. Then Aquillino Lopez faced three batters, Brad Fullmer hit his second homer of the night to tie it, and Alex Cintron singled home the winner in the ninth. Bees 6, Orioles 5.

In March, in our Orioles preview, I wrote that every projection I’d seen called Baltimore’s bullpen a strength, and that I wasn’t so sure. I said it had “more volatility than its reputation suggests.” I’d like to report that I take no pleasure in what happened on Saturday night in Salt Lake City.

I take a little pleasure. Not a lot. Mostly I feel bad for Brian Sweeney.

Sweeney, the Orioles’ 27-year-old right-hander, gave them 7⅓ innings and two runs, the kind of start that ought to get a team a win, and left with a 5-2 lead. The bullpen gave up four runs in an inning and a half, and the Salt Lake Bees won 6-5 on Alex Cintron’s single with two outs in the ninth.

How a 5-2 lead becomes a 6-5 loss

Baltimore had done everything right for seven innings. Darren Lewis doubled in two runs in the third. Benny Agbayani, the Hawaiian who’s been the Orioles’ best hitter all April, singled in two more in the eighth and finished 3-for-4.

The only thing Salt Lake had managed against Sweeney was Brad Fullmer, who homered with a man on in the sixth. Fullmer came into the night hitting .397 and is, quietly, one of the best hitters in the ABL.

Then, in the eighth, Mike Hargrove went to Aquillino Lopez. Lopez had pitched one inning all season and given up four runs in it, a 36.00 ERA, which the numbers people will tell you is a sample size problem and the eyes people will tell you is a warning. Lopez faced three batters and didn’t get any of them out. Fullmer, the third, hit a three-run homer, and the game was tied.

“I had a feeling,” Fullmer said. “Not about him, I didn’t know him. About the inning. Sometimes you can feel it.”

The ninth

Jason Kershner, who had a 0.84 ERA and has been the steadiest arm in the Baltimore bullpen, came on to clean it up and did, getting the Orioles out of the eighth. He gave up a run in the ninth anyway: two baserunners and then, with two outs, Cintron’s line drive to right.

Bob Scanlan, a 35-year-old who threw 1⅓ scoreless innings, got the win.

The broader point

I don’t want to make too much of one game. That’s the whole thing I keep telling people about April. But here’s what’s true after 19 games: Baltimore’s offense has been better than I projected, scoring over five runs a game, and its bullpen has been about what I worried it would be. Guillermo Mota hasn’t settled in as a closer. The middle relief is a coin flip.

Salt Lake, meanwhile, is the most extreme team in the ABL: a lineup that hits (Fullmer, Jason Michaels and Bill Selby are all over .350) and a pitching staff where nobody has an ERA under 6.00. The Bees are 10-9. They will win a lot of games 9-8. They will lose a lot of them 9-8 too.

“We’re not a pretty team,” Bees manager Chris Wampler said. “We’re an exciting one. I’ll take exciting.”

The Orioles are 10-9. Hargrove was asked about Lopez.

“I’ll take the blame for that one,” he said. “I’ll make better decisions.”
