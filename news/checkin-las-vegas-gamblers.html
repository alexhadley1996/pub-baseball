The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Kevin Brown Is 0-6, and Las Vegas Has Gone 7-1 Behind Josh Towers

The worry in March was that Josh Towers would pitch far too many meaningful innings. The Gamblers have won seven of Towers' eight starts and two of Kevin Brown's nine.

Las Vegas Gamblers · 20-20 · 218 runs scored, 251 allowed

The concern about the Las Vegas rotation in March came down to one name. Kevin Brown and Brandon Duckworth made a solid top of the rotation, but after that it fell off, with Josh Towers pitching far too many meaningful innings. The preseason consensus ranked the Gamblers 10th, and the projections had them at 80-71.

Here's how those starts have gone. Towers has made eight, and Las Vegas has won seven. Brown has made nine, and Las Vegas has won two.

Two starters, two seasons

By the numbers that best measure pitching, Brown has been one of the best starters in the ABL. He has 68 strikeouts and 12 walks in 59.2 innings, a 3.53 FIP and 1.9 WAR, the kind of pitcher who looks better than his ERA. The ERA is 5.43, and the record is 0-6. On Opening Day in Montreal he went nine innings, and the Gamblers lost in 13. On May 8 he struck out 13 Cleveland hitters in seven innings, allowed two runs and got a no-decision in a 5-2 win. On Monday he lost 10-2 in Mexico City.

Towers is 3-0 with a 7.54 ERA. The only one of his starts Las Vegas lost was April 26 against Minnesota, when he allowed seven runs in two innings. Otherwise the offense has bailed him out: 12 runs at Portland on May 6, 11 against San Juan on April 15 and six in a walk-off win over Cincinnati on May 1, when the Gamblers came back from five down.

Duckworth has been as good as expected, with a 3.60 ERA and two home runs allowed. The rest of the staff is the problem. Bud Smith struck out nine without a walk in 7.1 innings against New Orleans on April 8, but his ERA is 5.71, and the bullpen's is 6.32. Las Vegas allows the second-most runs per game in the ABL.

No true star

The lineup came in full of solid players in their prime, but without a true prospect or a true star. Magglio Ordonez has come close to being the star. He has 11 home runs and a 138 OPS+, and on April 16 he homered twice and drove in six against San Juan. Jason Kendall is hitting .380 and has struck out four times in 145 plate appearances. Desi Relaford went 5-for-6 with five doubles on Opening Day, beat New Orleans with a walk-off single on April 9 and is hitting .368. The weak spot among the veterans has been Jeff Conine, with an 84 OPS+.

The record

A 7-3 stretch from April 12 to 21 was one of the ABL's great weeks. Since then the Gamblers have gone 10-10, and they're 20-20, six games back in the Western Conference. Their -33 run differential looks more like a 17-23 team, and a 6-4 record in one-run games is what's holding them at .500. The site puts their relegation chance at 27.7%. Next come Nashville at home, three in Salt Lake and three in Baltimore.
