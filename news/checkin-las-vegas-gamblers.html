The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Kevin Brown Is 0-6, and Las Vegas Has Gone 7-1 Behind Josh Towers

Alexander worried that Josh Towers would pitch "far too many meaningful innings." The Gamblers have won seven of Towers' eight starts and two of Kevin Brown's nine.

Las Vegas Gamblers · 20-20 · 218 runs scored, 251 allowed

Alexander's preseason concern about the Las Vegas rotation came down to one name. "Kevin Brown and Brandon Duckworth pair as a solid top of rotation arms, but after that it falls off with Josh Towers pitching far too many meaningful innings." He ranked the Gamblers tenth and predicted 80-71.

Here is how those starts have gone. Towers has made eight starts, and Las Vegas has won seven of them. Brown has made nine, and Las Vegas has won two.

The two starters

In the stats that best measure pitching, Brown has been one of the ABL's best pitchers. He has 68 strikeouts and 12 walks in 59.2 innings, a 3.53 FIP and 1.9 WAR. The April 21 trends piece described him as having a "looks-better-than-the-ERA" profile. The ERA is 5.43, and his record is 0-6. On opening day in Montreal he pitched nine innings and the Gamblers lost in 13. On May 8 he struck out 13 Cleveland hitters in seven innings, allowed two runs, and got a no-decision in a 5-2 win. On Monday he lost 10-2 in Mexico City.

Towers is 3-0 with a 7.54 ERA. He allowed seven runs in two innings against Minnesota on April 26, the only one of his starts Las Vegas lost. The offense has scored for him: 12 runs at Portland on May 6, 11 against San Juan on April 15, and six in the walk-off win over Cincinnati on May 1, when Las Vegas came back from five down.

Duckworth has been as good as Alexander expected, with a 3.60 ERA and only two home runs allowed. The rest of the staff has been the problem. Bud Smith struck out nine without a walk in 7.1 innings against New Orleans on April 8, which the roundup noted, but his ERA is 5.71. The bullpen has a 6.32 ERA. Las Vegas allows the second-most runs per game in the ABL.

No true star

Alexander's take on the lineup was that it had solid players in their prime but "lack[s] a true prospect, and a true star." Magglio Ordonez has been close to a star. He has 11 home runs and a 138 OPS+, and on April 16 he hit two homers and drove in six against San Juan. Jason Kendall is hitting .380/.448/.473 and has struck out four times in 145 plate appearances. Desi Relaford went 5-for-6 with five doubles on opening day. On April 9 his walk-off single beat New Orleans. He is hitting .368. Jeff Conine has been the weak spot among the veterans. The trends piece noted that he "has struggled," and his OPS+ is 84.

The record

The trends piece listed Las Vegas among the ABL's "great weeks" after a 7-3 stretch from April 12 to 21. The Gamblers have gone 10-10 since and are 20-20 overall, six games back in the Western Conference. Their run differential is -33, which projects closer to 17-23. A 6-4 record in one-run games has kept them at .500. The site puts their relegation chance at 27.7%. Next up are three at home against Nashville, three in Salt Lake and three in Baltimore.
