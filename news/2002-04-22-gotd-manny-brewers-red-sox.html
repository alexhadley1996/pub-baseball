MLB · 2002-04-22 · By Owen Kirby, Diamond Daily

Manny Being Manny, at Boston's Expense

Aaron Harang, carrying a 7.43 ERA, held Boston to one run over 8⅓ innings. Marco Scutaro tied it in the eighth, and Manny Ramirez, facing his old team, doubled home the winning run in the ninth. Milwaukee 2, Boston 1.

MILWAUKEE — Manny Ramirez spent the first part of Monday’s game pretending this was just another game. He took batting practice, joked with the clubhouse attendants, and said he didn’t have anything special to say about the Red Sox, his former team.

He went hitless in three trips against Brian Sikorski. In the ninth, with one out, a man on first and the game tied, he doubled into the left-field corner off Matt Belisle, and the Milwaukee Brewers beat Boston 2-1.

Then he wasn’t pretending anymore. He threw his helmet in the air on his way to second and pointed at the Red Sox dugout, where several of his former teammates were laughing.

“I love those guys,” Ramirez said. “That’s why it’s fun. If you hate them, it’s not fun. I love them. So it’s fun.”

Two starters nobody expected

For seven innings, this was a game between two pitchers who had no business looking this good. Boston’s Sikorski came in with a 6.27 ERA and threw 6⅓ scoreless innings. Milwaukee’s Aaron Harang came in with a 7.43 ERA and allowed a solo homer to Javier Valentin in the fifth and nothing else for 8⅓ innings.

“Aaron’s been hit hard this year,” Brewers manager Davey Lopes said. “Tonight he was the guy we drafted. That’s the guy we’re waiting for.”

Edwin Jackson’s night

Boston led 1-0 into the eighth, and manager Grady Little gave the ball to Edwin Jackson, the 18-year-old who has been the talk of the Red Sox camp since spring training, when Randy Johnson took more pictures of him than of anybody else. Jackson had been starting. This was a bullpen experiment.

It didn’t go well. Jackson walked two in the eighth, and with two outs and a runner on second, Marco Scutaro, hitting .156, doubled to tie the game.

“He’s 18,” Little said. “Tonight I put him in a spot he’s not ready for yet. That’s on me, not him.”

Manny

John Bale got the last two outs of the ninth for Milwaukee and picked up the win. In the bottom half, with a man on first and one out, Little went to Matt Belisle, the 21-year-old with a 0.82 ERA. Belisle threw Ramirez one pitch he liked.

Ramirez, who leads the Brewers with six home runs, was asked if he had a message for Boston.

“Tell them I said hi,” he said. “And tell Pedro I’m still waiting.”

The Brewers are 12-10. Boston is 10-12, and the late innings have become the place where its season keeps going wrong.
