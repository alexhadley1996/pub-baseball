MLB · 2002-04-07 · By Frank DeLuca, New York Evening Star

Thirteen Hits, Two Runs, Fifteen Innings

The Yankees put 21 men on base in Atlanta and scored twice. The Braves, losers of five straight, scraped out three runs in 15 innings, the last on Esteban German's walk-off homer off Nelson Cruz.

ATLANTA — The Yankees had 13 hits and drew eight walks at Truist Park on Sunday. That’s 21 baserunners. They scored two runs.

The Braves, who had lost five in a row, didn’t do much better. They had 12 hits and went eight straight innings, the seventh through the 14th, without a run. Then Esteban German, a 24-year-old second baseman listed at 5-foot-9, hit a one-out homer off Nelson Cruz in the bottom of the 15th, and Atlanta won 3-2.

“I’m not going to tell you we deserved that one,” Bobby Cox said. “I’ll tell you we needed it. Those are two different things, and I’ll take the second one every time.”

Maddux in the other dugout

There’s no way to write about the Braves this spring without writing about the two pitchers they lost, and on Sunday one of them was sitting in the visitors’ dugout. Greg Maddux, 2-0 with a 1.72 ERA for New York, wasn’t scheduled to pitch, and spent most of the afternoon at the end of the bench with a towel around his neck, watching his old team.

“I’ve got a lot of friends over there,” he said afterward. “I’ll admit I was rooting for us. But I didn’t hate how that ended. They’ve been through a lot this week.”

Not much offense

Kris Benson pitched 7⅔ innings for the Yankees and was very good. Tim Harikkala, who came in with an 11.25 ERA, gave up 10 hits in seven innings for Atlanta and somehow allowed only two runs. Ben Broussard led off the fifth with a homer for the Braves. Adam Kennedy singled in the tying run in the sixth, Geronimo Gil singled in the go-ahead run in the bottom half, and Bengie Molina homered in the seventh to make it 2-2.

That was the last run either team scored for eight innings.

The middle

The innings between were a slow, odd sort of torture for the Yankees. Carlos Baerga, playing second, had four hits. Kennedy had three. Torii Hunter, who came in hitting .407, went 1-for-6. And every time New York put men on, the Atlanta bullpen walked off the mound with nothing having happened.

Keith Foulke threw two scoreless innings. Jose Rijo, 37 years old and in the middle of an unlikely comeback, threw 2⅓ more and walked three. Carlos Marmol, who is 19, got four outs. Jose Valverde threw a perfect inning. And Dennis Ulacia, 21, walked three in 1⅓ innings and didn’t allow a run.

“I walked the bases loaded, I think,” Ulacia said. “Then I got the out. You have to get the out.”

“It’s the best bullpen in the league if you only count the runs,” Joe Torre said. “We sure helped them look good.”

German

Cruz came on for New York in the 14th and got the first four outs he needed. Then German, who’d singled twice already, hit a 2-1 pitch into the left-field seats, and the Braves came out of the dugout like they’d won something much bigger.

“I don’t hit home runs,” German said. “I hit singles, I run, I try to steal. That one, I don’t know where it came from. Maybe from the losing. You get tired of it.”

The Braves are 3-5. The Yankees, 4-4, fly home. Their bullpen will be rested, at least. Atlanta used six pitchers. New York used four.
