The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Carolina Is 13-4 When Burkett or Castillo Starts, and 6-16 When They Don't

The question for the Disco Turkeys was whether the lineup could outhit the rotation. Two veteran starters are 13-1, Ken Griffey Jr. has 11 home runs, and Carolina is still a game under .500.

Carolina Disco Turkeys · 19-20 · 201 runs scored, 195 allowed

Carolina's question in March was whether its lineup could outhit its rotation. The lineup looked legitimately scary for this level. The rotation was John Burkett, a veteran who might still have something in the tank, and Frank Castillo and Blake Stein, who both needed to corral their control. The March preview figured them for 88-63 and a promotion push, though the OOTP projections were cooler, at 74-77.

Thirty-nine games in, the rotation has split in two. Burkett, 37, is 6-1 with a 2.79 ERA, and Castillo is 7-0. Carolina is 13-4 in their starts and 6-16 in everyone else's. Stein has a 6.87 ERA, Ben Rivera is 0-3 at 6.49, and Stan Spencer is 1-4. Add it up and you get 19-20, fifth in the East Group and five games behind Louisville.

The lineup came around

Three weeks in, Terrence Long, Ken Griffey Jr. and Mike Lieberthal had all been major disappointments. Griffey has since fixed his part. He has 11 homers, 40 RBI and a 144 OPS+, and on Monday he went 4-for-5 with two homers in a 12-0 win over Hoboken. He also hit a grand slam in the opener and drove in five with two homers against Richmond on April 23. Lieberthal has climbed to a 105 OPS+. Long, at 85, is still below average.

Chad Tracy, 21, is hitting .320 with a 127 OPS+, second on the club to Griffey. Pablo Sanchez, the Mexican rookie who was Carolina's wild card, went 0-for-5 with four strikeouts on April 3, then six days later hit a grand slam in a 5-0 win at Eugene. He's settled in at .241. Orlando Cabrera, half of the veteran star power up the middle, is hitting .235 without a homer. As a team, Carolina is ninth in the FBL in runs. That's good, not scary.

A bullpen nobody has to worry about

Brendan Donnelly was supposed to be a real late-inning weapon, and he has been: 10 saves in 10 chances, a 1.32 ERA and 19 strikeouts in 13.2 innings. Carolina hasn't blown a save all year. The leads have slipped away earlier in games. The Disco Turkeys have lost four games after leading by four, three of them during a five-game slide from April 18 to 22 that started with a sweep in Savannah and ended with two home losses to a Richmond team that had gone 1-7 that week. Josias Manzanillo has an 8.10 ERA.

May has been better

Carolina is 7-5 in May. It took two of three in San Antonio and two of three in Sacramento, the second win in 10 innings. On May 11 it rallied from five down to beat Hoboken 8-7 on a walk-off in the 10th, and then came Monday's 12-0 win, with Burkett throwing eight scoreless innings. The run differential is +6. The home record is 13-8, the road record 6-12.

So can the lineup outhit the rotation? Partly. It can outhit the top two. The back three are another matter. The site gives Carolina a 20.4% chance at the playoffs and a 4.2% chance of promotion, a long way from that March preview. Seven straight road games come next: three in Santo Domingo, three in Charlotte against the East's last-place club and one in Inland Empire before the 66ers visit for two. The front office went into the year looking for a stabilizing starter. Six weeks in, it could use three.
