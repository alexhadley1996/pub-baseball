The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Carolina Is 13-4 When Burkett or Castillo Starts, and 6-16 When They Don't

Alexander's question for the Disco Turkeys was whether the lineup could outhit the rotation. Two veteran starters are 13-1, Ken Griffey Jr. has 11 home runs, and Carolina is still a game under .500.

Carolina Disco Turkeys · 19-20 · 201 runs scored, 195 allowed

Carolina's March question, from Alexander's Teams to Watch preview, was "Can Carolina's lineup outhit its rotation?" He liked the lineup, "legitimately scary for this level," and worried about the starters: John Burkett as the "maybe there's still something in the tank" veteran, Frank Castillo and Blake Stein needing to "corral their control." He picked the Disco Turkeys for promotion and an 88-63 record.

Thirty-nine games in, the rotation has split in two. Burkett, 37, is 6-1 with a 2.79 ERA. Castillo is 7-0. Carolina is 13-4 in their starts. In everyone else's starts, the team is 6-16. Stein has a 6.87 ERA. Ben Rivera is 0-3 with a 6.49 ERA. Stan Spencer is 1-4. The result is a 19-20 record, fifth in the FBL's East Group and five games behind Louisville.

The lineup came around

At three weeks, Alexander named the lineup's problem: "Terrence Long, Ken Griffey Jr., and Mike Lieberthal have all been major disappointments." Griffey has since fixed his part. He's at .290/.365/.594 with 11 home runs, 40 RBI and a 144 OPS+. On Monday he went 4-for-5 with two home runs in a 12-0 win over Hoboken. He also hit a grand slam in the opener and drove in five with two homers against Richmond on April 23. Lieberthal now has a 105 OPS+. Long, at 85, is still below average.

Chad Tracy, 21, is hitting .320 with a 127 OPS+, second on the club to Griffey. Pablo Sanchez, the Mexican rookie Alexander called Carolina's "wild card," went 0-for-5 with four strikeouts on April 3, according to that day's roundup. Six days later he hit a grand slam in a 5-0 win at Eugene. He's settled in at .241 with a 73 OPS+. Orlando Cabrera, half of the "veteran star power up the middle," is at .235/.323/.309 with no home runs. As a team Carolina is ninth in the FBL in runs. That's good, not scary.

The bullpen nobody has to worry about

The March preview singled out Brendan Donnelly as "a real late-inning weapon," and he has been exactly that: 10 saves in 10 chances, a 1.32 ERA, 19 strikeouts in 13.2 innings. Carolina has not blown a save all season. The blown leads have come earlier in games. The Disco Turkeys have lost four games after leading by four runs, three of them during the five-game slide from April 18 to 22. That slide started with a three-game sweep in Savannah and ended with two home losses to Richmond, a team the week-three notes said had gone 1-7 that week. Josias Manzanillo, another of the March bullpen names, has an 8.10 ERA.

May has been better

Carolina is 7-5 in May. It won two of three in San Antonio and two of three in Sacramento, the second win coming in 10 innings. On May 11 it rallied from five runs down to beat Hoboken 8-7 on a walk-off in the 10th. Then came Monday's 12-0 win, with Burkett throwing eight scoreless innings. Carolina has a +6 run differential and a 13-8 home record. On the road it's 6-12.

So can the lineup outhit the rotation? Partly. It outhits the back three starters about as well as a .432 slugging percentage can. The site gives Carolina a 20.4% playoff chance and a 4.2% chance of promotion, well short of the March pick. Seven straight road games come next: three in Santo Domingo, three in Charlotte against the East's last-place club, and one in Inland Empire before the 66ers visit Carolina for two. In the preview, Alexander suggested the front office might need "a stabilizing starter." Six weeks in, they need three of them.
