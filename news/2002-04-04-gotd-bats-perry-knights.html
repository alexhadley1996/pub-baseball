FBL · 2002-04-04 · By Marty Kessler, Diamond Daily

Louisville Finally Wins One

Roger Clemens' Louisville team was 0-3 and trailed Curt Schilling's Charlotte 6-3 in the ninth. Herb Perry hit a three-run homer to tie it, then a sacrifice fly in the 11th to win it, 7-6, and the Bats have their first win.

LOUISVILLE, Ky. — Roger Clemens didn’t pitch on Thursday. Curt Schilling didn’t either. It was a game between the two teams with the most famous right-handers in the FBL, and the two most famous right-handers spent it in their respective dugouts, watching a game that their teammates made a great deal more dramatic than it needed to be.

Louisville, 0-3 and starting to hear about it from its own crowd, trailed Charlotte 6-3 going into the bottom of the ninth. Herb Perry hit a three-run homer to tie it. In the 11th, Perry hit a sacrifice fly to win it, and the Bats beat the Knights 7-6 for their first win of the season.

“Everybody was waiting on Roger to save us,” Perry said. “Roger’s Roger. He can’t play every day. Somebody else had to do something.”

Charlotte’s seventh

For six innings Louisville looked like it would win this the easy way. Wendell Magee hit a two-run homer in the second, Perry singled in another run, and Eric Junge held the Knights scoreless into the seventh.

Then Charlotte’s lineup, the one everyone said in March was “Grady Sizemore or bust,” scored six runs in one inning. Edgardo Alfonzo led off with a homer. Travis Fryman singled in a run. Eddie Perez, the catcher who spent his previous career catching Greg Maddux, singled in two. And Sizemore, the 19-year-old whom Schilling has spent the spring tutoring on bus rides, tripled in a run. Willie Harris added one more, and it was 6-3.

“Grady’s triple was the loudest our dugout’s been all year,” Charlotte manager Nate Jacobson said. “Curt was standing on the bench. I had to tell him to sit down.”

Perry

Justin Speier came on for Charlotte in the ninth. Two men got on, and Perry, a 32-year-old third baseman who came in 3-for-12 on the season, hit a three-run homer to left. 6-6.

Paul Quantrill threw two scoreless innings for Louisville and got the win. In the 11th, Charlotte’s Omar Olivares put runners on second and third with one out, and Perry hit a fly ball deep enough to win the game.

Perry finished 2-for-5 with five RBI. Magee had three hits.

The two aces

Clemens, asked about the game afterward, was brief.

“Good win,” he said. “We needed one. I’m pitching Saturday. Somebody tell Curt.”

Schilling, reached in the visitors’ clubhouse, was not brief. He had opinions about the strike zone in the ninth, about Speier’s pitch selection, about the infield dirt at the Louisville park, and about the fact that his team had lost a three-run lead in an inning he wasn’t allowed to pitch.

“I’ll see him soon enough,” Schilling said of Clemens. “That’s a game I’ll want.”

Louisville is 1-3. Charlotte is 2-2.
