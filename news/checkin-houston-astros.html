40-Game Check-In · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Loud Season of the Astros

Houston Astros, 21-19 through 40 games

There are subtle teams and there are the Astros, who have hit 60 home runs in 40 games and seem unbothered by subtlety.

40 games, by the numbers

  • 21-19 record
  • +21 run differential (209 scored, 188 allowed)
  • 7-5 in one-run games
  • 12-9 at home
  • 9-10 on the road
  • 4.62 / 3.48 starter / reliever ERA
  • 60 home runs
  • 22 stolen bases

Through 40 games they were 21-19, scoring 209 runs and allowing 188 (+21). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 22-19, 4th in the West Division, 4 games back, and 8th of 20 in MLB. That run differential would usually produce a record around 22-18. They are 7-5 in one-run games and 3-3 in extra innings.

Alex Rodriguez has been the Astros hitter opponents least want to see: .336/.416/.621, 10 homers, 32 driven in. Sammy Sosa leads the club with 11 home runs. As a team they are hitting .262/.337/.465, 1st of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 60 home runs.

In the rotation, Chris Carpenter has been the steadiest: 2-3 with a 3.60 ERA, 38 strikeouts and 11 walks in 55.0 innings. Dustin Hermanson (4.10 ERA in 48.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.25 ERA, 14th of 20 in MLB; starters 4.62, relievers 3.48. Closing has mostly meant Scott Elarton: 5 saves, 6.66 ERA.

Sammy Sosa has not looked like himself, or at least like his 2001 self: 1.174 OPS then, .881 now.

Six losses in a row is a long time. It is long enough for a team to try everything, and the Astros had from Apr 18 to Apr 23 to do so. They are 21-19 now.

Odds and ends: When they score three runs or fewer they are 2-12; when they score six or more, 12-3. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks. Jeff Cirillo has walked 17 times and struck out 10, a ratio that belongs to a different, more patient century.

The best night was May 11, a 20-3 win over the Athletics. The worst was Apr 18, a 14-7 loss to the Brewers. Their best 10-game stretch went 7-3 (Apr 7 to Apr 17); their worst, 3-7 (Apr 18 to Apr 28).

MLB has no wild card and no consolation prize: division winners play for the title, the last-place club in each division goes down to the ABL. The Astros are 4 games behind the Athletics in the West Division. The floor is 5 games below them, where the Dodgers currently stand on relegation pace.

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 7 of them at home, against the Cherry Blossoms, the Braves, the Seals, the Diamondbacks, opponents with a combined 194-216 record (.473). Neither a gift nor an ambush. There is no luck to give back and none to collect: 21 wins, about 22 expected. What changes the picture from here is health and Alex Rodriguez, not arithmetic.
