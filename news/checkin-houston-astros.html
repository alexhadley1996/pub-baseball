The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Astros Scored 20 in Oakland and Are Still Paying for April

Houston leads MLB in runs per game and OPS, has won six straight, and sits fourth in the West anyway, mostly because of one bad week against Milwaukee and Dallas.

Houston Astros · 22-19 · 220 runs scored, 191 allowed

On May 11 the Astros went into the Coliseum, home of one of the two best records in MLB, and won 20-3. Alex Rodriguez went 5-for-6 with a home run and seven RBI. It was the middle game of a sweep of Oakland and the fifth win of a six-game streak that is still going after Monday's 11-3 win over Washington. The streak began with two wins over the Yankees. Against those two clubs, both among the strongest in MLB, the Astros have gone 5-1 in the last week.

They are 22-19, fourth in the West, four games behind Oakland and San Francisco. Their +29 run differential is better than their record. The catch-up job exists because of one week.

April 18-23

Houston was 11-6 on April 17 after six straight wins, the last four at home against Boston and Milwaukee. Then the Brewers won 14-7 and won again in 13 innings, a game in which Dustin Hermanson had thrown eight innings of one-hit ball and the bullpen gave back a four-run lead. The Astros went to Dallas and were swept by the Griffins, "their hated intra-state rival," as Alexander put it, scoring five runs in three games. Brooklyn made it six losses in a row. They climbed back to 15-13. Then they lost seven of eight, including a home sweep by Miami, and were 16-19 on May 6.

The lineup

Alexander called this "the most intimidating offense in the league, even more than the Cubs" in March and gave it an A. Houston leads MLB in runs per game and in OPS and is second in home runs with 63. Rodriguez is at .324/.404/.600 with 10 homers and a 168 OPS+. Sammy Sosa has 12 home runs, Jeff Kent 10 and Ron Gant, at 37, nine in 161 plate appearances. Marvin Benard has a 138 OPS+ in center. The weak spot is left field, where Juan Encarnacion is at .253/.304/.348.

Alexander's notes point out the oddest thing about Sosa's season. "His OPS plunges from over 1.000 in low leverage situations, to 800 in medium leverage, and 600 in high leverage!" he wrote, and he doesn't expect it to last. He also made a case for Jeff Cirillo, whose 91 OPS+ undersells a third baseman Alexander says "has been superb on defense," so much so that he "should end the year as one of the more valuable 3rd baseman in the MLB." The early highlights include Tony Clark's 12th-inning walk-off single against the Cubs on April 9 and the eight late runs that turned a 6-4 deficit in Colorado into a 12-7 win two days later.

The pitching

The March grade for the staff was a C+. The Astros have allowed 191 runs, 16th of 20, and the rotation has a 4.61 ERA. Alexander's take: "high-contact, low-walk. ... for now that seems to be working out ok, but in the long run, it seems dicey." Chris Carpenter (3.67 ERA, 11 walks in 61.1 innings) "looks legit." Jon Lieber, the other name at the top in March, has a 5.72 ERA. His 4.21 FIP suggests some of that is bad luck, but he's also allowed 10 home runs. Brian Powell, the fifth starter, has a 5.04 ERA and in Alexander's word has been "dreadful." He also threw the club's best start of the year, eight shutout innings in a 1-0 win over Toronto on April 4.

The bullpen has a 3.40 ERA. Scott Eyre has allowed one earned run in 13.2 innings. Alexander gave him a "shoutout" while noting that he's struck out only ten.

Now or never

The line from Alexander's notes that sticks is the one about the calendar: "It's now or never too for this Houston side as they are one of the oldest teams in the PBA and have essentially no prospects to speak of." His preseason table had them at 77-74. In MLB only the division winners reach the World Series, so finishing fourth gets them nothing. The site gives them a 5.8% chance at the West, while Alexander is a "believer they can stitch it together." The next two series are at home against Washington and Atlanta. After that comes a three-game trip to San Francisco, one of the two teams they need to catch.
