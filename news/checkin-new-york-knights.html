The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Lance Berkman Is Hitting .405 for the Worst Team in the ABL

The projections had New York in a four-way tie atop the East. Instead the Knights are last, 3-10 in one-run games and allowing more runs than anyone. Berkman has the best OPS+ of any ABL regular.

New York Knights · 15-25 · 222 runs scored, 252 allowed

The preseason projections saw the ABL East as a four-way tie at 80-71, and New York was one of the four. Forty games in, the Knights are 15-25, the worst record in the ABL and eight games behind Cleveland and Montreal. The site gives them a 32.5% chance of relegation.

None of that is Lance Berkman's fault. He came in as part of a formidable trio at the top of the order, with Eric Chavez and Carlos Guillen, that looked a little light on power. Berkman is hitting .405 with 10 home runs and a 192 OPS+, the best of any regular in the ABL. He is not light on power.

Berkman's May

He's been even better lately. From May 10 to 12 he hit five home runs and drove in 12 in three games: two homers against Portland, a 4-for-5 night in Cleveland on Saturday and two more homers there on Sunday, in a 9-8 walk-off loss. Guillen has also been as advertised. He's hitting .342, and on May 5 in Cincinnati he went 6-for-6 with a triple and a homer. Chavez, the third member of the trio, has a 96 OPS+.

Of the two exciting prospects, Ty Wigginton has come through, with a .408 on-base percentage, eight homers and a 134 OPS+. Jhonny Peralta, 19, hasn't. By April 21 the offense had collapsed outside Berkman and Guillen, especially Ramon E. Martinez and Peralta. Martinez has recovered to a 95 OPS+. Peralta is hitting .197 with 32 strikeouts. The catchers, Kelly Stinnett and Danny Ardoin, have OPS+ marks of 36 and 20.

Hudson and the rest

Tim Hudson was always the rotation's one real talent, and he's been the one bright spot. He has a 3.79 ERA and 2.2 WAR and has allowed one home run in 61.2 innings. On May 2 he threw a two-hit shutout of Pittsburgh with 10 strikeouts. His record is 1-2, and New York is 4-5 in his starts. Behind him, the rotation is just very weak. Andy Benes is 1-5 with a 9.31 ERA. Victor Alvarez has been both versions of himself, giving up six runs in two innings on April 3 and throwing eight shutout innings in Tampa Bay on April 25. New York allows more runs per game than anyone in the ABL.

The bullpen hasn't bailed them out. Scott Stewart, excellent coming in, has a 7.30 ERA. J.C. Romero has pitched better than his results, with a 3.68 FIP, but has three blown saves. Joe Nathan has a 3.71 ERA.

One-run games

The Knights came in as one of the league's most volatile clubs, and mostly that has meant close losses. They're 3-10 in one-run games. The worst came on April 12 in New Orleans, when they led by seven, gave up seven in the seventh and lost 12-11 in 10. They struggled to score after that and lost eight straight from April 9 to 17, part of a 1-9 stretch.

The past two weeks have been better. New York went 7-3 from May 1 to 11, including a 16-8 win in Cincinnati on May 4, before losing the last two in Cleveland. Next come Mexico City at home, three in Nashville and Salt Lake at home. With Berkman this hot, the Knights don't have a better time to make up ground.
