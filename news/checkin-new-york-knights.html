40-Game Check-In · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

Knights at 40: Coin Flips, Counted

New York Knights, 15-25 through 40 games

One run. That is the margin that has defined the New York season: thirteen one-run games, a 3-10 record in them, and a 15-25 record overall that would look very different with a couple of those flipped.

The ABL is the middle rung, so it is the only league here where you can fall and climb in the same breath: each conference winner gets a bye, second and third play in, both finalists of the American Championship are promoted to MLB, and the bottom three are relegated to the FBL. They sit 10th in the Eastern Conference, 8 games back of the Spiders. The less fun line: the site has them on relegation pace, 1 game behind the Stars, the nearest club above the drop.

Through 40 games they were 15-25, scoring 222 runs and allowing 252 (-30). As of the May 13 export they are 10th in the Eastern Conference, 8 games back, and 20th of 20 in the ABL. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 18 wins in 40 games. They are 3-10 in one-run games and 2-2 in extra innings.

The hitters

Their most productive hitter is Lance Berkman (LF, 26), at .405/.485/.709 with 10 HR. Gene Kingsale has 12 steals (caught 3 times). As a team they are hitting .295/.357/.444, 15th of 20 in the ABL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 40 home runs. The surprise is Carlos Guillen. His 2001, by the export's count, was a .689 OPS over 523 plate appearances; this year it is .935. Players do not usually get 247 points better by accident, though they do sometimes get there by April. The weakest regular bat has been Jhonny Peralta, at .197/.259/.295 in 143 plate appearances.

The pitchers

Tim Hudson leads the starters with a 3.79 ERA over 61.2 innings (44 K, 23 BB). Chris Holt (5.60 ERA in 45.0 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 5.77 ERA, 17th of 20 in the ABL; starters 5.94, relievers 5.52. Scott Stewart has 4 saves and 1 blown. In the rotation, Andy Benes has a 9.31 ERA over 38.2 innings, after a 7.38 ERA in 2001.

The best and worst of it

The best night was Apr 18, an 11-2 win over the Coquis. The worst was Apr 29, an 18-3 loss to the Loons. Their best 10-game stretch went 7-3 (May 1 to May 11); their worst, 1-9 (Apr 7 to Apr 17).

Odds and ends: Home crowds have averaged 25,069 over 18 dates in a park that holds 55,000, about 46% full. There is room to be convinced. When they score three runs or fewer they are 1-11; when they score six or more, 10-5. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

What next

The next stretch is 10 games (6 at home) against the Golden Eagles, the Stars, the Bees, the Orioles. Those clubs are 176-224 combined, a .440 winning percentage, so this is where a record can get fatter. The hopeful read is the run differential: if runs keep coming at this rate, the wins tend to follow, and the Knights are about 3 wins short of where that differential usually lands.
