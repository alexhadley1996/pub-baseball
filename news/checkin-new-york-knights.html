The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Lance Berkman Is Hitting .405 for the Worst Team in the ABL

New York was supposed to be in a four-way tie atop the East. Instead the Knights are last, 3-10 in one-run games, and allowing more runs than anyone. Berkman has the best OPS+ of any ABL regular.

New York Knights · 15-25 · 222 runs scored, 252 allowed

Alexander's predicted standings had the ABL East as "a four-way tie at 80-71," and New York was one of the four. Forty games in, the Knights are 15-25, the worst record in the ABL, eight games behind Cleveland and Montreal. The site gives them a 32.5% chance of relegation.

He was right about the best hitter in the lineup. He called Lance Berkman, Eric Chavez and Carlos Guillen "a trio at the front of this lineup that is formidable but a little lacking in power." Berkman is hitting .405/.485/.709 with 10 home runs and a 192 OPS+. That's the best OPS+ of any regular in the ABL, and it isn't lacking power.

Berkman's May

He has been even better lately. In three games from May 10 to 12, he hit five home runs and drove in 12: two homers against Portland, a 4-for-5 game in Cleveland on Saturday, and two more homers in Cleveland on Sunday, which New York lost 9-8 on a walk-off. Guillen has also been as advertised. He's hitting .342/.385/.550, and on May 5 in Cincinnati he went 6-for-6 with a triple and a home run. Chavez, the third member of the trio, has a 96 OPS+.

Alexander also named two "exciting prospects." Ty Wigginton has been a success, with a .408 on-base percentage, eight homers and a 134 OPS+. Jhonny Peralta, 19, has not. The April 21 trends piece said the offense had "collapsed" outside Berkman and Guillen, "especially Ramon E. Martinez and 19-year-old Jhonny Peralta." Martinez has recovered to a 95 OPS+. Peralta is hitting .197/.259/.295 with 32 strikeouts. The Knights' catchers, Kelly Stinnett and Danny Ardoin, have OPS+ marks of 36 and 20.

The rotation

"Tim Hudson is all they got talent-wise," Alexander wrote, "but the rotation is just very weak." Both parts were right. Hudson has a 3.79 ERA, a 3.63 FIP and 2.2 WAR. He has allowed one home run in 61.2 innings. On May 2 he threw a two-hit shutout of Pittsburgh with 10 strikeouts. His record is 1-2, and New York is 4-5 in his starts. Andy Benes is 1-5 with a 9.31 ERA. Victor Alvarez allowed six runs in two innings on April 3 and threw eight shutout innings in Tampa Bay on April 25. New York allows more runs per game than any team in the ABL.

The bullpen hasn't been the help Alexander hoped. He called Scott Stewart "excellent," and Stewart has a 7.30 ERA. J.C. Romero has a 3.68 FIP but three blown saves. Joe Nathan, the other "exciting talent," has a 3.71 ERA.

One-run games

The personalities report listed the Knights among the "most volatile" teams. So far that has mostly meant close losses. They are 3-10 in one-run games. The worst of them came on April 12 in New Orleans. The Knights led by seven, gave up a seven-run seventh, and lost 12-11 in 10 innings. The trends piece said they "struggled to score since the chaos" of that game. They lost eight straight from April 9 to 17 and went 1-9 over one 10-game stretch.

The past two weeks have been better. New York went 7-3 from May 1 to 11, including a 16-8 win in Cincinnati on May 4. Then it lost the last two in Cleveland. Next come three at home against Mexico City, three in Nashville and three at home against Salt Lake.
