The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Barry Bonds Has 20 Home Runs, and the Yankees Are Still Third in Line

Bonds has hit 10 homers in 50 May plate appearances and carries a 230 OPS+. The preseason No. 1 Yankees are 22-19, three games out, and their next nine games run straight through the clubs ahead of them.

New York Yankees · 22-19 · 201 runs scored, 184 allowed

Barry Bonds has come to the plate 50 times in May and hit 10 home runs. That's the kind of month that ends arguments, and for the season he's at .306/.466/.776 with 20 homers, 37 walks and 3.2 WAR, as much as Greg Maddux and Chuck Smith have produced together. Twenty home runs in his team's first 41 games, with summer in Yankee Stadium still ahead of him, is a pace nobody needs to project out loud.

And yet the Yankees are 22-19, fourth in the East and three games behind Miami. Bonds has been worth the price of admission. The question is what happens in the other eight spots.

Bonds and vibes

The preseason consensus put the Yankees first in MLB, and the scouting line on the offense was "Bonds and vibes": one superstar, good defenders around him, and the risk that opponents would simply pitch around Bonds and dare everyone else. They have walked him 37 times. Everyone else has mostly held up. Lenny Harris, 37, is hitting .319, and Torii Hunter has an .851 OPS, both helped along by friendly batting averages on balls in play.

The holes are in right field, at second base and at DH. Fernando Seguignol has a .262 on-base percentage in right and is at -0.8 WAR. At second, Joe Torre has stayed with Mark Grudzielanek, which leaves Carlos Baerga, hitting .328 with a .531 slugging percentage, mostly on the bench. Adam Kennedy has gotten the DH at-bats instead, 196 trips to the plate, more than anyone on the team, without a home run. The fix looks obvious from the outside. Torre seems to want to go with the young man.

The rotation

The pitching came into the year with an A and a reputation as the best rotation in the league, and Maddux has done his part. He's 6-1 with a 2.48 ERA and two complete games, one of them a shutout of Washington on April 5, and he beat Toronto 1-0 with eight scoreless innings. Smith has been nearly as good, with 51 strikeouts and a 2.92 FIP. Kris Benson's 1-5 record is mostly bad luck; his FIP is 3.56, and he finally got a win with a complete game in Dallas on Monday.

The trouble is the back end. Ariel Prieto has a 7.22 ERA in the fifth spot, and Jack Cressend's 14.62 ERA in eight innings has dragged down a bullpen that is otherwise solid. Help should be on the way for both.

Long nights

It has been a season of long nights. The Yankees are 3-4 in extra innings. In Atlanta in April they lost a 15-inning game 3-2 and won a 14-inning game two days later. In Phoenix on April 15 they blew an eight-run lead and lost 9-8 in 12. A 3-7 stretch from April 7 to 17, including a three-game sweep by the Seals at the Stadium, left them at 14-15 at the end of a thoroughly mediocre April.

May has been different, and it has had a sense of humor. On April 29 in Philadelphia they trailed by seven and won 14-12, with Bonds homering twice and walking three times. Two nights later they lost 15-1 at Wrigley. The next day they fell behind by five, and Bonds hit two more home runs and drove in five in an 8-7 win in 10 innings. They're 8-4 this month.

Nine games

The preseason projections had the Yankees at 82-69, third in the East, and the site's simulations, working from a 41-game record and a modest +17 run differential, give them only an 11.4% chance at the division. In MLB that's the only thing that counts: division winners go to the best-of-nine World Series, and last place goes down to the ABL.

The case for the Yankees is simple. Bonds is rolling, the rotation is clicking, and Detroit and Miami have both shown cracks. The schedule will settle it quickly. After two games in Dallas come three at home against Brooklyn, three in Detroit and one with Miami, the three clubs ahead of them, in order.
