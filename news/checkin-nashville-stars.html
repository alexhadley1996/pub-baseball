40-Game Check-In · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Stars Are 16-24. Their Runs Disagree.

Nashville Stars, 16-24 through 40 games

Start with the gap, because the gap is the story. The Stars are 16-24. Their run differential (-3) is the run differential of a team that is about 20-20. They have been, in the most literal sense, worse than they have played.

The ABL is the middle rung, so it is the only league here where you can fall and climb in the same breath: each conference winner gets a bye, second and third play in, both finalists of the American Championship are promoted to MLB, and the bottom three are relegated to the FBL. They sit 9th in the Eastern Conference, 7 games back of the Spiders. The drop is 2 games below them, where the Violets is the best of the clubs on relegation pace.

Through 40 games they were 16-24, scoring 223 runs and allowing 226 (-3). As of the May 13 export they are 9th in the Eastern Conference, 7 games back, and 19th of 20 in the ABL. By the old Pythagorean arithmetic, that is a 20-win team so far. They are 4-8 in one-run games and 1-2 in extra innings.

The hitters

The best bat has belonged to Bobby Kielty, 25, who is hitting .314/.404/.541 with 8 home runs and 29 RBI in 183 plate appearances. Carlos Pena leads the club with 10 home runs. As a team they are hitting .294/.361/.477, 5th of 20 in the ABL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 49 home runs. Nobody should have expected Milton Bradley to be hitting .297/.339/.458. He posted a .620 OPS in 262 plate appearances in 2001. Forty games is enough to notice and not enough to be sure.

The pitchers

Miguel Batista leads the starters with a 3.00 ERA over 51.0 innings (41 K, 24 BB). Russ Ortiz (6.23 ERA in 43.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 5.44 ERA, 14th of 20 in the ABL; starters 5.89, relievers 4.63. Closing has mostly meant Scott Williamson: 6 saves, 4.61 ERA. In the rotation, Tim Redding has a 7.14 ERA over 40.1 innings, after a 5.50 ERA in 2001.

One run. That is the margin that has defined the Nashville season: twelve one-run games, a 4-8 record in them, and a 16-24 record overall that would look very different with a couple of those flipped.

The best and worst of it

The best night was Apr 26, a 17-7 win over the Royeaux. The worst was Apr 15, a 16-4 loss to the Orioles. Their best 10-game stretch went 8-2 (Apr 9 to Apr 19); their worst, 2-8 (Apr 1 to Apr 10).

Odds and ends: They once scored 17 runs in a game (Apr 26, against the Royeaux); that one game is 8% of their season's runs.

What next

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 4 of them at home, against the Gamblers, the Knights, the Loons, the Pirates, opponents with a combined 188-212 record (.470). That is a chance to bank wins. The hopeful read is the run differential: if runs keep coming at this rate, the wins tend to follow, and the Stars are about 4 wins short of where that differential usually lands.
