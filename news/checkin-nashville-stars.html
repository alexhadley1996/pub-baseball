The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Nashville's Run Differential Says 20-20. Its Record Is 16-24

The Stars have a top-five OPS, an elite closer who has blown three saves and the biggest gap in the ABL between their record and their run differential.

Nashville Stars · 16-24 · 223 runs scored, 226 allowed

Nashville has scored 223 runs and allowed 226. A team with that run differential usually wins about 20 of its first 40 games. The Stars have won 16. That gap of nearly four wins is the biggest in the ABL, and it's why a team with the league's fifth-best OPS is ninth of 10 in the Eastern Conference, seven games out.

The preseason consensus had them ninth, and the projections had them at 69-82. The offense looked young and exciting, particularly in the outfield, and the bullpen had an elite closer in Scott Williamson. Both of those have mostly held. The problem has been the margins.

Close games

Nashville is 4-8 in one-run games and 1-2 in extras, and the bullpen has eight saves and eight blown saves. Williamson has six saves, three blown and a 4.61 ERA, though his FIP is a more reasonable 3.46. Rheal Cormier has a 3.00 ERA and four blown saves. Twice the Stars have blown five-run leads: on April 21 in Austin, where they lost 8-6, and on April 30 in Anaheim, where they lost 7-6 in 10. They also gave back four-run leads in a 13-12 loss to San Diego on April 20 and a 10-inning loss to Southside on May 10. Hold even a couple of those and Nashville is in the race.

The rotation

The starters came in sorted by confidence: Russ Ortiz the promising one, Miguel Batista less so and Matt Clement even less so. The season has turned that list upside down. Batista, one of the steadier arms in the league, has the rotation's best ERA at 3.00. Ortiz has a 6.23 ERA and has walked 35 in 43.1 innings, and on April 28 in Anaheim he got five outs and gave up eight runs. Clement is 1-5 with a 6.71 ERA, but he has 55 strikeouts and a 4.62 FIP. He struck out 10 Salt Lake hitters on April 12, and on May 2 he struck out 10 more in 6.1 scoreless innings against Kansas City and lost 1-0.

The back of the rotation has been rough. Its ERA is 5.89, and Tim Redding's WHIP is close to 3.00. He gave up nine runs in 2.1 innings in the San Diego loss.

The outfield

The hitters have done their part. Bobby Kielty leads the team with a 132 OPS+. Carlos Pena has 10 home runs and 49 strikeouts. Michael Tucker has a .404 on-base percentage and 34 runs scored, and he was the hero of Nashville's first win, a 9-6 comeback in Mexico City on April 7 in which he went 5-for-5. Milton Bradley has piled up big days: four hits against Tampa Bay on April 9, six RBI against Salt Lake on April 13 and five against Montreal on April 26. Bobby Abreu, the already-developed one, has a 102 OPS+. Catcher has been the hole it looked like in March. Jorge Fabregas has a .274 on-base percentage.

The record

The Stars lost their first six games, then won nine of 12, one of the league's great weeks. Since April 20 they're 7-15. In the past week they lost two of three to Southside and two of three to San Juan. Next come three in Las Vegas, three at home against New York and three in Minnesota.
