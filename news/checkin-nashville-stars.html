The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Nashville's Run Differential Says 20-20. Its Record Is 16-24

The Stars have a top-five OPS, a closer Alexander called elite who has blown three saves, and the biggest gap in the ABL between their record and their run differential.

Nashville Stars · 16-24 · 223 runs scored, 226 allowed

Nashville has scored 223 runs and allowed 226. A team with that run differential usually wins about 20 of its first 40 games. The Stars have won 16. That's a gap of almost four wins, the biggest in the ABL, and it's why a team with the fifth-best OPS in the league is ninth of 10 in the Eastern Conference, seven games out.

Alexander ranked the Stars ninth and predicted 69-82. He called the offense "young" and "exciting," "particularly in the outfield," and said the bullpen had "an elite Scott Williamson."

Close games

Nashville is 4-8 in one-run games and 1-2 in extra innings. The bullpen has 8 saves and 8 blown saves. Williamson has six saves, three blown saves and a 4.61 ERA, though his FIP is 3.46. Rheal Cormier, the other reliever Alexander named, has a 3.00 ERA and four blown saves. The Stars have blown five-run leads twice. On April 21 they led by five in Austin and lost 8-6. On April 30 they led by five in Anaheim and lost 7-6 in 10 innings. They blew four-run leads in a 13-12 loss to San Diego on April 20 and a 10-inning loss to Southside on May 10.

The rotation

Alexander's take on the starters was ranked by confidence. "Russ Ortiz is a promising young pitcher with Miguel Batista less so, and Matt Clement even less so." So far it has been the reverse. Batista has the best ERA in the rotation at 3.00, and the trends piece called him one of "the steadier arms." Ortiz has a 6.23 ERA and a 6.54 FIP and has walked 35 batters in 43.1 innings. On April 28 in Anaheim he recorded five outs and allowed eight runs. Clement is 1-5 with a 6.71 ERA, but he has 55 strikeouts and a 4.62 FIP. He struck out 10 Salt Lake hitters on April 12, a start the roundup noted. On May 2 he struck out 10 more in 6.1 scoreless innings against Kansas City and lost 1-0. The rotation's ERA is 5.89. The trends piece said Tim Redding "still has a WHIP close to 3.00." Redding allowed nine runs in 2.1 innings in the San Diego loss.

The outfield

The hitters have done their part. Bobby Kielty, whom Alexander didn't mention, leads the team with a 132 OPS+. Carlos Pena, one of the young players he named, has 10 home runs and 49 strikeouts. Michael Tucker is hitting .308/.404/.500 and has scored 34 runs. The April 7 roundup called him "the hero" of Nashville's first win, a 9-6 comeback in Mexico City in which he went 5-for-5. Milton Bradley had four hits against Tampa Bay on April 9, according to the roundup. He drove in six against Salt Lake on April 13 and five against Montreal on April 26. Bobby Abreu, the "already developed" one, has a 102 OPS+. As Alexander expected, catcher has been the hole. Jorge Fabregas is at .268/.274/.375.

The record

The Stars lost their first six games. Then they won nine of 12, and the trends piece listed them among the "great weeks." Since April 20 they're 7-15. In the past week they lost two of three to Southside, then two of three to San Juan. Next come three in Las Vegas, three at home against New York and three in Minnesota.
