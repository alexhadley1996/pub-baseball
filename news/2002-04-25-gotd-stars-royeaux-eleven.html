ABL · 2002-04-25 · By Hal Brennan, Diamond Daily

Laney's Bullpen, Robinson's Closer

Nashville's bullpen threw six scoreless innings, Bobby Kielty tied it off Montreal closer Billy Koch with two outs in the ninth, and Aaron Guiel won it in the 11th. Nashville's four-game losing streak and Montreal's five-game winning streak both ended at once.

NASHVILLE, Tenn. — There are two kinds of managers in the ABL this spring, and on Thursday night they were standing in opposite dugouts.

Frank Robinson, who has been managing professional teams since the 1970s, runs the Montreal Royeaux the way he has run every team: the starter goes as long as he can, the closer gets the ninth, and nobody needs a spreadsheet to tell him what a baseball game is. Matt Laney, in his first season with Nashville, carries a binder full of matchup numbers into the dugout and uses his bullpen in ways that make old baseball men squint.

Thursday, the binder won. Laney’s relievers threw six scoreless innings, Bobby Kielty tied the game with two outs in the ninth off Robinson’s closer, and Aaron Guiel singled home the winner in the 11th. Nashville won 4-3.

“Frank’s forgotten more baseball than I’ll ever know,” Laney said. “Tonight I just had a little more bullpen than he did.”

Two streaks

The Royeaux came in winning, five straight and 12-10, behind a lineup led by Rafael Palmeiro, who at 37 was hitting .400. Palmeiro’s two-run double in the second gave Montreal a 3-1 lead, and Chan-ho Park took it from there, striking out nine over 6⅔ innings.

The Stars came in losing, four straight and 9-13, with a pitching staff that had allowed nearly six runs a game. Their one reliable starter, Miguel Batista, wasn’t sharp. After the second inning, though, Batista didn’t allow anything else, and when he left after five, Laney’s bullpen took the game over. Scott Williamson, Rheal Cormier and Lance Carter combined to allow three hits and no runs the rest of the way.

“That’s the plan when it works,” Laney said. “Starters get you to the middle. Then you have guys for every situation. Sometimes it looks like I’m overmanaging. Tonight it looked like a plan.”

Kielty

Aaron Guiel singled in a run in the sixth, and the Stars went to the ninth down 3-2 against Billy Koch, who had a 1.12 ERA and a reputation for not giving up much of anything. He got two outs. Then Kielty, who had homered in the first, doubled to tie the game.

Kielty is 25 and was hitting .354 coming in, one of a handful of Nashville hitters who have kept a bad team in games all month. He finished 2-for-4 with two walks, the homer and two RBI.

“I’ve been seeing it well,” he said. “It doesn’t always mean anything. Tonight it meant something.”

The 11th

Will Cunnane loaded the bases for Montreal in the bottom of the 11th, and with two outs Guiel, the third baseman, singled up the middle. Carter, who got four outs, got the win.

Robinson was gracious afterward, in his way.

“They played better,” he said. “That’s all it is. You don’t win every night. Five in a row is a good week. Six would’ve been better.”

Montreal is 12-11. Nashville is 10-13, still well under .500 and still, as Laney put it, “a work in progress, but tonight progress worked.”
