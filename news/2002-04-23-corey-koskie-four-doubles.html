MLB · 2002-04-23 · By Rob Lindqvist, Diamond Daily

Corey Koskie's Two-Base Night

Corey Koskie doubled in the first, the third, the fifth and the seventh, and homered in the sixth. Five-for-five, four doubles, the only four-double game in MLB this season, and an Oakland rout of the slumping Dodgers, 13-2.

OAKLAND, Calif. — Corey Koskie grew up in Manitoba, where the ballfields freeze over for half the year and, he says, every kid he knew wanted to be a hockey player. He was one too, a pretty good one. He switched to baseball as a teenager, mostly because he liked hitting things more than he liked getting hit.

On Tuesday night at the Coliseum, he hit everything. Koskie went 5-for-5 with four doubles and a home run, scored four runs, and Oakland beat the Los Angeles Dodgers 13-2.

“I don’t know where they all went,” Koskie said. “I know they went somewhere.”

Four doubles

He led off the first inning with a double to left-center off Calvin Maduro. He led off the third with a double down the right-field line. He led off the fifth with a double into the left-field corner. In the sixth, against Mario Ramos, he hit a solo homer to right, which broke the pattern, and in the seventh, against Michael Wuertz, he went back to doubling, this time into the gap in right-center.

Nobody else in MLB has hit four doubles in a game this season.

“That’s a weird stat,” Koskie said, when it was explained to him. “I’d have guessed it happened every week. It’s just a ball in the gap.”

His swing, A’s hitting coach Mark Rose said, has been short and quick all month. “He’s not trying to hit home runs,” Rose said. “He’s trying to hit line drives. When you hit line drives in this park, they go to the gaps, because the gaps are huge. It’s not complicated. He’s just doing it better than anyone.”

The rest

Koskie wasn’t alone. Ramon Castro, the A’s catcher, homered twice, both two-run shots with two outs, and drove in four. Keith Lockhart hit a two-run homer. Roosevelt Brown doubled twice, the second driving in two runs in the eighth.

Evan Thomas, Oakland’s 27-year-old starter, struck out five and allowed two runs in eight innings. He’d come into the night with a 1.21 ERA and left it with a 1.48.

The Dodgers

The Dodgers have gone 2-8 in their last 10, a fall that has been as fast as their early rise. A week and a half ago they were 9-3 and had won five straight. They’re now 11-12.

“We’re not hitting,” Jim Tracy said. “There’s no other way to say it. We’re not hitting, and when we’re not hitting we’re not doing anything else right either. I’m not going to make excuses.”

The swing

Koskie came into the night hitting .372 and left it at .410. The A’s are 13-10, and Koskie is a large part of the reason.

Afterward, he was asked whether he’d ever had a game like this before, in hockey or baseball.

“I had a hat trick once,” he said. “Bantam hockey, in Anola. I was 13. This is better. My mom was at that one. I should call her.”
