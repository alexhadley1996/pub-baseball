ABL · 2002-04-10 · By Rob Lindqvist, Diamond Daily

Five Ties, Three Lead Changes and Brian Simmons

Minnesota and Anaheim were tied five different times on Wednesday night. Rickey Henderson, 43, had three hits, Freddy Sanchez homered in the 11th for Anaheim, Chipper Jones tied it again in the bottom half, and Brian Simmons won it 11-10 in the 14th.

MINNEAPOLIS — Rickey Henderson is 43 years old. He has played in more major league games than almost anyone alive, in a lot of uniforms, in a lot of leagues, in a lot of very long nights. On Wednesday, around the 13th inning, he walked down the dugout steps, sat next to the bat rack and announced to anyone in range that this was the longest regular-season game Rickey had ever played in.

It wasn’t. It just felt that way. The Loons beat the Angels 11-10 in 14 innings, on Brian Simmons’ single, after the teams had been tied 3-3, 5-5, 8-8, 9-9 and 10-10.

“Every time we thought we had it, they’d come back,” Loons manager Ron Gardenhire said. “Every time they thought they had it, we’d come back. At some point it stops being baseball and starts being a staring contest.”

The early part

Nothing about the first inning suggested a short night. The Angels scored three in the top half, with Mike Sweeney doubling in the first run, and the Loons answered with three in the bottom, the last two on David Ortiz’s homer. Anaheim pulled ahead again, 5-3, and Minnesota tied it in the fifth when Seth Greisinger hit David DeJesus with the bases loaded and Juan Rivera singled in another run.

The Angels went up 7-5 in the sixth on singles by Sweeney and Austin Kearns. Then the Loons hit three solo-sized blows in two innings: Simmons homered leading off the sixth, DeJesus homered in the seventh, and Henderson singled in the go-ahead run with two out. Minnesota led 8-7.

The middle part

Anaheim tied it on Jose Valentin’s sacrifice fly in the eighth. Minnesota went ahead on Rivera’s two-out single in the bottom half. Aaron Fultz came on for the ninth to close it, and Scott Podsednik’s ground ball made it 9-9.

“I’ve been doing this a long time,” Henderson said afterward, which is how many of his sentences start. “When you get to the ninth inning up one, you’re supposed to win. Tonight, nobody was supposed to win.”

Sanchez

Freddy Sanchez, a 24-year-old the Angels called up this week, came into the night 0-for-2 on the season. He left it with four hits: two doubles, a single and, with two outs in the 11th, a home run off Fultz that put Anaheim ahead 10-9.

“I’ll remember that one,” Sanchez said. “I’d like to remember it as a winner.”

He didn’t get to. LaTroy Hawkins, Anaheim’s best reliever, came on in the 11th, and Henderson and Willie Bloomquist both singled. Chipper Jones, who came into the night hitting .207 and has spent his first two weeks in Minnesota hearing about it, singled to tie the game again.

Jones finished with four hits.

“First good night I’ve had here,” he said. “I’d have liked it to be about four hours shorter.”

Simmons

Hawkins threw three innings. Mike Koplove threw two scoreless for Minnesota, and Scott Mullen pitched a scoreless 14th. In the bottom half, Keith Ginter singled off Luis Ayala and Juan Rivera doubled. After a fly ball, Simmons singled to bring the winning run home.

Simmons, a 28-year-old left fielder who started the season on the bench, finished with four hits, including the homer. He was asked if this was the best game of his career.

“Ask me tomorrow when I wake up,” he said. “If I wake up.”

Henderson, three hits and a walk to his name, was the last one out of the clubhouse. He said he would be back early Thursday. “Rickey doesn’t get tired,” he said, and then yawned.

The Loons are 6-4. The Angels are 4-6.
