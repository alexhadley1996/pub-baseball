MLB · 2002-03-30 · By Grady Briggs, The Cove Dispatch

2002 San Francisco Seals Season Preview: Everything Is Different and Nothing Is Fine, Except Maybe the Pitching

Bonds is a Yankee, the Giants are the Seals, and Dusty Baker is using an opener. A fan's-eye preview of a team with a terrifying young rotation and a lineup that can't find first base.

Before we begin, a housekeeping note. Several of you have emailed to ask whether this website will be changing its name now that the team it covers isn’t called the Giants anymore. Some of you suggested “The Seal Rock Dispatch.” One of you suggested “The O’Doul Dispatch,” which is honestly pretty good. One of you suggested something I can’t print.

The answer is no. This website is called The Cove Dispatch, after McCovey Cove. Willie McCovey didn’t change his name when they moved the fences, and I’m not changing ours because a group of guys from Palo Alto decided “Giants” didn’t test well with focus groups. We will, however, be covering the San Francisco Seals, because they’re what we have now, and you love what you have.

I think. Let’s find out.

What happened

If you’ve been living in a cave, or in Los Angeles, which is the same thing, here’s a quick recap of the last two years.

  • The players went on strike before the 2000 season.
  • The Dodgers and the Giants broke the line, founded something called the California league, and played baseball all winter. In the winter. In California. We had baseball games in January and nobody thought this was strange, because nothing was strange anymore.
  • A group of Silicon Valley tech executives bought the Giants, said the word “rebrand” in a meeting, and nobody stopped them.
  • The Giants became the Seals, after the old Pacific Coast League team. That team had Joe DiMaggio, who once hit in 61 straight games for them, and Lefty O’Doul, who managed them for 17 years and is the reason there’s a bar downtown where you can get a Bloody Mary at 10 a.m. So the bar is low, and also the bar is a real bar.
  • Barry Bonds plays for the Yankees.

Let me type that last one again to see if it hurts less.

Barry Bonds plays for the Yankees.

No. It doesn’t hurt less. I’m going to move on, because if I think about it for one more second I’ll have to lie down on the floor of my apartment, and I’ve done that enough for one winter.

Jeff Kent is in Houston, where he’ll be much happier, by which I mean exactly as grumpy, just with more cattle around. He’s now in a lineup with Sammy Sosa and Alex Rodriguez, and he’s been asked about Bonds approximately 9,000 times this spring. He has answered every time by staring at the reporter until the reporter leaves. We’ll miss him. Kind of. Mostly the doubles.

The kids

Here’s the good part, and it’s very good.

The tech guys didn’t just change the name. They looked at a roster of 33-year-olds and decided, correctly, that what this franchise needed was children. Specifically, three of them:

  • Jimmy Rollins, 23, a shortstop who grew up across the bay in Alameda. He was the Phillies’ best prospect until the Phillies, a franchise of principle and spite, lost every player they had during the strike. The A’s wanted him too, but their offer came with fine print saying he might get shipped to New Orleans. The Seals’ offer came with stock options. Rollins came home. This is the most Bay Area thing that has ever happened.
  • Jake Peavy, 20, an Alabama kid who was in the Padres system when the Padres stopped existing for a while.
  • Carlos Zambrano, 20, a Venezuelan right-hander who throws very hard and, from what I’ve seen this spring, treats every pitch like the hitter personally insulted his family.

Peavy and Zambrano are 20 years old. Added together, they’re exactly as old as Andres Galarraga.

I need you to sit with that.

The rotation

One preseason ranking has the Seals’ pitching staff at B+ and says it “could lead the league.” I don’t want to get your hopes up, but I’m going to get your hopes up.

Peavy and Zambrano are the headliners, and they’re both terrifyingly good. Behind them is Mike Hampton, who signed here two winters ago and announced at his introductory press conference that he’d chosen San Francisco for the quality of the local school system. San Francisco does have many fine schools. None of them is why Mike Hampton is here. Hampton is here because the tech guys offered him roughly the gross national product of Belgium, and that’s fine. Somebody has to be the adult in the rotation, and Dave Burba, who’s 35, is the other one.

Then there’s the bullpen, which is deep and weird and full of guys with names like Julio Mateo and Shawn Sedlacek that I’ll pretend I’ve always known. Scot Shields is the one to watch. He’s 26, he was an Angels prospect, and I have a feeling about him.

The feeling is mostly that he’ll pitch the first inning of a lot of games, because the Seals are going to use an opener.

Dusty Baker and the opener

Let me explain this, because I’ve had to explain it to my father four times and he still doesn’t believe me.

Dusty Baker is still the manager. Dusty is 52 years old and has a toothpick in his mouth at all times, including, I assume, while sleeping. He has spent his entire managerial career believing that starting pitchers should start the game, pitch until they’re tired, and then pitch two more innings because character is built in the seventh inning. He’s a lovely man, and in all the years I’ve watched him, he has never once voluntarily removed a starter before the fifth inning.

This spring the tech guys sat Dusty down with a laptop. Somebody, presumably named Brent, explained that on some nights the best way to get through the top of an opposing lineup might be to start the game with a reliever for one inning, then bring in the “real” starter for the second. Dusty listened politely. He chewed his toothpick. Then, according to someone who was in the room, he said, “Brent, I’ve been doing this for 30 years.”

And then he agreed to do it.

I don’t know what they showed him, or what was in that spreadsheet. But the Seals will open some games with Scot Shields throwing one inning, and Dusty Baker will be the one making that call. Let that be a lesson to all of us that anyone can change, even a man whose toothpick has its own Wikipedia entry in my heart.

Dusty will also “play the platoon matchups,” according to the Seals’ media guide, which is a phrase I’ve never heard Dusty say out loud and which I suspect was also written by Brent.

The lineup, or: where did all the walks go?

Here’s where I have to get serious, which I hate.

The offense has a problem, and the problem is getting on base. The same preseason ranking that loved the pitching gave the offense a C and said “OBP is a real issue,” which is the kind of sentence that makes me want to drive to the ballpark and personally hand walks to our hitters.

Let’s go through it:

  • Rollins should be fine. He can run, he’ll hit some doubles, and he’s 23. Leave him alone.
  • Tony Batista plays third base and has the most open batting stance in the history of the sport. He basically faces the pitcher like they’re about to have a conversation. He’ll hit 30 homers and have an on-base percentage you could slide under a door.
  • Benji Gil plays second base. He’s a nice person. I’m sure of it. Let’s move on.
  • Andres Galarraga is 40, plays first base, and is called the Big Cat. I’ll be emotionally invested in every one of his at-bats this season, regardless of outcome.
  • Rondell White and Jacque Jones are fine, solid outfielders. “Fine” and “solid” are doing a lot of work in that sentence.
  • Ruben Sierra is 36 and starts the season on the 60-day injured list, which is the most Ruben Sierra sentence I’ve ever typed.
  • Drew Henson is 22, plays third base, and is here as a bench player and a project. Here’s the fun fact: Henson was such a famous quarterback prospect at Michigan that the Wolverines had a guy named Tom Brady splitting time with him. You may remember Tom Brady from two months ago, when he won the Super Bowl. Drew Henson, meanwhile, is a third baseman for the San Francisco Seals. Life is a series of choices, and some of them are very funny.

Also, a 21-year-old named Ryan Doumit will do a lot of the catching. I don’t know much about him. Neither do the Seals, probably, but they’re very excited.

Reasons for optimism

  • The rotation is young, filthy and potentially the best in the league.
  • Dusty Baker is using an opener, which means anything is possible.
  • The owners have more money than God and are willing to spend it on things like “stock options for shortstops.”
  • Oakland has three teams. We have one, and it’s ours.
  • Barry Bonds doesn’t play here anymore, which means he can’t break our hearts by leaving. He already did that. We’re free.

Reasons for despair

  • The lineup can’t get on base.
  • Barry Bonds plays for the Yankees.
  • Peavy and Zambrano are 20, and 20-year-old pitchers sometimes do 20-year-old pitcher things, like losing the strike zone for a month or deciding to throw 140 pitches because they feel great.
  • The West has Houston, which bought everybody, and Oakland, which can apparently teleport players from Alaska.
  • Barry Bonds plays for the Yankees.

I know I listed that twice. I meant to.

Prediction

The one projection I trust least has the Seals at 75-76, which seems insane to me, but projections are a cold, uncaring science. Another preseason ranking has them fifth in MLB. I’m going with: somewhere between 72 and 95 wins, which is a 23-game range I’m comfortable defending in court.

If the pitching is what I think it is, this team can win the West. If the offense is what I’m afraid it is, this team will lose a lot of 2-1 games, and I’ll write about every single one of them.

The Seals open Sunday in Brooklyn, against the Gothams, a sentence that would have gotten me committed in 1999. Then they come home on Tuesday to the ballpark by the water, which is named after a phone company now. It’ll probably be named after a different phone company in a couple of years, and then after a database company, because that’s how things go out here.

Let’s go, Seals. That still sounds weird. Let’s go anyway.
