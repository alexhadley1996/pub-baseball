The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Louisville Is in First, and Roger Clemens's Arm Is Still Attached

Alexander called the Bats a promotion-ready lineup with a thin staff, and that's what they've been. They lead the FBL East at 24-15, with help from a 4-0 record in extra innings.

Louisville Bats · 24-15 · 215 runs scored, 206 allowed

Alexander ranked Louisville second in the FBL in March. He called it "the best lineup in the league, especially up the middle... this looks like a promotion-ready lineup." He was less impressed by the pitching, which he graded C-: "They've got some big pieces, none bigger than old man Roger Clemens... but its a thin staff. Theyll be hoping to make a promotion push this season before Clemens' arm falls off."

The promotion push is on schedule. Louisville is 24-15 and leads the East Group by two games over Hartford. The site gives the Bats a 78.7% chance at the playoffs and a 40.1% chance of promotion. Its record is the third-best in the FBL. They started 0-3. The April 3 roundup noted that "Louisville fell to 0-3" on the day Charlotte's Erik Milton threw a complete game against them. Since then, the Bats are 24-12.

Clemens

Clemens is 39, has made all nine of his starts and leads the FBL with 75 strikeouts. His 4.40 ERA is higher than his 3.35 FIP. His best start came on May 2 against Honolulu: nine innings, three hits and 13 strikeouts in a game Louisville won on a 10th-inning walk-off. Alexander listed him among his three FBL Cy Young candidates, and at three weeks wrote that he "has been excellent." Paul Quantrill, the closer he praised, has 13 saves and a 1.12 ERA and has allowed no home runs.

The staff behind them is as thin as predicted. Jarrod Washburn has a 3.57 ERA and struck out 10 against Memphis on April 18. Rolando Arrojo (6.17) and Eric Junge (6.81) have been rough in the middle of the rotation, which has a 4.96 ERA overall. Only three FBL teams allow more runs per game. Arrojo did throw eight shutout innings in Friday's 11-0 win over Richmond.

The lineup

The offense is fourth in the FBL in runs per game. It hasn't come from the players Alexander expected. The week-three notes said: "the real story is the top of the lineup: Wendell Magee has been on fire (193 OPS+), and that's with Barry Larkin and Andruw Jones both still playing below their standards." Magee has cooled to .365/.397/.555, still a 144 OPS+. Jones has recovered and leads the team with 2.0 WAR and has a 134 OPS+. He hit two home runs in the 11-0 win. Ryan Klesko is at 128. Larkin, 38, is at 89, and David Eckstein is at 94. "Up the middle" has been the weaker part of the lineup.

Jack Cust, the 23-year-old "legit prospect, though yet unproven," leads the team with nine home runs, but he also has 43 strikeouts and a .215 average. Herb Perry, a 32-year-old third baseman not mentioned in March, has eight home runs and two of the season's biggest hits. On April 4 against Charlotte, according to that day's roundup, he "hit a 3-run HR in the 9th, then a game-winning sac fly in the 11th." On April 15 he homered twice in an 8-6 comeback win over Santo Domingo.

The fine print

Louisville has scored 215 runs and allowed 206. A +9 run differential usually means a team around 20-19, not 24-15. The gap comes from close games: the Bats are 6-2 in one-run games and 4-0 in extra innings, with five walk-off wins. They're also 14-6 at home. On Monday they lost 12-4 in Orlando with Clemens on the mound. Hartford, two games back, comes to Louisville for three games starting Tuesday. A sweep by either team would settle who leads the East. San Antonio follows, then three games in Alaska.
