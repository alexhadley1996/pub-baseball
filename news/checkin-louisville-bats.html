40-Game Check-In · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

Bats at 40: Minding the Gap

Louisville Bats, 24-15 through 39 games

There is a version of the Bats season that the scoreboard has seen and the box scores have not. The scoreboard says 24-15. The box scores, which only know that the Bats scored 215 runs and allowed 206, would have guessed something closer to 20-19. Somewhere between those two numbers is either a skill nobody can measure yet or a bill that has not arrived.

Their most productive hitter is Wendell Magee (LF, 29), at .365/.397/.555 with 2 HR. Jack Cust leads the club with 9 home runs. As a team they are hitting .291/.354/.458, 6th of 20 in the FBL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 46 home runs.

Jarrod Washburn leads the starters with a 3.57 ERA over 53.0 innings (40 K, 17 BB). Roger Clemens (4.40 ERA in 61.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.62 ERA, 8th of 20 in the FBL; starters 4.96, relievers 3.89. Paul Quantrill has 13 saves and 1 blown.

Through 39 games, all they had played when the export was taken on May 13, they were 24-15, with 215 runs scored and 206 allowed (+9). As of the May 13 export they are in first place in the East Group, and 3rd of 20 in the FBL. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 20 wins in 39 games. They are 6-2 in one-run games and 4-0 in extra innings.

And it is not the line anyone would have written for him: his 2001 OPS was .670 in 233 plate appearances. In the rotation, Eric Junge has a 6.81 ERA over 39.2 innings.

A couple of small, true things. When they score three runs or fewer they are 3-9; when they score six or more, 15-1. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

The best night was May 10, an 11-0 win over the Flying Squirrels. The worst was Apr 22, a 12-3 loss to the Spirit. Their best 10-game stretch went 8-2 (Apr 4 to Apr 14); their worst, 4-6 (Apr 17 to Apr 28).

The Bats have played eight games decided by one run. They are 6-2 in them. Nobody is quite sure how much of that is skill, which is what makes it the most interesting thing about a 24-15 team.

The FBL is the bottom rung and nobody gets relegated from it; the whole point is the way up. The top seven make a ladder playoff, seeds one and two are promoted outright, and the winner of the Federal Promotion round makes three. They are the 3rd seed today: in the ladder, ½ games out of an automatic promotion spot and 3½ games clear of the first team outside.

The next stretch is 10 games (7 at home) against the Yard Goats, the Rebeldes, the Athletics, the Solons. Those clubs are 202-195 combined, a .509 winning percentage, so it will mostly be up to them. The longer-range question is whether the 4 or so wins they have banked beyond their run differential stay banked. History says to expect the runs to win that argument eventually; history is not always right in May.

A quarter of a season tells you what a team is capable of. The other three quarters tell you what it is. For the Bats, the interesting part starts now.
