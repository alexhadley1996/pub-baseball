The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Louisville Is in First, and Roger Clemens's Arm Is Still Attached

The Bats came in with a promotion-ready lineup and a thin staff, and that's what they've been. They lead the FBL East at 24-15, with help from a 4-0 record in extra innings.

Louisville Bats · 24-15 · 215 runs scored, 206 allowed

The preseason consensus ranked Louisville second in the FBL, on the strength of the best lineup in the league, especially up the middle. It looked promotion-ready. The pitching got a C-. There were some big pieces, none bigger than old man Roger Clemens, but it was a thin staff, and the Bats would be hoping to make their promotion push before Clemens's arm fell off.

The push is on schedule. Louisville is 24-15 and leads the East Group by two games over Hartford, with the third-best record in the FBL, a 78.7% chance at the playoffs and a 40.1% chance of promotion. It didn't start that way. The Bats lost their first three, the third to a complete game by Charlotte's Eric Milton, and have gone 24-12 since.

Clemens

Clemens is 39. He has made all nine of his starts and leads the FBL with 75 strikeouts, and his 3.35 FIP is a good deal better than his 4.40 ERA. His best start came on May 2 against Honolulu: nine innings, three hits and 13 strikeouts in a game Louisville won on a 10th-inning walk-off. He was one of Alex Miller's three preseason Cy Young candidates, and he's pitched like it. Paul Quantrill, the closer, has 13 saves and a 1.12 ERA and hasn't allowed a home run.

Behind them, the staff is as thin as expected. Jarrod Washburn has a 3.57 ERA and struck out 10 against Memphis on April 18. Rolando Arrojo, at 6.17, and Eric Junge, at 6.81, have been rough in the middle of a rotation with a 4.96 ERA. Only three FBL teams allow more runs per game. Arrojo did throw eight shutout innings in Friday's 11-0 win over Richmond.

The lineup

The offense is fourth in the FBL in runs per game, though the production hasn't come from where it was expected. Three weeks in, the story was the top of the order: Wendell Magee was on fire with a 193 OPS+, and that was with Barry Larkin and Andruw Jones both playing below their standards. Magee has cooled to .365, still a 144 OPS+. Jones has recovered to lead the team with 2.0 WAR and a 134 OPS+, and he hit two homers in the 11-0 win. Ryan Klesko is at 128. Up the middle, Larkin, 38, is at 89, and David Eckstein is at 94.

Jack Cust, the 23-year-old legit-but-unproven prospect, leads the team with nine home runs to go with 43 strikeouts and a .215 average. Herb Perry, a 32-year-old third baseman, has eight homers and two of the biggest hits of the season. On April 4 against Charlotte he hit a three-run homer in the ninth, then won it with a sacrifice fly in the 11th. On April 15 he homered twice in an 8-6 comeback win over Santo Domingo.

The fine print

Louisville has scored 215 runs and allowed 206. A +9 run differential usually means a team around 20-19, not 24-15. The difference is close games: the Bats are 6-2 in one-run games and 4-0 in extras, with five walk-off wins, and they're 14-6 at home. On Monday they lost 12-4 in Orlando with Clemens on the mound. Hartford, two games back, comes to Louisville for three starting Tuesday, and a sweep either way would settle who leads the East. San Antonio follows, then three in Alaska.
