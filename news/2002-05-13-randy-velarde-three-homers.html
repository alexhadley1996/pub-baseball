ABL · 2002-05-13 · By Paul Ricci, Sporting Weekly

Randy Velarde, 39, Hits Three

Randy Velarde grounded out his first two times up in Kansas City. Then he homered in the fourth, the seventh and the eighth, drove in seven runs, and the Padres beat the Monarchs 16-2. He's 39, and it's the season's second three-homer game.

KANSAS CITY, Mo. — Randy Velarde has more than 15 seasons of major league experience, and he spent most of them as the kind of player who is described as “valuable.” A utility man. A guy who could play second, short, third and the outfield and wouldn’t complain about any of them. Nobody ever confused him with a slugger.

On Monday night at Kauffman Field, at 39 years old, he hit three in one game. Velarde homered in the fourth, the seventh and the eighth innings, drove in seven runs, and the San Diego Padres beat the Kansas City Monarchs 16-2.

“I’m as surprised as anyone,” Velarde said. “Well. Maybe not as surprised as Kenny [Rogers].”

The first two

He didn’t start well. In his first two at-bats against Rogers, he grounded out to second. The second one, a soft roller on a changeup, made him so angry that he slammed his bat into the rack hard enough to crack it.

“I was mad at myself,” he said. “Not at Kenny. Kenny’s been getting me out for 15 years. I was mad that I was letting him do it again.”

He went up to the clubhouse between innings and got a new bat, which the Padres’ clubhouse staff now regards as the most important bat in the building.

In the fourth inning, with two outs and a man on second, Rogers threw him a fastball that cut back over the plate, and Velarde hit it into the Padres’ bullpen in left. It made it 5-0.

The second and third

Rogers retired him on a fly ball in the sixth. By the seventh Rogers was gone and the Padres were scoring in bunches. With a man on second, Velarde homered off Ron Mahay to make it 11-0. In the eighth, with two on and two out, he got Mark Guthrie, the Monarchs’ closer, who was in the game only because Kansas City had run out of other options, and hit a three-run homer to left-center. 16-0.

He jogged around the bases without much ceremony. When he got to the dugout, Padres manager Bruce Bochy shook his hand and asked him how old he was.

“I told him 39,” Velarde said. “He said, ‘Can you do it again Tuesday?’ I said, ‘Sure, Boch.’ He’s going to hold me to it.”

Macias and Smoltz

Velarde wasn’t the only Padre with a big night. Jose Macias, the second baseman, went 4-for-5 with two doubles and five RBI. Robert Fick hit a bases-clearing double in the seventh. Hank Blalock homered in the second for the first run.

And John Smoltz, who has quietly become one of the best pitchers in the ABL, threw eight innings, allowed two runs and improved to 7-0.

“John’s the reason we’re in every game,” Bochy said. “Tonight he didn’t have to be. That was nice for a change.”

The second this year

It’s only the second three-homer game of the season in the top three leagues. The other belongs to Marty Cordova, who did it for San Juan on April 2, in an 11-homer, 13-inning game against Anaheim.

“I remember that game,” Velarde said. “Everybody does. That was a crazy one. This one, nothing crazy. We just hit.”

The win moved San Diego into a tie with Kansas City at 23-17. The Monarchs came in with one of the ABL’s best offenses. On Monday the Padres had a better one.

Velarde is hitting .331 with eight home runs. He had five coming into the night.

“Don’t tell anybody,” he said. “They’ll start pitching me like a power hitter.”
