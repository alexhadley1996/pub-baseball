MLB · 2002-04-17 · By Grady Briggs, The Cove Dispatch

Stranded in Washington

The Seals came back from 4-1 down, led 6-4 into the ninth, and lost to Washington 7-6 in 12 when Marlon Anderson hit Shawn Sedlacek's second pitch over the wall. A Seals fan's lament.

WASHINGTON — I want to start by saying that the San Francisco Seals have one of the best records in baseball, that they are 12-5 and that this is going to be a very good season. I want to say that first, because I’m going to spend the rest of this post being upset.

On Wednesday night in Washington, the Seals came back from three runs down, led by two going into the ninth inning, and lost 7-6 in 12 innings to the Cherry Blossoms. They lost it on the second pitch Shawn Sedlacek threw. Marlon Anderson hit it into the seats in right, and I threw a pillow across my living room.

The good part

Let’s talk about the good part first, because there was one.

Dave Burba didn’t have it early. Damian Miller hit a three-run homer off him with two outs in the first, and Damion Easley doubled in another, and it was 4-1 before most of us had finished dinner. But this team doesn’t quit, and it didn’t.

Rondell White doubled in a run in the second. In the fourth, White doubled in two more to tie it, and Tony Batista singled in the go-ahead run. Andres Galarraga, who is 40 and has four hits on a bad night more often than most guys have them on a good one, singled in an insurance run in the sixth. Luke Hudson, Washington’s best starter, gave up 11 hits in 3⅔ innings.

And then there was Mike Hampton. Our starting pitcher. As the designated hitter. Dusty Baker put him there because Hampton has been begging for at-bats since spring training, and Hampton went 3-for-6 with a double. I have no notes. Let the man hit.

“He asked me every day for three weeks,” Baker said. “I finally said yes so he’d stop. Then he got three hits, so now he’s never going to stop.”

The ninth

David Weathers gave back one run in the seventh, on Edgar Renteria’s sacrifice fly. It was 6-5 when Scot Shields came out for the ninth.

Shields got the first out. Then Mark Kotsay singled, Travis Hafner singled, and Renteria singled in the tying run. Shields struck out Miller, Javier Lopez came on and got Anderson to fly out, and the game went to extra innings. At the time I said “that was a good escape.” I want that back.

The bullpen I hate

Here’s the part that bugs me. The Seals had six runs by the sixth inning. After Galarraga’s single, Joe Borowski, Geoff Geary and Kelly Wunsch didn’t let them score again. Wunsch struck out Hampton and Ryan Doumit in the top of the 12th, and I had a bad feeling.

Sedlacek, who had been one of the best young relievers in the league for two weeks, came in for the bottom of the 12th. He threw one pitch. He threw another one. Anderson, leading off the inning, hit it out.

In conclusion

We are 12-5. We are fine. I am not fine. I’ll be fine by Friday.

Go Seals.
