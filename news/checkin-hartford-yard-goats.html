The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Gary Sheffield Isn't Old Yet. Hartford's Kids Are Still Kids.

The Yard Goats lead the FBL in runs and OPS behind a 33-year-old shortstop with a 168 OPS+, a 39-year-old ace and a 37-year-old left fielder. The young players Alexander asked about have mostly waited their turn.

Hartford Yard Goats · 22-17 · 243 runs scored, 221 allowed

Alexander's March question for Hartford came with a clock on it: "Can Hartford's kids grow up before Gary Sheffield gets old?" Six weeks in, the clock hasn't moved much. Sheffield, 33, playing shortstop, is hitting .336/.412/.638 with 11 home runs, 40 RBI and a 168 OPS+. Ellis Burks, 37, has a 134 OPS+. Chuck Finley, 39, has a 2.92 ERA and two complete games. The kids are another story.

Hartford is 22-17, second in the FBL's East Group and two games behind Louisville, and leads the league in both runs per game and OPS. The site gives the Yard Goats a 64.1% chance at the playoffs and a 24.8% chance of promotion.

The kids

The March preview said "the 'next wave' hinges on José Guillén and Adam LaRoche." Guillen, the "classic post-hype bat: loud power, questionable discipline," has been about what that implies: six home runs, 28 strikeouts, 11 walks and a 114 OPS+. LaRoche hit a grand slam against Savannah on April 4. He's now at .246/.276/.391 with 36 strikeouts, four walks and -0.9 WAR. Tim Spooneybarger, the 22-year-old reliever with "a lively fastball and a slurve," has an 8.71 ERA and three blown saves. The first came on April 7 in Richmond, when he gave up a walk-off home run without recording an out in a 10-9 loss. Third baseman Santiago Perez, 26, has a 59 OPS+.

Alexander's personalities report had flagged Hartford for "Selfish Tendencies" and "internal tension between veterans and young players." The veterans are carrying the team, which may not help that tension.

The veterans

Randy Winn, part of the "steady big-league competence" in the middle of the lineup, is hitting .346/.404/.603 with 10 steals, a 157 OPS+ and 1.8 WAR. Ray Durham is at .298/.358/.516. Greg Myers, a 36-year-old catcher, has a .321 average and a 131 OPS+. He went 4-for-5 with four RBI in Santo Domingo on April 12. Burks has walked 16 times, stolen five bases without being caught, and hit eight home runs. On April 3 the Yard Goats trailed Savannah 9-0 after two innings and won 11-10 on 16 hits, which the roundup called the "comeback of the season."

The pitching is the problem, as Alexander expected. He called Hartford "another strong offense / low-ceiling pitching club, similar to Carolina — but with less margin for error." The staff ERA is 5.12, and only one FBL club allows more runs per game. Joe Mays, the "rotation headliner" in March, is 1-4 with a 7.04 ERA. He also lasted just 1.2 innings and allowed nine runs in the start that ended in that 11-10 comeback. Doug Davis has a 7.38 ERA. Finley is the exception: 42 strikeouts and 12 walks, and on May 3 he threw a complete game, allowed no earned runs and lost 1-0 to Charlotte.

The record

Hartford started 5-0, then went 2-8. Five of those eight losses were by one run. On April 14, Ryan Vogelsong struck out 10 in 7.2 innings and left with a lead. The week-three notes describe what happened next: "a gem that got ruined by a four-run ninth from the Gallos in yet another Yard Goats close loss." Since then the Yard Goats have gone 14-9, though they lost 17-3 in Oklahoma City on Saturday.

Alexander's preview predicted 78-73 and warned that "Sheffield/Burks may become midseason trade dominoes if the standings break the wrong way." So far the standings have broken the right way. The next three games are in Louisville against the first-place Bats, followed by Memphis at home and three in Albuquerque. A sweep there would put Hartford in first place.
