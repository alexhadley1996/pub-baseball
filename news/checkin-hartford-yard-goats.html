The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Gary Sheffield Isn't Old Yet. Hartford's Kids Are Still Kids.

The Yard Goats lead the FBL in runs and OPS behind a 33-year-old shortstop with a 168 OPS+, a 39-year-old ace and a 37-year-old left fielder. The young players have mostly waited their turn.

Hartford Yard Goats · 22-17 · 243 runs scored, 221 allowed

Hartford's March question came with a clock on it: could the kids grow up before Gary Sheffield got old? Six weeks in, the clock has barely moved. Sheffield, 33 and playing shortstop, is hitting .336 with 11 homers, 40 RBI and a 168 OPS+. Ellis Burks, 37, has a 134 OPS+. Chuck Finley, 39, has a 2.92 ERA and two complete games. The kids are another story.

Hartford is 22-17, second in the East Group, two games behind Louisville, and leads the FBL in both runs per game and OPS. The site gives the Yard Goats a 64.1% chance at the playoffs and a 24.8% chance of promotion.

The kids

The next wave hinged on Jose Guillen and Adam LaRoche. Guillen, a classic post-hype bat with loud power and questionable discipline, has been about what that implies: six homers, 28 strikeouts, 11 walks and a 114 OPS+. LaRoche hit a grand slam against Savannah on April 4 and has struggled since. He's hitting .246 with 36 strikeouts, four walks and -0.9 WAR. Tim Spooneybarger, the 22-year-old reliever with a lively fastball and a slurve, has an 8.71 ERA and three blown saves, the first on April 7 in Richmond, when he gave up a walk-off homer without getting an out in a 10-9 loss. Third baseman Santiago Perez, 26, has a 59 OPS+.

That matters beyond the box scores. The clubhouse came in with selfish tendencies and some tension between veterans and young players, and right now the veterans are carrying the team.

The veterans

They're carrying it well. Randy Winn, part of the steady big-league core, is hitting .346 with 10 steals, a 157 OPS+ and 1.8 WAR. Ray Durham is slugging .516. Greg Myers, a 36-year-old catcher, is hitting .321 and went 4-for-5 with four RBI in Santo Domingo on April 12. Burks has eight homers, 16 walks and five steals without being caught. The signature win came on April 3, when the Yard Goats trailed Savannah 9-0 after two innings and won 11-10 on 16 hits, the comeback of the season.

The pitching is the weak spot. This was always a strong offense with a low-ceiling staff, like Carolina's but with less margin for error. The staff ERA is 5.12, and only one FBL club allows more runs per game. Joe Mays, the rotation headliner, is 1-4 with a 7.04 ERA; he lasted 1.2 innings and gave up nine runs in the start that turned into that 11-10 comeback. Doug Davis is at 7.38. Finley is the exception: 42 strikeouts and 12 walks, and on May 3 he threw a complete game without an earned run and still lost 1-0 to Charlotte.

The record

Hartford started 5-0, then went 2-8, with five of the eight losses by one run. The hardest came April 14, when Ryan Vogelsong struck out 10 in 7.2 innings and left with a lead, only for the Gallos to score four in the ninth. Since then the Yard Goats are 14-9, though they lost 17-3 in Oklahoma City on Saturday.

The March preview figured the Yard Goats for 78-73, and the OOTP projections were more bullish, at 87-64. If the standings broke the wrong way, Sheffield and Burks looked like midseason trade chips. So far the standings have broken the right way. Next come three in Louisville against the first-place Bats, then Memphis at home and three in Albuquerque. A sweep in Louisville would put Hartford in first.
