40-Game Check-In · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Rays Run, Therefore They Are

Tampa Bay Rays, 17-23 through 40 games

53 stolen bases. That is the first number on the Rays season, partly because it is so large and partly because it tells you how they want to play: like the next 90 feet are always available.

The ABL is the middle rung, so it is the only league here where you can fall and climb in the same breath: each conference winner gets a bye, second and third play in, both finalists of the American Championship are promoted to MLB, and the bottom three are relegated to the FBL. They sit 8th in the Eastern Conference, 6 games back of the Spiders. The drop is 1 game below them, where the Violets is the best of the clubs on relegation pace.

Through 40 games they were 17-23, scoring 168 runs and allowing 189 (-21). As of the May 13 export they are 8th in the Eastern Conference, 6 games back, and 17th of 20 in the ABL. That run differential would usually produce a record around 18-22. They are 5-8 in one-run games and 2-2 in extra innings.

The hitters

Their most productive hitter is Chase Utley (3B, 23), at .287/.366/.441 with 5 HR. J.J. Davis leads the club with 9 home runs. On the bases, Alex Sanchez is 12-for-15 stealing. As a team they are hitting .258/.320/.370, 20th of 20 in the ABL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 31 home runs. The surprise might be Chase Utley, who arrived without a 2001 top-tier line worth the name (under 150 plate appearances) and is hitting .287/.366/.441. The weakest regular bat has been Alex Sanchez, at .235/.287/.259 in 174 plate appearances.

The pitchers

Jerome Williams leads the starters with a 3.58 ERA over 50.1 innings (35 K, 17 BB). Ben Sheets (3.69 ERA in 61.0 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.66 ERA, 5th of 20 in the ABL; starters 4.58, relievers 4.81. Closing has mostly meant Rafael Betancourt: 5 saves, 2.53 ERA. In the rotation, Josh Fogg has a 6.80 ERA over 41.0 innings.

The best and worst of it

The best night was Apr 18, an 11-4 win over the Gamblers. The worst was May 12, a 17-6 loss to the Royeaux. Their best 10-game stretch went 6-4 (Apr 2 to Apr 12); their worst, 3-7 (Apr 12 to Apr 21).

Some details worth knowing. Juan Pierre has walked 14 times and struck out 12, a ratio that belongs to a different, more patient century.

What next

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 6 of them at home, against the Reds, the Angels, the Beavers, the Monarchs, opponents with a combined 206-194 record (.515). Neither a gift nor an ambush. The record matches the runs, so there is no regression bill coming and no hidden credit either. The next 122 games will mostly be about whether Chase Utley keeps doing this.
