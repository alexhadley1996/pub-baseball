The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Tampa Bay's Pitching Is Better Than Anyone Expected, and the Prospects Aren't Hitting Yet

Ben Sheets looked awfully lonely in March. Dave Bush and Jerome Williams have kept him company, and the Rays rank fifth in ERA. They're also last in the ABL in runs, and they've lost five straight.

Tampa Bay Rays · 17-23 · 168 runs scored, 189 allowed

Tampa Bay came into the season as a long-term project. The preseason consensus ranked the Rays 19th, the projections had them at 66-85, the worst record in the East, and the roster was basically an offense of prospects. If the Rays could weather the relegation battles, they could be great a few years down the road. At 17-23 they're on roughly that pace, but they've gotten there in a way nobody expected.

Not so lonely

The worry was the rotation. Ben Sheets was not the worst pitcher to build a staff around, but he looked awfully lonely, and it wasn't clear where the rest of the innings would come from. The answer has been the other young starters. Sheets has a 3.69 ERA and 2.1 WAR, one of the best lines in the ABL. Dave Bush, 22, has a 3.73 ERA and has walked seven batters in 50.2 innings; on April 2 he threw eight scoreless innings in a 1-0 win over New Orleans. Jerome Williams, 20, is at 3.58. Tampa Bay ranks fifth in the ABL in ERA and fourth in fewest runs allowed. Even the weak-looking bullpen has been passable, at 4.81, and Rafael Betancourt has five saves and one walk.

Low scores have been the theme. The season opened with a 1-0 loss to New Orleans, followed by a 1-0 win. On April 22 in Baltimore, Sheets gave up 11 hits and no earned runs over eight innings in a 2-1 win. Tampa Bay has also been shut out five times.

The prospects

That's the other half. The Rays are last in the ABL in runs per game and OPS and 18th in home runs, and the young hitters are mostly still learning. Chase Utley has a 99 OPS+, and Juan Pierre is hitting .347 with a 98 OPS+. Garrett Atkins is at 72. Center fielder Alex Sanchez is hitting .235 with four doubles and no other extra-base hits, a 37 OPS+. Hanley Ramirez, 18, got off to a weak start and is still at .198.

The best power so far has come from J.J. Davis, 23, who has nine homers and drove in seven in a 9-3 win over San Juan on April 14, the deciding game of the Caribbean series. And catcher John Buck, 21, homered twice in an 11-10, 11-inning walk-off win over Minnesota on May 1, the kind of game that shows what this lineup could be.

Lately

For now, the relegation fight is close. Tampa Bay is eighth in the Eastern Conference, six games out, with a 15.9% chance of going down. It has lost five straight, including three in Montreal by a combined 31-9. Next come Cincinnati at home, three in Anaheim and Portland at home.
