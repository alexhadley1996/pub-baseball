The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Tampa Bay's Pitching Is Better Than Anyone Expected, and the Prospects Aren't Hitting Yet

Alexander worried that Ben Sheets would be "awfully lonely." Dave Bush and Jerome Williams have kept him company, and the Rays rank fifth in ERA. They are also last in the ABL in runs, and they've lost five straight.

Tampa Bay Rays · 17-23 · 168 runs scored, 189 allowed

Alexander ranked Tampa Bay 19th and predicted 66-85, the ABL's worst record. His view was long-term. The roster was "basically an offense of prospects," and "if the Rays can weather relegation battles, they could be great a few years in the future." At 17-23, they're on pace for roughly the record he predicted, but they've gotten there in the opposite way from what he expected.

Not so lonely

"Ben Sheets is not the worst pitcher to build your staff around, but right now he looks awfully lonely," Alexander wrote. "Where will the innings go?" The answer has been the other young starters. Sheets has a 3.69 ERA, a 3.33 FIP and 2.1 WAR, one of the best lines in the ABL. Dave Bush, 22, has a 3.73 ERA and has walked seven batters in 50.2 innings. On April 2 he threw eight scoreless innings in a 1-0 win over New Orleans, which the roundup noted. Jerome Williams, who is 20, has a 3.58 ERA. Alexander said those two "look alright," and that has been right. Tampa Bay ranks fifth in the ABL in ERA and fourth in fewest runs allowed per game. Alexander called the bullpen "weak," but its ERA is 4.81. Rafael Betancourt has five saves and has walked one batter.

Low run totals have been the theme on both sides. The season opened with a 1-0 loss to New Orleans, and the next day's game was a 1-0 win. On April 22 in Baltimore, Sheets allowed 11 hits and no earned runs over eight innings in a 2-1 win. Tampa Bay has been shut out five times.

The prospects

The Rays rank last in the ABL in runs per game and OPS, and 18th in home runs. Alexander listed five prospects. Chase Utley (99 OPS+) and Juan Pierre (.347, 98 OPS+) have been about average. The trends piece named Pierre as one of the two hitters who had "been strong." Garrett Atkins has a 72 OPS+. Alex Sanchez, the center fielder, is hitting .235/.287/.259 with no extra-base hits other than four doubles, for a 37 OPS+. Hanley Ramirez, 18, was singled out in the trends piece for a weak start. He's now at .198 with a 39 OPS+. The best power hitter so far is J.J. Davis, 23, the other player the trends piece praised. He has nine home runs, and he drove in seven in a 9-3 win over San Juan on April 14. That was the deciding game of what the trends piece called "the Caribbean series." Catcher John Buck, 21, homered twice in an 11-10, 11-inning walk-off win over Minnesota on May 1.

Lately

That relegation battle is close. Tampa Bay is eighth in the Eastern Conference, six games out, with a 15.9% chance of relegation. It has lost five straight, including three in Montreal by a combined 31-9. Next come three at home against Cincinnati, three in Anaheim and three at home against Portland.
