The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Dodgers Were 9-3. Pedro Has 80 Strikeouts. They're in Last Place.

Los Angeles swept Brooklyn and beat Detroit in the first two weeks, then went 8-21. The worst run differential in MLB sits behind one of the best pitchers in it, and the DH is a 21-year-old with a .229 OBP.

Los Angeles Dodgers · 17-24 · 153 runs scored, 206 allowed

On the morning of April 12 the Dodgers were 9-3. They had just won five straight, swept the Gothams in Brooklyn and beaten Detroit twice at home, the second time on Wilson Betemit's 12th-inning walk-off hit after Mickey Callaway's 7.1 shutout innings. Alexander had predicted them to go 55-96 in March, so this was not part of the plan.

Since then they are 8-21. At 17-24 they are tied with Arizona for last in the West and hold the relegation spot on tiebreak. Their run differential is -53. The site's simulations give them a 37.8% chance of going down, the highest in the West. Alexander's 40-game entry opens: "The LA Dodgers looks absolutely terrible, and there's no way around it."

Pedro, and then

The March line on the pitching was "It's Pedro Martinez and then not much for the Dodgers." Pedro has been Pedro. He has 80 strikeouts and 20 walks in 65.2 innings, a 2.31 FIP, four complete games and 2.5 WAR, yet his ERA is 3.97 and his record is 4-5. He has struck out 10 or more in five of his nine starts. On Saturday he threw a complete game against St. Louis with 10 strikeouts.

The "and then" part has been better than expected, at least on the scoreboard. Jose Lima, who had a 5.54 ERA last year, is at 3.44. He has thrown 8.1 shutout innings twice, in Seattle and against Washington, and threw a four-hit complete game against St. Louis on Friday. Callaway has a 3.22 ERA. Alexander says he "has survived on a diet of groundballs and clutch moments despite low K numbers and too high homers and walk numbers." The bullpen is where it falls apart, with a 4.97 ERA. Michael Wuertz and Hector Carrasco are the only relievers Alexander found "respectable." Justin Kaye, Russ Springer and David Lee "have combined for far too many long balls allowed." Springer gave up three runs in a third of an inning in the 10-inning loss in Arizona on May 8.

The worst offense in MLB, give or take Dallas

The Dodgers' .299 OBP is the lowest in MLB, and the team ranks 19th in runs. At three weeks, Alexander wrote that "most nights the offense feels like it's trying to win 3–2 games without ever putting pressure on the pitcher." Jason Varitek has been the exception: 10 home runs and a 124 OPS+, and a 4-for-4 night with three doubles and a homer on Saturday. Juan Gonzalez has a 118 OPS+ and David Segui 107. The rest of the regulars are below average. Tomas Perez is hitting .214, though Alexander values his defense at second.

The strangest decision is at DH. Shane Victorino, 21, the "prospect" Alexander hoped "can make a difference" in March, has 159 plate appearances and is at .200/.229/.327 with a 49 OPS+ and -1.1 WAR. Alexander's verdict is that the Dodgers' "decision to play Victorino at DH is borne of necessity and not idiocy," because "the lack of organizational depth in terms of hitters is pretty shocking." Betemit, 20, the walk-off hero in April, is playing shortstop with a .262 OBP.

How 9-3 became 17-24

The slide started with a six-game losing streak from April 12 to 18: the last game of the Detroit series, a home sweep by Miami, then two losses in Seattle. The A's outscored them 29-5 in a three-game sweep in Oakland. Then came the worst week of all, a seven-game losing streak from April 30 to May 6: swept in Atlanta, swept at home by the Seals, then a loss in Arizona. Adam Wainwright, the 20-year-old who replaced Calvin Maduro in the rotation, started two of those games and allowed seven runs in two innings in the first.

A home sweep of the Cardinals last weekend was the first sweep the Dodgers had managed since early April. They lost 7-2 in Boston on Monday. The next seven are all against East clubs: one more in Boston, three in Toronto and three with Philadelphia. Arizona is tied with them, and Dallas is one game ahead.
