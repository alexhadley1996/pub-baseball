40-Game Check-In · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Dodgers Are Still Here

Los Angeles Dodgers, 17-23 through 40 games

There was a stretch, Apr 30 to May 6, when the Dodgers lost seven straight. Seasons recover from that sort of thing all the time, but they do not forget it, and neither does the standings page.

Jason Varitek has been the Dodgers hitter opponents least want to see: .244/.305/.559, 10 homers, 25 driven in. As a team they are hitting .236/.299/.392, 19th of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 38 home runs.

The rotation's anchor is Mickey Callaway: 9 starts, 58.2 innings, 3.22 ERA. Jose Lima (3.44 ERA in 52.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.57 ERA, 18th of 20 in MLB; starters 4.32, relievers 5.12. Closing has mostly meant Hector Carrasco: 5 saves, 3.86 ERA.

Through 40 games they were 17-23, scoring 151 runs and allowing 199 (-48). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 17-24, 10th in the West Division, 9 games back, and 18th of 20 in MLB. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 15 wins in 40 games. They are 5-5 in one-run games and 3-1 in extra innings.

The weakest regular bat has been Shane Victorino, at .193/.224/.324 in 154 plate appearances. In the rotation, Dave Mlicki has a 5.83 ERA over 41.2 innings, after a 6.17 ERA in 2001.

The fine print is fun here. When they score three runs or fewer they are 5-19; when they score six or more, 5-1. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

The best night was Apr 19, a 7-0 win over the Mariners. The worst was Apr 23, a 13-2 loss to the Athletics. Their best 10-game stretch went 7-3 (Mar 31 to Apr 9); their worst, 2-8 (Apr 11 to Apr 21).

MLB has no wild card and no consolation prize: division winners play for the title, the last-place club in each division goes down to the ABL. The Dodgers are 9 games behind the Athletics in the West Division. They are also last, which in this league is not a sad statistic but a trapdoor: dead level with the Diamondbacks for the right to stay up, and sorted out only by a tiebreaker for now.

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 2 of them at home, against the Red Sox, the Blue Jays, the Phillies, the Cubs, opponents with a combined 189-221 record (.461). That is a chance to bank wins. Their record is about what their runs say it should be, which is reassuring or alarming depending on which side of .500 you are standing on. Watch Jason Varitek: the offense has run through him.
