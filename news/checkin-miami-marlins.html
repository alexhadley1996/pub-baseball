40-Game Check-In · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Marlins Changed Somewhere Around Game 20

Miami Marlins, 24-16 through 40 games

The first 20 games and the next 20 games of the Marlins season do not seem to be about the same team. 16-4, then 8-12. Something stopped working, or stopped being lucky.

The best bat has belonged to Cliff Floyd, 29, who is hitting .290/.360/.579 with 9 home runs and 31 RBI in 161 plate appearances. Carl Crawford has 11 steals without being caught. As a team they are hitting .258/.322/.420, 10th of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 34 home runs.

The rotation's anchor is Cory Lidle: 9 starts, 66.2 innings, 2.16 ERA. Brett Laxton (3.27 ERA in 41.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 3.76 ERA, 4th of 20 in MLB; starters 3.96, relievers 3.32. Kazuhiro Sasaki has 7 saves and 1 blown.

Through 40 games they were 24-16, scoring 177 runs and allowing 160 (+17). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 25-16, in first place in the East Division, and 3rd of 20 in MLB. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 22 wins in 40 games. They are 6-6 in one-run games and 1-2 in extra innings.

The disappointment, at least so far, is Jason Giambi: a 1.137 OPS in 2001, .889 through 165 plate appearances this year. In the rotation, Ruben Quevedo has a 6.92 ERA over 39.0 innings, after a 4.61 ERA in 2001.

A couple of small, true things. When they score three runs or fewer they are 6-11; when they score six or more, 10-3. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

The best night was Apr 16, an 8-0 win over the Dodgers. The worst was May 11, an 8-0 loss to the Gothams. Their best 10-game stretch went 8-2 (Mar 31 to Apr 9); their worst, 3-7 (May 2 to May 12).

For seven straight games, from Apr 2 to Apr 8, the Marlins did not lose. Every season has a stretch that it will be remembered for, and the Marlins may have already had theirs.

In MLB the stakes are blunt: win the division and you are in the World Series, a best-of-nine; finish last and you are relegated to the ABL. The Marlins lead the East Division by 1 game over the Wolverines.

The next stretch is 10 games (6 at home) against the Wolverines, the Red Sox, the Mariners, the Yankees. Those clubs are 209-201 combined, a .510 winning percentage, so it will mostly be up to them. The longer-range question is whether the 2 or so wins they have banked beyond their run differential stay banked. History says to expect the runs to win that argument eventually; history is not always right in May.

In six weeks the Marlins have shown us who they might be. The next six will show us who they are willing to keep being.
