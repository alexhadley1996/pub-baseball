The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Marlins Are 16-4 on the Road and Their First Baseman Plays Center

Miami leads the East at 25-16 with a run differential that says 22-19, a 9-12 home record and Lee Stevens, a career first baseman, starting in center field. Whether Miami is for real is still an open question.

Miami Marlins · 25-16 · 183 runs scored, 165 allowed

The question about Miami in March was a simple one: is it for real? Six weeks later the Marlins lead the East at 25-16, and nobody has a good answer yet.

Start with the travel, because it's the strangest part. Miami is 16-4 on the road and 9-12 at home. It went 6-0 in Atlanta and Los Angeles, swept three games in Houston and took two of three at Wrigley. Then it came home, and the Gothams spent May 10-12 outscoring the Marlins 21-4 in a sweep at loanDepot Park. Nobody thinks that split is a skill. It's still very strange.

The center fielder

So is the man in center. Lee Stevens played 152 games at first base last season and has never played center in his big-league career. This year he's started 36 games there. He's 34, and he's hitting .288 with eight home runs and a 139 OPS+. His best moment came April 5 against the Seals: Miami had blown a 4-0 lead, and Stevens won it with a walk-off homer in the ninth. He has been a nice surprise at the plate, and a surprise everywhere else too.

The middle of the order

Miami came into the year looking high-variance, with real upside built on two star-level bats in the middle of the order. Both have shown up. Cliff Floyd has 10 home runs, 36 RBI and a .600 slugging percentage, and on Monday in Detroit he drove in five as Miami came back from 5-1 down to win 6-5 and stop a four-game losing streak. Jason Giambi has a .412 on-base percentage and, as of this month, an eight-year extension at $10 million a year, the big news of May.

The worry was always the hitters in front of them, and that's where it has gone wrong. Cristian Guzman, an .814 OPS shortstop a year ago, has a .274 on-base percentage. Carl Crawford, 20, is 13-for-13 stealing bases but gets on only 30.5% of the time. The catchers, Brian Schneider and Corky Miller, have OPS+ marks of 73 and 39. Floyd and Giambi have often come up with nobody on.

The pitching, meanwhile, has been better than the C- it got in the preseason rankings. Cory Lidle opened the season with eight innings against Washington, a start in which Marlon Anderson popped a sacrifice bunt straight to Schneider, and he hasn't really stopped: he's 6-1 with a 2.16 ERA, 54 strikeouts and 11 walks. In Los Angeles on April 16, Bruce Chen threw 8.2 innings of one-hit ball with 11 strikeouts. Kazuhiro Sasaki has eight saves in nine chances, and the bullpen ERA is 3.19. The weak spot is Ruben Quevedo, 0-4 with a 6.92 ERA, who has given up home runs at a rate that's a real accomplishment in a park that suppresses them.

Timing

By April 21 Miami had the best record in baseball, and a lot of it came down to timing. The Marlins led MLB in Win Probability Added for both hitters and pitchers and had a 1.143 OPS in high-leverage spots. The run differential is the sober version. They've scored 183 and allowed 165, which usually buys about 22 wins in 41 games, not 25. And the clutch hitting has cooled. Miami went 20-9 in April, is 5-7 in May and was 3-7 over its last 10.

From here

MLB division winners go to a best-of-nine World Series, and last place goes to the ABL. Miami leads Detroit by a game, Brooklyn by two and the Yankees by three. The site's simulations give the Marlins a 37.9% chance to win the East and project about 83 wins, which would still beat the preseason projection of 70-81, ninth in the East, by a lot.

The road ahead looks harder. No help is coming from the minors, the rotation is one injury away from two real black holes, and the Yankees, Gothams and Wolverines all look like stronger teams. The schedule tests that right away: one more in Detroit, then Boston and Seattle at home, then a trip to Yankee Stadium. If the home record is going to turn around, a week at home against Boston is a good place to start.
