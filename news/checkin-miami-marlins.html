The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Marlins Are 16-4 on the Road and Their First Baseman Plays Center

Miami leads the East at 25-16 with a run differential that says 22-19, a home record of 9-12, and Lee Stevens, a career first baseman, starting in center field. Alexander asked in March whether Miami was for real. The answer is still pending.

Miami Marlins · 25-16 · 183 runs scored, 165 allowed

Alexander ended his preseason list of teams to follow with one simple question: "is Miami for real?" Six weeks later the Marlins lead the East at 25-16. They've given him plenty to wonder about.

Start with the travel. Miami is 16-4 on the road and 9-12 at home. It went 6-0 in Atlanta and Los Angeles, swept a three-game series in Houston, and won two of three at Wrigley Field. Then it came home, and the Gothams spent May 10 to 12 outscoring the Marlins 21-4 in a three-game sweep at loanDepot Park. This isn't a skill, and nobody thinks it is. It's still very strange.

The center fielder

Lee Stevens played 152 games at first base last season. In the export's records of his top-level career, he has played first, left and right, and never center. This year he has started 36 games in center field for Miami. He is 34, and he is hitting .288/.340/.561 with eight home runs and an OPS+ of 139. His best moment came April 5 against the Seals. Miami had blown a 4-0 lead, and Stevens won it in the ninth with a walk-off solo homer, a moment Alexander's April 5 roundup led with. Alexander's 40-game notes called Stevens "a nice surprise at the dish." He is a surprise everywhere.

What the preview got right

The March preview called Miami "high-variance" with "real upside," built on "star-level middle-of-the-order threats." Both of those threats have shown up. Cliff Floyd is hitting .293/.361/.600 with 10 home runs and 36 RBI. On May 13 in Detroit he drove in five, and Miami came back from 5-1 down to win 6-5 and stop a four-game losing streak. Jason Giambi has a .412 OBP. In his 40-game piece Alexander reported "the big news of the month," an eight-year extension for Giambi at $10 million a year. The preview's worry was the supporting cast getting on base ahead of the sluggers, and that is where it has gone wrong. Cristian Guzman, last year's .814 OPS shortstop, is hitting .239/.274/.335. Carl Crawford, 20, has 13 stolen bases without being caught, but a .305 OBP. The catchers, Brian Schneider and Corky Miller, have OPS+ marks of 73 and 39.

The pitching has been better than the C- grade he gave it in March. Cory Lidle opened the season with eight innings against Washington, which Alexander recapped in detail (including Marlon Anderson's sacrifice bunt popped straight to Schneider). He is now 6-1 with a 2.16 ERA, 54 strikeouts and 11 walks. In Los Angeles on April 16, Bruce Chen threw 8.2 innings of one-hit ball with 11 strikeouts. Kazuhiro Sasaki has eight saves and one blown save. The bullpen ERA is 3.19. The weak spot is Ruben Quevedo, 0-4 with a 6.92 ERA, who Alexander says has been hit hard by the long ball, "a real accomplishment" in a park that suppresses home runs.

Timing, again

By the April 21 trends piece Miami had the best record in baseball, and Alexander's explanation was timing. The Marlins led MLB in Win Probability Added for hitters and pitchers, and had a 1.143 OPS in high-leverage spots. The run differential is the sober version. Miami has scored 183 and allowed 165, which usually buys about 22 wins in 41 games, not 25. The Marlins went 20-9 through April and are 5-7 in May, 3-7 over their last 10. They were 24-12 before a four-game losing streak, the last three games of it against the Gothams.

From here

MLB division winners go straight to a best-of-nine World Series, and last place goes to the ABL. Miami is in first, one game ahead of Detroit and two ahead of Brooklyn, with the Yankees three back. The site's simulations give the Marlins a 37.9% chance to win the East and project about 83 wins, which would still beat Alexander's March prediction of 70-81 (ninth in the East) by a lot. His May outlook was cooler: no help coming from the minors, "one starting pitching injury away from two real black holes," and Yankees, Gothams and Wolverines that "all look stronger." The schedule tests that right away: one more game in Detroit, then Boston and Seattle at home, then a trip to Yankee Stadium. If the home record is going to normalize, a week at home against Boston is a good place for it to start.
