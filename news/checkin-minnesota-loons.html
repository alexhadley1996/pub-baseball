The Loons and the Map Problem

Minnesota Loons, 21-19 through 40 games

The Loons are two teams. One of them plays at home and is 15-5. The other one travels and is 6-14. Together they are 21-19, which describes neither of them.

40 games, by the numbers

  • 21-19 record
  • +10 run differential (250 scored, 240 allowed)
  • 5-3 in one-run games
  • 15-5 at home
  • 6-14 on the road
  • 4.81 / 6.23 starter / reliever ERA
  • 53 home runs
  • 26 stolen bases

Through 40 games they were 21-19, scoring 250 runs and allowing 240 (+10). As of the May 13 export they are 6th in the Western Conference, 5 games back, and 8th of 20 in the ABL. By the old Pythagorean arithmetic, that is a 21-win team so far. They are 5-3 in one-run games and 3-2 in extra innings.

David Ortiz has been the Loons hitter opponents least want to see: .331/.388/.595, 8 homers, 37 driven in. As a team they are hitting .307/.372/.484, 2nd of 20 in the ABL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 53 home runs.

In the rotation, Ryan Rupe has been the steadiest: 4-2 with a 2.98 ERA, 39 strikeouts and 14 walks in 48.1 innings. Joe Blanton (4.95 ERA in 40.0 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 5.33 ERA, 12th of 20 in the ABL; starters 4.81, relievers 6.23. Aaron Fultz has 9 saves and 1 blown.

And it is not the line anyone would have written for him: his 2001 OPS was .799 in 347 plate appearances. On the other side of the ledger, Chipper Jones is at .299/.379/.481, a long way from the 1.032 OPS he managed in 2001.

Some details worth knowing. When they score three runs or fewer they are 2-10; when they score six or more, 16-5. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

The best night was Apr 29, an 18-3 win over the Knights. The worst was Apr 14, an 18-1 loss to the Monarchs. Their best 10-game stretch went 7-3 (Apr 22 to May 2); their worst, 2-8 (Apr 12 to Apr 21).

The ABL is the middle rung, so it is the only league here where you can fall and climb in the same breath: each conference winner gets a bye, second and third play in, both finalists of the American Championship are promoted to MLB, and the bottom three are relegated to the FBL. They sit 6th in the Western Conference, 5 games back of the Black Sox. The drop is 3 games below them, where the Violets is the best of the clubs on relegation pace.

The next stretch is 10 games (6 at home) against the Spiders, the Golden Eagles, the Stars, the Bees. Those clubs are 190-210 combined, a .475 winning percentage, so it will mostly be up to them. The record matches the runs, so there is no regression bill coming and no hidden credit either. The next 122 games will mostly be about whether David Ortiz keeps doing this.
