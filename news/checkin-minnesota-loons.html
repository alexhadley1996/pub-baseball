The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Minnesota Was Projected to Lose 85 Games and Leads the ABL in Scoring

Picked 18th in the preseason consensus, with a middle of the lineup that looked extremely weak, the Loons score more runs than anyone in the ABL, are 15-5 at home and have watched Chipper Jones recover from a slow start.

Minnesota Loons · 21-19 · 250 runs scored, 240 allowed

Nobody saw much in Minnesota. The preseason projections had the Loons at 66-85, one of the worst records in the ABL, and the consensus ranked them 18th. The offense got a C-/D+: strong at the corners with Chipper Jones and David Ortiz, extremely weak up the middle. The pitching got a D+, and the clubhouse came in with a reputation as selfish.

After 40 games, Minnesota is 21-19 and has scored more runs than anyone in the ABL. It ranks second in home runs and OPS. It's sixth in the Western Conference, five games behind Southside, with a +10 run differential.

Up the middle

The weak spots haven't been weak. Second baseman Keith Ginter is slugging .513 with 30 RBI, eight of them in one game, a 4-for-4 day against Anaheim on April 9 that included a grand slam. Shortstop Earl Snyder has seven homers and a 108 OPS+. Shea Hillenbrand, a critical piece behind the plate, is hitting .306. The exception is Rickey Henderson in center. He's 43 and hitting .237, but 25 walks give him a .368 on-base percentage, and he's scored a team-high 34 runs.

The corners have been as good as expected, once they got going. Ortiz is hitting .331 with 37 RBI, eight of them with two homers in a 16-5 win over New Orleans on April 17 and six more on April 30. Jones was the slow one. On April 21 he had no homers and a 50 wRC+, and Henderson was hitting .152. Jones has hit five home runs since and pushed his OPS+ up to 112. Juan Rivera, one of the young outfielders, is hitting .342 and went 5-for-5 in an 18-3 win over New York. Brian Simmons, one of the few hitters producing in April, has a 134 OPS+, and on May 9 he homered twice and drove in six as Minnesota came back from four down to beat Cincinnati.

The bullpen floor

The pitching was supposed to have a high floor in the bullpen. It hasn't. The bullpen ERA is 6.23, with Brad Voyles at 8.22, Mike Garcia at 8.84 and Brian Shouse at 8.03. Mike Koplove has been the exception, with a 3.43 ERA and no walks in 21 innings, and Aaron Fultz has nine saves. The rotation has been better than expected, with a 4.81 ERA. Ryan Rupe, a 6.59 ERA pitcher in 2001, is at 2.98 and threw 6.1 shutout innings against Anaheim on April 8. Darryl Kile, extended on a four-year deal in April, has a 5.56 ERA.

Home and away

Minnesota is 15-5 at home and 6-14 on the road. All three walk-off wins have come at home, two of them in extras: 14 innings against Anaheim on April 10 and 13 against New Orleans on April 16. The Loons have swept Anaheim, New York and Cincinnati at home. On the road they were swept in Kansas City, outscored 31-7, and at Southside.

A 2-8 stretch from April 12 to 21 was one of the league's bad weeks. Since then the Loons are 13-7, with a five-game winning streak from May 5 to 10, and on Sunday in Portland they won a 12-11 game in 10 innings. Next come Cleveland at home, three in Mexico City and Nashville at home, which suits a team that has been this good at home.
