The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Minnesota Was Picked to Lose 85 Games and Leads the ABL in Scoring

Alexander ranked the Loons 18th and called the middle of their lineup "extremely weak." They score more runs than anyone in the ABL, they're 15-5 at home, and Chipper Jones has recovered from his slow start.

Minnesota Loons · 21-19 · 250 runs scored, 240 allowed

Alexander's predicted standings had Minnesota at 66-85, one of the worst records in the ABL. His power rankings put the Loons 18th. He graded the offense C-/D+: strong at the corners with Chipper Jones and David Ortiz, but "the middle of the field is extremely weak." The pitching got a D+, and the team personalities report gave the club a "selfish" tag.

After 40 games, Minnesota is 21-19 and has scored more runs than any team in the ABL. It ranks second in home runs and OPS. It's sixth in the Western Conference, five games behind Southside, with a +10 run differential.

The middle of the field

The positions Alexander worried about have been fine. Second baseman Keith Ginter has a .513 slugging percentage and 30 RBI. Eight of those came in one game, a 4-for-4 day against Anaheim on April 9 that the roundup said included "a grand slam and multiple run-scoring doubles." Shortstop Earl Snyder has seven home runs and a 108 OPS+. Shea Hillenbrand, the catcher Alexander called "critical," is hitting .306 with a 95 OPS+. Center fielder Rickey Henderson, 43, is the exception. He's hitting .237, though his 25 walks give him a .368 on-base percentage and he has scored 34 runs, the most on the team.

The corners have been as good as expected. Ortiz is hitting .331/.388/.595 with 37 RBI. He drove in eight with two homers in the 16-5 win over New Orleans on April 17 and six more on April 30. In the April 21 trends piece, Alexander wrote that Jones "has zero homers and a 50 wRC+ start" and that Henderson was hitting .152. Since then Jones has hit five home runs and raised his OPS+ to 112. Juan Rivera, one of the two young outfielders Alexander named, is hitting .342 and went 5-for-5 in an 18-3 win over New York. Brian Simmons, whom the trends piece listed with Ortiz and Hillenbrand as the only hitters producing, has a 134 OPS+. On May 9 he homered twice and drove in six as Minnesota came back from four runs down to beat Cincinnati.

The floor

Alexander said the pitching had "a high floor in the bullpen." It hasn't so far. The bullpen ERA is 6.23. Brad Voyles is at 8.22, Mike Garcia 8.84 and Brian Shouse 8.03. Mike Koplove has been the exception, as the trends piece noted, with a 3.43 ERA and no walks in 21 innings. Aaron Fultz has nine saves. The rotation has been better than expected, with a 4.81 ERA. Ryan Rupe, who had a 6.59 ERA in 2001, has a 2.98 ERA, and he threw 6.1 shutout innings against Anaheim on April 8. Darryl Kile, whom the trends piece said the club had extended on a four-year deal, has a 5.56 ERA and a 4.62 FIP.

Home and away

Minnesota is 15-5 at home and 6-14 on the road. Its three walk-off wins have all come at home, two of them in extra innings: 14 innings against Anaheim on April 10 and 13 against New Orleans on April 16. It swept Anaheim, New York and Cincinnati at home. It was swept in Kansas City, where it was outscored 31-7, and at Southside.

The trends piece listed Minnesota among the ABL's "bad weeks" after a 2-8 stretch from April 12 to 21. Since then the Loons are 13-7, including a five-game winning streak from May 5 to 10. On Sunday in Portland they won a 12-11 game in 10 innings. Next they have three at home against Cleveland, three in Mexico City and three at home against Nashville.
