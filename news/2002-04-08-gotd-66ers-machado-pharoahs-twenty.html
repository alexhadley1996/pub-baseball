FBL · 2002-04-08 · By Colin Mercer, Ball Theory

Twenty Innings in Memphis

Memphis led Inland Empire 2-0 going to the ninth, and then the 66ers tied it and the game kept going for eleven more innings. Robert Machado, who hadn't homered all season, broke it with a solo shot in the 20th.

MEMPHIS, Tenn. — On Monday night the Inland Empire 66ers and Memphis Pharoahs played 20 innings, the equivalent of two full games and two extra innings besides. Between the 10th and the 19th, a stretch of 10 innings, nobody scored. Twelve pitchers combined to throw 535 pitches.

Then Robert Machado, the 66ers’ catcher, who had not homered all season and had caught every one of those pitches from the Inland Empire side, hit one over the left-field wall with one out in the 20th, and the 66ers won 3-2.

“I’d have caught 30,” Machado said. “I’m just glad I didn’t have to.”

The numbers

This is a numbers column, so let’s start there. Memphis came into the game averaging 7.1 runs a game, with Lou Merloni hitting .517 and Greg Norton already at five homers. Over 20 innings the Pharoahs scored two runs. Norton went 1-for-8 with three strikeouts. Merloni drove in one of the two.

Inland Empire, whose lineup the preseason rankings described as having a “sinkhole” up the middle, wasn’t much better for most of the night. Carl Everett went 1-for-9.

What happened, in other words, was pitching, for a long time, on both sides.

Nine innings

Erik Bedard, Memphis’s 23-year-old left-hander, walked five in six innings but allowed only two hits and no runs. Omar Daal gave up two runs in 7⅔ innings for the 66ers, one in the third and one on Merloni’s two-out single in the fifth. Memphis led 2-0 going into the ninth.

Felix Rodriguez came on to close it. The 66ers loaded the bases with nobody out, and Mark Ellis walked to force in a run. Rodriguez got the next two outs, and then Brandon Berger, the designated hitter, singled in the tying run.

“I needed one more out,” Rodriguez said. “I’m sorry. Then I sat and watched for three more hours, so I got punished.”

Eleven more

The extra innings belonged to two pitchers nobody would have predicted. For Memphis, Rich Rodriguez, the 39-year-old left-hander, threw 5⅔ scoreless innings, the longest outing of his career in relief. For Inland Empire, Joe Roa went 4⅔ innings and allowed one hit.

Scott Sullivan and Mike Timlin held the line in between. Antonio Alfonseca threw three scoreless innings for Memphis. Aaron Heilman, a 23-year-old rookie, threw 3⅓ scoreless at the end for the 66ers and got the win.

“Every guy who came in,” 66ers manager Tim Bloomfield said, “I told him, ‘Throw strikes, let them hit it at somebody.’ And then they did. For about five hours.”

Machado

Tom Davey, who’d come on in the 18th, retired the first man he faced in the 20th. Machado, 3-for-9 by the end of the night, got a sinker that didn’t sink.

Juan Acevedo pitched a perfect bottom of the 20th for the save. The game took nearly six hours, and maybe a third of the 18,424 fans were still there when it ended.

Inland Empire is 5-3. Memphis is 4-4 and plays again Tuesday night.

“I’m going to sleep in the clubhouse,” Norton said. “It saves time.”
