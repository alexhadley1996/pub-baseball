MLB · 2002-04-15 · By Frank DeLuca, New York Evening Star

Maddux Gets Knocked Around, and the Yankees Blow Eight

The Yankees led last-place Arizona 8-0 in the fifth with Greg Maddux on the mound. Arizona scored two in the fifth and six in the seventh, its bullpen threw eight scoreless innings, and Erubiel Durazo's two-out double in the 12th won it 9-8.

PHOENIX — The Arizona Diamondbacks came into Monday night 4-10, with two wins in their last 10 games, and their manager, Bob Brenly, had spent the afternoon answering questions about whether he’d still have a job in May. By the fifth inning they were losing to the Yankees 8-0 and Greg Maddux was on the mound.

They won 9-8 in 12 innings.

“I’ve never been part of something like that,” Brenly said. “I’ve never been on the losing side of it either, and I’m sure I will be someday, but tonight I don’t want to think about it.”

Eight runs

The Yankees spent the first five innings making Tom Gordon’s night miserable. Chan Perry’s sacrifice fly opened the scoring in the third, and Torii Hunter followed with a three-run homer. In the fifth, Jose Vizcaino, Hunter and Fernando Seguignol singled in runs, and Bengie Molina added a sacrifice fly. Gordon didn’t get an out in the fifth and was charged with eight runs.

Hunter, who has quietly become the most reliable hitter in a lineup with Barry Bonds in it, finished with four RBI.

Maddux, meanwhile, was coasting. He gave up two runs in the fifth, on Durazo’s single and Matt Lawton’s sacrifice fly, and nobody thought much of it.

The seventh

Maddux came out for the seventh with an 8-2 lead and didn’t get an out. Jay Woolf doubled. Raul Gonzalez singled. Maddux walked Durazo to load the bases, then walked Lawton to force in a run, and Robin Ventura doubled in two more. It was 8-5, and Joe Torre came to get him.

“I walked two guys in a row with a six-run lead,” Maddux said, in a tone that suggested he would be thinking about it for some time. “I don’t do that. I did it tonight.”

It got worse. Chad Bradford came on and gave up a run-scoring groundout to Shane Spencer, a single to Chris Stynes and a two-run single to Enrique Wilson. 8-8.

“Greg’s going to be fine,” Torre said. “He’s Greg Maddux. But I’d like to have that inning back. All of us would.”

The bullpen nobody talks about

Then Arizona’s bullpen did something it hasn’t done much this season, which was hold the other team down. Anthony Telford, Jose Paniagua, Hipolito Pichardo, Paxton Crawford, Dave Veres, Dan Plesac and Felix Heredia combined for eight scoreless innings. The Yankees, with Bonds and Hunter and everybody else, didn’t score after the fifth.

Heredia, making his debut, faced one batter in the 12th. He struck out Hunter.

“I was nervous,” Heredia said. “Then I saw it was the guy who hit the home run, and I was more nervous. Then he struck out, and I wasn’t nervous anymore.”

Durazo

Cliff Politte got the first two outs in the bottom of the 12th. Gonzalez, who finished with four hits, singled. Durazo doubled into the gap in left-center, and Gonzalez came all the way around from first.

“I saw the third base coach waving and I thought, he’s crazy,” Gonzalez said. “Then I was home.”

Heredia, one batter into his career, has a win. Maddux, who was 2-1 with a 3.13 ERA coming in, did not get a decision. His ERA is now 4.65.

The Yankees are 7-8. They have Bonds, Maddux and a great deal of money, and they’re under .500 in the middle of April. Torre was asked whether that bothered him.

“Ask me in July,” he said.
