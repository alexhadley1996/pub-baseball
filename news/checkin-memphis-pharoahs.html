The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Memphis Was Supposed to Be Bad. It's Close to .500, and It Walks Everybody.

Alexander predicted 63-88 for the Pharoahs. They're 19-21 with a +10 run differential and a lineup that ranks fifth in the FBL in scoring. Their pitchers have also walked more batters than any staff in the league.

Memphis Pharoahs · 19-21 · 220 runs scored, 210 allowed

Of all 20 FBL teams, Memphis got Alexander's worst predicted record in March: 63-88. He ranked the Pharoahs 13th, graded the pitching D+ and described the rotation as "particularly weak with only Erik Bedard showing any promise." His personalities report did list Memphis among the three "healthiest clubhouses" in all three leagues.

Forty games in, Memphis is 19-21, sixth in the East Group and 5.5 games out. It has outscored opponents 220-210. That run differential suggests a team about two games better, but the Pharoahs are 3-7 in one-run games. The site projects about 74 wins.

A lineup that hits

Alexander called it a "very young offense with a surprising degree of experience" and said Memphis would look for Aaron Rowand, Luis Castillo, Geoff Jenkins, Ramon Hernandez and Greg Norton to produce "around league average." Most of them have been better than that. The team ranks fifth in the FBL in runs per game. Rowand has a 119 OPS+ and Jenkins a 126. Jenkins's two-out, three-run home run beat Inland Empire on a walk-off on April 9, the night after Memphis lost to the 66ers in 20 innings. The roundup credited "Josh" Jenkins.

Norton's season has been odd. He homered in five straight games from April 2 to 6. The April 6 roundup noted he was at "5 HR in 6 games." He hasn't homered since, though he's still at .302/.360/.470. Castillo has a 73 OPS+ but has stolen 13 bases in 15 attempts for a team that's second in the FBL in steals.

The two best hitters were mostly afterthoughts in March. Lou Merloni, a 31-year-old shortstop, is hitting .356/.393/.562 with 32 RBI and a 145 OPS+. On April 4 he went 4-for-5 with three doubles and a homer against Carolina. Adrian Gonzalez, who Alexander said "could be great in a few years but they'll have to wait a bit," is hitting .336/.391/.496 with a 129 OPS+ at 20. Memphis may not have to wait.

The walks

The pitching has been about what Alexander predicted. Memphis pitchers have walked 186 batters, 18 more than any other FBL staff, and the team ERA is 4.70. Bedard, the one prospect he liked, is 3-0 with a 3.95 ERA. He also has 29 walks in 43.1 innings, more than any other Memphis starter. The April 3 roundup described him as "solid but wild." Wilson Alvarez has walked 25 batters. He also threw eight shutout innings against Oklahoma City on April 17, one of the starts the week-three notes highlighted. Shawn Chacon is 1-4 with a 5.62 ERA. Ryan Drese, at 3.65, has been the most effective starter.

Felix Rodriguez is "pretty good but isolated on this staff," Alexander wrote. He has seven saves and a 3.27 ERA. The bullpen around him has blown eight saves, and Britt Reames has blown four of them. On April 8 Rodriguez gave up the ninth-inning tying run in the 20-inning loss to Inland Empire.

Home and road

Memphis is 13-7 at home and 6-14 on the road. Lately they've been winning both places. The Pharoahs won four straight from May 8 to 11, a home sweep of Savannah followed by a win in Richmond. On Monday they beat Richmond 12-8 in 10 innings. Next comes a home series against Orlando, followed by three games in Hartford and a home series against San Antonio. A 21.7% playoff chance isn't great, but it's better than what anyone expected from the team picked to finish last.
