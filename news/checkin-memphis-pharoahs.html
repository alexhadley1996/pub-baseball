40-Game Check-In · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Pharoahs and the Map Problem

Memphis Pharoahs, 19-21 through 40 games

Some teams are built for their park; some just happen to be good there. Whatever the Pharoahs are, they have been far better at home: 13-7 in their own park, 6-14 in everyone else's.

Through 40 games they were 19-21, scoring 220 runs and allowing 210 (+10). As of the May 13 export they are 6th in the East Group, 5½ games back, and 13th of 20 in the FBL. That run differential would usually produce a record around 21-19. They are 3-7 in one-run games and 1-2 in extra innings.

What is driving it

The best bat has belonged to Lou Merloni, 31, who is hitting .356/.393/.562 with 5 home runs and 32 RBI in 175 plate appearances. On the bases, Luis Castillo is 13-for-15 stealing. As a team they are hitting .284/.347/.440, 8th of 20 in the FBL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 38 home runs.

In the rotation, Ryan Drese has been the steadiest: 3-3 with a 3.65 ERA, 23 strikeouts and 22 walks in 44.1 innings. Erik Bedard (3.95 ERA in 43.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.70 ERA, 11th of 20 in the FBL; starters 4.67, relievers 4.75. Closing has mostly meant Felix Rodriguez: 7 saves, 3.27 ERA.

That .956 OPS is also the surprise. In 2001, per the export, Lou Merloni managed .703 over 159 plate appearances, which is a long way from here. The weakest regular bat has been Julio Zuleta, at .221/.281/.356 in 167 plate appearances.

The Pharoahs have played ten games decided by one run. They are 3-7 in them. Nobody is quite sure how much of that is skill, which is what makes it the most interesting thing about a 19-21 team.

The fine print is fun here. Luis Castillo has walked 23 times and struck out 21, a ratio that belongs to a different, more patient century.

The stakes

The FBL is the bottom rung and nobody gets relegated from it; the whole point is the way up. The top seven make a ladder playoff, seeds one and two are promoted outright, and the winner of the Federal Promotion round makes three. They are 13th of 20 in the table, 2½ games behind the Larks for the last ladder place.

The best night was Apr 19, a 10-2 win over the Bats. The worst was May 12, an 11-1 loss to the Flying Squirrels. Their best 10-game stretch went 6-4 (Apr 14 to Apr 23); their worst, 3-7 (Apr 5 to Apr 15).

Looking ahead

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 7 of them at home, against the Juicers, the Yard Goats, the Rebeldes, the Athletics, opponents with a combined 185-212 record (.466). That is a chance to bank wins. The record matches the runs, so there is no regression bill coming and no hidden credit either. The next 122 games will mostly be about whether Lou Merloni keeps doing this. In six weeks the Pharoahs have shown us who they might be. The next six will show us who they are willing to keep being.
