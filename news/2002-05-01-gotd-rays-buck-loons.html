ABL · 2002-05-01 · By Nate Fischer, Ball Theory

John Buck's Long Night

Minnesota erased a 6-1 deficit and led Tampa Bay 10-6. Tampa Bay sent 10 men up in the eighth to tie it, and John Buck, a 21-year-old catcher who had already homered twice, doubled home the winner in the 11th.

Here’s a fun exercise. Take the win probability chart from Wednesday’s Loons-Rays game, print it out, and hand it to someone who has never seen a baseball game. Ask them what sport it is. My guess is they’ll say “seismology.”

Tampa Bay led 6-1 after five. Minnesota led 10-6 after seven and a half. It was 10-10 after eight. And then, for two and a half innings, nothing, until a 21-year-old catcher named John Buck hit a double into the gap with one out in the 11th, and the Rays won 11-10.

Buck had four hits: a homer in the second, a homer in the eighth, a single in the ninth and the game-winning double. He came in hitting .217.

“I’m a catcher,” Buck said. “We’re supposed to be the ones who don’t hit. Nobody told me tonight.”

The first swing

The Rays built their 6-1 lead mostly off Joe Blanton, Minnesota’s 21-year-old starter, who allowed six runs in four innings. After Buck’s solo homer in the second, Garrett Atkins and Juan Pierre doubled in runs in the third, and Chase Utley, another of the Rays’ 20-somethings who are supposed to be the future, hit a two-run homer. Pierre added another run on a groundout in the fifth.

Then Kevin Beirne happened. The Rays’ reliever came in with a 14.85 ERA, and in the sixth and seventh the Loons scored seven runs, most of them on his watch. David DeJesus, who had homered in the third, doubled in two. Aaron Miles singled in two more. David Ortiz grounded in the tying run. In the seventh Brian Simmons and Earl Snyder singled in runs to make it 8-6. In the eighth Chipper Jones singled, one of his four hits, and Ortiz homered off Grant Balfour. Minnesota led 10-6.

Our in-house model put Minnesota’s chances at that point a little over 95 percent. I mention this only so you can laugh at our in-house model.

The second swing

Ramon Santiago, the Rays’ 22-year-old second baseman, homered to lead off the bottom of the eighth. Buck followed with another. Mike Koplove came on, and the Rays kept coming: Chad Hermansen doubled, Ivanon Coffie singled, Alex Sanchez grounded in a run, Atkins singled and Pierre singled to tie it.

Ten Rays came to the plate in the inning. The last of them was Santiago again, with the bases loaded and a chance to finish what he’d started, and he grounded out.

“I hit the first one and I made the last out,” Santiago said. “I’ll take one of two.”

The quiet part

After that the bullpens took over. Tampa Bay’s Rafael Betancourt threw two hitless innings in the 10th and 11th. Balfour, who had given up Ortiz’s homer, retired the side in the ninth with two strikeouts. Minnesota’s Aaron Fultz got through the 10th.

In the 11th, Fultz got J.J. Davis on a ground ball. Santiago singled. And Buck, on the 22nd pitch Fultz threw, doubled, and Santiago came all the way around from first.

Betancourt got the win. Fultz took the loss, ending a three-game winning streak for the Loons, who are 14-15. The Rays are 13-16 and have the youngest lineup in the ABL, which means more nights like this. Some of them will even go the other way.

“Kids,” Rays manager Hal McRae said, shaking his head, though he was smiling when he did it. “They don’t know when it’s over. Nobody tells them.”
