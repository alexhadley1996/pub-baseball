The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Gothams Never Do Anything Halfway, Except Their Batting Line

Brooklyn has had five streaks of four games or more and is 23-18, two games out. In between, a manager-GM argument about Bobby Higginson's glove, and a zone rating that makes the argument more interesting.

Brooklyn Gothams · 23-18 · 194 runs scored, 179 allowed

Here is the Brooklyn Gothams' season as a string of letters: LLL WWWW LLLL WWWW LLLL, a little noise, WWWWWWW, more noise, WWW and an L. They lost their first three, then won four, lost four, won four and lost four, as if someone had set a metronome in the clubhouse. From April 27 to May 3 they won seven straight, sweeping the Brewers at home and the Griffins in Dallas. Then they went to Miami, which had the best record in the East, and won 6-3, 8-0 and 7-1. Nobody in the league runs hotter or colder, and it adds up to 23-18, two games behind the Marlins.

Middle of the road

For a team that never does anything halfway, the batting line is awfully average. The Gothams are 10th in MLB in OPS and sixth in runs per game, and in almost every team stat they land right in the middle. Nobody on the roster has an OPS+ above 121. Derrek Lee and Fernando Tatis share that mark, and Tatis, the third baseman, has been mashing. The surprise is Jack Wilson, a .550 OPS hitter last year, who is hitting .278 and leads the team with 1.2 WAR. He and Alex Cora give Brooklyn the one thing it does better than almost anyone, infield defense.

Catcher has been the problem since Tom Lampkin strained his hip, an injury expected to cost him about four months. Yorvit Torrealba, his replacement, has a 55 OPS+.

The Higginson question

The best argument in the organization is about right field. Bobby Higginson is hitting .298 with a .459 slugging percentage and has started only nine games in the field, because manager Matt Swanson keeps him at DH. GM Tom Smith has demanded that Higginson play the outfield more. Swanson says he trusts Angel Echevarria's glove, and Echevarria has started 37 games in right. He's hitting .213, and his zone rating is -3.4 runs, worse than anything Higginson has done in his small samples out there. A left-handed hitter like Higginson should be a star at Shea. Whatever Swanson sees in Echevarria's defense, the numbers don't see it.

One ace, a lot of luck

Kevin Millwood is the real thing. He has a 2.76 ERA, 45 strikeouts against 10 walks and 1.8 WAR. On April 5 he threw eight shutout innings in St. Louis to hand the Cardinals their first loss, and ten days later he threw eight more against the Phillies. Behind him, the rotation has been highly fortunate. Mike Smith has a 2.68 ERA despite walking 27 and striking out 26, and Erik Hiljus has a 2.66 ERA in eight starts without a single decision. Jason Isringhausen, the star rookie closer of the preseason rankings, has nine saves and a 1.50 ERA.

The trouble is getting the ball to him. Chris George, Ken Pumphrey, Jayson Durocher and Elmer Dessens have pitched plenty of high-leverage innings with a combined 133 FIP-, which is just terrible. Pumphrey once gave up six runs while getting one out in Houston.

Sixteen innings

That bullpen was part of the best game of the Gothams' season. On May 5 they and the Red Sox played 16 innings at Shea. Brooklyn trailed by five at one point and won 11-10 in the bottom of the 16th, with Ben Petrick driving in three runs. The next day the Gothams went to extras again and beat Detroit 6-5 in 11, with Petrick going 4-for-6. Those were two of their four walk-off wins.

From here

In MLB only the division winner advances, to a best-of-nine World Series, and last place drops to the ABL. Brooklyn is two behind Miami and one behind Detroit, and the site gives it a 17.0% chance at the East with about 80 projected wins, close to the preseason projection of 81-70. The preseason consensus had them 14th, and the clubhouse came into the year with a reputation as unmotivated, so they're already ahead of schedule. They're probably playing over their heads, too, and a fall back to mid-table could come soon. Their last game was a 14-2 loss in Seattle. With this team, that's either the start of the slide or the start of another losing streak that ends in another winning streak. Give it four days.
