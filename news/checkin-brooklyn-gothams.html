Gothams at 40: The Run That Made the Record

Brooklyn Gothams, 23-17 through 40 games

For seven straight games, from Apr 27 to May 3, the Gothams did not lose. Every season has a stretch that it will be remembered for, and the Gothams may have already had theirs.

Fernando Tatis has been the Gothams hitter opponents least want to see: .280/.370/.470, 5 homers, 18 driven in. Bobby Higginson leads the club with 8 home runs. As a team they are hitting .261/.321/.421, 11th of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 47 home runs.

The rotation's anchor is Erik Hiljus: 8 starts, 44.0 innings, 2.66 ERA. Mike Smith (2.68 ERA in 47.0 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 3.65 ERA, 3rd of 20 in MLB; starters 3.34, relievers 4.24. Closing has mostly meant Jason Isringhausen: 9 saves, 1.50 ERA.

Through 40 games they were 23-17, scoring 192 runs and allowing 165 (+27). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 23-18, 3rd in the East Division, 2 games back, and 6th of 20 in MLB. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 23 wins in 40 games. They are 7-6 in one-run games and 3-2 in extra innings.

Then there is Jack Wilson, whose OPS has gone from .550 in 2001 to .760 now. Some of that is the sample. The question is how much. The disappointment, at least so far, is Moises Alou: a .949 OPS in 2001, .777 through 164 plate appearances this year.

The fine print is fun here. Home crowds have averaged 25,378 over 20 dates in a park that holds 57,405, about 44% full. There is room to be convinced.

The best night was Apr 6, a 10-1 win over the Cardinals. The worst was May 7, an 11-5 loss to the Wolverines. Their best 10-game stretch went 9-1 (Apr 27 to May 6); their worst, 3-7 (Apr 16 to Apr 25).

MLB has no wild card and no consolation prize: division winners play for the title, the last-place club in each division goes down to the ABL. The Gothams are 2 games behind the Marlins in the East Division. The floor is 9 games below them, where the Braves currently stand on relegation pace.

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 4 of them at home, against the Mariners, the Yankees, the Athletics, the Cherry Blossoms, opponents with a combined 230-180 record (.561). That is a real test. One more thing to watch: Fernando Tatis is on the milestone watch, closing on 100 home runs.
