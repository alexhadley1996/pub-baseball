The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Gothams Never Do Anything Halfway, Except Their Batting Line

Brooklyn has had five streaks of four games or more and is 23-18, two games out. In between, a manager-GM argument about Bobby Higginson's glove, and an Echevarria zone rating that makes the argument more interesting.

Brooklyn Gothams · 23-18 · 194 runs scored, 179 allowed

Here is the Brooklyn Gothams' season as a string of letters: LLL WWWW LLLL WWWW LLLL, then a little noise, then WWWWWWW, more noise, WWW and an L. Alexander called them "the league's ultimate streaky team" in his 40-game notes. They lost their first three games, then won four, lost four, won four and lost four, as if someone had set a metronome. From April 27 to May 3 they won seven straight, including three-game sweeps of the Brewers at home and the Griffins in Dallas. Then they went to Miami, which had the best record in the East, and won three games 6-3, 8-0 and 7-1. The season so far has been sudden rallies and sudden collapses, with very little in between, and it adds up to 23-18, two games behind Miami.

Average, on purpose or otherwise

As a team, the Gothams are hitting .260/.319/.421, which is 10th in MLB in OPS, and they are sixth in runs per game. Alexander's line was that "in almost all stats as a team they're middle of the road," and the numbers back him up. Nobody on the roster has an OPS+ above 121. Derrek Lee and Fernando Tatis share that top mark. Tatis, the third baseman, is the one Alexander says has "been mashing." Last year Jack Wilson hit .550 in OPS. This year he is at .278/.318/.451 and leads the team in WAR at 1.2. Alexander singled out the infield defense of Wilson and Alex Cora as the one place this team stands out. The catching has been the problem since Tom Lampkin's hip strain, which Alexander's injury report expects to keep him out about four months. Yorvit Torrealba has a 55 OPS+, just as Alexander said.

The Higginson question

This is the most entertaining argument in the organization. Bobby Higginson is hitting .298/.350/.459, but he has started only nine games in the field, because manager Matt Swanson keeps him at DH. Alexander reports that GM Tom Smith has "demanded" Higginson play the outfield more often. Swanson's answer is that he trusts Angel Echevarria's defense more. Echevarria has started 37 games in right field. He is hitting .213/.291/.326, and the site's zone rating has him at -3.4 runs, which is worse than any of Higginson's small samples in the field. Higginson, Alexander wrote, "should be a left-handed star" at Shea. Swanson's reason for keeping him out of the field doesn't show up in Echevarria's zone rating.

Pitching: one ace and a lot of good luck

Kevin Millwood is the real thing: 2.76 ERA, 2.93 FIP, 45 strikeouts against 10 walks and 1.8 WAR. On April 5 he threw eight shutout innings in St. Louis and handed the Cardinals their first loss, which Alexander's roundup recorded, and he threw eight more against the Phillies ten days later. Alexander calls the rest of the rotation "highly fortunate," and the numbers agree. Mike Smith has a 2.68 ERA and has walked 27 batters while striking out 26. Erik Hiljus has a 2.66 ERA in eight starts and no decisions at all. Jason Isringhausen, the "star rookie closer" of Alexander's preseason rankings, has nine saves and a 1.50 ERA. The middle relief is where Brooklyn loses games. Alexander singled out Chris George, Ken Pumphrey, Jayson Durocher and Elmer Dessens as high-leverage arms with "a 133 FIP-. Just terrible." Pumphrey once gave up six runs while recording one out in Houston.

The game of the year, maybe

On May 5 the Gothams and Red Sox played 16 innings at Shea. Brooklyn trailed by five runs at one point and won 11-10 in the bottom of the 16th. Ben Petrick went 2-for-8 with three RBI and added more win probability than anyone else on the Brooklyn side. The next day Brooklyn won again in extra innings, 6-5 over Detroit in 11, with Petrick going 4-for-6. Those were two of their four walk-off wins.

From here

In MLB only the division winner advances, to a best-of-nine World Series, and last place drops to the ABL. Brooklyn is two games behind Miami and one behind Detroit. The site gives the Gothams a 17.0% chance to win the East and projects about 80 wins, close to Alexander's March prediction of 81-70. He ranked them 14th in his preseason power rankings, and the personality report flagged the clubhouse as "unmotivated," so they are already ahead of him in one sense. His May outlook says they are "probably playing in over their heads" and predicts they'll come "crashing down to earth (or mid-table) pretty soon." The export ends with a 14-2 loss in Seattle, which is either the start of that or the start of another losing streak that ends in another winning streak. With this team, you just wait four days and see.
