MLB · 2002-05-01 · By Frank DeLuca, New York Evening Star

Shawn Green Hits for the Cycle, and Then Some

Shawn Green came into Wednesday hitting .229. He went 5-for-5 against the Yankees at Wrigley Field, with a triple, a grand slam, a single, a double and another single, the season's first cycle. The Cubs won 15-1.

CHICAGO — Shawn Green spent most of April looking at video. He watched his swing from the first week, when he was hitting everything, and his swing from the last two weeks, when he wasn’t, and he couldn’t find the difference.

“Everybody had a theory,” Green said Wednesday night. “My hands. My front foot. My head. My wife thought it was my haircut. I tried everything except the haircut.”

He came into the Cubs’ game against the Yankees hitting .229. He left it hitting .264, after going 5-for-5 with a triple, a grand slam, a single, a double and another single. It was the first cycle of the season in MLB, the ABL or the FBL, and the Cubs won 15-1.

“Maybe it was the haircut,” Green said. “I didn’t get one. Maybe that was it.”

The triple and the slam

The Yankees went ahead in the first, on Barry Bonds’ 11th homer of the season. In the bottom of the inning, with two outs and a man on second, Green lined a ball into the right-field corner off Ariel Prieto. It kicked off the bricks and away from the right fielder, and Green, who isn’t fast, chugged into third standing up.

“I don’t hit a lot of triples,” he said. “When I do, I’m out of breath for an inning.”

In the third, with the bases loaded and one out, Prieto threw him a fastball on the inside half. Green, a left-handed hitter, turned on it and hit it onto Sheffield Avenue. It was his eighth homer of the season.

“I’ve been late on that pitch for two weeks,” he said. “Tonight I was on time. That’s the whole thing. Hitting is timing. When it’s off, it’s off by a hundredth of a second, and you can’t see it on video, and it drives you crazy.”

The single, then the double

The Yankees changed pitchers. It didn’t help. Green singled to center off Jack Cressend in the fifth. In the sixth, against Lou Pote, he doubled into the gap in left-center to drive in a run and complete the cycle.

He didn’t know. Nobody in the Cubs’ dugout told him, out of superstition or because they’d lost track. The Wrigley scoreboard did, and when it flashed the news, the 32,852 fans stood and cheered for long enough that Green, standing at second base, eventually tipped his helmet.

“I thought they were cheering because we were up 10,” he said. “Then Todd [Helton] told me. I said, ‘Are you sure?’ He said, ‘I can count, Shawn.’”

One more

In the eighth Green came up a fifth time and singled off Nelson Cruz. He was 5-for-5 with six RBI and three runs scored.

He also never struck out, which, after the month he’d had, was a point of pride.

“Four weeks of strikeouts,” he said. “Four weeks of guys looking at me funny in the dugout. Tonight I didn’t swing and miss once. I’ll take that over the cycle.”

The rest of it

It wasn’t all Green. Miguel Cairo hit a bases-clearing double in the fifth, Orlando Palmeiro hit a three-run homer in the eighth, and Brett Myers, the 21-year-old, allowed a run in 5⅓ innings. Ricky Bottalico got a save by finishing the last 3⅔ innings, which is a save only a rulebook could love.

For the Yankees it was an ugly night in a strange season. They’re 14-16, with Bonds, Maddux and a payroll that most teams can only dream about.

“We got beat by a great hitter having a great night,” Joe Torre said. “And then we got beat by everybody else, too.”

The Cubs are 16-14. Green, back in the clubhouse, was asked whether he’d keep the bat.

“I’m going to keep using it,” he said. “It’s got some hits in it.”
