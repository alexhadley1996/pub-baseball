ABL · 2002-05-12 · By Ned Calloway, Diamond Daily

Austin Kearns Saves the Single for Last

Austin Kearns, 21, tripled in the first, doubled in the third, homered in the fourth and singled in the sixth to hit for the cycle against Austin. It was the second cycle of the season, 11 days after Shawn Green's. The Angels won 9-0.

ANAHEIM, Calif. — Austin Kearns has been told, more than once this spring, that he’s going to be a star. He’s 21, he’s 6-foot-3, and he hits the ball as hard as anyone in the Angels’ lineup. What he hadn’t done, until Sunday afternoon, was have the kind of game that makes people say it out loud.

Against the Austin Violets, he tripled in the first inning, doubled in the third, homered in the fourth and singled in the sixth. That’s a cycle, the second this season in MLB, the ABL or the FBL, and he did it the hard way: triple first, single last. The Angels won 9-0 in front of 37,009 on Mother’s Day.

“My mom was here,” Kearns said. “She came out from Kentucky. She doesn’t come to many games. I’m going to have to buy her a plane ticket for every Sunday now.”

The hard part first

He started with the hardest part. In the first inning, with a runner on first and one out, Kearns hit a line drive off Jason Middlebrook into the right-center gap, and by the time the Violets got the ball back to the infield, he was at third. It drove in the first run of the game.

In the third, he doubled into the left-field corner to drive in another. In the fourth, with a man on and Nate Teut pitching, he hit a two-run homer to left-center. Three innings, three hits, and the only thing left was the easiest one.

“Everybody kept saying, ‘You need a single,’” Kearns said. “And I’m thinking, I hit the ball hard. My singles are the hardest thing for me. They go to people.”

The single

In the sixth he came up with a runner on first and one out. He took a fastball for a strike. Then he hit a ground ball up the middle, past the shortstop’s glove, into center field.

“I’ve never been happier to hit a groundball single in my life,” he said. “I looked at it rolling out there and thought, that’s the prettiest one I’ve ever hit.”

The crowd, which had been counting along since the fourth, stood up. Angels manager Mike Scioscia, who doesn’t do this kind of thing, came to the top step of the dugout and clapped.

“He’s a special kid,” Scioscia said. “We’ve known it since spring training. Now everybody knows it.”

The fifth trip

In the eighth inning Kearns came up for a fifth time, and Austin reliever Steve Schrenk hit him with a pitch.

“I don’t think it was on purpose,” Kearns said, smiling. “I’m going to say it wasn’t on purpose.”

Asked about it, Violets manager Tony Sanchez said, “The kid had four hits. I wasn’t going to give him five.”

Green

The only other cycle this season belongs to Shawn Green, who did it for the Cubs against the Yankees on May 1. Kearns was asked if he’d been aware of that.

“I watched the highlight,” he said. “Everybody watched it. I didn’t think I’d be next. I figured it’d be somebody like him, a guy who’s been doing it for 10 years.”

He finished 4-for-4 with three runs scored and four RBI. Jose Valentin hit a two-run homer, and Seth Greisinger, who’d been having a rough season, threw eight shutout innings.

The Angels are 21-18. Kearns is hitting .312.

“My mom says I should’ve done it sooner,” he said. “She’s from Kentucky. She’s very honest.”
