The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Richmond Was Projected to Win the East. It Has the FBL's Best Hitter and a Losing Record.

The preseason projections had the Flying Squirrels winning the East with 921 runs. Greg Colbrunn leads the league in home runs and RBI, but the rest of the offense and the rotation haven't kept up, and Richmond is 19-21.

Richmond Flying Squirrels · 19-21 · 192 runs scored, 208 allowed

The preseason projections had Richmond winning the FBL East at 88-63, with a league-best 921 runs. The power rankings were cooler, putting the Flying Squirrels 11th, with a C+ for an experienced, solid infield and a weak outfield, and a D+ for a staff with little experience but a relatively high floor. So far the rankings look closer.

Richmond is 19-21, seventh in the East Group and 5.5 games behind Louisville, and it ranks 14th in the FBL in runs per game. Only a few teams have fallen further from their preseason projection: 88 wins in March, 73 now. The site gives the Flying Squirrels a 16.6% chance at the playoffs.

Colbrunn

None of that is Greg Colbrunn's fault. The 32-year-old catcher is hitting .385 and slugging .777. He leads the FBL with 13 home runs and 42 RBI and has a 205 OPS+ and 2.3 WAR. On April 6 he went 3-for-3 with a double and two RBI as Richmond handed Hartford its first loss. Doug Mientkiewicz is hitting .354 with three four-hit games, including a 4-for-4, three-double day on May 4. Reggie Taylor, with eight homers, and Todd Zeile have also hit.

Not many others have. Bret Boone, a .950 OPS hitter in 2001, is at .248 with an 83 OPS+, though he did have the best single night of anyone. On April 7 he hit two homers, the second a two-run walk-off off Tim Spooneybarger in a 10-9 win over Hartford. The weak outfield has been weak. Eric Owens has a 79 OPS+ and one homer, and Corey Patterson has a 79 and a .287 on-base percentage. Eric Hinske is hitting .184. Shortstop Michael Barrett is at 68.

Three seasons in one

Richmond started 9-1, from April 3 to 13. It won 1-0 in seven innings in Orlando behind six shutout innings from Jeremi Gonzalez, and it won in San Antonio on Mike Lowell's ninth-inning homer. Then it lost seven straight. The third week was a brutal one, 1-7, with sweeps by Hoboken and Sacramento and an offense that stopped producing. An 8-5 win in Carolina ended the streak, a sign of life, and it was: Richmond went 9-5 over its next 14.

Then came five straight losses from May 6 to 11, including a sweep in Louisville and Friday's 11-0 loss. The Flying Squirrels are 3-7 in their last 10. Monday's 12-8 loss to Memphis came in 10 innings, after closer Antonio Osuna gave up five runs in the 10th. Osuna has 10 saves, four blown saves, an 0-3 record and a 9.60 ERA.

The staff

The rotation's ERA is 5.15. Brian Anderson has been reliable, with a 3.28 ERA and only 11 walks, and on May 5 he went 8.2 scoreless innings against Oklahoma City. Carl Pavano is 0-3 with a 6.66 ERA, though his 3.92 FIP suggests bad luck. Steve Woodard is 1-5 at 8.08. Adam Bernero and Aaron Small have pitched well in middle relief, with ERAs of 2.48 and 2.35, only to watch Osuna give it back at the end.

A -16 run differential fits a team around 18-22, so the record isn't misleading, and an 8-4 mark in one-run games is the only thing keeping it from being worse. Next come three in Albuquerque, Eugene at home and three in Monterrey against the FBL's best team.
