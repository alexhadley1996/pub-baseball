The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Richmond Was Alexander's East Favorite. It Has the FBL's Best Hitter and a Losing Record.

The March standings had the Flying Squirrels winning the East with 921 runs. Greg Colbrunn leads the league in home runs and RBI, but the rest of the offense and the rotation haven't kept up, and Richmond is 19-21.

Richmond Flying Squirrels · 19-21 · 192 runs scored, 208 allowed

Alexander's predicted standings picked Richmond to win the FBL East at 88-63, scoring "a league-best 921 runs," and to be promoted. The power rankings he published the same day were less excited: 11th, with a C+ for "an experienced, solid infield, but a weak outfield" and a D+ for a staff with "little experience but a relatively high floor." So far the rankings look closer.

The Flying Squirrels are 19-21, seventh in the East Group and 5.5 games behind Louisville. They rank 14th in the FBL in runs per game. The site's one-month review listed them among the largest drops on the board: 88 predicted wins, 73 projected now. They have a 16.6% chance at the playoffs.

Colbrunn

None of that is Greg Colbrunn's fault. The 32-year-old catcher is hitting .385/.430/.777. He leads the FBL with 13 home runs and 42 RBI, and has a 205 OPS+ and 2.3 WAR. The April 6 roundup had him going "3-for-3, 2 RBI, double" as Richmond "handed Hartford its first loss." Doug Mientkiewicz is hitting .354 with a 134 OPS+. He's had three 4-hit games, including a 4-for-4 with three doubles on May 4. Reggie Taylor (129 OPS+, eight homers) and Todd Zeile (125) have also hit.

The rest of the lineup hasn't. Bret Boone had a .950 OPS in 2001. He's at .248/.319/.386 now, an 83 OPS+. He did have the best night of anyone, on April 7: two home runs, including a two-run walk-off homer off Tim Spooneybarger in a 10-9 win over Hartford. The outfield Alexander called weak has been weak. Eric Owens has a 79 OPS+ and one home run. Corey Patterson has a 79 and a .287 on-base percentage. Eric Hinske has hit .184 with a 40 OPS+. Shortstop Michael Barrett is at 68.

Three seasons in one

Richmond started 9-1 from April 3 to 13. During that stretch it won 1-0 in seven innings in Orlando, the game the roundup described as "Jeremi González threw six shutout," and won in San Antonio on a ninth-inning Mike Lowell home run. Then it lost seven straight. The week-three notes called it "a brutal week, going 1-7 and getting swept by both Hoboken and Sacramento. The core issue has been a lack of offensive production." They also called the 8-5 win in Carolina that ended the streak "a sign of life." It was. Richmond went 9-5 over its next 14 games.

Then came a five-game losing streak from May 6 to 11, including a sweep in Louisville and an 11-0 loss on Friday. The Flying Squirrels are 3-7 in their last 10. Monday's 12-8 loss in 10 innings to Memphis came after closer Antonio Osuna allowed five runs in the 10th. Osuna has 10 saves, four blown saves, an 0-3 record and a 9.60 ERA.

The staff

The rotation's ERA is 5.15. Brian Anderson has been reliable with a 3.28 ERA and only 11 walks. On May 5 he went 8.2 scoreless innings against Oklahoma City. Carl Pavano is 0-3 with a 6.66 ERA, though his 3.92 FIP suggests some bad luck. Steve Woodard is 1-5 with an 8.08 ERA. Adam Bernero (2.48) and Aaron Small (2.35) have pitched well in middle relief, but Osuna's struggles at the end of games have undone some of that work.

A -16 run differential fits a team around 18-22, so Richmond's record isn't misleading. Its 8-4 record in one-run games is the only reason it isn't worse. Next is a three-game series in Albuquerque, then Eugene at home, then three games in Monterrey against the FBL's best team.
