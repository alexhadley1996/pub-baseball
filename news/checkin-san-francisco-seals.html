Seals at 40: The Run That Made the Record

San Francisco Seals, 25-15 through 40 games

The Seals season, so far, is one long exhale. A 7-game winning streak (Apr 7 to Apr 14) sits in the middle of it, and everything else is context.

Andres Galarraga has been the Seals hitter opponents least want to see: .326/.396/.504, 5 homers, 21 driven in. Tony Batista leads the club with 9 home runs. As a team they are hitting .272/.324/.437, 6th of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 46 home runs.

In the rotation, Dave Burba has been the steadiest: 3-1 with a 3.20 ERA, 41 strikeouts and 13 walks in 50.2 innings. Jake Peavy (3.35 ERA in 53.2 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 3.42 ERA, 1st of 20 in MLB; starters 3.88, relievers 2.44. Garrett Stephenson has 4 saves and 1 blown.

Through 40 games they were 25-15, scoring 192 runs and allowing 146 (+46). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 26-15, 2nd in the West Division, tied for first, and 2nd of 20 in MLB. By the old Pythagorean arithmetic, that is a 25-win team so far. They are 10-9 in one-run games and 0-2 in extra innings.

The surprise might be Adrian Brown, who arrived without a 2001 top-tier line worth the name (under 150 plate appearances) and is hitting .317/.359/.446. The weakest regular bat has been Andres Torres, at .240/.263/.354 in 101 plate appearances.

Odds and ends: When they score three runs or fewer they are 4-8; when they score six or more, 11-3. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks. Jake Peavy has 63 strikeouts in 53.2 innings, more than one an inning and then some.

The best night was Apr 4, a 14-2 win over the Wolverines. The worst was Apr 19, an 11-6 loss to the Cherry Blossoms. Their best 10-game stretch went 8-2 (Apr 3 to Apr 12); their worst, 3-7 (Apr 19 to Apr 29).

MLB has no wild card and no consolation prize: division winners play for the title, the last-place club in each division goes down to the ABL. The Seals are tied with the Athletics atop the West Division; only one of them gets to go to the World Series. The floor is 9 games below them, where the Dodgers currently stand on relegation pace.

The next stretch is 10 games (5 at home) against the Cubs, the Rockies, the Astros, the Brewers. Those clubs are 204-206 combined, a .498 winning percentage, so it will mostly be up to them. One more thing to watch: Andres Galarraga is on the milestone watch, closing on 400 home runs.

Forty games is when baseball stops being a rumor and starts being a record. The Seals have one now. It is theirs to keep or to fix.
