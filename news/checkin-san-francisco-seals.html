The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Same Record as Oakland, 19 Fewer Runs Allowed: The Seals Are Built to Last

San Francisco has MLB's best run prevention, six shutouts and a bullpen ERA of 2.39. It is tied with Oakland because the coin flips went the other way.

San Francisco Seals · 26-15 · 194 runs scored, 146 allowed

Here is a fact that should bother Oakland a little. The Athletics and the San Francisco Seals are both 26-15, and both have scored exactly 194 runs. The Seals have allowed 146 and the A's 165. That's how two teams with the same record get there in different ways. Oakland is 10-5 in one-run games and 6-1 in extra innings. San Francisco is 10-9 and 0-2. One of these teams has been lucky and the other one hasn't. Alexander made the same point more bluntly in his 40-game notes: the Seals have been "arguably the all-around best team in the MLB so far, despite their half game deficit to the A's." (The deficit has since closed.)

Run prevention, in several flavors

The Seals have thrown six shutouts and have not been shut out once. Their 3.33 team ERA leads MLB. The bullpen has a 2.39 ERA in 116.2 innings, and four relievers have ERAs under 2.25: Julio Mateo (1.54), Scot Shields (1.59, with 19 strikeouts and two walks), David Weathers (1.76) and Garrett Stephenson (2.20, five saves). Alexander's explanation was defense, plus "an extremely pitcher-friendly park." He pointed out that the Seals had hit 46 home runs and allowed 28, and both numbers still hold.

The rotation is younger than the bullpen. Jake Peavy is 20, has struck out 63 in 53.2 innings, and has a 2.61 FIP. On May 12 in Philadelphia he allowed one hit in seven innings. Miguel Cabrera went 0-for-4 that day, which ended the on-base streak Alexander had been tracking. Carlos Zambrano, also 20, has 50 strikeouts and 27 walks, the kind of line that makes a pitching coach both happy and nervous. The best starter, though, is 35. Dave Burba had a 6.21 ERA in 2001. This year he has a 2.83 ERA, a 2.65 FIP and 1.9 WAR, the most on the team. He has also thrown six shutout innings in a 1-0 win at Yankee Stadium. In March Alexander projected this group as a rotation that "could be the best in the league." So far he looks right.

An offense that doesn't walk

The preseason power rankings gave the Seals offense a C, citing "a real lack of on base skills." That has held up too. The Seals have walked 107 times, and their .323 team OBP is ordinary. They score anyway, sixth in MLB in runs per game, because, as Alexander put it in his week-three notes, they are "hitting everything." Andres Galarraga is 40 and hitting .326/.395/.500 with a team-best 142 OPS+. Tony Batista has nine home runs. Jimmy Rollins, who had a .743 OPS last year, is at .281/.347/.425 with 1.3 WAR. Benji Gil is hitting .225 with a .255 OBP and still has 0.9 WAR, which is what defense is worth; Alexander called him "an absolute beast defensively." The lineup has also done this mostly without Ruben Sierra, who has 24 plate appearances and, according to Alexander's injury report, a fractured ankle that will keep him out four to five months.

The shape of it

The season has had three phases. Early: a seven-game winning streak from April 7 to 14 that included a three-game sweep at Yankee Stadium (5-3, 8-6, 1-0). Then a 3-7 slide from April 19 to 29. The low point was in Phoenix on April 28, when the Seals lost a six-run lead and then the game, 8-7, on a walk-off, the middle loss of a three-game sweep by the Diamondbacks. Since then: 10-3, with sweeps of St. Louis and the Dodgers, a 6-0 win in Philadelphia and a 2-0 win over the Cubs on May 13.

Alexander's March numbers had the Seals at 75-76, seventh in the West. By May he had moved them all the way to "early favorites to win the West and play for the series." He described a front office, owner Jose Contreras and GM Alex Reyes, that wants pitching and defense first, and the team on the field is exactly that.

From here

There's no wild card in MLB. Win the division and you play a best-of-nine World Series; finish last and you drop to the ABL. The Seals are nine games clear of last place and level with Oakland at the top. The site's simulations make them a slight favorite over the A's, 46.3% to win the West, with about 87 projected wins. Alexander flagged this week as a test, with the Cubs at home, three games at Coors Field and then the Astros. The Cubs and Astros are the two clubs he called "the preseason favorites." Coors will test the pitching; Houston, which just outscored Oakland 32-9 in three games, will test everything.
