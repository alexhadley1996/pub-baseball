The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Same Record as Oakland, 19 Fewer Runs Allowed: The Seals Are Built to Last

San Francisco has MLB's best run prevention, six shutouts and a bullpen ERA of 2.39. It's tied with Oakland because the coin flips went the other way.

San Francisco Seals · 26-15 · 194 runs scored, 146 allowed

Here's a number that should bother Oakland a little. The A's and the San Francisco Seals are both 26-15, and both have scored exactly 194 runs. The Seals have allowed 146. The A's have allowed 165. The difference is in the close games: Oakland is 10-5 in one-run games and 6-1 in extra innings, and San Francisco is 10-9 and 0-2. By most measures the Seals have been the best all-around team in MLB, and they've finally caught up in the standings too.

Pitching and defense

The Seals have thrown six shutouts and haven't been shut out once. Their 3.33 team ERA leads MLB, and the bullpen has been even better, at 2.39 over 116.2 innings. Four relievers are under 2.25: Julio Mateo, Scot Shields (19 strikeouts, two walks), David Weathers and Garrett Stephenson, who has five saves. The defense is a big part of it, and so is an extremely pitcher-friendly park. San Francisco has hit 46 home runs and allowed 28.

The rotation is a mix of very young and fairly old. Jake Peavy is 20 and has 63 strikeouts in 53.2 innings. On May 12 in Philadelphia he allowed one hit in seven innings, and Miguel Cabrera's 0-for-4 that day ended his on-base streak. Carlos Zambrano, also 20, has 50 strikeouts and 27 walks, the kind of line that makes a pitching coach happy and nervous at once. The best of them is 35. Dave Burba had a 6.21 ERA in 2001; this year he's at 2.83 with a team-high 1.9 WAR, and he threw six shutout innings in a 1-0 win at Yankee Stadium. The preseason rankings said this rotation could be the best in the league. So far it has been.

An offense that swings

The offense got a C in the preseason rankings for "a real lack of on base skills," and that has held. The Seals have drawn only 107 walks, and their .323 team on-base percentage is ordinary. They're sixth in MLB in scoring anyway, because they've been hitting everything. Andres Galarraga, 40, has a team-best 142 OPS+. Tony Batista has nine home runs. Jimmy Rollins has 1.3 WAR, and Benji Gil, an absolute beast with the glove, has 0.9 WAR despite a .255 on-base percentage. They've done it mostly without Ruben Sierra, who has a fractured ankle that will keep him out four to five months.

Three phases

The season has come in three acts. First a seven-game winning streak from April 7 to 14, including a three-game sweep at Yankee Stadium. Then a 3-7 slide from April 19 to 29, which bottomed out in Phoenix on April 28, when the Seals lost a six-run lead and then lost 8-7 on a walk-off in the middle of a Diamondbacks sweep. Since then they're 10-3, with sweeps of St. Louis and the Dodgers and a 2-0 win over the Cubs on Monday.

The preseason projections had the Seals at 75-76, seventh in the West. By May they had become the early favorites to win the division. The front office, owner Jose Contreras and GM Alex Reyes, wanted pitching and defense first, and that's exactly the team on the field.

From here

There's no wild card in MLB. Win the division and you play a best-of-nine World Series; finish last and you drop to the ABL. The Seals are nine games clear of last place and level with Oakland, and the site's simulations make them a slight favorite, 46.3% to win the West with about 87 projected wins. This week is a real test: the Cubs at home, three games at Coors Field, then the Astros. The Cubs and Astros were the preseason favorites. Coors will test the pitching, and Houston, which just outscored Oakland 32-9, will test everything.
