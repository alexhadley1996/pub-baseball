The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

New Orleans Lost Its Stars and Is Still at .500

Two of the three stars Alexander named are playing in Oakland, and two of the best on-base hitters are hurt. New Orleans is 20-20 and on playoff pace, mostly because of close wins.

New Orleans Athletics · 20-20 · 231 runs scored, 244 allowed

Alexander's preseason question about New Orleans was whether the stars at the top of "an extremely top-heavy lineup" could carry the team. He named Brian Giles, Corey Koskie and Raul Mondesi as those stars and called the rest "veritable scrubs." He answered his own question: "It seems unlikely." He ranked New Orleans 16th, graded the pitching D+, and concluded that "this staff's weaknesses are, however, going to be too much to overcome."

Six weeks later, Giles is in Oakland. Koskie went there too, traded on April 8 for second baseman Brian Roberts. He had been hitting .469 in 35 plate appearances. The May 12 injury report said New Orleans was "the club the injury list has picked on," after Eric Young and Bernie Williams, "two of its three best on-base bats," both got hurt. And New Orleans is 20-20, third in the Eastern Conference and three games out of first. That's in a playoff spot, and the site gives it a 35.5% chance of making the postseason.

Who's left

Mondesi, the one star still here, is hitting .325/.377/.525 and leads the team with 36 runs. Nick Swisher, 21, has been the best hitter on the roster, with a .403 on-base percentage, 35 RBI and a 126 OPS+. On Friday in Salt Lake he hit two home runs and drove in six, though New Orleans lost 17-9. Roberts debuted with a bang. The April 10 roundup said he "exploded" in a 19-8 win in Las Vegas, and on April 12 he went 3-for-4 with four RBI. His season OPS+ is 52, the same as shortstop Tim Bogar's. As a team, New Orleans ranks 19th of 20 in home runs and 17th in OPS. It still ranks eighth in runs per game.

Close games

The April 21 trends piece said the pitching staff had been "the defining strength, led by Kerry Wood and Casey Fossum." That was true for the first couple of days. Wood threw eight scoreless innings in a 1-0 opening-day win at Tampa Bay ("a minimalist win," the roundup said). The next day Fossum allowed no earned runs in eight innings and lost 1-0. Since then, the staff has played closer to Alexander's grade. The ERA is 5.89, 19th in the ABL, and the rotation is at 6.27. Denny Lail is 0-3 with an 8.05 ERA. Roberto Ramirez has allowed seven home runs in 14.2 innings. Of the three relievers Alexander said "had great 2001's," Ray King has 10 saves and a 2.33 ERA. Danys Baez has a 6.75 ERA, and Chad Fox a 7.11 ERA with three blown saves.

The record holds up anyway because New Orleans has won games it had no business winning. On April 6 it scored six in the seventh to beat San Juan 6-5 after trailing by five. On April 12 it trailed New York by seven. According to the roundup, it put up a seven-run seventh, the Knights answered, and Eric Young tied the game with a two-run single before New Orleans won 12-11 in the 10th. The trends piece later called that game "the chaos." New Orleans is 7-6 in one-run games and 3-2 in extra innings. It has two 1-0 wins, including one in Cleveland on April 29.

Lately

Its run differential is -13, which suggests about 19 wins, so the .500 record is only slightly lucky. New Orleans lost four straight from May 9 to 12, then beat Baltimore 11-2 on Monday. Next come three in San Diego, three at home against Austin and three in Montreal.
