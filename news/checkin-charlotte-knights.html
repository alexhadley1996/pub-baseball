40-Game Check-In · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

One Run at a Time in Charlotte

Charlotte Knights, 13-27 through 40 games

Close games are where baseball goes to flip coins, and the Knights have flipped a lot of them: 11 one-run games in their first 40, and they are 2-9 in them.

Through 40 games they were 13-27, scoring 161 runs and allowing 195 (-34). As of the May 13 export they are 10th in the East Group, 11½ games back, and 20th of 20 in the FBL. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 17 wins in 40 games. They are 2-9 in one-run games and 0-4 in extra innings.

What is driving it

Their most productive hitter is Edgardo Alfonzo (3B, 28), at .286/.347/.500 with 7 HR. On the bases, Willie Harris is 15-for-17 stealing. As a team they are hitting .257/.320/.396, 17th of 20 in the FBL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 37 home runs.

The rotation's anchor is Eric Milton: 8 starts, 56.1 innings, 3.36 ERA. Curt Schilling (3.61 ERA in 62.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.71 ERA, 12th of 20 in the FBL; starters 4.33, relievers 5.73. Closing has mostly meant Justin Speier: 6 saves, 5.87 ERA.

Nobody should have expected Travis Fryman to be hitting .292/.346/.451. He posted a .662 OPS in 370 plate appearances in 2001. Forty games is enough to notice and not enough to be sure. The weakest regular bat has been Mario Valdez, at .188/.300/.282 in 100 plate appearances. In the rotation, Brian Rose has a 7.64 ERA over 35.1 innings.

There was a stretch, Apr 18 to Apr 26, when the Knights lost eight straight. Seasons recover from that sort of thing all the time, but they do not forget it, and neither does the standings page.

A couple of small, true things. Curt Schilling has 74 strikeouts in 62.1 innings, more than one an inning and then some. Home crowds have averaged 18,806 over 22 dates in a park that holds 42,000, about 45% full. There is room to be convinced.

The stakes

The FBL is the bottom rung and nobody gets relegated from it; the whole point is the way up. The top seven make a ladder playoff, seeds one and two are promoted outright, and the winner of the Federal Promotion round makes three. They are 20th of 20 in the table, 8½ games behind the Larks for the last ladder place.

The best night was Apr 2, a 12-3 win over the Twisters. The worst was May 13, a 14-1 loss to the Solons. Their best 10-game stretch went 5-5 (Apr 1 to Apr 10); their worst, 1-9 (Apr 15 to Apr 25).

Looking ahead

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 3 of them at home, against the Zephyrs, the Disco Turkeys, the Gallos, the 66ers, opponents with a combined 190-204 record (.482). Neither a gift nor an ambush. The hopeful read is the run differential: if runs keep coming at this rate, the wins tend to follow, and the Knights are about 4 wins short of where that differential usually lands. None of this is settled, which is the point of May. But the Knights have given us enough to argue about, and that is all you can ask of 40 games.
