The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Curt Schilling Is Pitching Like a Cy Young Pick. Charlotte Is 3-6 When He Starts.

Alexander gave the Knights a D- for offense and a B for pitching. Both grades have held up, and that combination has left Charlotte 13-27, last in the FBL.

Charlotte Knights · 13-27 · 161 runs scored, 195 allowed

Few March grades have held up as well as Charlotte's. Alexander gave the offense a D-: "Edgardo Alfonzo is the only current talent and after that, its Grady Sizemore or bust." He gave the pitching a B: "Curt Schilling, Eric Milton, and Gil Meche make this one of the strongest and deepest FBL rotations." He also picked Schilling for the FBL Cy Young.

All of that has been true, and the Knights are 13-27, the worst record in the FBL and 11.5 games out in the East Group. The rotation has a 4.33 ERA. The lineup has scored 161 runs, and only three FBL teams score less per game.

Schilling

Schilling, 35, has a 3.61 ERA, 74 strikeouts and 11 walks in 62.1 innings. Only one FBL pitcher has more strikeouts, and he has just one more. His record is 2-4, and Charlotte is 3-6 in his starts. The roundups show how that happens:

  • April 6: He struck out 12 against Memphis. That day's notes said the "only real damage [was] a 2-run HR by Greg Norton." Charlotte won 4-3 on a walk-off.
  • April 12: He went 8.2 innings with 10 strikeouts against Eugene and "still didn't get the win." The Larks scored four in the 10th off Justin Speier and won 7-3.
  • May 2: He threw an eight-inning complete game with 10 strikeouts and no walks in Hartford and lost 2-1.

Milton has been almost as good. He threw a complete game in Louisville on April 3, which the roundup noted as the Bats fell to 0-3. On May 5 he allowed one hit over eight innings against San Antonio. His ERA is 3.36. Todd Ritchie, not mentioned in March, has a 3.74 ERA and won Charlotte's only 1-0 game in Hartford on May 3. On May 11 Meche threw eight shutout innings against Sacramento. Brian Rose, the fifth starter, has a 7.64 ERA.

"Sizemore or bust"

For now, it's bust. Sizemore is 19, the youngest regular in the lineup, and leads the team with 176 plate appearances. He's hitting .237/.301/.381 with a 76 OPS+. Alfonzo is the sure thing he was supposed to be, with a .286/.347/.500 line, seven home runs and a 117 OPS+. Craig Monroe (111) and Travis Fryman (106) have been above average. Catcher Eddie Perez has a 60 OPS+ and no home runs. Mark Whiten, Willie Harris and Mario Valdez have split time in the outfield with OPS+ marks of 76, 72 and 53.

With that offense, the close games have gone badly. Charlotte is 2-9 in one-run games and 0-4 in extra innings, including back-to-back extra-inning walk-off losses in Louisville on April 4 and 5. The low point was an eight-game losing streak from April 18 to 26, all against Honolulu, Savannah and Richmond, in which the Knights never scored more than five runs. They're 6-16 at home.

Patience

Alexander's personalities report described Charlotte's clubhouse as "Selfish Tendencies" and a "results-driven culture; patience thin early in the season." The results so far are 13-27, a -34 run differential, a 0.2% playoff chance and a projection of about 63 wins. Monday's 14-1 loss to Sacramento, in which Omar Olivares allowed 10 runs in three innings of relief, ended a weekend that started with Meche's best start. Next come three games in Hoboken, three at home against Carolina and three in Santo Domingo.
