FBL · 2002-04-06 · By Ray Dalton, The Houston Gazette

Down Eight, and Still Unbeaten

Sacramento led Monterrey 8-0 in the third inning. The Sultanes chipped away, Alex Ochoa tied it with a two-out, three-run homer off Octavio Dotel in the ninth, and Augie Ojeda won it in the 10th. Monterrey is 6-0.

MONTERREY, Mexico — By the third inning on Saturday, the Sultanes trailed the Sacramento Solons 8-0, and a good part of the crowd of 25,935 at Estadio 5 de Septiembre had turned its attention to the sunset over the Cerro de la Silla.

They came back to the game in time. Monterrey scored in the fifth, sixth, seventh and eighth, tied it in the ninth on Alex Ochoa’s three-run homer, and won it 10-9 in the 10th on Augie Ojeda’s single. The Sultanes are 6-0, the only unbeaten team left in the FBL, and they did not have to play well for the first three innings to stay that way.

“We have a saying in this clubhouse now,” Craig Biggio said. “Nobody leaves. Not the players, not the fans. You stay until it’s over.”

The hole

Bronson Arroyo never found it. Sacramento sent nine men to the plate in the first: David Dellucci singled in two, Hiram Bocachica singled in another, and Jose Leon grounded out to make it 4-0. In the second Olmedo Saenz doubled in two more, and Royce Clayton added a sacrifice fly. Arroyo was gone after two innings, charged with eight runs after Aubrey Huff singled in the last of them off Alan Levine in the third.

Across the way the Solons had Dontrelle Willis, their 20-year-old left-hander, and with an eight-run cushion he was having a fine time, his leg kick getting higher, it seemed, by the inning.

“You’re down eight to a kid who’s smiling,” Jeff Bagwell said. “That’ll wake you up.”

The chip

Levine settled things down with four innings of one-run relief, and Todd Van Poppel followed with three scoreless. In the meantime Monterrey kept taking little pieces. Robb Quinlan doubled in a run in the fifth. Biggio, back behind the plate on Saturday, singled in two in the sixth. Dave Roberts grounded in one in the seventh, and Ochoa homered off Travis Harper in the eighth. Sacramento had added a run along the way, and it was 9-5 going to the ninth.

Ochoa

Travis Harper put two men on in the ninth, and Octavio Dotel, the hard-throwing former Astro, came on to finish it. He didn’t. A run scored when Dotel walked Roberts, and with two outs and two runners still on, Ochoa hit a fastball over the wall in left. 9-9.

It was Ochoa’s second homer of the game and his fourth hit. He drove in four.

“I was looking fastball,” Ochoa said. “Everybody knows Dotel throws hard. The question is if you can catch up to it. Tonight I was a little early, and a little early is perfect.”

Bagwell and Biggio, who played behind Dotel in Houston, were standing together at the dugout rail when the ball went out.

“I felt bad for about one second,” Biggio said. “Then I didn’t.”

Ojeda

Francisco Rodriguez, the 20-year-old who has closed out two games already this week, pitched a scoreless 10th. In the bottom half Dotel was still out there. Runners got to second and third with one out, and Ojeda lined a single into right.

“Augie is the best little hitter I ever saw,” manager Terry Booker said. “Everybody looks at Biggio and Bagwell. Augie does the little things.”

Sacramento, which had won three of its first five, is 3-3. Its manager, Steve Ward, did not have much to say.

“Eight runs is a lot of runs,” he said. “We didn’t think we’d need nine. Turns out we needed eleven.”
