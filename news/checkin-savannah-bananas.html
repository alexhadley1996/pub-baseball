The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Alexander Left Savannah's Preview Blank. The Bananas Have Filled It In.

The Bananas got a D, a C and no comments in March. After a 3-8 start, they're 22-18 and third in the East, with one of the FBL's best lineups and one of its worst pitching staffs.

Savannah Bananas · 22-18 · 224 runs scored, 219 allowed

Savannah is the one FBL team Alexander's March power rankings didn't say anything about. The Bananas got a D for offense and a C for pitching, the comment boxes were left empty, and they were ranked 17th. The predicted standings had them at 72-79.

The early results fit that. Savannah went 3-8 to start the season and lost four in a row twice. The worst loss came on April 3 in Hartford: the Bananas led 9-0 after two innings and lost 11-10. The roundup called it Hartford's "comeback of the season." Since April 13, though, Savannah is 19-10. At 22-18 it's third in the East Group, 2.5 games behind Louisville, with a 51.8% chance at the playoffs.

The hitting

The D for offense hasn't held up. Savannah ranks third in the FBL in runs per game and fourth in OPS, and it hasn't been shut out. Garret Anderson is hitting .327/.366/.538 with 41 RBI, second in the league behind Richmond's Greg Colbrunn. Bill Mueller, whom the week-three notes credited with "steady work," has a .404 on-base percentage and a 128 OPS+. Jorge Cantu, a 20-year-old first baseman, is hitting .302 with a 122 OPS+. He drove in five on opening day. José Vidro hit his fourth home run in five games on April 5, according to that day's roundup, and has a 119 OPS+. Backup catcher Mitch Meluskey has a 171 OPS+ in 50 plate appearances. The week-three notes called him "Mitch Milewski" in describing his two-homer game in the 12-7 win over Carolina on April 20.

That game was part of an eight-game winning streak from April 18 to 26, a three-game home sweep of Carolina followed by sweeps in Charlotte and two wins in Inland Empire. The first two Carolina wins came from four runs down and on a walk-off.

The pitching

The C for pitching has been generous. Savannah's 5.16 team ERA is 19th of 20, and its staff has walked 168 batters, second-most in the FBL. Barry Zito won on opening day, going 7.1 innings and allowing one earned run, and at three weeks Alexander called him "off to a solid start (3.51 ERA)." He's at 3.79 now, but with 33 walks in 54.2 innings and a 5.02 FIP. Hideo Nomo has 54 strikeouts in 48 innings and a 5-2 record. Robert Ramsay is 1-5 with a 9.91 ERA and has allowed 10 home runs in 26.1 innings.

The same week-three notes said "Steve Kline still hasn't allowed a run in his first nine innings." He has nine saves and no blown saves, but on May 3 in Oklahoma City he allowed seven runs while getting two outs. His ERA is now 3.57. Gabe White has been the best reliever, with a 2.19 ERA in 24.2 innings and no home runs allowed.

Next

Savannah has outscored opponents by only five runs, 224-219, so its record is a couple of games better than its run differential suggests. Most of that comes from home games, where the Bananas are 14-5. They're 8-13 on the road. They just swept Albuquerque at home and now play Eugene twice at home and once on the road. Then first-place Monterrey comes in for three games before a trip to Honolulu.
