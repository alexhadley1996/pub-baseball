ABL at 40 Games: Everyone Is 23-17

Five clubs sit at 23-17, Southside has pulled away in the West, Portland refuses to be relegated on schedule, and the site's first odds say the Eastern Conference belongs to Cleveland and Montreal.

Forty games into the American Baseball League season, the most common record in the league is 23-17. Kansas City, Portland, San Diego, Cleveland and Montreal all own it. They did not get there the same way, which is the fun part.

Western Conference: Southside, then a pileup

The Southside Black Sox are 26-14, three games clear of the 23-17 trio behind them, and they are doing it with run prevention: first in the ABL in ERA and first in FIP, plus first in stolen bases (59). Brad Radke has made nine starts with a 3.77 ERA and six walks. Six. Jorge Posada (.326/.416/.594, OPS+ 147) and Ivan Rodriguez (.386/.411/.571) have given the lineup a pair of catchers' bats that most clubs would take at any position. Alexander had Southside at 77-74 in his preseason standings, but he also had them in his promotion picks, so he is allowed to feel good about this.

Behind them, the three 23-17 clubs tell three different stories. San Diego has the best run differential in the conference (246 scored, 186 allowed, a Pythagorean record of 25-15), which means the Padres have, if anything, been unlucky to be merely tied. John Smoltz is 7-0 with a 2.63 ERA and 72 strikeouts in 68.1 innings. Kansas City is the opposite case: 245 runs scored and 242 allowed, which is the run differential of a .500 team wearing a 23-17 record. Luis Gonzalez (.342/.429/.610) and Larry Walker (.346/.444/.566) are hitting, and the pitching ranks 15th in ERA.

And then there is Portland. Alexander's predicted standings had the Beavers last in the conference at 64-87, with the note that they would "probably still get dragged toward relegation." They are 23-17 with a +29 run differential, fifth in the league in runs per game, and the bullpen ranks first in ERA. Ross Gload is slugging .648. Rafael Furcal has 1.5 WAR. The site's projection still regresses them hard toward .500, but it gives them a 46% chance at a playoff spot and only a 3.6% chance of going down. A relegation pick at 3.6% is the kind of thing you write down so you can be wrong about it in public later. We are writing it down.

Eastern Conference: two clubs at the top, five at .500 or worse

Cleveland and Montreal are both 23-17 and both in the top half of the bracket on our odds (79% and 74% to make the playoffs). They are not similar teams. The Spiders hit: fourth in runs per game, third in home runs, with Tino Martinez at .416/.456/.714 and 12 homers in 171 plate appearances, which is the kind of slash line that makes you check whether the decimal points are in the right place. Montreal pitches and is 17th in runs per game; Derek Lowe has a 2.47 ERA in 65.2 innings, and Craig Counsell (.389/.465/.550, 2.5 WAR) has quietly been one of the most valuable players in the league.

Below them, New Orleans, Pittsburgh and San Juan are all 20-20. Pittsburgh's case is interesting: fourth in FIP, 13th in ERA. Joel Pineiro is 1-7 with a 6.27 ERA, and Tomo Ohka has a 5.96 ERA with a 3.76 FIP and seven walks in 48.1 innings. If you believe in FIP, the Pirates' staff has been much better than its results; if you are a Pirates fan, you are tired of hearing that.

The relegation end

The ABL sends three clubs down, split by conference to match where the promoted FBL clubs come from, so the races at the bottom depend on what happens a tier below. Today the West carries most of the risk: Austin (18-22, 55.5% to be relegated), Mexico City (18-22, 44.5%) and Salt Lake (19-21, 33.0%). Salt Lake might be the strangest team in the league: Brad Fullmer is hitting .379/.458/.683 with 11 homers, and the Bees rank 20th of 20 in ERA and 19th in FIP. In the East, the New York Knights (15-25) lead the watch at 32.5%, despite Lance Berkman's .405/.485/.709, because their staff ranks 17th in ERA and 18th in FIP.

Alexander's relegation picks were Portland, Tampa and the Knights. One of those three is in real danger.

Odds are the site's own Monte Carlo projection (10,000 simulations of the remaining schedule); see the Playoffs page for the method and the full table.
