ABL at 40 Games: Everyone Is 23-17

Five clubs sit at 23-17, Southside has pulled away in the West, Portland refuses to be relegated on schedule, and the site's first odds say the Eastern Conference belongs to Cleveland and Montreal.

Forty games into the ABL season, the most common record in the league is 23-17. Kansas City, Portland, San Diego, Cleveland and Montreal all have it, and no two of them got there the same way.

Western Conference: Southside, then a pileup

The Southside Black Sox are the exception, at 26-14 and three games clear of the pack. They've done it by preventing runs. Southside leads the ABL in ERA and FIP and, for good measure, in stolen bases, with 59. Brad Radke has made nine starts with a 3.77 ERA and has walked six batters all year. Jorge Posada and Ivan Rodriguez, hitting .326 and .386, give the Black Sox two catchers' bats that most clubs would take at any position. The preseason projections had Southside at 77-74. The preseason consensus had it second, which looks a lot closer.

The three 23-17 clubs behind them tell three different stories. San Diego has the best run differential in the league, 246 scored and 186 allowed, which works out to a Pythagorean record of 25-15, so the Padres have, if anything, been unlucky to be merely tied. John Smoltz is 7-0 with a 2.63 ERA and 72 strikeouts in 68.1 innings. Kansas City is the opposite case: 245 scored and 242 allowed, a .500 team's run differential wearing a 23-17 record. Luis Gonzalez and Larry Walker are both hitting around .345, and the pitching ranks 15th in ERA, so most nights are a slugfest.

And then there's Portland. The preseason projections had the Beavers last in the conference at 64-87, squarely in the relegation fight. Instead they're 23-17 with a +29 run differential, fifth in the league in scoring, with the best bullpen ERA in the ABL. Ross Gload is slugging .648, and Rafael Furcal has 1.5 WAR. The site's projection still pulls them hard toward .500, but it gives them a 46% chance at the playoffs and only a 3.6% chance of going down.

Eastern Conference: two at the top

The East has a clearer shape. Cleveland and Montreal are both 23-17, and the site gives them 79% and 74% chances at the playoffs. They aren't similar teams. The Spiders hit, fourth in runs per game and third in home runs, and Tino Martinez is batting .416 with 12 homers in 171 plate appearances, the kind of line that makes you check whether the decimal point is in the right place. Montreal pitches. It's 17th in scoring, but Derek Lowe has a 2.47 ERA in 65.2 innings, and Craig Counsell, hitting .389 with 2.5 WAR, has quietly been one of the most valuable players in the league.

Below them, New Orleans, Pittsburgh and San Juan are all 20-20. Pittsburgh is the interesting one, fourth in FIP and 13th in ERA. Joel Pineiro is 1-7 with a 6.27 ERA, and Tomo Ohka has a 5.96 ERA despite a 3.76 FIP and seven walks in 48.1 innings. If you believe in FIP, the Pirates' staff has been much better than its results. If you're a Pirates fan, you're tired of hearing it.

The relegation end

The ABL sends three clubs down, split by conference to match where the promoted FBL clubs come from, so the races at the bottom depend partly on what happens a tier below. For now the West carries most of the risk. Austin, at 18-22, has a 55.5% chance of relegation, Mexico City, also 18-22, has 44.5%, and Salt Lake, 19-21, has 33.0%. Salt Lake might be the strangest team in the league. Brad Fullmer is hitting .379 with 11 homers, and the Bees rank 20th of 20 in ERA. In the East, the New York Knights, at 15-25, are the most likely to go down, at 32.5%. Lance Berkman is hitting .405, and it hasn't mattered, because the staff ranks 17th in ERA and 18th in FIP.

Portland, projected last in the West, has climbed out of the picture. Tampa Bay, projected last in the East, is at 15.9%. The Knights, projected to share first in the East, are in real danger.

Odds are the site's own Monte Carlo projection (10,000 simulations of the remaining schedule); see the Playoffs page for the method and the full table.
