FBL at 40 Games: Monterrey Did Not Read the Preview

Alexander picked Monterrey ninth in the West Group. They're 25-13. Alaska's pitching is historically stingy and its offense historically absent, and the top two seeds, who go straight up, may already be a two-team race.

The Federal Baseball League rewards you for finishing first or second in a way no other tier does: the top two seeds are promoted outright, and the five clubs below them play a ladder for one more spot. So the most important question at the 40-game mark is who is in the top two. Right now it is two West Group teams that Alexander's preseason standings had in the bottom half.

Monterrey and Sacramento

The Monterrey Sultanes were predicted to go 70-81, ninth of ten in the West Group. They are 25-13, the best record in the league, with an .860 team OPS and a bullpen ERA of 2.51. Jeff Bagwell (.348/.453/.652, OPS+ 183) and Alex Ochoa (.358/.429/.669, 11 homers) are the middle of a lineup that has scored 226 runs. The rotation has a 5.56 ERA, which makes it a strange team to be leading anything: Monterrey wins by getting a lead to Francisco Rodriguez (12 saves, 18 strikeouts and one walk in 15.2 innings) and Mike Remlinger (19 strikeouts, one walk in 13 innings).

Sacramento (25-15) is built the other way. The rotation ERA is 3.46 and the bullpen's is 4.94. Brandon Knight has a 1.96 ERA, though his 27 walks against 36 strikeouts suggest the number is doing some heavy lifting. Aubrey Huff is hitting .410/.450/.660. The Solons were predicted at 75-76.

The site gives Monterrey a 56.5% chance at a top-two seed and Sacramento 45.3%. Nobody else is above 27%.

The East Group: Louisville, with asterisks

Louisville is 24-15 and leads the East, but scored 215 and allowed 206, a Pythagorean record of 20-19. Roger Clemens has a 4.40 ERA, a 3.35 FIP and 75 strikeouts in 61.1 innings, so if anything, the pitching should get better; the record is another matter. Hartford (22-17) has the best offense in the league by runs per game (6.23), with Gary Sheffield at .336/.412/.638 and Randy Winn at .346/.404/.603, and a pitching staff that has allowed 221. Alexander predicted 87-64 for the Yard Goats; they are on roughly that pace.

Richmond, his preseason East favorite at 88-63, is 19-21. It is not Greg Colbrunn's fault: he is hitting .385/.430/.777 with 13 home runs, an OPS+ of 205. Carl Pavano is 0-3 with a 6.66 ERA and a 3.92 FIP, which is the most Richmond-in-May line on the staff.

Alaska, the 151-run team

Alexander's predicted favorite, the Alaska Athletics (94-57 in his standings), are 20-20. They have scored 151 runs, 19th of 20, and allowed 155, the fewest in the league. Matt Morris has a 1.83 ERA in 68.2 innings and Jeff Suppan a 2.11 ERA. The lineup's best hitter by OPS+ is Rontrez Johnson at 121. A team that gives up 3.9 runs a game should not be a .500 team, but a team that scores 3.8 a game should not be either, and Alaska has found the precise point where those two things cancel out.

The bottom

Charlotte is 13-27, the worst record in the league, with Curt Schilling (2.1 WAR, 74 strikeouts, 11 walks) as close to blameless as a pitcher on a 13-27 team can be. The Knights are fifth in FIP and 17th in runs per game. Orlando (16-24) and Inland Empire (16-24) are next; Inland Empire leads the FBL in FIP and has a 16-24 record, which is a very specific kind of frustrating.
