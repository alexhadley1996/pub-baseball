FBL at 40 Games: Monterrey Is Ahead of Schedule

The preseason projections had Monterrey ninth in the West Group. They're 25-13. Alaska's pitching is historically stingy and its offense historically absent, and the top two seeds, who go straight up, may already be a two-team race.

The FBL rewards a top-two finish like no other tier: the top two seeds go straight up to the ABL, and the five clubs below them play a ladder for one more spot. So the question that matters most at 40 games is who's in the top two. Right now it's two West Group teams the preseason projections had in the bottom half.

Monterrey and Sacramento

The Monterrey Sultanes were projected to go 70-81, ninth of 10 in the West. They're 25-13, the best record in the league, with an .860 team OPS and a 2.51 bullpen ERA. Jeff Bagwell, with a 183 OPS+, and Alex Ochoa, with 11 homers, anchor a lineup that has scored 226 runs. The rotation, though, has a 5.56 ERA, which makes Monterrey a strange team to be leading anything. It wins by getting a lead to Francisco Rodriguez, who has 12 saves, 18 strikeouts and one walk in 15.2 innings, and Mike Remlinger, who has 19 strikeouts and one walk in 13 innings.

Sacramento, at 25-15, is built the other way around. Its rotation ERA is 3.46 and its bullpen's is 4.94. Brandon Knight has a 1.96 ERA, though 27 walks against 36 strikeouts suggest that number is doing some heavy lifting, and Aubrey Huff is hitting .410. The Solons were projected at 75-76.

The site gives Monterrey a 56.5% chance at a top-two seed and Sacramento 45.3%. Nobody else is above 27%.

The East: Louisville, with an asterisk

Louisville is 24-15 and leads the East, but it has scored 215 and allowed 206, more like a 20-19 team. Roger Clemens has a 4.40 ERA, a 3.35 FIP and 75 strikeouts in 61.1 innings, so the pitching should get better. The record is another matter. Hartford, at 22-17, has the league's best offense at 6.23 runs per game, with Gary Sheffield slugging .638 and Randy Winn hitting .346, and a pitching staff that has given up 221. The projections had the Yard Goats at 87-64, and they're on roughly that pace.

Richmond, the projected East favorite at 88-63, is 19-21. Don't blame Greg Colbrunn, who is hitting .385 with 13 homers and a 205 OPS+. Blame, among others, Carl Pavano, 0-3 with a 6.66 ERA and a 3.92 FIP, as good a summary of Richmond's May as any.

Alaska, the 151-run team

The projected favorite, Alaska, was supposed to win 94 games. The Athletics are 20-20. They've scored 151 runs, 19th of 20, and allowed 155, the fewest in the league. Matt Morris has a 1.83 ERA in 68.2 innings and Jeff Suppan a 2.11. The best hitter in the lineup by OPS+ is Rontrez Johnson, at 121. A team that allows 3.9 runs a game shouldn't be a .500 team, and neither should one that scores 3.8, and Alaska has found the exact point where the two cancel out.

The bottom

Charlotte is 13-27, the worst record in the league, and Curt Schilling, with 2.1 WAR, 74 strikeouts and 11 walks, is about as blameless as a pitcher on a 13-27 team can be. The Knights are fifth in FIP and 17th in runs per game. Orlando and Inland Empire are next, both 16-24. Inland Empire leads the FBL in FIP, which makes its record a very specific kind of frustrating.
