FBL · 2002-05-12 · By Ray Dalton, The Houston Gazette

The Other Brian Hunter

Santo Domingo scored five in the first, Monterrey came all the way back, the lead changed hands twice more, and the game ended with a .205 hitter named Brian Hunter beating a team that also has a Brian Hunter.

MONTERREY, Mexico — Two Brian Hunters played center field and right field in the same game on Sunday afternoon, one for each team, and for most of the day the wrong one was winning.

Santo Domingo’s Brian Hunter, the 31-year-old center fielder, singled home two runs in the first inning to finish a five-run rally against Don Lemon. Monterrey’s Brian Hunter, the 34-year-old right fielder hitting .205, came up in the second and hit a three-run homer off Kevin Jarvis. And with one out in the bottom of the ninth, in a tie game, Monterrey’s Hunter singled home Robb Quinlan to beat the Gallos 9-8 in the first game of a doubleheader.

“Somebody asked me after if I knew there was another one,” Hunter said. “I’ve been the other Brian Hunter my whole career. Today I wasn’t.”

A slump, and a hole

The Sultanes came into the day 23-12, still the best record in the FBL, but they had lost eight of their last ten, and the first inning looked like more of the same. Lemon loaded the bases, walked Scott Hatteberg to force in a run, and then gave up an RBI grounder to Kevin Orie, an RBI single to Ricky Ledee and the two-run single to the Gallos’ Hunter. Before Monterrey came to bat, it trailed 5-0.

Lemon lasted only into the fourth. The bullpen behind him held, starting with Alan Levine, who allowed one hit over 2⅔ innings, and the Sultanes chipped away. Hunter’s homer made it 5-3 in the second. Vinny Castilla’s sacrifice fly made it 5-4 in the third. Augie Ojeda’s single off Eric Schmitt tied it in the sixth.

In the seventh, Alex Ochoa singled, Castilla doubled, and Jay Bell doubled them both home for a 7-5 lead.

Three swings in the eighth

It didn’t hold. In the top of the eighth the Gallos’ Hunter and Jose Nieves singled off Todd Van Poppel, and manager Terry Booker went to Mike Remlinger, who came in with a 0.79 ERA. Ryan Freel singled to load the bases, and Orlando Hudson doubled all three runners home. Santo Domingo led 8-7.

It didn’t hold either. With one out in the bottom half, Craig Biggio, catching on Sunday, doubled. After Jeff Bagwell struck out, Ochoa doubled Biggio home to tie it 8-8. Ochoa finished 3-for-4 with a double, a triple and a walk.

Remlinger, given a second chance, struck out the side in the ninth: Victor Martinez, Morgan Burkhart and the Gallos’ Hunter.

“He blew it and then he fixed it,” Booker said of Remlinger, who got the win. “That’s a veteran. The kids watch that.”

The finish

Billy Traber, the Gallos’ 22-year-old left-hander, who had come in to end the eighth, stayed on for the ninth. Quinlan singled, moved up on Ojeda’s sacrifice, and scored when Hunter singled. Hunter finished 2-for-5 with four RBI.

The win stopped a two-game skid, but only for a few hours. Santo Domingo took the nightcap, so Monterrey ended the day 24-13, a game ahead of Sacramento in the West Group. The Gallos are 21-18.

Bagwell, who went 1-for-5 in the opener, was asked whether eight losses in ten games had started to wear on a clubhouse that had spent April winning almost every night.

“We’re in first place,” he said. “If that’s a slump, I’ll take a few more of them.”
