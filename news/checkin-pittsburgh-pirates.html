The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Pittsburgh Is 1-8 When Joel Pineiro Starts

Alexander called Pineiro the exciting one and Tomo Ohka "decidedly below average." Pineiro is 1-7, Ohka leads the staff in WAR, and the Pirates are 12-18 since an 8-2 start.

Pittsburgh Pirates · 20-20 · 232 runs scored, 230 allowed

The first 10 games went the way Alexander drew it up. He ranked Pittsburgh eighth, predicted 80-71, and described "one of the deeper lineups in the ABL," with Shannon Stewart, Nomar Garciaparra and Aramis Ramirez at the front. The Pirates went 8-2. They won six straight from April 2 to 7, including a three-game sweep in San Diego. In the April 5 game, Garciaparra's three-run homer in the ninth won it, 6-3. The roundup described the win over Austin on April 6 by saying "the bottom of the order went berserk," and Pittsburgh won 13-0.

Since then they are 12-18. At 20-20 they're fourth in the Eastern Conference, three games out, with a +2 run differential and a 39.2% chance of making the playoffs.

The exciting pair

Alexander's view of the pitching: "Joel Pineiro and BJ Ryan are an exciting pair but otherwise there's not much." He called the rest of the rotation, Tomo Ohka, Bryan Rekar and Steve Sparks, "decidedly below average."

Ryan has been what he expected. He has nine saves, a 2.77 FIP and no walks in 14.1 innings. Pineiro has not. He had a 2.03 ERA in 2001, and he threw 6.2 shutout innings in that 13-0 win over Austin. He is 1-7 with a 6.27 ERA, and Pittsburgh is 1-8 in his starts. The one win was the Austin game. His 4.71 FIP is better than his ERA, but he has still walked 22 batters in 56 innings. On Monday in Cincinnati he lost again, 4-3.

Ohka, the first of the "below average" group, has been the best starter by the numbers. He has a 3.76 FIP, has walked seven batters in 48.1 innings, and leads the staff with 1.4 WAR. On April 7 he struck out nine in eight innings against Austin, and the roundup said he "stayed hot." His ERA is 5.96, and the team is 4-4 in his starts. The rotation's ERA is 5.72 overall. The bullpen has been better. Ben Weber and Vic Darensbourg haven't walked anyone, and Darensbourg's ERA is 1.74.

The lineup

Stewart is hitting .370/.466/.486. Garciaparra has a .514 slugging percentage and 30 RBI. Ramirez, the third bat Alexander named, is hitting .247/.300/.409 with a 74 OPS+. The power he expected has come from Brian Daubach, who has seven homers and a 121 OPS+, and from someone he didn't mention, Jay Gibbons. Gibbons leads the team with 10 home runs. The trends piece highlighted Gibbons and Jose Reyes for combining for "nine hits, 8 RBI, three doubles, and two HR" in a 12-8 win over Kansas City on April 16. Reyes went 5-for-5 that day. Reyes is 18. He leads off, has 22 stolen bases and 35 runs, and the April 9 roundup noted his two-homer, four-hit game against Montreal. His OPS+ is 94, a reasonable number for an 18-year-old. Center field has been the hole. Jeff Liefer is hitting .179 with a 35 OPS+.

Next

The low point was a 22-4 loss in Anaheim on April 13. Pittsburgh is 0-3 against the Angels and was outscored 36-15 in those games. It is 4-7 in one-run games and 1-3 in extra innings. It won two of three in Tampa Bay last week, then lost two of three in Cincinnati. Next come three at home against Portland, three in Cleveland and three at home against Mexico City.
