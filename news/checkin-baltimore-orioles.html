The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Baltimore Has the Arms. It Doesn't Have the Home Runs.

The March question was whether the Orioles had the pitching. They allow the second-fewest runs in the ABL and have won three games 1-0, and they're 17-23 because they've hit 23 home runs, the fewest in the league. Now Noah Lowry's shoulder is gone.

Baltimore Orioles · 17-23 · 183 runs scored, 183 allowed

Baltimore was one of the teams to follow this season, and the question for the Orioles in March was a short one: did they have the arms? Behind John Thomson and Roy Oswalt, the rotation was asking a lot of innings from young pitchers, and that kind of plan can go south fast. The March preview figured them for 75-76; the projections were a little kinder, at 79-72.

Forty games in, the arms are fine. Baltimore has allowed 183 runs, second-fewest in the ABL, with the league's third-best staff ERA, and it has won three games 1-0. Thomson threw eight scoreless innings in two of them, at Portland on April 7 and against San Diego on April 25. In the third, Eleazar Mora allowed one hit in eight innings against Tampa Bay, his second straight excellent start despite modest stuff.

The Orioles are 17-23 anyway, sixth in the Eastern Conference and six games behind Cleveland and Montreal. They've scored exactly as many runs as they've allowed.

Singles hitters

The lineup came in with a solid, professional core of John Vander Wal, Carlos Lee, Juan Uribe and Frank Catalanotto, with enough on-base skill and power to avoid long droughts. Half of that has shown up. Baltimore is hitting .289 as a team, with Catalanotto at .344 and Darren Lewis at .331. The power hasn't. The Orioles have 23 home runs, last in the ABL, and rank 18th in runs and 19th in OPS. Catalanotto, their best hitter, has three. Lewis, Rey Ordonez and Charles Gipson have no homers in 452 combined plate appearances. Xavier Nady, a sneaky important piece in March, hasn't drawn a walk. And the Orioles don't make up for it on the bases: they're last in steals, 17-for-32.

When the hits do bunch together, it gets loud. Baltimore beat Nashville 16-4 on April 15, with Catalanotto driving in six, and Kansas City 15-1 on May 8, with Lee driving in five and Oswalt allowing one run in 8.1 innings. It has also scored one run or fewer eight times, three of them shutouts.

The young arms

The two 21-year-olds behind Thomson and Oswalt were always the risk: Noah Lowry, a lefty changeup specialist, and Sergio Mitre. Lowry made seven starts with a 4.81 ERA. Then his shoulder went, a catastrophic loss for the rotation's depth that will likely force an early trade or an innings gamble. Mitre made his first start on May 9 and gave up five earned runs in 5.1 innings.

The veterans have issues of their own. Oswalt, a 2.73 ERA pitcher last year, is 2-5 with a 4.57 ERA. Guillermo Mota, who looked like the real thing at closer, has nine saves and a 4.70 ERA. He gave up two runs in his inning of a 7-6 loss to Kansas City on May 10, a game Baltimore had led by four. Rich Garces has the best FIP in the bullpen, 3.14.

A good week, a bad month

The best stretch came early. Baltimore went 7-3 from April 4 to 14, including a 1-0 win in 10 innings at Portland, when Ordonez drove in the run with a double and then threw out the tying run at the plate. Then came a 2-8 slide from April 22 to May 2, with 24 runs in 10 games. It ended the next night with a 10-9 walk-off comeback from five down against Montreal.

The site gives Baltimore a 12.2% chance of relegation and a 12.1% chance at the playoffs, a rare dead heat. Southside comes to town next, then three in San Juan and three at home against Las Vegas. The pitching will keep the Orioles in most of those games. March's question has an answer. The harder one is what happens when the pitching is good and the hitters still can't produce.
