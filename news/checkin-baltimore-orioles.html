40-Game Check-In · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Orioles Have to Earn Every Run

Baltimore Orioles, 17-23 through 40 games

A team without much power has to be good at everything else, all the time. That is the Orioles problem and the Orioles project: 23 home runs, 183 runs, 17-23.

Their most productive hitter is Frank Catalanotto (1B, 28), at .344/.393/.497 with 3 HR. John Vander Wal leads the club with 6 home runs. As a team they are hitting .289/.348/.401, 19th of 20 in the ABL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 23 home runs.

In the rotation, John Thomson has been the steadiest: 1-3 with a 3.44 ERA, 24 strikeouts and 9 walks in 55.0 innings. Eleazar Mora (3.63 ERA in 44.2 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.36 ERA, 3rd of 20 in the ABL; starters 4.31, relievers 4.50. Guillermo Mota has 9 saves and 2 blown.

Through 40 games they were 17-23, scoring 183 runs and allowing 183 (+0). As of the May 13 export they are 6th in the Eastern Conference, 6 games back, and 16th of 20 in the ABL. That run differential would usually produce a record around 20-20. They are 7-7 in one-run games and 2-1 in extra innings.

The weakest regular bat has been A.J. Hinch, at .248/.305/.314 in 132 plate appearances.

Odds and ends: When they score three runs or fewer they are 4-16; when they score six or more, 11-3. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

The best night was May 8, a 15-1 win over the Monarchs. The worst was May 13, an 11-2 loss to the Athletics. Their best 10-game stretch went 7-3 (Apr 4 to Apr 14); their worst, 2-8 (Apr 22 to May 2).

The ABL is the middle rung, so it is the only league here where you can fall and climb in the same breath: each conference winner gets a bye, second and third play in, both finalists of the American Championship are promoted to MLB, and the bottom three are relegated to the FBL. They sit 6th in the Eastern Conference, 6 games back of the Spiders. The drop is 1 game below them, where the Violets is the best of the clubs on relegation pace.

The next stretch is 10 games (7 at home) against the Black Sox, the Coquis, the Gamblers, the Knights. Those clubs are 213-187 combined, a .532 winning percentage, so the schedule is not doing them any favors. The hopeful read is the run differential: if runs keep coming at this rate, the wins tend to follow, and the Orioles are about 3 wins short of where that differential usually lands.

Forty games is when baseball stops being a rumor and starts being a record. The Orioles have one now. It is theirs to keep or to fix.
