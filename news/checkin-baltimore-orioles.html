The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Baltimore Has the Arms. It Doesn't Have the Home Runs.

Alexander asked in March whether the Orioles had the pitching. They allow the second-fewest runs in the ABL, have won three games 1-0, and are 17-23 because they've hit 23 home runs, fewest in the league. Now Noah Lowry's shoulder is gone.

Baltimore Orioles · 17-23 · 183 runs scored, 183 allowed

Baltimore was one of the six teams Alexander picked to follow all season, and his question for them was short: "Does Baltimore have the arms?" He worried that the rotation behind John Thomson and Roy Oswalt was "a lot of innings being asked from young arms — and it can go south fast." His prediction was 75-76.

Forty games in, the arms are fine. The Orioles have allowed 183 runs, the second-fewest in the ABL, and their staff ERA is third. They've won three games by 1-0. Thomson threw eight scoreless innings in two of them, at Portland on April 7 and against San Diego on April 25. In the third, Eleazar Mora allowed one hit in eight innings against Tampa Bay. The week-three notes called it his "second straight excellent outing despite modest raw stuff."

The Orioles are 17-23 anyway, sixth in the Eastern Conference and six games behind Cleveland and Montreal. They've scored exactly as many runs as they've allowed.

Singles hitters

The March preview praised "a solid, professional core" of John Vander Wal, Carlos Lee, Juan Uribe and Frank Catalanotto with "enough on-base skill and power to prevent long scoring droughts." The on-base skill has shown up. Baltimore is hitting .289 as a team, Catalanotto is at .344 and Darren Lewis at .331. The power hasn't. The Orioles have 23 home runs, last in the ABL, and are 18th in runs and 19th in OPS. Catalanotto, the team's best hitter, has three. Lewis, Rey Ordonez and Charles Gipson have no home runs in 452 combined plate appearances. Xavier Nady, whom Alexander called "a sneaky important piece," has no walks and a .265 OBP. Baltimore is also last in steals, at 17-for-32.

When the hits bunch together, it gets loud. The Orioles beat Nashville 16-4 on April 15, with Catalanotto driving in six, and Kansas City 15-1 on May 8, with Lee driving in five and Oswalt allowing one run in 8.1 innings. They have also scored one run or fewer eight times, including three shutout losses.

The young arms

Alexander built his concern around the two 21-year-olds behind Thomson and Oswalt: Noah Lowry, "lefty changeup specialist," and Sergio Mitre. Lowry made seven starts and had a 4.81 ERA. Then his shoulder went. The injury report calls it "catastrophic for Baltimore's rotation depth" and says it "likely forces an early trade or innings gamble." Mitre made his first start on May 9 and allowed five earned runs in 5.1 innings.

The rest of the staff has its own issues. Oswalt, the March headliner, had a 2.73 ERA last year and is 2-5 with a 4.57 ERA this year. Guillermo Mota, the closer Alexander thought "looks like the real thing," has nine saves and a 4.70 ERA. He gave up two runs in his inning of a 7-6 loss to Kansas City on May 10, a game Baltimore had led by four. Rich Garces has the best FIP in the bullpen, 3.14.

A good week, a bad month

The best stretch was early. Baltimore went 7-3 from April 4 to 14, including a 1-0 win in 10 innings at Portland. Ordonez drove in the run with a double and then threw out the tying run at the plate, according to the April 7 roundup. Then came a 2-8 slide from April 22 to May 2 in which they scored 24 runs in 10 games. It ended the next night with a 10-9 walk-off comeback from five down against Montreal.

The site gives Baltimore a 12.2% chance of relegation and a 12.1% chance of making the playoffs, a rare even split. Southside comes to Baltimore next, then three in San Juan and three at home against Las Vegas. The pitching will keep them in most of those games. The question Alexander asked in March has an answer, but the follow-up is harder: what happens when the pitching is good and the hitters still don't produce?
