40-Game Check-In · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

Ross Gload and the Beavers: A 1.060 OPS Goes a Long Way

Portland Beavers, 23-17 through 40 games

Ross Gload has a 1.060 OPS. You can stop reading there if you like; the rest of this is about the Beavers, who are 23-17, but the most interesting thing about them is a RF hitting .359/.411/.648.

40 games, by the numbers

  • 23-17 record
  • +29 run differential (234 scored, 205 allowed)
  • 4-7 in one-run games
  • 13-9 at home
  • 10-8 on the road
  • 5.70 / 3.52 starter / reliever ERA
  • 44 home runs
  • 45 stolen bases

Through 40 games they were 23-17, scoring 234 runs and allowing 205 (+29). As of the May 13 export they are 3rd in the Western Conference, 3 games back, and 6th of 20 in the ABL. By the old Pythagorean arithmetic, that is a 22-win team so far. They are 4-7 in one-run games and 2-3 in extra innings.

Ross Gload has 46 hits, 12 walks and 20 strikeouts in 141 trips. Rafael Furcal has been the Beavers hitter opponents least want to see: .323/.391/.491, 5 homers, 24 driven in. Ross Gload leads the club with 8 home runs. Nick Punto has 12 steals (caught 3 times). As a team they are hitting .304/.373/.462, 8th of 20 in the ABL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 44 home runs.

In the rotation, Steve Parris has been the steadiest: 4-0 with a 3.67 ERA, 32 strikeouts and 18 walks in 54.0 innings. Dave Williams (3.92 ERA in 41.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.78 ERA, 8th of 20 in the ABL; starters 5.70, relievers 3.52. Eric Gagne has 9 saves and 1 blown.

Then there is Rafael Furcal, whose OPS has gone from .691 in 2001 to .882 now. Some of that is the sample. The question is how much. In the rotation, Chris Michalak has a 8.74 ERA over 34.0 innings, after a 4.41 ERA in 2001.

Nine innings have not been enough for the Beavers, five times. They are 2-3 in those games and 23-17 overall.

Odds and ends: When they score three runs or fewer they are 3-9; when they score six or more, 17-4. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

The best night was May 4, a 16-8 win over the Gamblers. The worst was May 1, a 10-2 loss to the Coquis. Their best 10-game stretch went 7-3 (Apr 12 to Apr 21); their worst, 4-6 (Apr 2 to Apr 12).

The ABL is the middle rung, so it is the only league here where you can fall and climb in the same breath: each conference winner gets a bye, second and third play in, both finalists of the American Championship are promoted to MLB, and the bottom three are relegated to the FBL. They sit 3rd in the Western Conference, 3 games back of the Black Sox, inside the play-in places. The drop is 5 games below them, where the Violets is the best of the clubs on relegation pace.

The next stretch is 10 games (3 at home) against the Pirates, the Reds, the Rays, the Spiders. Those clubs are 185-215 combined, a .463 winning percentage, so this is where a record can get fatter. The record matches the runs, so there is no regression bill coming and no hidden credit either. The next 122 games will mostly be about whether Ross Gload keeps doing this. In six weeks the Beavers have shown us who they might be. The next six will show us who they are willing to keep being.
