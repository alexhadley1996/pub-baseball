The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Is Portland Really the Worst Team in the ABL? No. It's 23-17.

The ABL's last-ranked preseason club is hitting .304 as a team and would make the playoffs if the season ended today. The rotation has a 5.70 ERA, and the bullpen has thrown 150 innings to cover for it.

Portland Beavers · 23-17 · 234 runs scored, 205 allowed

Of all the questions coming into the season, Portland's was the bluntest: was it really the worst team in the ABL? It looked that way. The preseason consensus ranked the Beavers 20th of 20, the projections had them at 64-87, the worst record in the league, and they were the odds-on relegation pick, with limited offense and power and what might have been the weakest staff in the league.

Six weeks in, the answer is no. Portland is 23-17, tied for second in the Western Conference and on a playoff pace. The site gives the Beavers a 46.4% chance at the playoffs and a 3.6% chance of relegation. They started 4-0, with Eric Gagne nasty in four straight appearances, slipped to 5-6 on April 12 and have gone 18-11 since.

A lineup that wasn't supposed to hit

The offense got a D in March, light on star power, with Rafael Furcal and Alex Gonzalez as two of the only good players. Instead Portland is hitting .304 as a team and ranks fifth in the ABL in runs. Ross Gload has been the best hitter on the club, at .359 with eight homers and a 158 OPS+. Furcal is hitting .323 with nine steals. Marlon Byrd is at .338, Nick Punto has a .420 on-base percentage and 12 steals, and J.T. Snow has scored 34 runs. Gonzalez has been the one disappointment, with a 75 OPS+.

The big games have been loud. Morgan Ensberg went 4-for-4 with five RBI in a 13-9 win over Anaheim. On Sunday Angel Pena hit two homers and drove in six against Minnesota, and Portland still lost 12-11 in 10 innings.

A rotation that wasn't supposed to pitch

That part of the scouting report has held up. The rotation's ERA is 5.70. Chris Michalak is at 8.74 and Denny Wagner at 7.75, and Salvador Arellano, the 19-year-old wild card with a good fastball and shaky control, has walked 26 in 38.1 innings. Dave Williams, the 23-year-old ace, has a 5.71 FIP. Only Steve Parris, 4-0 with a 3.67 ERA, has been steady.

The one real strength has covered for the rest. Portland's bullpen has thrown 150.2 innings, nearly three-quarters as many as the starters, with a 3.52 ERA. Toby Borland is at 2.12 and hasn't allowed a homer in 29.2 innings. Gagne has nine saves. Troy Percival has been less effective, with a 4.00 ERA and three losses, one of them on a late Baltimore rally on April 6. The pen doesn't always hold. T.J. Tucker gave up five runs while getting two outs in a loss in New York on May 9.

The trade question

The big question in March was whether Portland would flip Gagne and Percival at peak value. That was a seller's question. At 23-17, with a roster that has dodged major injuries, it's a different one. Promotion is in play, and the site puts Portland's chances at 13.2%.

The record does have some soft spots. Portland is 4-7 in one-run games, and its +29 run differential fits a 22-18 team, so this isn't luck. The schedule is about to get harder, with three in Pittsburgh, Cincinnati at home and three in Tampa Bay. But the preseason question has its answer. The worst team in the ABL is somebody else.
