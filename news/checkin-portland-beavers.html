The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Is Portland Really the Worst Team in the ABL? No. It's 23-17.

Alexander's last-ranked ABL club is hitting .304 as a team and would make the playoffs if the season ended today. The rotation has a 5.70 ERA, and the bullpen has thrown 150 innings to cover it.

Portland Beavers · 23-17 · 234 runs scored, 205 allowed

Of the six questions Alexander asked in his Teams to Watch preview, Portland's was the bluntest: "Is Portland really the worst team in the ABL?" He thought so. He ranked the Beavers 20th of 20, predicted a 64-87 record, the worst in his ABL table, and called them "the odds-on relegation pick," with "limited offense and power" and a pitching staff that "might be the weakest staff in the league."

The answer, six weeks in, is no. Portland is 23-17, tied for second in the ABL's Western Conference and on a playoff pace. The site gives them a 46.4% chance at the playoffs and a 3.6% chance of relegation. The Beavers started 4-0. The April 4 roundup notes that Eric Gagne "had now appeared in four games and still looked nasty." They slipped to 5-6 on April 12 and have gone 18-11 since.

A lineup that wasn't supposed to hit

The March preview had the offense at a D, "lacking a bit in star power," with Rafael Furcal and Alex Gonzalez as "two of the only good players." Portland is hitting .304/.373/.462 and ranks fifth in the ABL in runs. Ross Gload, whose name doesn't appear in the March preview, has been the best hitter: .359/.411/.648 with eight home runs and a 158 OPS+. Furcal is at .323/.391/.491 with nine steals. Marlon Byrd is hitting .338, Nick Punto has a .420 OBP and 12 steals, and J.T. Snow has scored 34 runs. Gonzalez, one of the two players Alexander liked, has a 75 OPS+.

The big individual games have been loud too. Morgan Ensberg went 4-for-4 with five RBI in a 13-9 win over Anaheim. Angel Pena hit two home runs and drove in six on May 12 against Minnesota, and Portland still lost 12-11 in 10 innings.

A rotation that wasn't supposed to pitch

That part of the March scouting has held up. The rotation ERA is 5.70. Chris Michalak has an 8.74 ERA and Denny Wagner 7.75, and Salvador Arellano, the 19-year-old "wild card" with "a good fastball but shaky control," has 26 walks in 38.1 innings. Dave Williams, the 23-year-old "ace," has a 5.71 FIP. Only veteran Steve Parris, at 4-0 with a 3.67 ERA, has been steady, and the week-three notes said the same: "The rotation outside of Steve Paris has been shaky."

What Alexander called "the one real strength" has covered for it. Portland's bullpen has thrown 150.2 innings, nearly three-quarters as many as the starters, with a 3.52 ERA. Toby Borland has a 2.12 ERA and hasn't allowed a home run in 29.2 innings. Gagne has nine saves. Troy Percival has been less effective, with a 4.00 ERA and three losses, one of which, according to the April 6 roundup, came on a "late rally" by Baltimore. The bullpen doesn't always hold. T.J. Tucker allowed five runs in two-thirds of an inning in a loss in New York on May 9.

The trade question

Alexander's March verdict raised a specific question: "whether they flip Gagné/Percival at peak value." In March that was a seller's question. With a 23-17 record it's a different one. The injury report noted that Portland "dodges major injuries," with "trade leverage" intact. Its description of Portland's performance as "poor" seems out of date. Promotion is now in play. The site's odds put it at 13.2%.

The record does have some soft spots. Portland is 4-7 in one-run games and has a +29 run differential, which fits a 22-18 team. The schedule is about to get harder: three in Pittsburgh, three at home with Cincinnati and three in Tampa Bay. But the preseason question has an answer. The worst team in the ABL is somebody else.
