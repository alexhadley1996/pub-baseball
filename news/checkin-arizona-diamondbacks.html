The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Tom Gordon Has 65 Strikeouts and 10 Walks, and Arizona Is Tied for Last

The Diamondbacks swept the Seals, came back from eight down against the Yankees and are 0-7 against left-handed starters. They're 5-15 on the road and tied with the Dodgers at the bottom of the West.

Arizona Diamondbacks · 17-24 · 174 runs scored, 188 allowed

On May 5 in St. Louis, Tom Gordon threw a one-hit shutout with 11 strikeouts and no walks. It was the best start of a very good season: 65 strikeouts, 10 walks and four home runs allowed in 61.1 innings, a 2.12 FIP and 2.6 WAR. Coming into the year, nobody was sure whether Gordon was a reliever or a starter. He's made nine starts, and he's 3-5 with a 3.52 ERA.

That's the Diamondbacks in miniature. The pitching staff has been quite superb, and it doesn't look like a staff that belongs in a relegation fight. Arizona is 17-24 anyway, tied with the Dodgers for last in the West and nine games behind Oakland and San Francisco, and the site gives it a 22.7% chance of going down.

Strikeouts that don't add up

Arizona has struck out 321 batters, the most in MLB, and still has a 4.49 rotation ERA. The gap is sequencing. The staff strands only 68% of its baserunners, the worst rate in the league. Shawn Estes is the clearest case, with a 6.17 ERA and a 3.51 FIP. Robert Person, who struck out 10 Seals and 11 Dodgers in back-to-back turns, has a 3.94 ERA. It isn't the defense's fault, either. Matt Lawton in right, Shane Spencer in left and Robin Ventura at third have all been good with the glove. Three weeks in, a 91 FIP- suggested the staff should settle around average or better. Two weeks later, it's still waiting.

Two good hitters, and some surprises

The lineup came in with only two good hitters, Ventura and Lawton, and both have been about average. Ventura has drawn 30 walks for a .371 on-base percentage but is slugging .400, and Lawton's OPS+ is exactly 100. Erubiel Durazo, the first baseman with power and patience, has 37 strikeouts and a 95 OPS+. The help has come from elsewhere. Raul Gonzalez is hitting .345 in center. Todd Hundley, a .642 OPS hitter last year, has six homers and a 117 OPS+, and Michael Restovich, 23, has three homers in 38 plate appearances. The middle infield is as weak as feared. Enrique Wilson and Jay Woolf both have 62 OPS+ marks, and Chris Stynes, who splits time between second and center, is at 75.

There's also a streak nobody wants. Arizona still hasn't beaten a left-handed starter. It's 0-7, most recently against John Halama in Toronto on Saturday.

The good weeks

When it's gone right, it's gone right in a hurry. In mid-April the Yankees came to Phoenix and led by eight on April 15, and the Diamondbacks came all the way back to win 9-8 in 12. The next night, down a run with two outs in the ninth, Spencer hit a two-run walk-off homer. From April 24 to 29 they won five straight, two in Atlanta and a home sweep of the Seals, including an April 28 comeback from six down that Hundley finished with a four-RBI day and an 8-7 walk-off. On May 8 Durazo drove in five in a 9-8 walk-off over the Dodgers in 10.

The rest has mostly been road trips. Arizona is 5-15 away from home. It was swept in Detroit in the first week as part of a six-game losing streak, swept in Oakland, and swept in Toronto to start the current four-game skid. The preseason projections had the D-backs at 81-70. To get anywhere near that, their luck has to turn, and soon: two more at home against Philadelphia, then three at Wrigley and three at home with Colorado.
