The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Tom Gordon Has 65 Strikeouts and 10 Walks, and Arizona Is Tied for Last

The Diamondbacks swept the Seals, came back from eight down against the Yankees, and are 0-7 against left-handed starters. They are 5-15 on the road and tied with the Dodgers at the bottom of the West.

Arizona Diamondbacks · 17-24 · 174 runs scored, 188 allowed

On May 5 in St. Louis, Tom Gordon threw a one-hit shutout with 11 strikeouts and no walks. It was the best start of his season, and his season has been very good: 65 strikeouts, 10 walks, four home runs and a 2.12 FIP in 61.1 innings, worth 2.6 WAR. Alexander's March preview asked whether Gordon was "a reliever or starter." He has started nine games. His ERA is 3.52, and he's 3-5.

That is Arizona in short. Alexander's 40-game notes call the pitching staff "quite superb," one that "certainly doesn't seem like a staff that would be relegated." The Diamondbacks are 17-24 anyway, tied with the Dodgers for last in the West and nine games behind Oakland and San Francisco. The site gives them a 22.7% chance of relegation.

Strikeouts that don't add up

Arizona has struck out 321 batters, the most in MLB. The rotation ERA is 4.49. Alexander's explanation is sequencing: the staff's strand rate is "the worst in the league at 68%." Shawn Estes is the clearest case. He has a 6.17 ERA and a 3.51 FIP. Robert Person, who struck out 10 against the Seals and 11 against the Dodgers, has a 3.94 ERA. Alexander also praised the outfield and third-base defense, including Matt Lawton in right, Shane Spencer in left and Robin Ventura at third, so the ERA isn't the fielders' fault either. At three weeks he had written that the 91 FIP- "suggests they should be closer to average (or better) going forward." Two more weeks haven't proved him wrong or right.

The hitters he counted on

The March preview named "only two good offensive players (Robin Ventura and Matt Lawton)." Both have been average. Ventura has a .371 OBP on 30 walks but a .400 slugging percentage, and Lawton has a 100 OPS+ exactly. Erubiel Durazo, the "interesting piece with great power and patience" at first, has 37 strikeouts and a 95 OPS+. The surprises are elsewhere. Raul Gonzalez is hitting .345 in center. Todd Hundley, a .642 OPS hitter last year, has six home runs and a 117 OPS+. Michael Restovich, 23, has three homers in 38 plate appearances. The middle infield has been the hole Alexander predicted in March, the "depressingly weak middle infield." Enrique Wilson and Jay Woolf both have 62 OPS+, and Chris Stynes, who moves between second and center, is at 75.

There's also a pattern the club hasn't broken. Alexander noted at 40 games that they "still haven't beat a left-handed starting pitcher," and the count is now 0-7, the latest loss to John Halama in Toronto on Saturday.

The good week

Arizona has had two very good stretches. In mid-April the Yankees came to Arizona and led by eight runs on April 15. The Diamondbacks came back to win 9-8 in 12 innings. The next night, down a run with two outs in the ninth, Spencer hit a two-run walk-off homer. Then, from April 24 to 29, they won five straight: two at Atlanta and a home sweep of the Seals. That included an April 28 comeback from six down, when Hundley drove in four in an 8-7 walk-off. On May 8 Durazo had five RBI in a 9-8 walk-off over the Dodgers in 10.

The rest has been road trips. Arizona is 5-15 away from home, including a sweep at Detroit in the season's first week as part of a six-game losing streak, a sweep in Oakland, and the sweep in Toronto that started the current four-game skid. The predicted standings had them at 81-70. Alexander's outlook is short: "Arizona's luck certainly needs to turn." Next come two more at home against Philadelphia, three at Wrigley Field and three at home against Colorado.
