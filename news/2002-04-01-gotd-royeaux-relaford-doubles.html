ABL · 2002-04-01 · By Owen Kirby, Diamond Daily

Five Doubles, and Montreal Won Anyway

Desi Relaford doubled five times for Las Vegas, Kevin Brown pitched nine innings, and Montreal still won its home opener 4-3 in 13 innings on Frank Menechino's bases-loaded single.

MONTREAL — There were 40,190 people at Olympic Stadium on Monday for the Royeaux’s first home game, and for a long time it looked like most of them would go home remembering the visiting shortstop.

Desi Relaford, a 28-year-old journeyman playing his first game for the Las Vegas Gamblers, came to the plate six times and doubled in five of them. He drove in all three Las Vegas runs: one in the fourth, one in the sixth to tie the game, and one in the 10th to put the Gamblers ahead.

Montreal won anyway, 4-3, in 13 innings, on a bases-loaded single by Frank Menechino.

“Five doubles,” Relaford said, shaking his head. “I don’t think I’ve had five doubles in a week before. And we lost. That’s baseball, I guess. That’s a lot of baseball.”

Two aces

The game was supposed to be about the starters, and for most of nine innings it was. Kevin Brown, 37, signed by Las Vegas over the winter for a lot of casino money, threw nine innings, struck out seven, walked nobody and allowed two runs, both on two-out doubles in the third, by Craig Counsell and Greg Vaughn. He got no decision.

Derek Lowe, the former Red Sox reliever reborn as a Montreal starter, was nearly as good: seven innings, two runs, both on Relaford doubles.

“Kevin Brown was outstanding,” Royeaux manager Frank Robinson said. “We were lucky to get two. Then their bullpen came in and we got lucky again.”

Extra innings

Relaford’s third RBI double came in the 10th off Billy Koch, Montreal’s closer, and gave Las Vegas a 3-2 lead. In the bottom half, Menechino singled off Mike Stanton and a run scored, and it was tied again.

Koch stayed on and kept it there, three innings in all. John Riedling, a 26-year-old with no reputation to speak of, threw two scoreless innings and got the win.

In the 13th, with Jose Jimenez tiring, Gamblers manager Matt Richards brought in Salomon Torres. Torres gave up a single, the bases were loaded with one out, and Menechino singled home the winner. The Royeaux had their first win.

Menechino finished 3-for-6 with two of Montreal’s biggest hits. Rafael Palmeiro, 37 and new to left field, went 2-for-6.

The building

There was a moment in the seventh inning, with the game tied, when the crowd at Olympic Stadium started chanting something that the American reporters in the press box couldn’t make out. It turned out to be “Nos Amours,” the old nickname for the Expos, the team that played here before the strike and before the Royeaux.

Robinson heard it.

“They remember,” he said. “That’s good. We’d like to give them something new to remember too.”

The Royeaux are 1-0. The Gamblers are 0-1. Relaford is hitting .833.
