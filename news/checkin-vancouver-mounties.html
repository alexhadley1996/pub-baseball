The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Vancouver Got an F+ for Pitching. Jamie Moyer Didn't Read It.

Alexander ranked the Mounties 18th and said they had Dan Haren "and nothing else." Moyer has been the steadiest starter, the bullpen has blown two saves, and Vancouver is 17-6 at home.

Vancouver Mounties · 23-17 · 218 runs scored, 187 allowed

Alexander's March write-up on Vancouver was brief. The offense, graded C+, was three names: "Edgar Martinez, Todd Hollandsworth, Mark Bellhorn." The pitching got an F+: "They've got Dan Haren in the wings but nothing else." He ranked the Mounties 18th of 20 and predicted 75-76.

Vancouver is 23-17, third in the West Group and three games behind Monterrey, with a +31 run differential and a 70.0% chance at the playoffs. The site projects about 81 wins, and the Mounties have the fourth-best record in the FBL.

"Nothing else"

That "nothing else" included Jamie Moyer. The 39-year-old threw eight shutout innings in Inland Empire on opening day and eight more against Oklahoma City on April 6, a 13-0 win. The April 10 roundup noted he "sat at 2-0 with 16 scoreless innings," and two days later he was "3-0, with only two runs allowed in 24 innings." He's at 3.54 now, with 12 walks in 56 innings. Dicky Gonzalez, 23, is 5-2 with a 4.29 ERA and two eight-inning starts in which he allowed one run.

Haren, the one pitcher Alexander named, has had the strangest season on the staff. His first start of the year was the April 2 game the roundup described as "7 IP, 2 ER... until a late HR," a 3-2 loss. He's 2-4 with a 6.92 ERA, but his FIP is 3.45, with 34 strikeouts, 11 walks and two home runs in 40.1 innings. He's been knocked out in the third inning twice, at Monterrey and in Hartford. The team ERA is 4.54, seventh in the FBL. The bullpen's is 3.68, and it has blown two saves all season. Jose Mesa has 11 saves in 11 chances.

The three names, and a fourth

All three hitters Alexander named have been good. Bellhorn is hitting .308/.393/.610 with 11 home runs, 41 strikeouts and a 156 OPS+. Hollandsworth, who in the week-three notes had been "one of the league's best all-around players so far," is at .342/.416/.527 and doubled four times on opening day. Martinez, 39, has a .414 on-base percentage and a 143 OPS+. The fourth name is Bucky Jacobsen, a 26-year-old first baseman. He has 11 home runs and a team-leading 36 RBI, including seven in Friday's 10-7 win in Hartford.

After those four, the lineup drops off. Center fielder Bubba Crosby has a 66 OPS+. Second baseman Jeff Reboulet, 38, is at 69, and Marcus Thames is at 62 with no home runs. Vancouver ranks 18th in the FBL in stolen bases.

Home

The Mounties are 17-6 at home and 6-11 on the road. Alexander's personalities report listed them among the "energy-driven teams," which may help explain the home record. At home they swept Memphis, Eugene and Orlando and won their last series against San Antonio. On the road, they were swept in Albuquerque in mid-April, scoring five runs in three games, a series the week-three notes called "a shocking sweep." They went 7-3 in their last 10. Next come three games in Alaska. After that, Sacramento visits for three games in a series between the West's second- and third-place teams, and then the Mounties go to Hoboken.
