40-Game Check-In · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

Two Teams Called the Mounties

Vancouver Mounties, 23-17 through 40 games

Ask the Mounties where they would like to play tomorrow and the answer should be obvious. 17-6 at home. 6-11 on the road. Home cooking is real, or at least it has been for six weeks.

Mark Bellhorn has been the Mounties hitter opponents least want to see: .308/.393/.610, 11 homers, 29 driven in. On the bases, Bubba Crosby is 10-for-12 stealing. As a team they are hitting .289/.357/.456, 5th of 20 in the FBL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 45 home runs.

In the rotation, Jamie Moyer has been the steadiest: 4-2 with a 3.54 ERA, 36 strikeouts and 12 walks in 56.0 innings. Dicky Gonzalez (4.29 ERA in 50.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.54 ERA, 7th of 20 in the FBL; starters 5.01, relievers 3.68. Closing has mostly meant Jose Mesa: 11 saves, 2.13 ERA.

Through 40 games they were 23-17, scoring 218 runs and allowing 187 (+31). As of the May 13 export they are 3rd in the West Group, 3 games back, and 4th of 20 in the FBL. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 23 wins in 40 games. They are 5-6 in one-run games.

The surprise might be Mark Bellhorn, who arrived without a 2001 top-tier line worth the name (under 150 plate appearances) and is hitting .308/.393/.610. In the rotation, Dan Haren has a 6.92 ERA over 40.1 innings.

The fine print is fun here. Home crowds have averaged 23,352 over 23 dates in a park that holds 55,883, about 42% full. There is room to be convinced.

The best night was Apr 6, a 13-0 win over the Twisters. The worst was May 9, a 16-4 loss to the Yard Goats. Their best 10-game stretch went 8-2 (Apr 27 to May 6); their worst, 4-6 (Apr 1 to Apr 10).

The FBL is the bottom rung and nobody gets relegated from it; the whole point is the way up. The top seven make a ladder playoff, seeds one and two are promoted outright, and the winner of the Federal Promotion round makes three. They are the 4th seed today: in the ladder, 2 games out of an automatic promotion spot and 2 games clear of the first team outside.

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 4 of them at home, against the Athletics, the Solons, the Zephyrs, the Disco Turkeys, opponents with a combined 208-188 record (.525). Neither a gift nor an ambush. The record matches the runs, so there is no regression bill coming and no hidden credit either. The next 122 games will mostly be about whether Mark Bellhorn keeps doing this.
