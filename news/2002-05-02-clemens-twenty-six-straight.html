FBL · 2002-05-02 · By Marty Kessler, Diamond Daily

Clemens, Twenty-Six in a Row

Roger Clemens gave up three hits and a run to the first four Honolulu batters, then loaded the bases. He struck out the side to escape, retired the last 26 Makani in a row, finished with 13 strikeouts, and didn't get the win. Ryan Klesko took care of that in the 10th.

LOUISVILLE, Ky. — Roger Clemens doesn’t like to talk about the first inning, so let’s get it out of the way for him.

Tony Tarasco singled. B.J. Surhoff singled. Charles Johnson doubled in a run. Clemens struck out Chris Woodward, then walked Lou Collier, and on Thursday night, with the bases loaded and one out in the first inning, there was a moment at the ballpark in Louisville when 27,202 people wondered if this was going to be one of those nights.

Clemens struck out Mike Rivera. He struck out Justin Baughman. Then he got the next 24 Honolulu Makani in a row.

“First inning, I was trying too hard,” Clemens said. “I always try too hard in the first inning. By the second I’d calmed down.”

He finished with nine innings, three hits, one run, one walk and 13 strikeouts. He retired the last 26 batters he faced. He got no decision.

The splitter

Clemens is 39 now, and the fastball isn’t what it was. He’d be the first to tell you, and he’d be annoyed if you told him first. It still touches 93 on a good night. But the reason he’s still one of the best pitchers anywhere is everything else: a hard curveball, a slider, and above all the split-finger fastball, which he throws at 88 and which, at its best, simply disappears at the plate.

“It comes in looking like a fastball,” said Johnson, the Makani catcher, who doubled in the first and then struck out twice. “And then it isn’t there. You swing where it was going to be.”

Of his 13 strikeouts, by the count of the Bats’ bullpen, eight came on splitters and four on curveballs. He struck out Collier twice, Surhoff twice, Johnson twice and Kelly Johnson once. He threw 121 pitches.

“I wanted to go nine,” Clemens said. “I wanted the win, too, but I don’t get to decide that part.”

The other guy

Rich Harden, Honolulu’s 20-year-old right-hander, made sure he didn’t get it. Harden allowed one run in seven innings, on Ryan Klesko’s two-out single in the fourth, and struck out seven. In the seventh he loaded the bases with two outs and got Jack Cust on a fly ball.

Clemens watched every pitch Harden threw from the top step of the dugout.

“That kid’s going to be something,” Clemens said. “He’s got a better arm than I had at 20. I told him that after. I don’t know if he believed me.”

Harden said he did. “He said it to my face,” he said. “I’m going to believe it.”

The 10th

Clemens’s night ended at 1-1 after nine. Paul Quantrill, Louisville’s closer, came on in the 10th and gave up the lead: a leadoff double to Collier, a walk, and Kelly Johnson’s sacrifice fly.

Clemens, by then in a sweatshirt, didn’t leave the dugout.

In the bottom half, with two outs, Mike Nakamura walked Andruw Jones, and Klesko hit a three-run homer to right. Louisville won 4-2. Quantrill got the win.

“I’ll buy Roger dinner,” Quantrill said. “I’ll buy him dinner for a week. He did all the work.”

Schilling

The Bats are 18-11 and have won four straight. Charlotte is 9-21. Curt Schilling, whose season has gone very differently, has watched every Clemens box score this season the way some people check stock prices.

Asked whether he’d heard what Clemens did, Schilling, reached in Charlotte, said, “I heard he didn’t get the win.”

Clemens, told of the remark, smiled for the first time all night.

“Curt’s going to say what Curt says,” he said. “Thirteen strikeouts is thirteen strikeouts.”
