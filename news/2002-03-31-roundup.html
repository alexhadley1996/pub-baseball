Roundup, March 31: Marlins 3, Cherry Blossoms 1

1 tier (MLB), 6 games noted. Lead: Marlins 3, Cherry Blossoms 1.

MLB Marlins 3, Cherry Blossoms 1 Box: WCB 1, MIA 3

The Miami Marlins opened the 2002 season with a 3–1 win over the Washington Cherry Blossoms, powered by a dominant start from Cory Lidle. Lidle worked 8 strong innings, allowing only a solo home run and scattering five other hits, putting Miami in position to secure the first win of the year.

Washington starter Johan Santana was sharp as well—6 IP, 8 K—but Miami finally broke through in the middle innings. In the 6th and 7th, the Marlins pieced together enough traffic to flip the game, highlighted by two-out RBI hits from Cristian Guzmán and Lee Stevens.

Washington center fielder Mark Kotsay had a mixed defensive inning: he airmailed a throw on a two-out single with runners aboard, allowing an extra run to score and both runners to advance, but he immediately redeemed himself by throwing out Lee Stevens at the plate on the next play.

At the plate, Jason Giambi had an “un-Giambi” day—two singles, no walks—but still loomed over every inning. Meanwhile, Cliff Floyd had a brutal opener (0-for-4, 3 K) and stranded six as Miami missed chances to blow it open.

Lidle went out for the 9th to finish what he started, but back-to-back singles (to Travis Hafner and Damian Miller) forced Miami to the bullpen. Dan Miceli entered and shut it down, helped by a key moment of comedy: Marlon Anderson attempted a sacrifice bunt and popped it directly to catcher Brian Schneider. After that, Miceli induced a deep flyout and then struck out Endy Chávez on a full count to close out Opening Day for the Fish.

MLB Other notes

  • Arizona 13, Dallas 3: Erubiel Durazo and Chris Stynes (and others) went deep as Arizona blew it open with a nine-run 3rd and three-run 4th. Dallas rookie John Lackey struggled badly (5 BB in 2.2 IP). Box: DAL 3, ARI 13
  • Yankees over Red Sox: New York won a tense opener fueled by Bengie Molina’s 7th-inning grand slam. Box: NYY 9, BOS 3
  • Barry Bonds homered in his first at-bat of the season.
  • Atlanta 8, Detroit 4: Mark Prior threw 7 scoreless and Atlanta won a game where all 12 runs were scored in the final three innings. Detroit’s bullpen melted down after Bartolo Colón gave them six strong. Box: ATL 8, DET 4
  • San Francisco 4, Brooklyn 3: Derrick Lee went 3-for-4 with a 2-run HR, but the Seals scratched out four runs off Kevin Millwood and held on. Box: SFS 4, BrGo 3
