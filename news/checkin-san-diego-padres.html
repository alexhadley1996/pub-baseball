The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

San Diego Has the ABL's Best Run Differential, and John Smoltz Is 7-0

Alexander picked the Padres to win the West on the strength of their pitching. They're +60, Smoltz is 7-0 with 72 strikeouts, and their next series is in Southside.

San Diego Padres · 23-17 · 246 runs scored, 186 allowed

Alexander's predicted standings had San Diego winning the West at 84-67, "on the strength of a staff that allows 742, the fewest in the conference." He called the rotation "one of the best rotations in the ABL" with "John Smoltz and Mark Mulder at the top." He gave the pitching a B+ and ranked the Padres fourth.

Forty games in, San Diego has scored 246 runs and allowed 186. Its +60 run differential is the best in the ABL by 19 runs, ahead of Southside's +41. A differential like that usually produces about 25 wins. The Padres have 23, so they're 23-17, three games behind Southside. Their next series is three games in Southside.

The top of the rotation

Smoltz is 7-0 with a 2.63 ERA. He has 72 strikeouts and 15 walks in 68.1 innings, and San Diego is 8-1 in his starts. He has struck out 11 or more three times. The trends piece noted the April 17 start against Mexico City: eight innings, one run, 11 strikeouts. His best start came 10 days later in Baltimore, 8.1 shutout innings with 12 strikeouts. Mulder has a 3.39 ERA. Orlando Hernandez, whom Alexander said would "round out the solid staff," has 54 strikeouts in 46.1 innings. On April 25 in Baltimore he threw a complete game, allowed one run and lost 1-0. The rotation's ERA is 4.11, and only Montreal and Baltimore have gotten more innings from their starters.

Alexander said the bullpen was "uninspiring" but "doesn't have tons of gaping holes." There is one: the closer. Joey Dawley has seven saves and a 9.00 ERA. Mike Bacsik has allowed 11 home runs in 28.1 innings. The Padres are 4-5 in one-run games and haven't played an extra-inning game. That record in close games is the main reason they're behind their run differential.

The present and future

Alexander called the lineup one where "the highs aren't too high," and he was wrong about that. San Diego ranks second in the ABL in runs per game. Phil Nevin, the "present" at first base, has moved to third and is hitting .353/.438/.588 with a 152 OPS+. On April 9 against Portland he drove in seven runs with a three-run homer and a grand slam, according to the roundup. Hank Blalock, the "future," is 21 and has a .424 on-base percentage. Randy Velarde, 39, has a 142 OPS+. On Monday in Kansas City he hit three home runs and drove in seven in a 16-2 win. The April 7 roundup credited Dustan Mohr with six RBI on "a grand slam + 2-run double" in Cincinnati. Damon Minor has seven home runs in 112 plate appearances. Shortstop is the weak spot. Jolbert Cabrera has a 59 OPS+.

The race

San Diego started 1-4, including a three-game home sweep by Pittsburgh. It went 8-2 from April 20 to 30 and has won five of its last six, including a home sweep of Anaheim. Before the trip to Southside come three at home against New Orleans. After it, San Juan visits.
