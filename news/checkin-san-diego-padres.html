What the Padres Margin Is Saying

San Diego Padres, 23-17 through 40 games

+60. That is the Padres run differential after 40 games, and it is the kind of number that makes a record look modest.

40 games, by the numbers

  • 23-17 record
  • +60 run differential (246 scored, 186 allowed)
  • 4-5 in one-run games
  • 13-8 at home
  • 10-9 on the road
  • 4.11 / 4.92 starter / reliever ERA
  • 47 home runs
  • 21 stolen bases

Through 40 games they were 23-17, scoring 246 runs and allowing 186 (+60). As of the May 13 export they are 4th in the Western Conference, 3 games back, and 5th of 20 in the ABL. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 25 wins in 40 games. They are 4-5 in one-run games.

Phil Nevin has been the Padres hitter opponents least want to see: .353/.438/.588, 8 homers, 34 driven in. Randy Velarde leads the club with 8 home runs. As a team they are hitting .303/.373/.463, 7th of 20 in the ABL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 47 home runs.

The rotation's anchor is John Smoltz: 9 starts, 68.1 innings, 2.63 ERA. Mark Mulder (3.39 ERA in 58.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.34 ERA, 2nd of 20 in the ABL; starters 4.11, relievers 4.92. Closing has mostly meant Joey Dawley: 7 saves, 9.00 ERA.

Then there is Randy Velarde, whose OPS has gone from .780 in 2001 to .989 now. Some of that is the sample. The question is how much. In the rotation, Jason Johnson has a 7.36 ERA over 40.1 innings, after a 4.09 ERA in 2001.

A couple of small, true things. Orlando Hernandez has 54 strikeouts in 46.1 innings, more than one an inning and then some. They once scored 16 runs in a game (May 13, against the Monarchs); that one game is 7% of their season's runs.

The best night was May 13, a 16-2 win over the Monarchs. The worst was May 12, a 15-4 loss to the Monarchs. Their best 10-game stretch went 8-2 (Apr 20 to Apr 30); their worst, 4-6 (Apr 2 to Apr 12).

The ABL is the middle rung, so it is the only league here where you can fall and climb in the same breath: each conference winner gets a bye, second and third play in, both finalists of the American Championship are promoted to MLB, and the bottom three are relegated to the FBL. They sit 4th in the Western Conference, 3 games back of the Black Sox. The drop is 5 games below them, where the Violets is the best of the clubs on relegation pace.

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 6 of them at home, against the Athletics, the Black Sox, the Coquis, the Gamblers, opponents with a combined 218-182 record (.545). That is a real test. The hopeful read is the run differential: if runs keep coming at this rate, the wins tend to follow, and the Padres are about 2 wins short of where that differential usually lands.
