The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

San Diego Has the ABL's Best Run Differential, and John Smoltz Is 7-0

The preseason projections had the Padres winning the West on the strength of their pitching. They're +60, Smoltz is 7-0 with 72 strikeouts, and their next series is in Southside.

San Diego Padres · 23-17 · 246 runs scored, 186 allowed

The preseason projections had San Diego winning the West at 84-67, with a staff projected to allow 742 runs, the fewest in the conference. The consensus ranked the Padres fourth and gave the pitching a B+, built on one of the best rotations in the league, with John Smoltz and Mark Mulder at the top.

Forty games in, San Diego has scored 246 runs and allowed 186. That +60 is the best run differential in the ABL by 19 runs, ahead of Southside's +41, and it usually buys about 25 wins. The Padres have 23. At 23-17 they're three games behind Southside, and their next series is three games in Southside.

The top of the rotation

Smoltz is 7-0 with a 2.63 ERA, 72 strikeouts and 15 walks in 68.1 innings, and San Diego is 8-1 when he starts. He's struck out 11 or more three times. On April 17 against Mexico City he went eight innings, allowed one run and struck out 11. Ten days later in Baltimore he was better, with 8.1 shutout innings and 12 strikeouts. Mulder has a 3.39 ERA. Orlando Hernandez, who rounds out a solid staff, has 54 strikeouts in 46.1 innings, and on April 25 in Baltimore he threw a complete game, allowed one run and lost 1-0. The rotation's ERA is 4.11, and only Montreal and Baltimore have gotten more innings from their starters.

The bullpen looked uninspiring in March, without many gaping holes. It has one, at closer. Joey Dawley has seven saves and a 9.00 ERA. Mike Bacsik has allowed 11 home runs in 28.1 innings. San Diego is 4-5 in one-run games and hasn't played an extra-inning game, and those close losses are most of the gap between the run differential and the record.

The present and future

The lineup was supposed to be steady rather than spectacular, and it has been both. San Diego ranks second in the ABL in runs per game. Phil Nevin, the present at first base, has moved to third and is hitting .353 with a 152 OPS+. On April 9 against Portland he drove in seven runs with a three-run homer and a grand slam. Hank Blalock, the future, is 21 and has a .424 on-base percentage. Randy Velarde, 39, has a 142 OPS+, and on Monday in Kansas City he hit three home runs and drove in seven in a 16-2 win. Dustan Mohr had a six-RBI day in Cincinnati on April 7, with a grand slam and a two-run double, and Damon Minor has seven homers in 112 plate appearances. The weak spot is shortstop, where Jolbert Cabrera has a 59 OPS+.

The race

San Diego started 1-4, including a home sweep by Pittsburgh, then went 8-2 from April 20 to 30 and has won five of its last six, including a home sweep of Anaheim. Three at home against New Orleans come before the trip to Southside, and San Juan visits after it. By the end of next week the Padres will know whether the run differential is going to turn into a division lead.
