The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Monterrey Started 21-4. Its Bullpen Is Why It's Still in First.

Ranked 14th, the Sultanes were supposed to struggle to get the ball to Remlinger and K-Rod. They opened 11-0 and still have the FBL's best record, but they're 4-9 since April 29.

Monterrey Sultanes · 25-13 · 226 runs scored, 175 allowed

In March the question for Monterrey was simple. A fantastic bullpen, with veteran Mike Remlinger and young Francisco Rodriguez, already nicknamed K-Rod, would make the Sultanes dangerous late in close games. The trick would be getting there, with almost no starting experience on the roster. The preseason consensus ranked them 14th, and the projections had them at 70-81.

The Sultanes are 25-13, with the best record in the FBL and a 1.5-game lead over Sacramento in the West Group. No team in the FBL has moved further from its preseason projection: 70 wins in March, about 87 now. The site gives them a 68.4% chance of promotion.

Eleven straight

Monterrey won its first 11 games, and it won them every way. On Opening Day a six-run seventh, capped by a Jeff Bagwell two-run homer, beat San Antonio 9-5, and the next day the Sultanes won 16-1. They were 4-0 with Bagwell hitting .611. On April 5 in Alaska a four-run ninth, capped by David Ross's two-run homer, won another. On April 6 Sacramento led 8-0 in the third, and Alex Ochoa went 4-for-5 with two homers, including a tying three-run shot off Octavio Dotel in the ninth, as Monterrey won 10-9 in 10.

The streak ended in the second game of an April 13 doubleheader in Carolina. Monterrey went 10-3 over the next 13 and reached 21-4 on April 28 with a 2-0 win over Louisville. Through 20 games Bagwell was a clear early MVP, with eight homers, nine doubles, 13 walks and a 263 OPS+.

Since April 29

Monterrey is 4-9 since then. It lost two of three to Louisville, Memphis and Eugene and was swept in two games by Albuquerque. The lineup hasn't been the problem. Bagwell is still hitting .348 with a 183 OPS+, though he hasn't homered since April 21. Ochoa has taken over as the main power source, with 11 home runs and a 180 OPS+. Craig Biggio, 36, is still a stud, hitting .336. Augie Ojeda is at .333. The Sultanes rank second in the FBL in OPS and first in steals, 16 of them by Dave Roberts.

The rotation has been the problem, as expected. The starters have a 5.56 ERA. Jon Garland is 2-2 with a 9.42 ERA, and Sam McConnell is at 6.14. Bronson Arroyo, who needed to really excel, has been rough, with a 5.44 ERA, though he's 5-1 and allowed one run over eight innings against Santo Domingo on Monday. Chris Capuano, 23, has been the best of the group, with a 3.68 ERA and two homers allowed in 51.1 innings.

Once the starters get through their innings, the bullpen has been everything promised. Its ERA is 2.51 in 129.1 innings. Rodriguez, 20, is 12-for-12 in save chances with 18 strikeouts and one walk. Remlinger has a 1.38 ERA, 19 strikeouts and one walk. Todd Van Poppel is 4-0, and Scott Linebrink has a 1.71 ERA. Monterrey is 5-2 in one-run games and 14-5 on the road.

Next

The weekend ended with two wins in three against Santo Domingo, including a 9-8 walk-off from five runs down in Sunday's doubleheader. The +51 run differential is the best in the FBL, and the projection still says about 87 wins. Honolulu comes in next, then three in Savannah and Richmond at home.
