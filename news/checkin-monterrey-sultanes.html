Sultanes at 40: The Run That Made the Record

Monterrey Sultanes, 25-13 through 38 games

Take away eleven games and the Sultanes are 14-13. You cannot take them away, of course; the 11-game winning streak from Apr 1 to Apr 13 happened, and it is why the record reads 25-13.

Through 38 games, all they had played when the export was taken on May 13, they were 25-13, with 226 runs scored and 175 allowed (+51). As of the May 13 export they are in first place in the West Group, and 1st of 20 in the FBL. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 23 wins in 38 games. They are 5-2 in one-run games and 1-0 in extra innings.

What is driving it

Their most productive hitter is Alex Ochoa (RF, 30), at .361/.431/.673 with 11 HR. On the bases, Dave Roberts is 16-for-21 stealing. As a team they are hitting .300/.377/.483, 1st of 20 in the FBL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 45 home runs.

Chris Capuano leads the starters with a 3.68 ERA over 51.1 innings (37 K, 16 BB). Don Lemon (4.31 ERA in 39.2 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.39 ERA, 5th of 20 in the FBL; starters 5.56, relievers 2.51. Closing has mostly meant Francisco Rodriguez: 12 saves, 2.30 ERA.

That 1.105 OPS is also the surprise. In 2001, per the export, Alex Ochoa managed .737 over 593 plate appearances, which is a long way from here. In the rotation, Jon Garland has a 9.45 ERA over 33.1 innings, after a 3.69 ERA in 2001.

The fine print is fun here. Jeff Bagwell has walked 25 times and struck out 20, a ratio that belongs to a different, more patient century. They once scored 16 runs in a game (Apr 2, against the Rebeldes); that one game is 7% of their season's runs.

The stakes

The FBL is the bottom rung and nobody gets relegated from it; the whole point is the way up. The top seven make a ladder playoff, seeds one and two are promoted outright, and the winner of the Federal Promotion round makes three. Right now the Sultanes hold the 1st seed, an automatic promotion place, 1 game ahead of the Solons.

The best night was Apr 2, a 16-1 win over the Rebeldes. The worst was Apr 17, an 8-1 loss to the Knights. Their best 10-game stretch went 10-0 (Apr 1 to Apr 10); their worst, 2-8 (Apr 29 to May 10).

Looking ahead

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 6 of them at home, against the Makani, the Bananas, the Flying Squirrels, the Juicers, opponents with a combined 193-207 record (.482). Neither a gift nor an ambush. Their record is about what their runs say it should be, which is reassuring or alarming depending on which side of .500 you are standing on. Watch Alex Ochoa: the offense has run through him.
