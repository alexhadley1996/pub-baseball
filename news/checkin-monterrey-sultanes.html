The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Monterrey Started 21-4. Its Bullpen Is Why It's Still in First.

Alexander ranked the Sultanes 14th and said the challenge would be getting the game to Remlinger and K-Rod. They opened 11-0 and still have the FBL's best record, but they're 4-9 since April 29.

Monterrey Sultanes · 25-13 · 226 runs scored, 175 allowed

Alexander's March write-up on Monterrey said the bullpen would be the strength and the starters would be the problem. "A fantastic bullpen with veteran Mike Remlinger and young stud Francisco Rodriguez, already nicknamed 'K-Rod' make the Sultanes to be feared in close games late," he wrote, "but the key will be getting to that point. There's almost no starting pitching experience." He ranked them 14th and predicted 70-81.

The Sultanes are 25-13, with the best record in the FBL and a 1.5-game lead in the West Group over Sacramento. The site's one-month review listed Monterrey as the biggest gap between Alexander's March prediction and its current projection: 70 predicted wins, 87 projected. It gives them a 68.4% chance of promotion.

Eleven straight

Monterrey won its first 11 games, and the daily roundups described several of them:

  • Opening day: "a 6-run 7th capped by a Jeff Bagwell 2-run HR in a 9-5 win" over San Antonio. The next day they won 16-1.
  • April 4: "Sultanes to 4-0; Jeff Bagwell hitting .611 through four games."
  • April 5: "four-run 9th capped by David Ross 2-run HR" in Alaska.
  • April 6: Sacramento led 8-0 in the third. Alex Ochoa "went 4-for-5 with 2 HR, including a tying 3-run HR in the 9th off Octavio Dotel," and Monterrey won 10-9 in 10.

The streak ended in the second game of an April 13 doubleheader in Carolina. Monterrey went 10-3 over its next 13 games and reached 21-4 on April 28 with a 2-0 win over Louisville. The week-three notes called Bagwell "a clear early-season MVP" with "8 HR, 9 doubles, 13 walks, and a 263 OPS+" through 20 games.

Since April 29

Since then Monterrey is 4-9. It lost two of three to Louisville, Memphis and Eugene and was swept in two games by Albuquerque. Bagwell is still hitting .348/.453/.652 with a 183 OPS+, but he hasn't homered since April 21. Ochoa has taken over as the main power source, with 11 home runs and a 180 OPS+. Craig Biggio, 36, is still one of the "studs" Alexander described, hitting .336 with a 134 OPS+. Augie Ojeda is at .333. The lineup ranks second in the FBL in OPS and first in steals, with Dave Roberts accounting for 16. It hasn't been the problem.

The rotation has, as Alexander expected. The starters have a 5.56 ERA. Jon Garland is 2-2 with a 9.42 ERA, and Sam McConnell has a 6.14 ERA. The week-three notes said "Bronson Arroyo has been rough." Arroyo, the starter Alexander said would "need to really excel," has a 5.44 ERA but is 5-1. On Monday he allowed one run over eight innings against Santo Domingo. Chris Capuano, 23, has been the best of the group, with a 3.68 ERA and two home runs allowed in 51.1 innings.

Once the starters get through their innings, the bullpen has been exactly what was promised. Its ERA is 2.51 in 129.1 innings. Rodriguez, 20, has 12 saves in 12 chances, 18 strikeouts and one walk. Remlinger has a 1.38 ERA, 19 strikeouts and one walk. Todd Van Poppel is 4-0, and Scott Linebrink has a 1.71 ERA. Monterrey is 5-2 in one-run games and 14-5 on the road.

Next

The weekend ended with two wins in three games against Santo Domingo, including a 9-8 walk-off after trailing by five in Sunday's doubleheader. The +51 run differential is the best in the FBL, and the projected record is still about 87 wins. Honolulu comes in next, followed by three games in Savannah and a home series against Richmond.
