The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Cincinnati Leads the ABL in Home Runs and Has Allowed Even More

The Reds came in with one of the ABL's most intriguing offenses, and Beltre, Teixeira and Guerrero have delivered. The deep rotation hasn't, and Cincinnati has lost five games on walk-offs.

Cincinnati Reds · 17-23 · 210 runs scored, 228 allowed

Cincinnati's spring was mostly about potential. Adrian Beltre, Mark Teixeira and D'Angelo Jimenez had real, unrealized talent, and with Vladimir Guerrero already a star, the Reds had one of the most intriguing offenses in the ABL. The pitching got a B, with Trevor Hoffman and Armando Benitez at the back of the bullpen and a rotation that looked pleasingly deep behind Vicente Padilla, Jamey Wright and Woody Williams. The consensus ranked them seventh. The projections were more skeptical, at 71-80 with 974 runs allowed.

So far the projections look closer. Cincinnati is 17-23, seventh in the Eastern Conference and six games behind Cleveland and Montreal, with a 15.6% chance of relegation.

The potential

The home runs have come. The Reds lead the ABL with 57. Beltre, 23, has 12 of them and a 128 OPS+, including two on Saturday against Pittsburgh. Teixeira, 22, has 11 homers and 37 walks, and he hit two with five RBI in an 11-5 win at Southside on April 27. Jimenez is hitting .314 and starred in a 13-6 Opening Week win over Salt Lake. Guerrero has been the star he was supposed to be, hitting .329 with a 147 OPS+, and on May 6 he drove in five in a 10-7 comeback win over New York after the Reds had trailed by five.

Catcher and the middle infield threatened to make it a very short lineup. Catcher turned out fine: Miguel Ojeda has a .401 on-base percentage. Shortstop hasn't. Jeff Branson, 35, has a .304 slugging percentage and a 59 OPS+, and with him and corner outfielders Doug Glanville and Matt Diaz at the bottom of the order, the Reds are 15th in runs per game despite leading the league in home runs.

The depth

The rotation's ERA is 5.40. Greg Swindell, 37, is 0-3 with a 5.87 ERA and 10 homers allowed in 46 innings. Wright is 1-4 with a 6.17 ERA. Brett Tomko is at 5.72. Padilla has walked 23 in 53.2 innings, and on April 12 against Montreal he was ejected after a big homer sequence. Williams has been the best of the group, with a 3.77 FIP and two home runs allowed. As a staff, the Reds have given up 60 homers, three more than their hitters have hit.

The back of the bullpen has been superb, as expected, and very thin, also as expected. Hoffman has eight saves and a 2.25 ERA. Benitez has a 1.00 ERA in only nine innings. In front of them, Tom Jacquez has a 7.11 ERA and Kevin Ohme an 8.20 ERA with three blown saves. The Reds have lost five games on walk-offs, including back-to-back nights at Southside on April 25 and 26, and they're 4-6 in one-run games and 0-2 in extras.

Lately

Cincinnati started 3-0 and was 9-7 after April 17. Then it went 2-8 from April 29 to May 9, including a sweep in Minnesota that ended on a walk-off. It finished the weekend by taking two of three from Pittsburgh at home. Next come three in Tampa Bay, three in Portland and a series with Cleveland, the team it's chasing.
