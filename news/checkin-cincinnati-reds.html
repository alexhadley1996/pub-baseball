40-Game Check-In · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Loud Season of the Reds

Cincinnati Reds, 17-23 through 40 games

The Reds have hit 57 home runs in 40 games. That is a lot of trots, and it is the simplest explanation for a 17-23 team: when the ball leaves, so do the problems.

Through 40 games they were 17-23, scoring 210 runs and allowing 228 (-18). As of the May 13 export they are 7th in the Eastern Conference, 6 games back, and 18th of 20 in the ABL. That run differential would usually produce a record around 18-22. They are 4-6 in one-run games and 0-2 in extra innings.

What is driving it

The best bat has belonged to Vladimir Guerrero, 27, who is hitting .329/.401/.611 with 9 home runs and 28 RBI in 167 plate appearances. Adrian Beltre leads the club with 12 home runs. On the bases, Doug Glanville is 12-for-14 stealing. As a team they are hitting .290/.358/.475, 9th of 20 in the ABL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 57 home runs.

In the rotation, Woody Williams has been the steadiest: 4-2 with a 4.66 ERA, 38 strikeouts and 15 walks in 46.1 innings. Vicente Padilla (4.86 ERA in 53.2 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 5.32 ERA, 11th of 20 in the ABL; starters 5.40, relievers 5.19. Closing has mostly meant Trevor Hoffman: 8 saves, 2.25 ERA.

Then there is Adrian Beltre, whose OPS has gone from .720 in 2001 to .941 now. Some of that is the sample. The question is how much. The weakest regular bat has been Jeff Branson, at .256/.329/.304 in 143 plate appearances. In the rotation, Jamey Wright has a 6.17 ERA over 35.0 innings, after a 4.90 ERA in 2001.

Odds and ends: Miguel Ojeda has walked 21 times and struck out 18, a ratio that belongs to a different, more patient century.

The stakes

The ABL is the middle rung, so it is the only league here where you can fall and climb in the same breath: each conference winner gets a bye, second and third play in, both finalists of the American Championship are promoted to MLB, and the bottom three are relegated to the FBL. They sit 7th in the Eastern Conference, 6 games back of the Spiders. The drop is 1 game below them, where the Violets is the best of the clubs on relegation pace.

The best night was Apr 18, a 13-3 win over the Monarchs. The worst was May 4, a 16-8 loss to the Knights. Their best 10-game stretch went 6-4 (Apr 8 to Apr 18); their worst, 2-8 (Apr 29 to May 9).

Looking ahead

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 2 of them at home, against the Rays, the Beavers, the Spiders, the Golden Eagles, opponents with a combined 207-193 record (.517). Neither a gift nor an ambush. One more thing to watch: Vladimir Guerrero is on the milestone watch, closing on 200 home runs. Forty games is when baseball stops being a rumor and starts being a record. The Reds have one now. It is theirs to keep or to fix.
