Raul Ibanez and the Cardinals: A 1.224 OPS Goes a Long Way

St. Louis Cardinals, 19-21 through 40 games

What does a team do with a hitter who is reaching base at a .472 clip and slugging .752? If it is the Cardinals, it goes 19-21 and hopes he does not notice.

Through 40 games they were 19-21, scoring 165 runs and allowing 156 (+9). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 19-22, 7th in the East Division, 6 games back, and 15th of 20 in MLB. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 21 wins in 40 games. They are 4-3 in one-run games and 2-2 in extra innings.

What is driving it

Raul Ibanez has 56 hits, 9 walks and 12 strikeouts in 142 trips; in 2001 he had a .847 OPS over 312 PA. Their most productive hitter is Jermaine Dye (RF, 28), at .238/.305/.477 with 8 HR. Raul Ibanez leads the club with 8 home runs. As a team they are hitting .256/.314/.412, 14th of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 37 home runs.

Horacio Ramirez leads the starters with a 2.34 ERA over 61.2 innings (33 K, 12 BB). Corey Lee (3.46 ERA in 39.0 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 3.42 ERA, 2nd of 20 in MLB; starters 3.61, relievers 2.98. Francisco Cordero has 6 saves without a blown save.

And it is not the line anyone would have written for him: his 2001 OPS was .847 in 312 plate appearances. The weakest regular bat has been Barry Wesson, at .215/.287/.346 in 144 plate appearances.

Some teams are built for their park; some just happen to be good there. Whatever the Cardinals are, they have been far better at home: 12-7 in their own park, 7-14 in everyone else's.

The fine print is fun here. They once scored 14 runs in a game (Apr 28, against the Braves); that one game is 8% of their season's runs.

The stakes

MLB has no wild card and no consolation prize: division winners play for the title, the last-place club in each division goes down to the ABL. The Cardinals are 6 games behind the Marlins in the East Division. The floor is 5 games below them, where the Braves currently stand on relegation pace.

The best night was Apr 28, a 14-2 win over the Braves. The worst was Apr 6, a 10-1 loss to the Gothams. Their best 10-game stretch went 7-3 (Mar 31 to Apr 9); their worst, 3-7 (Apr 16 to Apr 25).

Looking ahead

The next stretch is 10 games (5 at home) against the Blue Jays, the Phillies, the Cubs, the Rockies. Those clubs are 198-212 combined, a .483 winning percentage, so it will mostly be up to them. The hopeful read is the run differential: if runs keep coming at this rate, the wins tend to follow, and the Cardinals are about 2 wins short of where that differential usually lands. In six weeks the Cardinals have shown us who they might be. The next six will show us who they are willing to keep being.
