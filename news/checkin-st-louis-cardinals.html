The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Raul Ibanez Is Hitting .429 as a Catcher, and the Cardinals Are Still Under .500

St. Louis has the second-best ERA in MLB, an even run differential, a 40-9 series against Atlanta and a five-game losing streak. Whatever is working, the Cardinals should keep doing it.

St. Louis Cardinals · 19-22 · 167 runs scored, 167 allowed

Whatever the St. Louis Cardinals are doing, they should keep doing it, except for most of it. Start with the part that's working, which is Raul Ibanez.

Ibanez has started 32 games at catcher and is hitting .429 with eight home runs, a 220 OPS+ and 2.9 WAR. That's 2.9 of the team's 3.7 batting WAR, one of the greatest runs anyone has seen in a while. On April 7 he went 5-for-5 with two homers in an 8-0 win over Detroit. Five days earlier his ninth-inning home run had tied a game in Dallas that the Cardinals won in 10, the third win of a 5-0 start that made them the league's last undefeated club.

Everything else

Take Ibanez out and the offense looks like the one projected in March, which showed less promise than any lineup in the league. St. Louis is 15th in runs and 16th in OPS and home runs. Kevin Mench, the DH, is hitting .215. Barry Wesson, a fourth outfielder by trade, has been starting in left and is hitting .212. Ken Harvey has a .308 slugging percentage at first base. And the middle infield got thinner when Khalil Greene sprained his knee legging out a double on April 5. He hasn't played since.

The pitching results are excellent, and the process is shakier. The Cardinals have a 3.58 ERA, second in MLB, and have allowed 167 runs, exactly as many as they've scored. Horacio Ramirez and Paul Byrd have been surviving on low batting averages on balls in play, Ramirez much more so, and a prayer. Ramirez has a 2.34 ERA, and his best start was a loss: eight innings, one hit, 1-0 in San Francisco on May 2. Byrd has a 3.52 FIP and an 0-7 record. Zack Greinke, the 18-year-old expected to steady the rotation, has a 4.20 ERA and one issue, which is a big one: 16 home runs allowed in 49.1 innings.

The bullpen is the one part of the staff that looks built to last. Justin Duchscherer hasn't allowed a run in 17 innings, Darren Holmes has a 0.95 ERA, and Francisco Cordero, with 22 strikeouts in 14.1 innings, looks like a legitimate closer.

Forty to nine

The season has come in streaks. After the 5-0 start, St. Louis went 1-7 from April 18 to 25 against the Yankees, Oakland and Washington. Then Atlanta came to town at the end of April, and the Cardinals won 13-3, 14-2 and 13-4, outscoring the Braves 40-9 in the beating of the year. If only every series could be against the Braves: outside those three games, the Cardinals have been outscored by 31 runs.

Since then they've been swept in San Francisco, taken two of three from Arizona and two of three in Boston, and lost five straight, three in Los Angeles in which they scored six runs total and an 11-2 home loss to Toronto on Monday. They're 7-14 on the road.

Ahead

The preseason projections had St. Louis at 73-78, and that still looks about right: a team that wins around 70 games, not one that finishes above .500. Greene's return and Greinke's growth should help. Ibanez will come back down to earth, unless he has suddenly become the best contact hitter in the game with some pop. The Cardinals are tied with Toronto for seventh in the East, three games ahead of Boston and five ahead of last-place Atlanta, and the site gives them a 4.2% chance of relegation. Next up are two more with the Blue Jays, three in Philadelphia and three at home with the Cubs. Another week like the last one would put the relegation race in view.
