The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Raul Ibanez Is Hitting .429 as a Catcher, and the Cardinals Are Still Under .500

St. Louis has the second-best ERA in MLB, an even run differential, a 40-9 series against Atlanta, and a five-game losing streak. Alexander's advice: "just keep doing it. Aside from all the process stuff."

St. Louis Cardinals · 19-22 · 167 runs scored, 167 allowed

Alexander's 40-game entry on St. Louis begins: "Whatever the St Louis Cardinals are doing, just keep doing it. Aside from all the process stuff. Get rid of that stuff." It's the most accurate summary anyone could write about this team.

Start with Raul Ibanez. He has started 32 games at catcher and is hitting .429/.466/.737 with eight home runs, a 220 OPS+ and 2.9 WAR. By Alexander's count that is 2.9 of the team's 3.7 batting WAR, "one of the greatest runs we've seen in a while." On April 7 he went 5-for-5 with two homers in an 8-0 win over Detroit. Back on April 2 his ninth-inning home run tied a game in Dallas that the Cardinals won in 10, the third win of a 5-0 start. The roundup that night called them "the league's lone undefeated club."

The process stuff

Take Ibanez out of the lineup and the offense is what Alexander expected in March, when he wrote that it "shows probably less promise than any team in the league." St. Louis is 15th in runs, 16th in OPS and 16th in home runs. Kevin Mench, the regular DH, is at .215/.298/.361. Barry Wesson, whom Alexander calls the fourth outfielder, has been starting in left and is hitting .212. Ken Harvey has a .308 slugging percentage at first base. The middle infield got worse when Khalil Greene sprained his knee "legging out a double on April 5th." He has been out ever since.

The pitching worries Alexander almost as much, even though the results are excellent. The Cardinals have a 3.58 ERA, second in MLB, and have allowed 167 runs, exactly as many as they've scored. Alexander's notes say Horacio Ramirez and Paul Byrd "have been surviving on low BABIPs (Horacio much more so) and a prayer." Ramirez has a 2.34 ERA. His best start was a loss: eight innings, one hit, 1-0 in San Francisco on May 2. Byrd has a 3.52 FIP and an 0-7 record. Zack Greinke, the 18-year-old Alexander thought could save the rotation, has a 4.20 ERA, but he's allowed 16 home runs in 49.1 innings. Alexander noted that "Greinke's only issue has been home runs, though that's been a big issue." The bullpen is the one part of the staff he's sold on. Justin Duchscherer hasn't allowed a run in 17 innings, Darren Holmes has a 0.95 ERA, and Francisco Cordero, with 22 strikeouts in 14.1 innings, "looks like a legit closer."

Forty to nine

The season has come in streaks. After the 5-0 start came a 1-7 stretch from April 18 to 25 against the Yankees, Oakland and Washington. Then Atlanta came to St. Louis for three games at the end of April, and St. Louis won 13-3, 14-2 and 13-4. "In three games in St. Louis, the Cards outscored the Braves 40-9 for the ass-whooping of the year," Alexander wrote. "If only every series could be against the Braves." Without those three games, the Cardinals have been outscored by 31 runs.

Since then they've been swept in San Francisco, won two of three over Arizona and two of three in Boston, and lost five straight: a sweep in Los Angeles in which they scored six runs and an 11-2 home loss to Toronto on Monday. They're 7-14 on the road.

Ahead

Alexander's March prediction was 73-78. His 40-game outlook is basically the same: "still around a 70 win team, definitely not above .500." He thinks Greene's return and Greinke's development will help, and that Ibanez "obviously will come back down to earth (that, or Ibanez is all the sudden the greatest contact hitter in the game along with some pop)." The site gives St. Louis a 4.2% chance of relegation. They're tied with Toronto for seventh in the East. Boston is three games behind them, and Atlanta, in last place, is five back. The next week brings two more against the Blue Jays, three in Philadelphia and three at home with the Cubs. Another week like the last one would bring the relegation race into view.
