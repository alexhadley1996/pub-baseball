Team Personalities Report: Clubhouses, Chemistry and Volatility

The clubhouse map for 2002: Atlanta's two disruptive arms, Montreal's Richard Hidalgo problem, Tike Redman's Phillies, and Orlando, the only club with two true fan favorites.

OOTP tracks personalities, which means somewhere in this universe a computer has decided that Richard Hidalgo is a problem. This is the clubhouse map for 2002: who is disruptive, who is selfish, who is a sparkplug, and which of those labels might actually show up in the standings.

A caveat before the tables: personality is the kind of thing that explains a season afterwards better than it predicts one beforehand. A humble clubhouse that goes 60-91 is still a 60-91 team. But the traits do move morale in the game, and morale moves performance at the margins, so here is where the margins are.

The MLB story is Philadelphia, which pairs a humble roster with the clearest leader in the league, Tike Redman, a .285 hitter with 24 steals in 2001. At the other end, Atlanta has two disruptive pitchers on a staff that already projects to allow the most runs in MLB (838), which is a bad place for friction.

In the ABL, Montreal is the case to watch: a disruptive Hidalgo (.275/.356/.455, 19 homers in 2001) in an outspoken clubhouse, on a team I ranked first in the preseason power rankings. Talent usually wins that argument. Minnesota and Salt Lake share the "selfish" tag, and in the FBL Orlando is the only club with two true fan favorites, which is the most fun sentence in this report.

High-Level Takeaways

  • Healthiest Clubhouses: Phillies, Angels, Memphis
  • Most Volatile: Montreal, New York Knights, Atlanta (pitching), Toronto
  • Energy-Driven Teams: Alaska, Vancouver, Seattle, Tampa
  • Motivation Risks: Santo Domingo, San Juan, Brooklyn
