The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Brewers Finally Beat a Left-Hander. It Was Tom Glavine.

Milwaukee lost its first six games against lefty starters, has a shortstop with a 7 OPS+ and a catcher outhitting Manny Ramirez. The preseason dark horse is 19-22 with roughly a one-in-ten chance of relegation.

Milwaukee Brewers · 19-22 · 163 runs scored, 183 allowed

For six weeks the Brewers could not beat a left-handed starter. Odalis Perez, Horacio Estrada, Randy Johnson, Ted Lilly, Chris George and Denny Neagle all came through and all won, which made little sense for a lineup whose best hitters are right-handed. Then on Monday, Oakland started Tom Glavine in Milwaukee, and the Brewers won 3-2 on a walk-off.

It was a good night in a season that hasn't had many. Milwaukee is 19-22, seventh in the West and seven games out, and even that flatters it a little. The run differential is -20, and the Brewers have stayed close to .500 by going 10-5 in one-run games.

Three stars and a catcher

The preseason consensus had the Brewers eighth, a dark horse built around three stars in the lineup: Carlos Beltran, Manny Ramirez and Marcus Giles. All three have been fine. Ramirez has a .393 on-base percentage and a 145 OPS+. Giles has seven home runs. Beltran has 15 steals in 18 tries, the most in the majors by a wide margin.

Quite unexpectedly, though, the best hitter on the team is the catcher. Jason LaRue, a .707 OPS hitter a year ago, is hitting .333 with 10 home runs and a 168 OPS+, outperforming all three stars. He set the tone on April 2, the Brewers' first win, with two homers and five RBI in an 11-3 rout of the Dodgers that included five home runs in all. Craig Paquette hit two of the others and had four homers in his first six games. He's cooled to .227 since. Julio Franco, 43, has a .320 on-base percentage and is 9-for-9 stealing.

And then there's shortstop. Jason Bartlett is hitting .134 in 135 plate appearances with a 7 OPS+ and -1.5 WAR, an utter fiasco. With that hole, Milwaukee is only 17th in MLB in runs.

Miller and the rest

The staff got a C in March, with Wade Miller at the top and Aaron Harang expected to take a big step forward. Miller has been an ace. He has a 3.00 ERA and 55 strikeouts in 60 innings, about 22% of the batters he's faced, and his 2-5 record tells you all you need to know about his run support. On Friday he struck out 10 at Yankee Stadium and lost 2-1. The rest of the rotation has been just terrible. Kurt Ainsworth has a 6.85 ERA and lasted 1.2 innings in New York on Sunday, and Victor Zambrano has walked 26 in 45 innings. Harang is at 5.06, though he was solid against Glavine on Monday, allowing two runs.

The bullpen has been the strength, with a 3.18 ERA. Alan Embree is the real star, with 27 strikeouts in 21.2 innings, and Jesus Colome has a 2.66 ERA.

Cubs, Rockies and the rest

The emotional peak came in mid-April. The Brewers lost a series to their rival Cubs, including a 13-inning win at Wrigley in which Franco delivered the key hit in extras. Then they swept Colorado in three tense games, 3-2, 4-2 in 10 and 2-1, and took two of three in Houston, including a 13-inning comeback from four runs down. That 7-3 stretch was their best of the year. A 3-7 slide followed, including a sweep in Brooklyn.

The tough start probably means Milwaukee spends most of the summer looking behind it rather than ahead. The offense should be fine, and a young staff should improve, which could get the Brewers back over .500, but not by much in this dogfight of a division. And the shortstop hole doesn't look pluggable from inside the organization. The site gives Milwaukee a 10.2% chance of relegation, fourth-highest in the West, behind Dallas, Arizona and the Dodgers. The Brewers are a game ahead of Dallas and two ahead of Arizona and Los Angeles. Oakland is in town for two more, then come three in Washington and three at home against Atlanta.
