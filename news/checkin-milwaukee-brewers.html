The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Brewers Finally Beat a Left-Hander. It Was Tom Glavine.

Milwaukee lost its first six games against lefty starters, has a shortstop with a 7 OPS+, and a catcher outhitting Manny Ramirez. The preseason dark horse is 19-22 with roughly a one-in-ten chance of relegation.

Milwaukee Brewers · 19-22 · 163 runs scored, 183 allowed

Alexander's 40-game notes on Milwaukee include a line that reads like a scouting report on the schedule: "They have yet to beat a lefty starter, which could be something to look for in the coming weeks, though it doesn't really make sense since their best hitters are righties." The Brewers went 0-6 against left-handed starters: Odalis Perez, Horacio Estrada, Randy Johnson, Ted Lilly, Chris George, Denny Neagle. Then on Monday, Oakland started Tom Glavine in Milwaukee, and the Brewers won 3-2 on a walk-off.

It was a good night in a season that hasn't had many. Milwaukee is 19-22, seventh in the West and seven games out. The record is better than the -20 run differential, which is mostly due to a 10-5 mark in one-run games.

Three stars and a catcher

In March the Brewers were Alexander's No. 8 club, a "dark horse" built around "three superstars in their lineup: Beltran, Manny Ramirez, and Marcus Giles." All three have been fine. Ramirez is at .268/.393/.514 with a 145 OPS+. Giles has seven home runs. Carlos Beltran has 15 steals in 18 tries, which Alexander notes leads the majors "by a wide margin." The best hitter on the team, though, is Jason LaRue. "QUITE UNEXPECTEDLY," Alexander wrote, in capitals, "CATCHER JASON LARUE has joined them at the top of the league leaders charts, and is in fact outperforming all of them." LaRue, who had a .707 OPS last year, is at .333/.376/.634 with 10 homers and a 168 OPS+. He set the tone on April 2, the Brewers' first win, with two home runs and five RBI in an 11-3 rout of the Dodgers. The roundup that day called it a "five-homer barrage."

Craig Paquette hit the other two that day and had four home runs in his first six games. Since then he has cooled to .227/.260/.383. Julio Franco, 43, has a .320 OBP and nine steals without being caught. Then there's shortstop. Jason Bartlett is hitting .134/.216/.168 in 135 plate appearances, with a 7 OPS+ and -1.5 WAR. Alexander called the experiment "an utter fiasco." As a team, Milwaukee is 17th in MLB in runs.

The rotation

Alexander's March grade for the staff was a C. He picked Wade Miller as the headliner and expected Aaron Harang to "take some big steps forward." Miller has a 3.00 ERA and 55 strikeouts in 60 innings; in Alexander's view he's the ace, with "a nice k rate of about 22%." His record is 2-5, which tells you about the run support. He struck out 10 at Yankee Stadium on Friday and lost 2-1. Behind him, the starters have been, in Alexander's words, "just terrible." Kurt Ainsworth has a 6.85 ERA and lasted 1.2 innings in New York on Sunday. Harang has a 5.06 ERA. Victor Zambrano has walked 26 in 45 innings. Harang started Monday's win over Glavine and allowed two runs.

The bullpen, with a 3.18 ERA, is the strength. Alan Embree, one of the two relief arms Alexander highlighted in March, has 27 strikeouts in 21.2 innings, and Alexander calls him "the real star." Jesus Colome has a 2.66 ERA.

Cubs, Rockies and the rest

Alexander's season summary for Milwaukee picks out mid-April: "an emotional series loss to their rival Cubs in mid-April was followed by a tense sweep of the Colorado Rockies." The Cubs series included a 13-inning win at Wrigley in which, per the roundup, Franco "delivered the key hit in extras." The Rockies sweep was 3-2, 4-2 in 10 and 2-1, and the Brewers then won two of three in Houston, including a 13-inning comeback from four runs down. That 7-3 run was the best of the year. A 3-7 slide followed, including a sweep in Brooklyn.

Alexander has two views of what comes next. In his team notes he worries that the "tough start probably means they'll mostly spend the season looking behind rather than ahead." His formal outlook is kinder: "The Brewers should be fine with this offense," the young staff should improve, and "They'll probably improve and get over .500, but not by much in this dogfight of a league." He also admits the shortstop hole "doesn't seem to be pluggable with what they have." The site leans toward the gloomier view. Milwaukee's 10.2% relegation chance is the fourth-highest in the West, behind Dallas, Arizona and the Dodgers. The Brewers are one game ahead of Dallas and two ahead of Arizona and the Dodgers. Oakland is in town for two more, then come three in Washington and three at home against Atlanta.
