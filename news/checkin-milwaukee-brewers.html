Brewers at 40: Coin Flips, Counted

Milwaukee Brewers, 18-22 through 40 games

Close games are where baseball goes to flip coins, and the Brewers have flipped a lot of them: 14 one-run games in their first 40, and they are 9-5 in them.

40 games, by the numbers

  • 18-22 record
  • -21 run differential (160 scored, 181 allowed)
  • 9-5 in one-run games
  • 11-9 at home
  • 7-13 on the road
  • 4.66 / 3.28 starter / reliever ERA
  • 47 home runs
  • 38 stolen bases

Through 40 games they were 18-22, scoring 160 runs and allowing 181 (-21). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 19-22, 7th in the West Division, 7 games back, and 14th of 20 in MLB. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 18 wins in 40 games. They are 9-5 in one-run games and 4-1 in extra innings.

Jason LaRue has been the Brewers hitter opponents least want to see: .319/.364/.630, 10 homers, 29 driven in. Carlos Beltran has 15 steals (caught 3 times). As a team they are hitting .243/.313/.401, 17th of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 47 home runs.

The rotation's anchor is Wade Miller: 9 starts, 60.0 innings, 3.00 ERA. Jay Spurgeon (4.17 ERA in 36.2 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 4.20 ERA, 12th of 20 in MLB; starters 4.66, relievers 3.28. John Bale has 6 saves and 1 blown.

That .995 OPS is also the surprise. In 2001, per the export, Jason LaRue managed .707 over 403 plate appearances, which is a long way from here. Craig Paquette has not looked like himself, or at least like his 2001 self: .791 OPS then, .640 now. In the rotation, Kurt Ainsworth has a 6.85 ERA over 47.1 innings.

The Brewers have played five extra-inning games already, going 4-1. Baseball keeps giving them more baseball, and they keep having to decide what to do with it.

Some details worth knowing. When they score three runs or fewer they are 6-14; when they score six or more, 6-1. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

The best night was Apr 2, an 11-3 win over the Dodgers. The worst was Apr 8, an 11-3 loss to the Phillies. Their best 10-game stretch went 7-3 (Apr 9 to Apr 19); their worst, 3-7 (Apr 20 to Apr 30).

MLB has no wild card and no consolation prize: division winners play for the title, the last-place club in each division goes down to the ABL. The Brewers are 7 games behind the Athletics in the West Division. The floor is 2 games below them, where the Dodgers currently stand on relegation pace.

The next stretch is 10 games (7 at home) against the Athletics, the Cherry Blossoms, the Braves, the Seals. Those clubs are 206-204 combined, a .502 winning percentage, so it will mostly be up to them. Their record is about what their runs say it should be, which is reassuring or alarming depending on which side of .500 you are standing on. Watch Jason LaRue: the offense has run through him. A quarter of a season tells you what a team is capable of. The other three quarters tell you what it is. For the Brewers, the interesting part starts now.
