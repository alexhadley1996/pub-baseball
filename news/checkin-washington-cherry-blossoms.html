The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Washington Is 13-5 at Home, 7-16 Away and Has Lost Travis Hafner

The Cherry Blossoms have a top-five OPS in MLB, some of the worst baserunning in it, a Johan Santana who strikes everyone out and a four-game losing streak that cost them Travis Hafner's foot.

Washington Cherry Blossoms · 20-21 · 187 runs scored, 195 allowed

The two preseason views of Washington could hardly have been further apart. The power rankings put the Cherry Blossoms 16th of 20, in the bottom tier, with a D for a pitching staff of castoffs and incompetents. The projections had them at 86-65, tied with Philadelphia for the best record in the East, on the strength of 1,025 projected strikeouts.

After 41 games Washington is 20-21, with a -8 run differential, the fifth-best OPS in MLB and the 17th-best run prevention. The jury is still out.

A good lineup that keeps getting thrown out

The Cherry Blossoms can hit. As a team they're batting .270 with a .437 slugging percentage. Travis Hafner, 24, was the best of them, hitting .312 with seven homers. Marlon Anderson has a 131 OPS+, Mark Kotsay a .388 on-base percentage and Jose Hernandez eight home runs. In March, Damion Easley, Hernandez and Anderson looked like hitters who shouldn't be a team's most reliable bats, but were. They still are, in a better way than anyone meant it.

The trouble starts once they're on base. Washington has stolen at a 59% clip and given away 16.5 runs on the bases, with Edgar Renteria, Hafner and Damian Miller doing most of the damage. Hafner and Miller rank among the seven worst baserunners in the league. The defense at short, second and center has been below average too, and the staff allows more fly balls than any in the league, which puts extra weight on an outfield of Kotsay, Endy Chavez and David Newhan. Chavez is hitting .201. His best night came April 15 in Atlanta, when he hit two home runs and drove in six.

Then came May 11 at Coors Field. In the eighth inning a spiked backfoot curveball from Gus Gandarillas hit Hafner and fractured his foot, and no injury of the young season looks more consequential. Washington has lost four straight, scoring 12 runs in a sweep at Colorado and then losing 11-3 in Houston.

Santana, and the rest

The pitching plan in March amounted to banking on Johan Santana panning out big time. He has. Santana, 23, has 73 strikeouts and 15 walks in 62.1 innings, and his stuff and control have been exceptional. Eleven home runs explain most of his 4.04 ERA. He struck out 12 at Colorado on Friday and lost 4-3.

The rest of the rotation has been better than its billing. Andy Ashby threw a two-hit shutout in Miami on April 1, the day after the opening loss there. Lindsay Gulin went nine innings with 10 strikeouts against St. Louis. Luke Hudson, who had caught fire with 49 strikeouts and only two homers allowed, lasted 2.1 innings and gave up seven runs in Houston on Monday. Bobby Jones, the most uninspiring of the starters in March, has a 5.05 ERA. The bullpen is in good shape. Joe Borowski and Geoff Geary both have ERAs under 3.15, and Washington has been smart about giving them the high-leverage innings.

Two sweeps up, one sweep down

The best stretch came at the end of April: home sweeps of the Cardinals and Blue Jays in a 7-3 run that pushed Washington over .500. The most memorable game came earlier. On April 9 the Cherry Blossoms trailed Oakland 6-2 and scored five times in the last three innings to win 7-6 on a walk-off, with Easley driving in four. They're 13-5 at home and 7-16 on the road, and all four walk-off wins have come at home, three of them in extras.

The road trip goes on with two more in Houston, then Milwaukee comes to town before three in Dallas. There's reason to worry. High batting averages on balls in play have propped up the offense, Hafner is gone, and the lack of pitching depth suggests a fade is coming. The floor is still well above the relegation zone, at a 2.5% chance. The question now is how long the home record can hold up everything else.
