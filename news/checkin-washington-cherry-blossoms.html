The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Washington Is 13-5 at Home, 7-16 Away and Has Lost Travis Hafner

The Cherry Blossoms have a top-five OPS in MLB, some of the worst baserunning in it, a Johan Santana who strikes everyone out, and a four-game losing streak that cost them Travis Hafner's foot.

Washington Cherry Blossoms · 20-21 · 187 runs scored, 195 allowed

Alexander had two opinions about Washington in March, and both were sincere. In his power rankings he put them 16th of 20, in the bottom tier, with a D for the pitching and a description of the staff as "castoffs and incompetents." In his predicted standings, published the same day, the Cherry Blossoms came out 86-65, tied with Philadelphia for the best record in the East, getting there, as he put it, "through 1,025 strikeouts."

After 41 games they are 20-21 with a -8 run differential, fifth-best in MLB by OPS and 17th in runs allowed. Neither March opinion has won yet.

A good lineup that keeps getting thrown out

Washington is hitting .270/.333/.437 as a team. Travis Hafner, 24, was the best of them at .312/.394/.549 with seven home runs. Marlon Anderson (.329, 131 OPS+), Mark Kotsay (.320/.388/.444) and Jose Hernandez (eight homers, 123 OPS+) were also above average, which is not what Alexander expected when he wrote that Damion Easley, Hernandez and Anderson "shouldn't be your most reliable hitters, but they are." That remains true, in a better way than he meant it.

The trouble starts when they get on base. At the 40-game mark Alexander counted "a 59% SB rate, but even worse, -16.5 BSR runs," and named Edgar Renteria, Hafner and Damian Miller as the main culprits. He wrote that the last two ranked "6th and 7th worst in the entire league." The defense at shortstop, second base and center field has been below average too. He also noticed that Washington allows the most fly balls in the league, which puts more of the load on an outfield of Kotsay, Endy Chavez and David Newhan. Chavez is hitting .201/.243/.292. His best night came on April 15 in Atlanta, when he hit two home runs and drove in six.

Then, on May 11 at Colorado, an eighth-inning pitch from Gus Gandarillas hit Hafner. In Alexander's words: "Perhaps no injury of the young season looks as consequential as Travis Hafner's fractured foot from a Gus Gandarillas' spiked backfoot curveball." His injury report called Hafner "the bigger offensive hole" in MLB. Washington has lost four straight, scoring 12 runs in a sweep at Colorado and an 11-3 loss in Houston.

Santana, and the rest of the staff

In March the plan was "banking on Johan Santana panning out big time." Santana, 23, has 73 strikeouts and 15 walks in 62.1 innings. He has also allowed 11 home runs, and that explains most of his 4.04 ERA. He struck out 12 at Colorado on Friday and lost 4-3. Alexander's 40-game view is that "his strikeout stuff and control have been exceptional, and he looks the real deal."

The rest of the rotation has been better than the March description. Andy Ashby threw a two-hit shutout in Miami on April 1, the day after the opening loss there. Lindsay Gulin went nine innings with 10 strikeouts against St. Louis. Luke Hudson has 49 strikeouts, has allowed two home runs, and was the pitcher Alexander said had "caught fire." Monday in Houston he lasted 2.1 innings and allowed seven runs. Bobby Jones, the "most uninspiring" starter in March, has a 5.05 ERA. The bullpen is in good shape: Joe Borowski and Geoff Geary both have ERAs under 3.15, and Alexander credits Washington for giving them the high-leverage innings.

Two sweeps up, one sweep down

The best stretch came at the end of April, with home sweeps of the Cardinals and the Blue Jays and a 7-3 run over 10 games that put them over .500. The most memorable game came earlier. On April 9 they trailed Oakland 6-2 and scored five runs in the last three innings to win 7-6 on a walk-off; Easley drove in four. Washington is 13-5 at home and 7-16 on the road, and all four of its walk-off wins have come at home, three of them in extra innings.

The road trip continues with two more games in Houston, then Milwaukee comes to town before three in Dallas. Alexander's outlook is blunt. He thinks the offense is "unrealistically buoyed by some high BABIPs," and the lack of pitching depth "freaks me out and makes me think they're doomed to fade pretty soon, though obviously with a floor above the relegation zone." The site puts their relegation chance at 2.5%. The season's next question is how long the home record can hold the rest up.
