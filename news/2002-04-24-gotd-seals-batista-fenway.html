MLB · 2002-04-24 · By Grady Briggs, The Cove Dispatch

Tony Batista's Stance Has Been Vindicated

Randy Johnson held the Seals to two runs, Boston led 4-2 going into the ninth, and then Tony Batista, he of the most open batting stance in the history of the sport, hit a two-run homer with two outs to tie it. Andres Torres singled home the winner. Seals 5, Red Sox 4.

I want to begin with an apology. In March, in this space, I made fun of Tony Batista’s batting stance. I said he faces the pitcher “like they’re about to have a conversation.” I said his on-base percentage was something “you could slide under a door.”

On Wednesday night at Fenway Park, with two outs in the ninth inning and the Seals down two runs, Tony Batista had a conversation with Oswaldo Verdugo, and the conversation ended in the screen above the Green Monster.

I was wrong. Not about the on-base percentage. But about the conversation.

Randy

Some context for those of you who were sensibly asleep. The Seals had lost two straight, and they walked into Fenway to face Randy Johnson, which is not what you want when you’ve lost two straight. Johnson was Johnson. He struck out six in 5⅓ innings, held the Seals to two runs, and Boston built a 4-2 lead behind him, mostly in a three-run third against Mike Hampton.

Hampton, for those keeping track, signed here for roughly the gross national product of Belgium and came into the night with a 5.64 ERA. He allowed four runs, two of them earned, in five innings. I’d like to say more about Hampton. I’d like to say a lot of things. I’ll just say that the school system in San Francisco remains excellent.

The Seals’ bullpen kept it at 4-2. David Weathers and Alex Graman got the game to the ninth. Graman, who got six outs without allowing a baserunner, got the win, and I’m pleased for him, and I’m also aware that it means very little to anybody but Alex Graman.

The ninth

Boston gave the ninth to Verdugo, who is 21 years old, throws hard and had a 9.39 ERA. I don’t know Grady Little. I’m sure he’s a fine man. I’m just saying, if I’m protecting a two-run lead against a team that came in 14-9, I’m maybe not handing the ball to the guy with the 9.39 ERA.

Verdugo got two outs. Somebody reached second. And Batista, standing in his stance, hit the ball where the people in Boston hit things when they’re happy. 4-4.

Then Andres Torres, the 24-year-old center fielder we got as a throw-in from somebody who didn’t want him, singled home the go-ahead run, and the Seals won 5-4.

A few other notes

  • Adrian Brown went 3-for-4 with two doubles and a stolen base. He is hitting .342. I’ve stopped trying to explain it.
  • Rondell White also doubled twice. “Fine” and “solid” continue to do important work.
  • Andres Galarraga is 40 and doubled. I’m not crying, you’re crying.
  • Jimmy Rollins stole a base and walked. He’s still hitting .245. Leave him alone.

The Seals are 15-9. The Red Sox are 11-13, and the people around me in the Fenway bleachers were saying things about their bullpen that I won’t repeat, partly because some of them were in Spanish and I only understood the gist.

Let’s go, Seals. It still sounds weird. It sounded a little better tonight.
