2002 Award Predictions: MVP and Cy Young, All Three Leagues

Eighteen names, three leagues: Bonds's 73 homers move to the Bronx, Clemens's 20-3 moves to Louisville, and a Mexican winter-league rookie sneaks onto the FBL MVP list.

Award picks are a way of saying, in March, which players you will be most annoyed to be wrong about in September. Here are mine: three MVP candidates and three Cy Young candidates in each league, eighteen names in all, and at least one of them is a rookie nobody had heard of in January.

Major League Baseball (MLB)

The MLB MVP race starts with the obvious. Barry Bonds hit .328/.515/.863 with 73 home runs in 2001, and he now does it in pinstripes. Alex Rodriguez (52 homers, 8.9 WAR in Dallas last year) moves to Houston, and Todd Helton (.336/.432/.685, 49 homers) leaves Coors Field for Wrigley, which is the only reason he is third. The Cy Young list is three aces in three new uniforms: Pedro Martinez, who threw only 116.2 innings in 2001 but struck out 163 with a 2.39 ERA, is a Dodger; Randy Johnson (21-6, 2.49, 372 strikeouts) is in Boston; and Mark Prior is the rookie wild card in Atlanta, on a team bad enough that my own projection sheet has him going 9-14 with a 3.62 ERA anyway.

The ABL MVP list is three hitters who would have been stars anywhere in 2001. Ivan Rodriguez (.308/.347/.541, 25 homers in 2001) catches for Southside; Luis Gonzalez hit 57 home runs last season and now gets to do it in Kansas City; Mike Sweeney (.304/.374/.542) anchors the Angels. On the mound, Roy Oswalt went 16-6 with a 3.02 ERA as a 2001 rookie and now carries Baltimore; Kerry Wood struck out 217 in 174.1 innings, along with 92 walks, which is the whole Kerry Wood experience; and Mike Mussina (17-11, 3.15, 214 strikeouts for the Yankees) takes his 42 walks a year to San Juan.

Federal Baseball League (FBL)

Cy YoungCurt SchillingCharlotte Knights

The FBL list is the most fun because it is the least safe. Gary Sheffield (.311/.417/.583, 36 homers) is the favorite, and he is playing shortstop for Hartford, which is a sentence that should worry Hartford's pitchers. Ichiro Suzuki hit .350 with 56 steals as a 2001 rookie in Seattle and is now an Orlando Juicer. Pablo Sanchez has no 2001 line at all: he is the Mexican winter-league find, and putting him here is a bet on bat-to-ball skill over track record. The Cy Young picks are old and very good: Curt Schilling (22-6, 2.98, 293 strikeouts against 39 walks) in Charlotte, Roy Halladay (3.08 ERA) in Hoboken, and Roger Clemens, 20-3 for the 2001 Yankees, now pitching in Louisville.
