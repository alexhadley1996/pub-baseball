The Wolverines Have a Glaus Problem (the Good Kind)

Detroit Wolverines, 24-16 through 40 games

Troy Glaus has a 1.094 OPS. You can stop reading there if you like; the rest of this is about the Wolverines, who are 24-16, but the most interesting thing about them is a 3B hitting .328/.470/.624.

Through 40 games they were 24-16, scoring 197 runs and allowing 175 (+22). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 24-17, 2nd in the East Division, 1 game back, and 4th of 20 in MLB. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 22 wins in 40 games. They are 5-7 in one-run games and 2-3 in extra innings.

What is driving it

Troy Glaus has 41 hits, 35 walks and 31 strikeouts in 164 trips; in 2001 he had a .898 OPS over 708 PA. Tim Raines has been the Wolverines hitter opponents least want to see: .296/.371/.496, 6 homers, 26 driven in. Troy Glaus leads the club with 8 home runs. As a team they are hitting .239/.336/.401, 12th of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 44 home runs.

David Cone leads the starters with a 2.84 ERA over 50.2 innings (43 K, 20 BB). Bartolo Colon (3.21 ERA in 56.0 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 3.84 ERA, 8th of 20 in MLB; starters 4.06, relievers 3.42. Closing has mostly meant Bob Howry: 5 saves, 2.05 ERA.

That 1.094 OPS is also the surprise. In 2001, per the export, Troy Glaus managed .898 over 708 plate appearances, which is a long way from here. The disappointment, at least so far, is Felipe Lopez: a .722 OPS in 2001, .525 through 160 plate appearances this year.

The Wolverines are two teams. One of them plays at home and is 15-5. The other one travels and is 9-11. Together they are 24-16, which describes neither of them.

Odds and ends: Troy Glaus has walked 35 times and struck out 31, a ratio that belongs to a different, more patient century.

The stakes

MLB has no wild card and no consolation prize: division winners play for the title, the last-place club in each division goes down to the ABL. The Wolverines are 1 game behind the Marlins in the East Division. The floor is 10 games below them, where the Braves currently stand on relegation pace.

The best night was Apr 23, a 12-3 win over the Rockies. The worst was Apr 4, a 14-2 loss to the Seals. Their best 10-game stretch went 9-1 (May 2 to May 12); their worst, 4-6 (Mar 31 to Apr 9).

Looking ahead

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 5 of them at home, against the Marlins, the Mariners, the Yankees, the Athletics, opponents with a combined 237-173 record (.578). That is a real test. There is no luck to give back and none to collect: 24 wins, about 22 expected. What changes the picture from here is health and Troy Glaus, not arithmetic. None of this is settled, which is the point of May. But the Wolverines have given us enough to argue about, and that is all you can ask of 40 games.
