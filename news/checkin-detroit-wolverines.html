The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Carlos Delgado, Catcher, and Other Things Detroit Is Getting Away With

The Wolverines are 9-1 in their last 10, a game out of first and third in MLB in runs. Along the way their career first baseman became their starting catcher, and their backup catcher started hitting like Barry Bonds.

Detroit Wolverines · 24-17 · 202 runs scored, 181 allowed

Carlos Delgado has played 761 games at first base in his big-league career and two at catcher. This season he has started 32 games behind the plate for the Detroit Wolverines, and the defensive metrics have him slightly above zero. That sounds like a joke. But Detroit is 24-17 and a game behind Miami in the East, and a lot of what this team has done falls somewhere between a joke and a plan.

A lineup that walks

Three weeks ago the lineup looked like the league's worst. On April 21 the Wolverines were striking out 23% of the time, about four points more than anyone else, and Delgado had three home runs. The one thing they did well was take a walk. They kept taking them. Detroit has drawn 186 walks, hits .241 as a team and is now third in MLB in runs per game, with 77 runs in 12 May games.

Troy Glaus is the engine. He has eight home runs, 35 walks, a .467 on-base percentage and a 194 OPS+, and he walks more than 20% of the time. On May 8 at Shea he went 4-for-5 with a homer. Delgado has a .354 on-base percentage while catching, and Tim Raines, 42, is hitting .287. Raines is now being tested at second base, too. Terry Shumpert's hamstring strain, expected to cost him about five weeks, pushed him in from left field, which can only crater the defense further.

The most absurd line on the roster belongs to the man Delgado displaced. B.J. Waszgis, the backup catcher, has five home runs in 42 plate appearances, an .806 slugging percentage and 1.0 WAR, the same as Delgado in less than a third of the playing time. The holes are just as real. Hee-seop Choi, the everyday DH, is hitting .186 with 46 strikeouts, though a .240 batting average on balls in play suggests better days ahead, unless he's just that slow. Shortstop Felipe Lopez is hitting .185 and is at -1.2 WAR.

Pitching in front of that defense

The pitching staff has been excellent by FIP and has had to play in front of a terrible defense. Bartolo Colon is the clearest case. He has a 3.21 ERA, a 2.52 FIP and 1.8 WAR, and on May 10 he threw a three-hit shutout of Boston. He also had the worst start on the staff, nine runs in 1.2 innings at Coors Field on April 24. David Cone, 39, has a 3.00 ERA with incredible luck on balls in play, which is shocking on this team. In the bullpen, Bob Howry has become the high-leverage arm, with five saves, 21 strikeouts in 22 innings and one home run allowed.

The preseason power rankings had Detroit seventh and called this "the most experienced pitching staff in the league," one that might allow too many homers. It has given up 35 in 41 games. The preseason projections had Detroit at 71-80, eighth in the East.

How they got here

None of this showed up right away. Detroit started 1-4, was 4-7 on April 11, and spent the rest of April trading wins and losses, finishing the month at 15-14. May has been the stretch that counts: 9-3, including three games at Shea in which the Wolverines scored 22 runs and a three-game home sweep of the Red Sox. Monday was the only sour note. Detroit blew a 5-1 lead to Miami and lost 6-5, the kind of game that comes back up in September.

From here

The East is a ten-team race for one spot in a best-of-nine World Series, with the last-place club relegated. Detroit is a game behind Miami, a game ahead of Brooklyn and two ahead of the Yankees, and the site's simulations give it a 24.7% chance at the division and about 82 projected wins. That makes the Wolverines credible contenders behind the Yankees, and there may be reinforcements: Mike Morse, 20, a super-utility man playing well in the high minors, could be up by July if they're still in it.

What decides their summer is whether the defense can possibly be this bad. Their 35-year-old second baseman is on the injured list, their 42-year-old left fielder is playing second and their first baseman is catching. It might be.
