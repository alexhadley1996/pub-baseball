The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Carlos Delgado, Catcher, and Other Things Detroit Is Getting Away With

The Wolverines are 9-1 in their last 10, a game out of first, and third in MLB in runs. Along the way their career first baseman became their starting catcher and their backup catcher started hitting like Barry Bonds.

Detroit Wolverines · 24-17 · 202 runs scored, 181 allowed

Carlos Delgado has played 761 games at first base in his top-level career, and two at catcher. This season he has started 32 games behind the plate for the Detroit Wolverines. Nobody seems to have told the defensive metrics, which have him slightly above zero. That sounds like a joke, but Detroit is 24-17 and a game behind Miami in the East, and a lot of what it's done this year falls somewhere between a joke and a plan.

The lineup Alexander gave up on (briefly)

On April 21, Alexander wrote that Detroit "might have the league's worst lineup in terms of overall production." He pointed to a 23% strikeout rate, "about four percentage points higher than anyone else," and to Delgado's three home runs. He also noted the one thing it did well: it walked, with the second-best walk rate in the league. Three weeks later the Wolverines are third in MLB in runs per game. They have drawn 186 walks and struck out 314 times. Their team batting average is .241, but they still score. They scored 125 runs in 29 games through April and 77 in 12 games in May.

The engine is Troy Glaus: .331/.467/.623, eight home runs, 35 walks, an OPS+ of 194 and 2.0 WAR. In his 40-game notes Alexander pointed to Glaus's walk rate of more than 20% and added, "Incredible!" On May 8 at Shea, Glaus went 4-for-5 with a homer. Delgado is at .230/.354/.443 while catching. Tim Raines, 42, is hitting .287/.361/.481, and the 42-year-old's defense is now being tested at second base. Terry Shumpert's hamstring strain (about five weeks, per Alexander's injury report) pushed Raines from left field to second. Alexander's comment was that it "can only crater his defense even further."

The most absurd line on the roster belongs to B.J. Waszgis, the backup catcher, who presumably would be catching more if Delgado weren't. He has 42 plate appearances, five home runs, a .389/.476/.806 line and 1.0 WAR, the same as Delgado in less than a third of the playing time. The holes are just as real. Hee-seop Choi, the full-time DH, is hitting .186 with 46 strikeouts. Shortstop Felipe Lopez is at .185/.261/.260, which works out to -1.2 WAR. Alexander thought Choi's .240 BABIP "bodes well potentially for the future (or maybe he's just that slow)."

Pitching in front of that defense

Alexander's summary of the Wolverines was blunt: "an excellent FIP pitching staff, but a TERRIBLE defense which perfectly explains it." Bartolo Colon is the clearest case. He has a 3.21 ERA, a 2.52 FIP and 1.8 WAR, and on May 10 he threw a three-hit shutout of Boston. Colon has also had the worst start on the staff: nine runs in 1.2 innings at Coors Field on April 24. David Cone, 39, has a 3.00 ERA. Alexander's view is that Cone has had "incredible BABIP help (shocking on this team)." Bob Howry has become the high-leverage reliever, and Alexander's line on him holds: one home run allowed, 21 strikeouts in 22 innings, and five saves.

The preseason power rankings, in which Alexander ranked Detroit seventh, called this "the most experienced pitching staff in the league" and predicted it would "allow far too many homers." It has allowed 35 in 41 games, which is not far too many. His predicted standings had Detroit 71-80, eighth in the East.

How they got here

Detroit started 1-4, was 4-7 on April 11, and spent the rest of April trading wins and losses, finishing the month at 15-14. May has been the stretch that matters: 9-3, including three games at Shea in which the Wolverines scored 22 runs, and a three-game home sweep of the Red Sox. The last game of the export was the only sour note. Detroit blew a 5-1 lead to Miami on May 13 and lost 6-5, the kind of game that decides a division in September.

From here

MLB's East is a ten-team race for one spot in a best-of-nine World Series, with the last-place club relegated. Detroit is one game behind Miami, one ahead of Brooklyn and two ahead of the Yankees. The site's simulations give the Wolverines a 24.7% chance to win the division and project about 82 wins. Alexander's outlook calls them "credible contenders" behind the Yankees. He mentioned that Mike Morse, 20, a super-utility man playing well in the high minors, could be up by July if Detroit is still in it. He finished with the question that will decide this team's summer: "The defense can't possibly be THIS bad, could it?" Detroit's 35-year-old second baseman is on the injured list, its 42-year-old left fielder is playing second, and its first baseman is catching. It might be.
