The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Dallas Has 25 Home Runs. Barry Bonds Has 20.

The Griffins have the worst offense in MLB by every measure on this site. They also swept Houston and Boston in an 8-2 April run, and they sit one game above the West's relegation line.

Dallas Griffins · 18-23 · 142 runs scored, 175 allowed

As a team, the Dallas Griffins have hit 25 home runs in 41 games. Barry Bonds, by himself, has hit 20. Dallas is last in MLB in runs per game, last in home runs and last in OPS, and the team is hitting .235 with a .304 on-base percentage.

The Griffins are also 18-23, eighth in the West and one game clear of the bottom two. In MLB the last-place team in each division goes down, and that race matters a lot more to Dallas than anything happening above it.

Two hitters and vibes

The lineup is two hitters and vibes. Jose Fernandez has nine homers, 33 RBI and a 145 OPS+, and on May 8 he drove in five in a 6-3 win over Miami. Lew Ford, 25, is hitting .291, is 8-for-8 stealing bases and ranks third in the league in outfield arm runs, a potential diamond in the rough. After that, seven regulars have OPS+ marks between 72 and 38. Mike Mordecai is at 52 at third. Emil Brown has 31 strikeouts and a .268 on-base percentage. Javy Lopez, a .747 OPS hitter a year ago, is hitting .224.

The one young bat Dallas was excited about in March has struggled the most. Edwin Encarnacion, the power prospect, is hitting .183 with three walks and 12 strikeouts, and he looks like he needs a trip back to the minors before big-league pitching spoils him. The Griffins also lost shortstop Adam Everett to a broken kneecap that will cost him 9-10 months. He was hitting .194, so what they lost was the glove, not the bat.

The pitching is fine, sort of

The glove matters here, because the pitching has been better than the D- it got in March. The Griffins' 3.90 ERA is ninth in MLB, and the infield defense of Pokey Reese and Everett helped them get groundballs on 54% of balls in play, the best rate in the league. The underlying numbers are less convincing. Dallas has walked 160 batters, the second-highest walk rate in the league, and doesn't strike many people out. Chris Haney is the whole story in one line: 30 strikeouts, 24 walks and a 2.45 ERA that can't be denied. The bullpen is better on every count. David Riske, Carlos Almanzar and Masao Kida all have ERAs under 1.90.

Eight of ten, then nothing

For about 10 days in April, the Griffins could beat anybody. They went 8-2 from April 19 to 29. They swept Houston, their hated in-state rival, allowing five runs in three games. They took two of three in Milwaukee. Then they swept Boston, starting with an 8-7 walk-off win in 13 innings on April 27 in which Mordecai had four hits. That got them to 15-13.

Then Brooklyn came to town and won 5-1, 9-2 and 6-0, and Detroit swept them after that. Six straight losses, 44 runs allowed and 13 scored. Dallas is 3-9 in May and lost 9-2 to the Yankees at home on Monday.

Where this goes

The two preseason views of Dallas disagreed. The projections had the Griffins at 83-68, while the power rankings put them 17th with a D on offense. Six weeks in, the rankings look closer. This offense looks pretty doomed, and Dallas probably falls to the bottom if either Arizona or the Dodgers pulls it together, which is far from guaranteed. The site gives the Griffins a 20.4% chance of relegation, lower than Arizona's or Los Angeles's, but not by much. Next up are one more with the Yankees, three in Oakland and Washington at home.
