The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Dallas Has 25 Home Runs. Barry Bonds Has 20.

The Griffins have the worst offense in MLB by every measure on this site. They also swept Houston and Boston in an 8-2 April run, and they sit one game above the West's relegation line.

Dallas Griffins · 18-23 · 142 runs scored, 175 allowed

Alexander's 40-game note on Dallas states the problem in two sentences: "As a TEAM, they only have 25. That's almost the same as Barry Bonds alone." Through 41 games the count is Dallas 25, Bonds 20. They're last in MLB in runs per game, last in home runs, last in OPS, and the team line is .235/.304/.348.

They are also 18-23, eighth in the West, and one game clear of the bottom two. In MLB, the last-place team in each division goes down. That race matters more to the Griffins than anything happening above them.

Two hitters

The week-three notes summed up Dallas as "two hitters and vibes," and that is still accurate. Jose Fernandez has nine home runs, 33 RBI and a 145 OPS+. On May 8 he drove in five in a 6-3 win over Miami. Lew Ford, 25, is hitting .291/.360/.480, has stolen eight bases without being caught, and in Alexander's notes ranks "third in the league in ARM runs." He called Ford "a potential diamond in the rough." The rest of the lineup is the reason "seven players with OPS+ between 72 and 38" is a sentence Alexander could write. Mike Mordecai has a 52 OPS+ at third. Emil Brown has 31 strikeouts and a .268 OBP. Javy Lopez, a .747 OPS hitter in 2001, is at .224/.298/.328.

The one young hitter Alexander was excited about in March, Edwin Encarnacion, "an exciting up and coming power prospect," is hitting .183/.222/.233 with three walks and 12 strikeouts. At 40 games Alexander thought he should go back to the minors: "he looks like he's about to spoil, left up here in the majors without the wherewithal to compete." Dallas also lost shortstop Adam Everett to a broken kneecap; the injury report lists him out 9-10 months. He was hitting .194, so as Alexander put it, "the loss is the glove, not the bat."

The pitching is fine, sort of

The Griffins' 3.90 ERA is ninth in MLB. The pitching grade in March was a D-. Alexander isn't convinced by the improvement. Dallas has walked 160 batters, which Alexander counts as "the second highest walk rate in the league," and the strikeout numbers are low. The infield defense of Pokey Reese and Everett, has helped too; Alexander points out that "their 54% GO record is tops in the league." Chris Haney has a 2.45 ERA with 30 strikeouts and 24 walks. "His 30/24 K/BB ratio doesn't inspire much confidence, yet his 2.49 ERA can't be denied," Alexander wrote. The bullpen is better on every count: David Riske, Carlos Almanzar and Masao Kida all have ERAs under 1.90.

Eight of ten, then nothing

For about 10 days in April, the Griffins could beat anybody. From April 19 to 29 they went 8-2. They swept Houston at home, allowing five runs in three games; the Astros' notes call Dallas their "hated intra-state rival." They took two of three in Milwaukee, then swept Boston, starting with an 8-7 walk-off win in 13 innings on April 27 in which Mordecai had four hits. The sweep put them at 15-13.

Then Brooklyn came to town and won 5-1, 9-2 and 6-0, and Detroit swept them in Detroit after that. That made six straight losses, with 44 runs allowed and 13 scored. Dallas is 3-9 in May and was beaten 9-2 by the Yankees at home on Monday.

Where this goes

Alexander's predicted standings had Dallas at 83-68 in March, even though his power rankings put them 17th with a D on offense. His 40-game outlook takes the second view: "This offense looks pretty doomed; expect the Griffins to fall to relegation if either the Diamondbacks or Dodgers pull it together, though I guess that's far from guaranteed." The site's simulations give Dallas a 20.4% chance of relegation. That's lower than Arizona's or Los Angeles's, but not by much. The next week brings the Yankees for one more, three in Oakland, then Washington at home.
