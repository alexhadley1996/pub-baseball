The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Oklahoma City Has the FBL's Worst Pitching and an 8-2 Record in One-Run Games

Alexander called the rotation "Sabathia and questions." Sabathia is 1-5, the Twisters' rotation ERA is 6.17, and Frank Thomas missed four weeks. Even so, OKC is only four games under .500.

Oklahoma City Twisters · 18-22 · 193 runs scored, 229 allowed

Alexander's March note on Oklahoma City's pitching was short: "Ricardo Rincon leads a solid bullpen, but CC Sabathia is the big piece. The big man just came off his rookie season and looks like a future ace. Other than him, the rotation leaves a lot to be desired." His summary table put it more simply: the rotation is "Sabathia and questions."

Both parts of that turned out worse than expected. The Twisters have a 5.41 team ERA, the worst in the FBL, and they've allowed more runs per game than anyone. The rotation's ERA is 6.17. Sabathia, 21, is 1-5 with a 5.85 ERA and 10 home runs allowed in 52.1 innings. He has struck out 41, including 10 on May 2 against Savannah in a game he still lost. The April 12 roundup noted a two-run homer Andruw Jones hit off him in an 8-7 loss to Louisville. The other starters are young or very old. Mike Gosling is 21, Kirk Saarloos 22, and Leonardo Gonzalez 18. All three have ERAs above 5.00. Mercedes Esquer, 42, has a 6.11 ERA in six starts.

How they're 18-22

Oklahoma City has allowed 229 runs and scored 193. A -36 run differential usually means a team around 17-23, so the Twisters are about a game ahead of that. The difference is close games. They're 8-2 in one-run games, and the bullpen, as Alexander expected, has been the solid part of the staff. Rincon has seven saves and no blown saves. Matt Miller has six saves, and Brian Fuentes is 4-1 with a 3.13 ERA. The relievers have blown two saves all season. Their ERA is 4.35, nearly two runs better than the rotation's.

The roundups describe several of those close wins. On April 4 they beat Inland Empire 8-7 on a walk-off after a "rally off closer Jerrod Riggan." Three days later, "Ben Grieve 3-run double provided the margin" in an 8-7 win in Vancouver, the day after the Mounties had beaten them 13-0.

Thomas

"An aging Frank Thomas looks to lead the Twisters," Alexander wrote. Thomas went down on April 5. Alexander's injury report listed a concussion and called it "Heart of lineup missing." He didn't play again until May 2, and Oklahoma City went 10-14 without him. Ed Sprague, who has played first base, is hitting .160 with a 40 OPS+. Thomas has been very good since coming back. He's hitting .321/.418/.661 with a 175 OPS+ in 67 plate appearances, including two homers and six RBI in a 14-3 win over Savannah on May 3. The Twisters are 6-5 since his return.

Paul Lo Duca, the "valuable catcher," has an 83 OPS+, down from a .917 OPS in 2001. His backup, 35-year-old Bill Haselman, is hitting .352. Danny Bautista has been the most productive regular, with a 116 OPS+ and 1.3 WAR. On May 1 he went 6-for-7 in a 13-inning loss to Savannah. On Saturday he drove in seven in the 17-3 win over Hartford. Bryant Nelson is hitting .322 in center. Shortstop Bobby Crosby, 22, has a 72 OPS+.

Next

The Twisters are 18-22, eighth in the West Group and eight games back, with a 7.0% chance at the playoffs. That's close to Alexander's 69-82 prediction. They have the odd split of a 9-7 road record and a 9-15 home record. They won two of three from Hartford over the weekend. Next come three games in San Antonio, a home series against Alaska and three in Sacramento.
