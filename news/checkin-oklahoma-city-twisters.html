The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Oklahoma City Has the FBL's Worst Pitching and an 8-2 Record in One-Run Games

The rotation came in as Sabathia and questions. Sabathia is 1-5, the Twisters' rotation ERA is 6.17 and Frank Thomas missed four weeks. Even so, OKC is only four games under .500.

Oklahoma City Twisters · 18-22 · 193 runs scored, 229 allowed

Oklahoma City's pitching came into the season as CC Sabathia and questions. Ricardo Rincon led a solid bullpen, and Sabathia, fresh off his rookie season, looked like a future ace. The rest of the rotation left a lot to be desired.

All of it has gone worse than expected. The Twisters have a 5.41 team ERA, the worst in the FBL, and allow more runs per game than anyone. The rotation is at 6.17. Sabathia, 21, is 1-5 with a 5.85 ERA and 10 homers allowed in 52.1 innings. He has 41 strikeouts, 10 of them on May 2 against Savannah in a game he still lost, and Andruw Jones took him deep for two runs in an 8-7 loss to Louisville on April 12. The rest of the rotation is either very young or very old. Mike Gosling is 21, Kirk Saarloos 22 and Leonardo Gonzalez 18, and all three have ERAs over 5.00. Mercedes Esquer, 42, has a 6.11 ERA in six starts.

How they're 18-22

Oklahoma City has allowed 229 runs and scored 193. A -36 run differential usually means a team around 17-23, so the Twisters are about a game ahead of where they should be, and the reason is close games. They're 8-2 in one-run games, and the bullpen has been the solid part of the staff it was supposed to be. Rincon has seven saves and no blown saves, Matt Miller has six saves, and Brian Fuentes is 4-1 with a 3.13 ERA. The relievers have blown two saves all year, and their 4.35 ERA is nearly two runs better than the rotation's.

The close wins started early. On April 4 they beat Inland Empire 8-7 on a walk-off after rallying against closer Jerrod Riggan. Three days later Ben Grieve's three-run double was the difference in an 8-7 win in Vancouver, the day after the Mounties had beaten them 13-0.

Thomas

An aging Frank Thomas was supposed to lead the lineup. He went down on April 5 with a concussion, leaving a hole in the heart of the order, and didn't play again until May 2. Oklahoma City went 10-14 without him. Ed Sprague, who played first in his place, is hitting .160 with a 40 OPS+. Thomas has been very good since he came back, hitting .321 with a 175 OPS+ in 67 plate appearances, including two homers and six RBI in a 14-3 win over Savannah on May 3. The Twisters are 6-5 with him back.

Paul Lo Duca, the valuable catcher, has an 83 OPS+, a long way from his .917 OPS in 2001. His backup, 35-year-old Bill Haselman, is hitting .352. Danny Bautista has been the most productive regular, with a 116 OPS+ and 1.3 WAR. He went 6-for-7 in a 13-inning loss to Savannah on May 1 and drove in seven in Saturday's 17-3 win over Hartford. Bryant Nelson is hitting .322 in center. Shortstop Bobby Crosby, 22, has a 72 OPS+.

Next

The Twisters are 18-22, eighth in the West Group and eight games back, with a 7.0% chance at the playoffs, close to the preseason projection of 69-82. Oddly, they've been better on the road, 9-7, than at home, 9-15. They took two of three from Hartford over the weekend. Next come three in San Antonio, Alaska at home and three in Sacramento.
