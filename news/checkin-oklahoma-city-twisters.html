40-Game Check-In · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Twisters Live by One Run

Oklahoma City Twisters, 18-22 through 40 games

One run. That is the margin that has defined the Oklahoma City season: ten one-run games, a 8-2 record in them, and a 18-22 record overall that would look very different with a couple of those flipped.

40 games, by the numbers

  • 18-22 record
  • -36 run differential (193 scored, 229 allowed)
  • 8-2 in one-run games
  • 9-15 at home
  • 9-7 on the road
  • 6.17 / 4.35 starter / reliever ERA
  • 34 home runs
  • 31 stolen bases

Through 40 games they were 18-22, scoring 193 runs and allowing 229 (-36). As of the May 13 export they are 8th in the West Group, 8 games back, and 16th of 20 in the FBL. By the old Pythagorean arithmetic, that is a 17-win team so far. They are 8-2 in one-run games and 0-2 in extra innings.

The best bat has belonged to Danny Bautista, 29, who is hitting .305/.355/.487 with 6 home runs and 29 RBI in 169 plate appearances. On the bases, Damian Jackson is 9-for-13 stealing. As a team they are hitting .277/.341/.421, 13th of 20 in the FBL by OPS through each club's first 40, with 34 home runs.

In the rotation, Mike Gosling has been the steadiest: 2-2 with a 5.74 ERA, 25 strikeouts and 18 walks in 42.1 innings. CC Sabathia (5.85 ERA in 52.1 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 5.41 ERA, 20th of 20 in the FBL; starters 6.17, relievers 4.35. Closing has mostly meant Ricardo Rincon: 7 saves, 3.50 ERA.

The surprise might be Bill Haselman, who arrived without a 2001 top-tier line worth the name (under 150 plate appearances) and is hitting .352/.412/.429. The disappointment, at least so far, is Paul Lo Duca: a .917 OPS in 2001, .707 through 159 plate appearances this year. In the rotation, Troy Mattes has a 6.96 ERA over 32.1 innings, after a 6.00 ERA in 2001.

Some details worth knowing. When they score three runs or fewer they are 3-14; when they score six or more, 9-8. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks. Paul Lo Duca has walked 14 times and struck out 10, a ratio that belongs to a different, more patient century.

The best night was May 11, a 17-3 win over the Yard Goats. The worst was Apr 6, a 13-0 loss to the Mounties. Their best 10-game stretch went 6-4 (Apr 28 to May 8); their worst, 3-7 (Apr 8 to Apr 18).

The FBL is the bottom rung and nobody gets relegated from it; the whole point is the way up. The top seven make a ladder playoff, seeds one and two are promoted outright, and the winner of the Federal Promotion round makes three. They are 16th of 20 in the table, 3½ games behind the Larks for the last ladder place.

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 4 of them at home, against the Rebeldes, the Athletics, the Solons, the Zephyrs, opponents with a combined 204-195 record (.511). Neither a gift nor an ambush. Their record is about what their runs say it should be, which is reassuring or alarming depending on which side of .500 you are standing on. Watch Danny Bautista: the offense has run through him.
