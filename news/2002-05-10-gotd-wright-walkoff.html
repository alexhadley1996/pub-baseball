MLB · 2002-05-10 · By Owen Kirby, Diamond Daily

The Kid Hits One

David Wright, 19 and hitting .222, drove in Seattle's first run, then led off the ninth with a home run to beat Dallas 4-3. Cliff Lee's eight innings set it up, and Robb Nen won it on six pitches.

SEATTLE — David Wright is 19 years old, plays third base in the top division of professional baseball, and came to the plate on Friday night hitting .222. That’s the part of his season people talk about.

Here’s the part they’ll talk about now. With the score tied 3-3 and nobody out in the bottom of the ninth, Wright hit Cory Bailey’s pitch over the fence at Safeco Field, and the Mariners beat the Dallas Griffins 4-3 in front of 30,456.

“I’ve been waiting a while to do something like that,” Wright said. “It’s been a long month. That helps.”

Lee’s night

The home run won it, but Cliff Lee made it possible. The 23-year-old pitched eight innings, struck out seven and walked two. He gave up three runs, all in a two-inning stretch: a solo homer by Jose Mayobanex Fernandez in the fourth, then a triple by 20-year-old Jeremy Reed that scored a run in the fifth and Jorge Nunez’s groundout that brought Reed home. Dallas led 3-1.

Lee didn’t give up another run. He set Dallas down in order in the seventh and eighth, striking out three, and when Javy Lopez singled to lead off the ninth, manager Lou Piniella went to Robb Nen. Nen got Fernandez to ground out, Lopez was thrown out trying to steal, and Mike Mordecai grounded out. Six pitches, and Nen had his third win.

“Cliff was outstanding,” Piniella said. “He gave us a chance all night. I wanted to give him the win and I couldn’t.”

Getting back even

The Mariners had taken a 1-0 lead in the first on Wright’s RBI single off Dallas starter John Lackey. They tied it back up in the fifth, with two outs. Rocco Baldelli, another 20-year-old, doubled home a run off Lackey. Dallas manager Jerry Narron brought in Masao Kida, and catcher Keith McDonald greeted him with a double that scored Baldelli. 3-3.

Lackey walked five in 4⅔ innings. Kida, Dave Riske and Bailey kept Seattle quiet until the ninth: Bailey struck out Jose Offerman and Sean Burroughs in a 1-2-3 eighth.

The ninth

Wright led off the bottom of the ninth. He had singled, walked and stolen a base earlier in the night. This time he hit Bailey’s pitch out.

“I knew,” he said. “I don’t usually know. This time I knew.”

Wright finished 2-for-4 with two RBI. He’s still under .240, and the Mariners know he’ll strike out, chase and look lost for stretches of the summer. They also know why he’s in the lineup at 19.

“People forget how young he is,” Piniella said. “He’ll be fine. Better than fine.”

The win moved Seattle to 21-17, third in the MLB West, five games behind Oakland. Dallas is 17-21.
