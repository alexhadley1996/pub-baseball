40-Game Check-In · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Staff

The Mariners and the Map Problem

Seattle Mariners, 22-18 through 40 games

Some teams are built for their park; some just happen to be good there. Whatever the Mariners are, they have been far better at home: 15-4 in their own park, 7-14 in everyone else's.

Through 40 games they were 22-18, scoring 166 runs and allowing 160 (+6). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 23-18, 3rd in the West Division, 3 games back, and 5th of 20 in MLB. Runs scored and allowed like that typically buy about 21 wins in 40 games. They are 5-5 in one-run games and 2-4 in extra innings.

What is driving it

Keith McDonald has been the Mariners hitter opponents least want to see: .306/.364/.613, 10 homers, 20 driven in. As a team they are hitting .265/.326/.419, 8th of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 42 home runs.

In the rotation, Jason Marquis has been the steadiest: 2-2 with a 2.68 ERA, 43 strikeouts and 20 walks in 53.2 innings. Jamie Brown (3.00 ERA in 48.0 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 3.81 ERA, 7th of 20 in MLB; starters 3.78, relievers 3.87. Closing has mostly meant Robb Nen: 7 saves, 3.94 ERA.

The surprise is Jose Offerman. His 2001, by the export's count, was a .716 OPS over 594 plate appearances; this year it is .892. Players do not usually get 176 points better by accident, though they do sometimes get there by April. The weakest regular bat has been Sean Burroughs, at .255/.313/.315 in 182 plate appearances. In the rotation, Kip Wells has a 5.98 ERA over 49.2 innings, after a 4.79 ERA in 2001.

The Mariners have played six extra-inning games already, going 2-4. Baseball keeps giving them more baseball, and they keep having to decide what to do with it.

A couple of small, true things. When they score three runs or fewer they are 0-12; when they score six or more, 11-0. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

The stakes

MLB has no wild card and no consolation prize: division winners play for the title, the last-place club in each division goes down to the ABL. The Mariners are 3 games behind the Athletics in the West Division. The floor is 6 games below them, where the Dodgers currently stand on relegation pace.

The best night was Apr 14, a 6-1 win over the Cardinals. The worst was May 1, an 11-4 loss to the Rockies. Their best 10-game stretch went 6-4 (Apr 17 to Apr 27); their worst, 4-6 (Apr 2 to Apr 11).

Looking ahead

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 6 of them at home, against the Gothams, the Wolverines, the Marlins, the Red Sox, opponents with a combined 225-185 record (.549). That is a real test. The record matches the runs, so there is no regression bill coming and no hidden credit either. The next 122 games will mostly be about whether Keith McDonald keeps doing this. Forty games is when baseball stops being a rumor and starts being a record. The Mariners have one now. It is theirs to keep or to fix.
