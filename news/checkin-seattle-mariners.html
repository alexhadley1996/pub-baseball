The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Mariners Were Supposed to Be Next Year's Team. They Are 16-4 at Home.

A relegation pick in March, Seattle is 23-18 and three games out in the West thanks to a run prevention unit that ranks second in MLB and a journeyman catcher with 10 home runs.

Seattle Mariners · 23-18 · 180 runs scored, 162 allowed

In March Alexander ranked the Mariners 13th of 20 and put them in the third tier, the group fighting to stay in MLB. His preview praised the youngest roster in the league and its future: David Wright, Coco Crisp, Alex Rios, Sean Burroughs, Omar Infante, Rocco Baldelli. Then he added the problem: "they'll have to survive the relegation battle to get to really see the fruits of their labor." He also called the catching "abysmal."

Forty-one games later Seattle is 23-18, third in the West, and has allowed fewer runs than every MLB club except one. The catcher leads the team in home runs. "A team that was projected for the future," Alexander wrote at the 40-game mark, "looks like they might be the team of the present."

Keith McDonald, of all people

McDonald is 29 and came into the year as a journeyman. He is hitting .302/.357/.595 with 10 home runs and a 153 OPS+, and Alexander says he "has been a stud throwing people out." The biggest of the 10 came on April 28 against the Cubs. Seattle trailed 4-0 after three innings. Two runs in the fourth and two in the seventh tied it, and with one out in the bottom of the ninth and the game tied 4-4, McDonald hit a walk-off home run. The next day finished a three-game sweep of Chicago, the club Alexander had ranked third in MLB in March. He called it "a statement sweep."

The rest of the lineup is a strange mix that works. Jose Offerman, 33, is at .338/.416/.510 and leads the club with 2.1 WAR. Miguel Tejada, "already fantastic" in the March preview, has a 129 OPS+ and went 4-for-4 with four RBI in Monday's 14-2 win over Brooklyn. The kids are holding their own, not starring. Wright is at .236 with six homers, Baldelli and Rios have OBPs in the .280s and .320s, and Burroughs has the most plate appearances on the team with one home run. Seattle is 12th in MLB in runs but third in stolen bases, and Alexander credits the baserunning and the defense of Tejada, Rios and Offerman for a lot of the gap between talent and results.

Run prevention, with an asterisk

The Mariners have allowed 162 runs, second-fewest in MLB. Jason Marquis has a 2.49 ERA and threw a one-hit shutout in Toronto on April 22. Jamie Brown has a 3.00 ERA and took a shutout into the ninth against Dallas on May 11. Cliff Lee, the "gem" of the March preview, has 52 strikeouts in 57.2 innings. Alexander's notes are skeptical of the whole thing. He says the rotation's peripherals are "poor," fifth starter Adrian Hernandez (6.52 FIP) is "abysmal," and Seattle has led the league in strand rate. Kip Wells, 5.98 ERA and 4.02 FIP, is the one he expects to get better.

The bullpen is the bigger worry. Robb Nen has 21 strikeouts and five walks in 16 innings and a 1.88 FIP, and has a 3.94 ERA anyway. Beyond Nen, Alexander wrote, "these guys are pretty awful with almost no swing and miss." Brian Bruney, 20, has allowed five homers in 8.1 innings.

Home and away

The split is the oddest number on the team's page. Seattle is 16-4 at home and 7-14 on the road. Their worst stretch came on the road, too. The Seals swept them in San Francisco in early April by 9-4, then 1-0 behind eight shutout innings from Mike Hampton, then 5-4 on a walk-off walk drawn by Jimmy Rollins. Colorado swept them on the road at the start of May, two of the three by a single run.

Alexander's preseason table had them at 74-77. They are already four games over .500, and the site's simulations give them an 8.6% chance at the division and a 0.4% chance of relegation. The next three series are at home to Brooklyn and Detroit and at Miami. That is three East contenders, so the road record will matter again quickly. Alexander's conditions for Seattle staying in the race are short: better pitching, "especially across the bullpen," and McDonald continuing to hit home runs.
