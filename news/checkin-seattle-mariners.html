The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Mariners Were Supposed to Be Next Year's Team. They're 16-4 at Home.

Ranked in the relegation tier in March, Seattle is 23-18 and three games out in the West, thanks to the second-best run prevention in MLB and a journeyman catcher with 10 home runs.

Seattle Mariners · 23-18 · 180 runs scored, 162 allowed

The Mariners came into the season as a team for later. The preseason consensus ranked them 13th of 20, in the tier fighting to stay in MLB, and the case for them was all about the future: the youngest roster in the league, with David Wright, Coco Crisp, Alex Rios, Sean Burroughs, Omar Infante and Rocco Baldelli. They'd have to survive the relegation fight to see the fruits of it. The catching, meanwhile, looked abysmal.

Forty-one games later Seattle is 23-18, third in the West, and has allowed fewer runs than every MLB club but one. The catcher leads the team in home runs. The team of the future looks like it might be the team of the present.

Keith McDonald, of all people

McDonald is 29 and came into the year a journeyman. He's hitting .302 with 10 home runs and a 153 OPS+, and he's been a stud throwing runners out. The biggest homer came on April 28 against the Cubs. Seattle trailed 4-0 after three, scratched back with two runs in the fourth and two in the seventh, and with one out in the bottom of the ninth McDonald hit a walk-off home run. The next day finished a three-game sweep of Chicago, the preseason No. 3, a statement series if there's been one.

The rest of the lineup is a strange mix that works. Jose Offerman, 33, is hitting .338 with a .416 on-base percentage and leads the club with 2.1 WAR. Miguel Tejada, already fantastic coming in, has a 129 OPS+ and went 4-for-4 with four RBI in Monday's 14-2 win over Brooklyn. The kids are holding their own rather than starring. Wright is hitting .236 with six homers, Baldelli and Rios haven't gotten on base much, and Burroughs leads the team in plate appearances with one home run. Seattle is 12th in MLB in runs but third in stolen bases, and the baserunning, along with the defense of Tejada, Rios and Offerman, covers a lot of the gap between talent and results.

Run prevention, with an asterisk

The Mariners have allowed 162 runs, second-fewest in MLB. Jason Marquis has a 2.49 ERA and threw a one-hit shutout in Toronto on April 22. Jamie Brown is at 3.00 and took a shutout into the ninth against Dallas on May 11. Cliff Lee, the gem of the staff in March, has 52 strikeouts in 57.2 innings.

The asterisk is that the underlying numbers are poor. Seattle has led the league in strand rate, and fifth starter Adrian Hernandez, with a 6.52 FIP, has been abysmal. Kip Wells, 5.98 ERA and 4.02 FIP, is the one starter likely to get better. The bullpen is the bigger worry. Robb Nen has 21 strikeouts and five walks in 16 innings and a 3.94 ERA anyway, and beyond him the relievers have been pretty awful, with almost no swing-and-miss. Brian Bruney, 20, has given up five homers in 8.1 innings.

Home and away

The oddest number on the page is the split: 16-4 at home, 7-14 on the road. The worst stretches have come on the road. The Seals swept Seattle in San Francisco in early April, 9-4, then 1-0 behind eight shutout innings from Mike Hampton, then 5-4 on a walk-off walk drawn by Jimmy Rollins. Colorado swept the Mariners at Coors at the start of May, two of the three by a run.

The preseason projections had Seattle at 74-77, and it's already four games over .500. The site gives the Mariners an 8.6% chance at the division and a 0.4% chance of relegation. Next come Brooklyn and Detroit at home and then a trip to Miami, three East contenders in a row. To stay in the race, Seattle needs better pitching, especially in the bullpen, and more home runs from its catcher.
