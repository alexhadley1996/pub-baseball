The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Inland Empire's Top Three Starters Have Held Up. Little Else Has.

Alexander ranked the 66ers last in the FBL and said days four and five would be "difficult for sure." Burnett, Lopez and Daal have been as good as promised, but the lineup's problems up the middle are worse than predicted.

Inland Empire 66ers · 16-24 · 165 runs scored, 186 allowed

Alexander ranked Inland Empire 20th of 20 in the FBL in March, and his write-up read like a list of problems. On offense, "Jeremy Giambi and Carl Everett are high OBP guys but lack power and defense and otherwise this team is sunk, especially up the middle at C, 2B, SS, and CF... it could be a slog." On the mound, "AJ Burnett, Rodrigo Lopez, and Omar Daal form a quality threesome at the top of the rotation... but the final two starters are well below replacement level and will make days 4 and 5 difficult for sure."

It has been a slog. The 66ers are 16-24, last in the West Group and 10 games behind Monterrey. They have the FBL's second-lowest OPS and are 8-14 at home. Still, his preseason prediction, 74-77, was more generous than the ranking, and the pitching has been good enough to keep the record from getting much worse.

The threesome

Daal has a 3.35 ERA, and Lopez is at 3.44. Each has allowed three home runs in about 49 innings. Burnett's ERA is 4.20, but he has 60 strikeouts in 60 innings and a 3.38 FIP. The week-three notes listed his April 17 start in Eugene, eight shutout innings in a 3-0 win, among the best of the week. Daal threw the 66ers' first shutout in Oklahoma City on April 3 ("Omar Daal shut it down"). On April 28, Lopez went 8.2 scoreless innings in a 13-0 win over Richmond. As a team, Inland Empire ranks sixth in the FBL in ERA.

The back of the rotation has been as difficult as predicted, and then some. Hideki Irabu is 3-4 with a 6.17 ERA and has allowed 12 home runs. Burnett, Lopez and Daal have allowed 11 combined in more than three times as many innings. Damian Moss had a 6.65 ERA in five starts. Alexander's injury report says his torn rotator cuff has him "effectively lost for the year." Of the veteran relievers he liked, Scott Sullivan has eight saves and a 2.77 ERA. Mike Timlin is at 4.50. Jerrod Riggan has a 10.12 ERA and three blown saves. The April 4 roundup described the "rally off closer Jerrod Riggan" in an 8-7 loss in Oklahoma City.

Sunk up the middle

The four positions Alexander named have mostly been as bad as he expected. Center fielder McKay Christensen is hitting .179/.260/.231 with a 29 OPS+. Second baseman Mark Ellis has a 67. Shortstop Luis Rivas, at 90, and catcher Robert Machado, at 96, have been closer to average. Machado also hit the season's strangest home run. On April 8 in Memphis, after the 66ers had tied the game in the ninth, "neither team scored for 10 innings until a Robert Machado solo HR in the 20th." Inland Empire won 3-2 in 20 innings. The next night, Geoff Jenkins hit a two-out, three-run walk-off home run that beat them 3-2.

The bigger problem is left field, which Alexander didn't mention. Timo Perez has a .221 on-base percentage, a 46 OPS+ and -1.2 WAR, the worst on the team. He did hit the three-run homer that capped a five-run sixth in a 6-4 win over Louisville on April 7. Giambi has been the high-OBP bat Alexander described, with a .391 on-base percentage and a 121 OPS+. Everett's on-base percentage is .324. He hit two home runs in Honolulu on April 21, in a game the 66ers lost 6-3.

What's next

Inland Empire won seven of its first 11 games, then went 2-8 and started a six-game losing streak that ran from April 20 to 26. It's 7-12 since April 23. Monday's 3-2 walk-off win over Alaska ended a two-game losing streak. The site gives the 66ers a 2.7% chance at the playoffs and projects about 68 wins. Next come three games in Sacramento, three at home against Hoboken, and a series with Carolina.
