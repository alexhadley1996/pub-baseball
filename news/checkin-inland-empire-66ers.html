The Season So Far · FBL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Inland Empire's Top Three Starters Have Held Up. Little Else Has.

The 66ers were ranked last in the FBL, with days four and five of the rotation sure to be difficult. Burnett, Lopez and Daal have been as good as promised, but the problems up the middle are even worse than expected.

Inland Empire 66ers · 16-24 · 165 runs scored, 186 allowed

The preseason consensus ranked Inland Empire 20th of 20 in the FBL, and the scouting report read like a list of problems. Jeremy Giambi and Carl Everett could get on base but lacked power and defense, and otherwise the lineup was sunk, especially up the middle at catcher, second, short and center. It could be a slog. On the mound, A.J. Burnett, Rodrigo Lopez and Omar Daal made a quality threesome at the top of the rotation, but the last two starters were well below replacement level, which would make days four and five difficult.

It has been a slog. The 66ers are 16-24, last in the West Group and 10 games behind Monterrey, with the FBL's second-lowest OPS and an 8-14 home record. The projections were a little kinder than the ranking, at 74-77, and the top of the rotation has kept things from getting much worse.

The threesome

Daal has a 3.35 ERA and Lopez a 3.44, and each has allowed three home runs in about 49 innings. Burnett's ERA is 4.20, but he has 60 strikeouts in 60 innings and a 3.38 FIP, and his April 17 start in Eugene, eight shutout innings in a 3-0 win, was one of the best of that week. Daal shut down Oklahoma City on April 3 for the 66ers' first shutout. On April 28 Lopez went 8.2 scoreless innings in a 13-0 win over Richmond. As a staff, Inland Empire ranks sixth in the FBL in ERA.

The back of the rotation has been as difficult as advertised. Hideki Irabu is 3-4 with a 6.17 ERA and has allowed 12 home runs, one more than Burnett, Lopez and Daal combined in more than three times the innings. Damian Moss had a 6.65 ERA in five starts before a torn rotator cuff effectively ended his season. Of the veteran relievers, Scott Sullivan has eight saves and a 2.77 ERA, Mike Timlin is at 4.50, and Jerrod Riggan has a 10.12 ERA and three blown saves, starting with a rally off him in an 8-7 loss in Oklahoma City on April 4.

Sunk up the middle

The four spots up the middle have mostly been as bad as feared. Center fielder McKay Christensen is hitting .179 with a 29 OPS+. Second baseman Mark Ellis is at 67. Shortstop Luis Rivas, at 90, and catcher Robert Machado, at 96, have been closer to average, and Machado hit the strangest home run of the season. On April 8 in Memphis, after the 66ers tied it in the ninth, neither team scored for 10 innings until Machado homered in the 20th, and Inland Empire won 3-2 in 20. The next night Geoff Jenkins hit a two-out, three-run walk-off homer to beat them 3-2.

The biggest hole has turned out to be in left field. Timo Perez has a .221 on-base percentage, a 46 OPS+ and -1.2 WAR, the worst on the team, though he did cap a five-run sixth with a three-run homer in a 6-4 win over Louisville on April 7. Giambi has been the on-base bat he was supposed to be, with a .391 on-base percentage and a 121 OPS+. Everett is at .324, and he homered twice in Honolulu on April 21 in a 6-3 loss.

What's next

Inland Empire won seven of its first 11, then went 2-8, a stretch that included a six-game losing streak from April 20 to 26. It's 7-12 since April 23. Monday's 3-2 walk-off win over Alaska snapped a two-game skid. The site gives the 66ers a 2.7% chance at the playoffs and projects about 68 wins. Next come three in Sacramento, Hoboken at home and a series with Carolina.
