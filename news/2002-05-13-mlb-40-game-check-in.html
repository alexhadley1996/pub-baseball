MLB at 40 Games: State of the League

Oakland’s shared-player machine, San Francisco’s run prevention, Miami’s RISP luck and a Dodgers team with no way around it: all 20 MLB clubs at the quarter mark.

Forty games is a quarter of a season, which is to say it is the point at which a hot start becomes a record and a slow start becomes a problem. Oakland and San Francisco have the best records in MLB, Miami has the best luck, and the Braves have exactly the season their projection promised. All 20 MLB clubs, from the top of the standings down.

The Oakland Atheletics have ridden their unique ‘shared player’ system to the very top of the Major Leagues. Superb run prevention thanks to very few walks and great sequencing (the highest LOB % in the league) have made an ok FIP into a great ERA (aa ERA plus of 109). Mariano Rivera hasn’t had a great start to the season with a 3.94 ERA, but the peripherals are excellent and only marred by a .370 BABIP. The rotation meanwhile, led by Freddy Garcia, Jeff D’Amico, and Tom Glavine, has been incredible. Garcia has been an absolute workhorse, pitching over 7 innings in seven straight starts, and d’Amico, despite a recent blow up in a 20-3 loss to the Astros this past week, has been a picture of consistency. Offensively, the acquisition of Corey Koskie from their feeder team, the New Orleans Athletics in early April, has helped propel the team to the 2nd highest batting average and batting WAR in MLB. Koskie started the season with New Orleans red-hot, batting .470 in his 8 games there, and after his ‘promotion’ of sorts, he has been just as good with Oakland, plus five homers. The second base position, now bereft of Brian Roberts, has been a sore spot, but the rest of the lineup has been doing its part to make up for it. Superstar rookie Albert Pujols in particular has been terrifying with 10 homers in 40 games and a .354 batting average. Pujols’ defense in left field has been terrible, and a move to first would probably be best at this point. However, manager Art Howe has stuck by his decision to keep the young player at an optimal defensive position to see if he can develop properly. The team has solid slug behind Pujols, Koskie, and a surprise barrage of homers by backup catcher Sal Fasano, and Brian Giles has been an on-base god with a 15% walk rate that outpaces his strikeout rate, making up for dismal center field defense. Some defensive reinforcements could be coming from Alaska or New Orleans though with Alaska’s Rontez Johnson tearing up the FBL with a 127 OPS plus and solid defense, and Raul Mondesi dominating the ABL for New Orleans with a 117 OPS plus and a great glove in right.

Oakland A’s Outlook: Can Corey Koskie continue his rise? Can the bullpen right the ship? As it is, this team looks like a real contender in the West as they challenge their cross-bay rivals (and superior rivals) the San Francisco Seals. The Seals took two of three in April in hotly contested, tight games, but the A’s will look to have their revenge in June and August.

The San Francisco Seals have been arguably the all-around best team in the MLB so far, despite their half game deficit to the A’s. The best defense in the league, probably the best pitching staff, and one of the better offenses in the game make the Seals a formidable opponent. Jake Peavy, not yet 20, has been incredible early in the season, particular in his past three starts in which he has pitched 19 innings and allowed only one run. 21 year old Carlos Zambrano, despite some control issues, has also been superb, and Dave Burba has backed them up. Scot Shields, David Weathers, and Julio Mateo have been excellent in the bullpen, btu the real secret sauce here is two things: the league’s best defense (particularly in the outfield) and an extremely pitcher-friendly park in ATT Park. The Seals have hit 46 home runs and allowed only 28. Benji Gil has been an absolute beast defensively to help out Jimmy Rollins in the infield who has hit well but defended poorly. The Seals have very little in terms of reinforncements, Outfielder Ruben Sierra will be out for 4 more months, but the Seals have weathered his loss pretty well. Early-season sweeps of the New York Yankees and the Seattle Mariners bode well for the Seals’ ability to succeed in the playoffs as those are tow of the stronger teams in the MLB.

San Francisco Seals Outlook: Owner Jose Contreras is extremely ambitious and will likely open the purse strings this offseason, and GM Alex Reyes looks already on his way to establishing the kind of team he wants, pitching and defense first. The Seals are the early favorites to win the West and play for the series. This week marks an important week in their early challenge, as they face both the Astros and Cubs, the preseason favorites who are hanging around .500

The Miami Marlins have undergone a recent four game skid, but before that they were the class of the east with a 24-12 record. As has been noted, their luck has been superb; the RISP numbers are incredible as are their high leverage hitting numbers as they have an extraordinary .909 OPS in those situations. They’ve been good, not great, in all facets of the game. The ballpark has suppressed home runs, but certainlyb not doubles as Bill Hall (pressed into an everyday role thanks to Carlos Febles’ injury), Carl Crawford, and Cliff Floyd have hit many, many extra base hits. Bill Hall’s perfroamnce has been adequate, but they are eager for him to return to his backup role, particularly as their current backup infielder Erick Almonte has about a .500 OPS, though recently promoted Gastulum shows promise after slugging over .600 in the high minors. Carl Crawford’s offense has been exactly average with too few walks and no homers, but his defense and baseruning have been elite; veteran Lee Stevens has been a nice surprise at the dish with nine dingers; Jason Giambi’s 14% walk rate will play and his power will catch up,and Cliff Floyd’s offensive output in his walk year has been typically excellent. The big news of the month was Giambi’s eight-year extension at 10 million dollars/year. Giambi has fallen in love with the nightlife of Miami and fits nicely with GM Bill Davidson’s emphasis on OBP guys, plus Giambi’s left-handed power won’t be too hampered by Miami’s ballpark, loanDepot Park. On the pitching side of things, Kazuhiro Sasaki has anchored a consistently solid bullpen, only blowing one save and never allowing more than a run as he sports a 5.2 K/BB ratio. Mark Wholers has been a strikeout demon and Andrew Lorraine a walk god and Dan Miceli’s been great. Ace Corey Lidle has controlled his way to a 2.16 ERA though his peripherals are less rosy, and the back of the rotation has a real problem in Ruben Quevado. He has a super-5.50 FIP and has gotten particularly hit hard via the longball, a real accomplishment in loan Depot park.

Miami Marlins Outlook: Miami looks like it will have trouble maintaining it’s position; a real lack of help coming from the minors means this is the team they’ve got, and they’re one starting pitching injury away from two real black holes there. The Yankees, Gothams, and Wolverines all look stronger, though the continued development of Giambi and Crawford cannot be discounted as they are two critical pieces in a lineup.

The Detroit Wolverines have had a strange season thus far. Look at the peripherals, and what do you find: an excellent FIP pitching staff, but a TERRIBLE defense which perfectly explains it. Look at the offense and what do you find: again, a terrible defense draining all value from its players, but actually a very solid offensive lineup, headlined by Tim Raines, Troy Glaus (201 OPS plus) and Carlos Delgado. Delgado strikes out too much but otherwise has been great and Glaus has over a 20% walk rate! Incredible! The recent injury to Terry Shumpert in the middle infield has them playing a 42-year-old Tim Raines at second, which can only crater his defense even further. Looking at the farm system, superutility-man Mike Morse is playing well in the high minors at 20 years old; don’t be surprised to see him in July if the Wolverines are hanging around. Hee-Seop Choi, their fulltime DH, has yet to take off offensively, striking out 26% of the time, but his .240 BABIP bodes well potentially for the future (or maybe he’s just that slow). Leo Prokopec and Mac Suzuki have flipped roles between #5 rotation arm and long arm in the ‘pen, but otherwise this staff has been good. Veteran David Cone has gotten incredible BABIP help (shocking on this team), though he’s been solid, but 29-year old Bartolo Colon looks like the real deal with his flyball repertoire and nasty mix. He just pitched an astounding three-hitter against the hapless Red Sox as part of the team’s recent 5 game win streak (plus, they won 9 of 10). The bullpen has taken a committee approach, but Bob Howry has emerged as their highest leverage arm, allowing only one homer and striking out 21 in 22 innings.

Detroit Wolverines Outlook: While not one of the favorites in the preseason, the Wolverines have established themselves as solid contenders in the east. The Yankees remain the favorites but among the Marlins, Gothams, and Wolverines, Detroit’s pitching staff and lineup potential make them a credible contender. The defense can’tpossibly be THIS bad, could it?

The Brooklyn Gothams, have been the league’s ultimate streaky team. They’ve been swept 3 times and swept others 4 times, but all in all, it’s lead to a respectable 23-17 record. Ironically, their offense has been incredibly average. A high contact rate and low BB and K % mean their a contact-oriented team, but in almost all stats as a team they’re middle of the road. The one place they stand out is in their infield defense, which is excellent with jack Wilson and Alex Cora, but behind the plate, a hip strain to starting catcher Tom Lampkin has left them with very little (and the effects can be seen in catcher Yorvit Torrealba’s 55 OPS plus and 25% RTO). Fernando Tatis at third base has been mashing, and Bobby Higginson and Moises Alou provide a capable corner outfield pairing (though manager continues to play Higginson at DH, which simply doesn’t make sense), but they seem to lack a true offensive star with pop to power the lineup. In Shea Stadium, Higginson should be a left-handed star, though only time will tell. Rumors are that GM Tom Smith has demanded that manager Matt Swason put Higginson in the field more often, but Swanson says he trusts Echevarria’s defense more. As with many teams in the league, the fifth rotation spot has been a problem spot; Wes Obermuller seems to have won the job from 22-year old Chris George. The peripherals of the rest of staff (other than Kevin Millwood, who has been fabulous) aren’t great, but the results are, with an excellent LOB % and a low BABIP. Millwood has been undeniably good and steady, but the rest of the rotation in Ted Lilly, Erik Hiljus, and Mike Smith (he of the more walks than strikeouts) have been highly fortunate to have their nice ERAs. Brooklyn Gothams Outlook:

Some managerial decisions could really swing things here, both in the playing of Higginson in the outfield, and in the bullpen leverage arms, but Brooklyn is probably playing in over their heads in the first six weeks of the season. The bullpen might just be that week, and the rotation almost certainly isn’t the best staff in the entire MLB with Kevin Millwood and Ted Lilly as its ‘aces.’ Expect Brooklyn to come crashing down to earth (or mid-table) pretty soon.

The Seattle Mariners have been one of the most surprising successes in the league, powered by an unusual combination of strengths covering up some significant underlying issues. The starting pitching has been solid ERA-wise, with Jason Marquis sporting a sub-3 ERA and Jaimie Brown and Cliff Lee right around there, but the underlying metrics are poor with a 105 FIP-. Most concerning is fifth starter Adrian Hernandez whose 162 FIP- is abysmal. Cliff Lee’s strikeout numbers look good though, and Kip Wells has been th victim of extremely batted ball misfortune, which bodes well. In the bullpen, Rob Nenn has been superb and just gotten extremely unlucky, but the rest of these guys are pretty awful with almost no swing and miss, and far too many fly balls. Pitching has been the beneficiary of good defense and timely outs (leading league in LOB% and lowest IRS%). On the offensive side, this team is built on good defense and baserunning. Jose Offerman has made great choices on the basepaths, and David WRight, Alex Rios, and Rocco Baldelli have combined good decisions with actual base-stealing speed. Miguel tejada, Alex Rios, and Offerman, have all shone defensively as well. The biggest shock has come at catcher, where journeyman catcher Keith Macdonald has been a stud throwing people out, and is batting over .300 at the dish. The lineup is a huge variety of players, from high contact guys like Tejada, to all pop guys like Macdonald, to high-walk, high-contact guys like Offerman, but it ends up grading out as middle-top of the league. The team has been steady, not getting too high,nor too low in the early part of the season. They suffered a key sweep to the mighty Seals early in April, but bounced back with a statement sweep of their own against the Chicago Cubs, highlighted by a Keith Macdonald walk off homer to cap off a 5 run rally on April 28th.

Seattle Mariners Outlook:

Very little in the way of reinforcements available, but it must be said, the Mariners have changed the perception of themselves greatly in these 40 games. A team that was projected for the future looks like they might be the team of the present, if their pitching can improve a bit (especailly across the bullpen) and if Keith Macdonald can keep hitting dingers.

The New York Yankees only have a 21-19 record, but they still look fearsome. They suffered their an intensely mediocre April to a 13-15 record, lowlighted by a terrible sweep at the hands of the Sanfrancisco Seals. But in May, they’ve been excellent, led by Barry Bonds, who has taken off with a 1.241 OPS, despite a terrible .220 BABIP. There’s no telling what Bonds will be able to do this year in Yankee Stadium with 20 HR in his team’s first 40 games. In May’s 10 games, Bonds has TEN homers. Stunning. Otherwise offensiveely, Lenny Harris and Torii Hunter have been great (though perhaps beneficiaries ofhigh BABIPS) with a few homers each and over 120 WwRc+. Carlos Baerga is the most intriguing player on the team, as he has been pushed out of a starting spot by a struggling Mark Grudilizanek. Will manager Joe torre make a change? It seems obvious, but he seems to want to go with the young man and keep Adam Kennedy at DH, also a confusing move. Baerga has made a comeback to the mLB for this season, and looks amazing at the plate as before with his bat-to-ball skills, but the Yankees are strangley holding him in reserve so far. They have more depth at AAA than any team in the league wil catcher Will Nieves, pitcher Ryan Glynn, and first baseman Jose Ortiz all as great pieces dominating the minors, just waiting to be deployed. In the rotation, Maddux has been exceptional, Tolberg has been good (though bit by the home run bug, particularly at home), and Chuck Smith has been great, while Kris Benson has been ok and unlucky. Take away Jack Cressend and the bullpen has actually been really strong, but his performances have made them below-average overall. Mike DeJean and Chad Bradford have been great groundballers, albeit with their own problems; Bradford can’t strike any one out, and Dejean walks far too many.

New York Yankees Outlook:

With Bonds rolling and the rotation clicking, look for the Yankees to take over the East from Detroit in the near future. Reinforcements, particularly for Cressend and fifth starter Ariel Prieto seem imminent, plus Jose Ortiz could be a more capable bench bat and Will Nieves would be a great backup for Molina. My prediction: East Division champions.

The Houston Astros have a fearsome lineup headlined of course by A-Rod and Sammy Sosa, though they are not the end of it. Six of their starting nine are solidly above average, while Jeff Cirillo, their 3rd baseman, has a 94 wRC+, but has been superb on defense and even has run into some batted ball misfortune, so he should end the year as one of the more valuable 3rd baseman in the MLB. Sosa has been a revelation defensively in right, while A-Rod has put together one of the best offensive campaigns so far, with a 10% walk rate and 10 dingers to go along with his .336 average. However, one of the real disappointments this year has been Sammy Sosa’s performance in the big moments. His OPS plunges from over 1.000 in low leverage situations, to 800 in medium leverage, and 600 in high leverage! This can’t possibly keep up, so expect Sosa, A-Rod, and Jeff Kent to slug their way to a high position on all the offensive charts. It’s now or never too for this Houston side as they are one of the oldest teams in the PBA and have essentially no prospects to speak of. On the pitching side of things, the Astros profile as a high-contact, low-walk team; for now that seems to be working out ok, but in the long run, it seems dicey. Chris Carpenter looks legit with his low walk numbers and solid sinker, but Jon Lieber has been hit around (including for dingers) and Brian Powell in the #5 spot has been dreadful. The bullpen has been good so far, but once again, the true lack of K’s is alarming. Shoutout to lefty Scott Eyre with his .66 ERA despite walking six and striking out only ten. The Astros have been prone to some pretty dramatic streaks, with a depressing six game losing run (including a sweep to their hated intra-state rival, the Griffins) in mid-April, but they’ve also racked off a 4-game winning streak, a six-gamer, and an ongoing 5-gamer. This particular streak has been impressive as they’ve done it against the Yankees and the A’s, two of the strongest sides in the MLB.

Houston Astros Outlook

Though the pitching is extremely concerning, I’m thinking the offense and defense will keep them in it; they have Cirillo, Kent, and Rodriguez in the infield, and Sosa in the outfield, so if they have the great seasons they are capable of, I’m a believer they can stitch it together well enough in ‘pen and rotation.

The Washington Cherry Blossoms came into the season as one of the relegation favorites, but their performance in the first six weeks of the season has them at 20-20, dead even, with a run differential of exactly zero. The story so far is of their strange lineup: an excellent OPS plus of 106 has been counteracted by a terrible baserunning record, with a 59% SB rate, but even worse, -16.5 BSR runs. The main culprits have been the aging Edgar Renteria, young slugger Travis Hafner, and catcher Damian Miller (the latter two are 6th and 7th worst in the entire league). Perhaps related to these baserunning struggles, Washington’s defense has also been abysmal. Jose Hernandez has been excellent in a super-infield-utility role, but the problem is that at key positions (SS, 2B, and CF), the team’s defense has been below average. With the bats in their hands however, this team has been solid - good walk numbers from a core of guys along with some serious pop between Hafner, Piatt, and Hernandez, has kept their offense solidly above average. The pitching has actually be ok, but their ERAs are all inflated due to the poor defense. Maybe the only problem with the pitching is the lowest GB% in the league as the fly balls have exposed their outfield defense of Kotsay, Chavez, and Newhan. There have been clamorings among reports that Kotsay and Chavez should probably switch, but nothing of the sort yet. Johan Santana, their young pitching stud, hasn’t had great results and has surrendured too many dingers, but his strikeout stuff and control have been exceptional, and he looks the real deal. The rest of the rotation, despite some elevated ERAs, actually looks likethe one of the more stable groups in the MLB; Luke Hudson in particular has caught fire with two straight one-run starts and and a real knack for preventing home runs with his mix of curves and changeups despite a soft heater. They’ve doen a good job distributing their leverage situations as Geoff Geary and Joe Borowski each have great ERAs and FIPs and have pitched the most high leverage innings. In late April they caught fire with huge sweeps of the Birds, the Cardinals and the Blue Jays, but a recent sweep at the hands of Rockies has got them feeling pretty down.

Washington Cherry Blossoms Outlook: Perhaps no injury of the young season looks as consequential as Travis Hafner’s fractured foot from a Gus Gandarillas’ spiked backfoot curveball. Otherwise, their young offense looks unrealistically buoyed by some high BABIPs; the lack of any real names in the pitching staff aside from Santana freaks me out and makes me think they’re doomed to fade pretty soon, though obviously with a floor above the relegation zone.

The philadelphia Phillies have been keeping it together despite 4 weeks without a critical piece, their starting catcher. The result was the worst catching OPS+ in the league. However, the offense was not without hope, with 18 year old phenom, Miguel Cabrara, dominating from the word go and establishing the league lead for consecutive games reaching base with 34, That was just broken yesterday (May 12) by Jake Peavy, with a huge average and high walk rate to go along with a bit of pop. his .417 BABIP is a bit foreboding, but hey, no one’s perfect. Joe Crede and Derek Jeter have also been hitting well and have even played well defensively, though the outfield is where the Phillies truly shine defensively. Mike Cameron, Roger Cedeno, and Trenidad Hubbard (as a super utility man) have all been excellent, both top 3 at their position in the league. They’ve also been the 3rd best baserunning team, with Alomar and Cameron running wild, backing up the prediction that they would be an athletic, highly potent team. On the pitching side, as expected, the bullpen has been terrible, with a dismal 35% save rate. Jonathan Johnson and Chris Hammond have blown 4 and 3 games respectively, taking the Phillies from the top of the east to mid-table singlehandedly. Ironically, otherwise Chris Hammond has been great, but in those critical outings, he totally blew it. Opponents are batting .333/.429/.778 in high leverage against him - wowza. The rotation has been equally terrible with only Runelvys Hernandez sporting a over 100 ERA+, but Loaiza has been promising despite a high ERA. Brad Penny, the young rookie, has been an unmitigated disaster, walking far too many along with Matt Tinny woh’s been destroyed as the #5 starter. There are capable reinforncements in the minors though in Andy van Hekken and Miguel Gomez, if they’re willing to pull the trigger. Of course, by FIP they’re actually above average, so who knows about all that.

Philadelphia Phillies Outlook:

The preseason projection systems loved them, but honestly I’m not seeing it. No way Cabrera can keep this up, nor can the bullpen sort itself out does it seem. I think they’ll stick around .500 for sure with a pretty high floor and some possible reinforcements in the pitching, but notv buying them as a whole.

The Colorado Rockies came into the season as major underdogs, and the first 40 games have resulted in…perhaps a good start? 19-21 is nothing to write home about, but in their push against relegation, it’s a good start. The offense had concerns at the start of the season and their 60 home runs are definitely a bit of an illusion; middling walk and strikeout numbers and the Coors effect reveal the true problems inherent in this lineup. Jose Canseco and Matt Stairs are near the top of the league in homers, but its obvious why that is. Stairs has been excellently defesnively at 2nd, which has been a nice boost, nd Brad Ausmus behind the plate has been a solid framer and good contact hitter. Jonny Gomes and Corey Hart have been terrible at the plate but have a bunch of steals between them, making the Rockies one ofthe most effective teams on the basepaths. Gary Glover and Oliver Perez in the bullpen have been great but Pat Neshek and Gus Gandarillas have been terrible. The rotation was projected as one of the worst in the league, but theyve actually been doing alright accordion to ERA plus - Tony Armas and Brian Moehler have actually been great despite their around 4 ERAs. It’s pretty hard to know what to make of this team as this point; definitely better than expected

Whatever the St Louis Cardinals are doing, just keep doing it. Aside from all the process stuff. Get rid of that stuff. The Cardinals are a semi-miaraculous 19-21 despite one of the worst offenses in the league, a pitching staff that can’t strike anyone out, and a bad defense in a big ballpark. If that sounds like a recipe for a disaster, then you’ve got your team. And yet, somehow the Cardinals AREN’T a disaster, at least record-wise. Perhaps its Raul Ibanez’s almost 3 WAR performance in just 35 games played, one of the greatest runs we’ve seen in a while. Amazingly, the Cardinals, by run differential and Pythagorean, actually ARE pretty good, with a +9 RD. They’ve been terribly uneven all year, winning 5 straight to start the season, losing 6 of 7 in the middle of APril to the Yankees, Oakland, and Washington, and just recently being swept by the Seals and the Dodgers. Perhaps their series against the Braves in late April is carrying a lot of weight here; in three games in St. Louis, the Cards outscored the Braves 40-9 for the ass-whooping of the year. If only every series could be against the Braves.

Offensively, Ibanez has accounted for 2.9 of the TEAM’s 3.7 batting WAR. Their unique combination of low walk rate, low home run rate, and mid-K rate is only buoyed by one of the better BABIPs in the league at .299 and they have hit their share of doubles with Dye, Mensch, Halter, and Matos all in double digitis already along with Ibanez. They don’t run the bases run, but they have gotten a fair amount of steals and haven’t been caught too much with Luis Matos leading the way (though he’s given away MORE than his share of runs with numerous other baserunning gaffes. 22-year old shortstop Khalil Greene missed time with a sprained knee legging out a double on APril 5th and his loss has proved pretty important, as the middle infield, never a strength, produced an abysmal 0.9 WAR. Though that’s nothing compared to the outifle,d which doesn’t have injuries to pin its problems on; Matos and Dye have been ok (though with obvious falws, from Matos’ range and baserunning, to Dye’s defensive limitations), but the rest of the outfield core have been pretty terrible. Kevin Mench has been DH’ing, but hitting like a fourth outfielder, their fourth outfielder Barry Wesson has been starting in left, and also hitting like a fourth outfielder, and Termel Slegde and Barker in spot appearances have been dreadful. On the defensive side of the ball, the Cardinals have combined poor defense with some extreme luck somehow; their high defensive efficiency belays an inability to get to distant balls as their ZR has been terrible, and they make more than anyone except the Dodgers. Their pitching has been excellent in terms of ERA, but the underlying stats are worrying. Justin Derscherder and Holmes are awesome, and Francisco Cordero looks like a legit closer, but Roy Smith and Isidro Marquez have been riding their luck with just terrible walk/strikeout numbers (esepecially Smith who has a 4.67 ERA, somehow), and starters Ziegler, Corey lee, and Grienke all have bad FIPs (albeit for different reasons, as Grienke’s only issue has been home runs, though that’s been a big issue). At the top of the rotation, Horacio ramirez and Paul Byrd have been surviving on low BABIPs (Horacio much more so) and a prayer.

St. Louis Caridnals Outlook: The comeback of Khalil Greene will do a lot, and you have to imagine that the 18-year-old Grienke will only get better, but the Cardinals have to view the first six weeks of the season realistically, as probably just the half-decent start that keeps them clear of the relegation battle. Raul Ibanez’s start will live on in the memories of Cardinals fans for a while, but it obviously will come back down to earth (that, or Ibanez is all the sudden the greatest contact hitter in the game along with some pop). My prediction: still around a 70 win team, definitely not above .500.

The Toronto Blue Jays have flown under the radar so far this season to a mediocre, but unspectacular 18-22. Their pitching has been incredibly middling, with remarkable perforamcnes in the bullpen, but pretty poor ERAs in the rotation. Aaron Sele and Gregg Olson both have ERAs over 5 thanks to a high home run rate and low K rate about around 12%. 23-year old ace Brandon Webb has been great though with a 3.47 ERA and 3.37 FIP thanks to some big strikeout numbers and 19-year Bonderman has been holding his own despite allowing a homer in all but one of his starts. In the pen, Nitching and Powell have been terrible, but the rest of the squad is doing great with Scott Atchinson and Brandon Backe yet to allow homers and Scott Sauerback and Paul Shuey fulfilling the preseason hype surrounding them. Sauerback in particular, with his power sinker from a sidearm angle, has been dominating hitters, limiting opponents to a .109 batting average. The defense has been relatively non-factor, a great outfield counteracting a terrible infield. Jim Edmonds’ 7.3 ZR is second in the league and, combined with his above average arm, he’s the best CF in the league (over Johnny Damon). The offense, other than Edmonds, has been terrible. A miniscule BABIP of .265 has doomed the Blue Jays to a low batting average and without good walk numbers or strikeouts, they’ve become the third worst offense in the league. But of course, the big problem is the baserunning; it has cost them 17 runs, plus over four runs just via their caught stealings. Ironically, Edmonds has been the main culprit with 4 CS and 0 SB. Otherwise offensively, Edmonds has been a beast; he’s second in th league in doubles and has a 150 wRC+. The team as a whole are actually on a hot streak: four in a row and 8 of 9 in May. We’ll see what the real Toronto Blue Jays are. In April they struggling, getting crushed by their hated local rivals, the Detroit Wolverines, and losing 2 of 3 in a tense, tight series against the Yankees.

Toronto Blue Jays OUTLOOK:

By both the projections and my own predictions, the Blue Jays were supposed to be pretty good, and I’m not totally lost on them.the signing of Edmonds to a 7 year deal gives them hope for the future, as does the combo of Brandon Webb and Jeremy Bonderman in the rotation. The rest of the offense needs to kick it inot gear for anything to happen this year (and honestly that sounds unlikely), but TOronot has some ok options at AAA if they’re willing to dip there.
