The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Blue Jays Lost Seven Straight, Then Won Nine of Ten

Toronto is still 19-22, with the 18th-ranked offense in MLB, a center fielder who is 0-for-4 stealing, and one of the best bullpens in the league. The "outspoken" clubhouse has had a lot to talk about.

Toronto Blue Jays · 19-22 · 161 runs scored, 174 allowed

In March, Alexander's personalities report described the Blue Jays clubhouse as "outspoken": a "vocal roster; confidence can border on volatility during losing streaks." He also listed Toronto among the league's most volatile clubs. Through 41 games, the volatility is the easy part to spot.

From April 25 to May 2 the Blue Jays lost seven straight. They were shut out 1-0 at Yankee Stadium, swept at home by Oakland, then swept in Washington, where they gave up 21 runs in the last two games. That left them 10-21. Since then they're 9-1. They swept Atlanta, took two of three in San Francisco, swept Arizona at home and won 11-2 in St. Louis on Monday, with Jim Edmonds driving in five. Toronto is 19-22, eighth in the East and six games behind Miami.

Edmonds and everyone else

The March preview ranked Toronto fourth, with an offense led by "Jim Edmonds, Dmitri Young, and Preston Wilson" and "a perchance for walking." Edmonds has been as good as expected: .312/.404/.572, 16 doubles, a 162 OPS+ and 2.4 WAR. Alexander also calls him "the best CF in the league (over Johnny Damon)," and the club has signed him to a seven-year contract. The other two headliners have not delivered. Young has a 109 OPS+, and Wilson is at .224/.281/.428. Toronto ranks 18th in MLB in runs and 18th in OPS. Alexander's verdict: "The offense, other than Edmonds, has been terrible."

The bigger problem, he says, is the baserunning, which "has cost them 17 runs, plus over four runs just via their caught stealings." The team is 11-for-25 stealing, last in MLB in steals. "Ironically, Edmonds has been the main culprit with 4 CS and 0 SB." Third base has been empty, too. Mike Gulan is hitting .172/.214/.269, which is a hard place to look for runs.

The young arms and the bullpen

At the three-week mark, the problem Alexander identified was the rotation: "Virtually every starter has been below average so far." The two exceptions were the prospects he had pointed to in March. Brandon Webb, 23, has a 3.43 ERA, 52 strikeouts and a complete-game win over Arizona on Friday. Jeremy Bonderman, 19, has 47 strikeouts and a 4.50 ERA, and Alexander notes he has "been holding his own despite allowing a homer in all but one of his starts." Aaron Sele, the nominal ace, has a 4.65 ERA.

The bullpen, with a 2.94 ERA, may be the best thing about this team. Scott Atchison (1.31 ERA) and Brandon Backe haven't allowed a home run. Paul Shuey has a 1.84 ERA. Scott Sauerbeck, the "excellent lefty" of the March preview, has a 1.40 ERA, and according to Alexander, "with his power sinker from a sidearm angle," he has held opponents to a .109 average.

The April trips

Alexander's notes single out two April series. Detroit, Toronto's "hated local rivals," swept the Jays in Detroit in mid-April, scoring 23 runs in three games. A week later Toronto took two of three at Yankee Stadium. Halama pitched 8.1 innings of a 3-2 win in 11, Eli Marrero homered, and Toronto won the next night 3-1 before losing the finale 1-0. The best single game of the year may have come against the Braves on May 3, when Joe Girardi, a backup catcher with 29 plate appearances, drove in six runs in an 11-6 win.

Still mediocre, on purpose

The predicted standings had Toronto at 81-70. "By both the projections and my own predictions, the Blue Jays were supposed to be pretty good, and I'm not totally lost on them," Alexander wrote at 40 games. "My prediction: continued mediocrity and a late push to above .500." The -13 run differential suggests mediocrity is about right. The site's 5.0% relegation chance is the highest among the clubs bunched between fifth and eighth in the East. Toronto plays two more in St. Louis, three at home against the Dodgers and three in Boston. Boston is the team three games behind them.
