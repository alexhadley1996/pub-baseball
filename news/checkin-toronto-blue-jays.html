The Blue Jays Are Still Here

Toronto Blue Jays, 18-22 through 40 games

Every losing streak eventually ends, which is the kind of comfort that is only useful afterward. The Blue Jays lost seven in a row between Apr 25 and May 2, and they are 18-22 after 40.

40 games, by the numbers

  • 18-22 record
  • -22 run differential (150 scored, 172 allowed)
  • 5-9 in one-run games
  • 12-10 at home
  • 6-12 on the road
  • 4.36 / 2.96 starter / reliever ERA
  • 40 home runs
  • 10 stolen bases

Through 40 games they were 18-22, scoring 150 runs and allowing 172 (-22). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 19-22, 8th in the East Division, 6 games back, and 13th of 20 in MLB. That run differential would usually produce a record around 18-22. They are 5-9 in one-run games and 2-1 in extra innings.

Their most productive hitter is Jim Edmonds (CF, 31), at .301/.397/.549 with 5 HR. Preston Wilson leads the club with 8 home runs. As a team they are hitting .242/.310/.397, 18th of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 40 home runs.

The rotation's anchor is Brandon Webb: 9 starts, 57.2 innings, 3.43 ERA. John Halama (4.07 ERA in 48.2 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 3.96 ERA, 10th of 20 in MLB; starters 4.36, relievers 2.96. Closing has mostly meant Scott Sauerbeck: 4 saves, 1.40 ERA.

The disappointment, at least so far, is Russ Johnson: a .816 OPS in 2001, .634 through 138 plate appearances this year.

If you want to understand the Blue Jays at 18-22, do not look at the blowouts. Look at the 14 games decided by a single run, in which they have gone 5-9. That is the difference between a good start and this one.

Odds and ends: When they score three runs or fewer they are 5-16; when they score six or more, 11-1. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

The best night was May 3, an 11-6 win over the Braves. The worst was May 1, an 11-2 loss to the Cherry Blossoms. Their best 10-game stretch went 8-2 (May 2 to May 12); their worst, 2-8 (Apr 22 to May 2).

MLB has no wild card and no consolation prize: division winners play for the title, the last-place club in each division goes down to the ABL. The Blue Jays are 6 games behind the Marlins in the East Division. The floor is 5 games below them, where the Braves currently stand on relegation pace.

What comes next: 10 games starting May 14, 3 of them at home, against the Cardinals, the Dodgers, the Red Sox, the Phillies, opponents with a combined 177-233 record (.432). That is a chance to bank wins. One more thing to watch: Jim Edmonds is on the milestone watch, closing on 200 home runs.
