The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

The Blue Jays Lost Seven Straight, Then Won Nine of Ten

Toronto is still 19-22, with the 18th-ranked offense in MLB, a center fielder who is 0-for-4 stealing and one of the best bullpens in the league. Its outspoken clubhouse has had plenty to talk about.

Toronto Blue Jays · 19-22 · 161 runs scored, 174 allowed

The Blue Jays came into the season with a vocal clubhouse, the kind where confidence can border on volatility during a losing streak, and through 41 games the volatility has been easy to spot.

From April 25 to May 2 Toronto lost seven straight. It was shut out 1-0 at Yankee Stadium, swept at home by Oakland and then swept in Washington, giving up 21 runs in the last two games. That left the Jays at 10-21. Since then they've gone 9-1. They swept Atlanta, took two of three in San Francisco, swept Arizona at home and won 11-2 in St. Louis on Monday, with Jim Edmonds driving in five. At 19-22 they're eighth in the East, six games behind Miami.

Edmonds and everyone else

The preseason consensus ranked Toronto fourth, with an offense led by Edmonds, Dmitri Young and Preston Wilson and a knack for drawing walks. Edmonds has been as good as advertised. He's hitting .312 with 16 doubles, a 162 OPS+ and 2.4 WAR, he's been the best center fielder in the league, ahead of Johnny Damon, and the club has signed him to a seven-year contract. The other two haven't delivered. Young has a 109 OPS+, and Wilson is hitting .224. Outside Edmonds, the offense has been terrible, and Toronto ranks 18th in MLB in both runs and OPS.

The baserunning has made it worse. It has cost the Jays 17 runs, plus four more just on caught stealings. They're 11-for-25 stealing, last in MLB, and the main culprit, ironically, is Edmonds, who is 0-for-4. Third base has been empty, too. Mike Gulan is hitting .172, a hard place to look for runs.

Young arms, good bullpen

Three weeks in, nearly every Toronto starter had been below average. The exceptions were the two prospects. Brandon Webb, 23, has a 3.43 ERA and 52 strikeouts and threw a complete-game win over Arizona on Friday. Jeremy Bonderman, 19, has 47 strikeouts and a 4.50 ERA, holding his own despite allowing a home run in all but one of his starts. Aaron Sele, the nominal ace, is at 4.65.

The bullpen, at 2.94, may be the best thing about this team. Scott Atchison, with a 1.31 ERA, and Brandon Backe haven't allowed a home run. Paul Shuey has a 1.84 ERA. Scott Sauerbeck, the excellent lefty with a power sinker from a sidearm angle, has a 1.40 ERA and has held hitters to a .109 average.

The April trips

Two April series stand out. Detroit, Toronto's hated local rival, swept the Jays in Detroit in mid-April and scored 23 runs doing it. A week later Toronto took two of three at Yankee Stadium. John Halama pitched 8.1 innings of a 3-2 win in 11, Eli Marrero homered, and the Jays won again the next night, 3-1, before losing the finale 1-0. The best single game may have come against the Braves on May 3, when Joe Girardi, a backup catcher with 29 plate appearances, drove in six runs in an 11-6 win.

Mediocre, and maybe a push

Both preseason views liked Toronto. The consensus had the Jays fourth, and the projections had them at 81-70. There's still reason to believe in them a little. The likeliest summer is more mediocrity and a late push above .500, and the -13 run differential agrees with the first part. The site's 5.0% relegation chance is the highest among the clubs bunched between fifth and eighth in the East. Toronto plays two more in St. Louis, then the Dodgers at home and three in Boston, the team three games behind it.
