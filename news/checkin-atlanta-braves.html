The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Mark Prior Is 3-6 With a 2.48 ERA, Which Tells You About Atlanta

The Braves hit for more power than anyone expected and pitch about as badly as feared. Their 21-year-old ace is the best thing in the East's last-place clubhouse, and the relegation math is closing in.

Atlanta Braves · 14-27 · 172 runs scored, 221 allowed

On April 10 in Oakland, Mark Prior allowed one hit in 7.2 innings, struck out 10 and lost 1-0. Freddy Garcia threw eight shutout innings on the other side, and it was a classic. It was also a typical Prior start. He's 21, he has a 2.48 ERA, 69 strikeouts and three home runs allowed in 61.2 innings, and he's been worth 2.5 WAR. His record is 3-6.

The Braves threw Prior in as an ace from the first day, and he has pitched like one. Not much else has gone according to plan, or rather, most of it has gone exactly according to plan. Atlanta is 14-27, last in the East and two games behind Boston, and the site gives it a 57.5% chance of relegation, the highest in MLB.

The pitching

The preseason projections had Atlanta at 54-97, allowing 838 runs, the most in MLB. The Braves have allowed 221, the most in MLB, and their 5.01 ERA is last. Past Prior and Eric DuBose, who has a 3.19 ERA, the rotation has been rough. Jose Contreras is 1-6 with a 7.08 ERA, and Tim Harikkala is at 6.39.

The back of the bullpen was supposed to be the bright spot, with Keith Foulke and Jose Valverde, and it has been fine: Foulke has six saves, and Valverde has 13 strikeouts in 10 innings. The problem is getting to them. The bullpen ERA is 5.80, and Juan Dominguez, 21, has an 18.47 ERA. The staff also came in with two disruptive personalities and a reputation as one of the league's most volatile groups. There's no public sign of trouble yet, but the losses keep piling up.

The surprise

The offense got an F in the preseason rankings, a stunningly inexperienced group with one reliable big leaguer, 37-year-old Steve Finley. Finley has been good, with a .370 on-base percentage and a 132 OPS+, though he's been thrown out eight times in 14 steal attempts. The bigger surprise is the power. Atlanta is third in MLB in home runs. Mo Vaughn has 10 and a 137 OPS+, and one of them, on April 4 against Miami, was the 300th of his career. Ernie Young has 11 in 139 plate appearances. Ben Broussard has nine and drove in six in a 15-1 win in Boston on April 19, a game in which catcher Geronimo Gil homered twice. Gil has a 109 OPS+.

The infield has been as bad as it looked in March. Lou Lucca is hitting .198 at third, and Kevin Nicholson and Esteban German both have OPS+ marks in the low 70s. Hideki Matsui, signed to a one-year deal in the first week, has an 87 OPS+ in left.

The season, briefly

Atlanta opened with two wins in Detroit, Prior throwing seven scoreless innings on Opening Day and DuBose beating the Wolverines the next day. Then the Braves lost 10 of 12. One of the two wins in that stretch was the strangest game of their year, a 3-2 win over the Yankees in 15 innings, ended by German's walk-off home run. Two days later they lost to New York in 14. There have been a few good series since: two of three in San Francisco in late April and a home sweep of the Dodgers. There was also the trip to St. Louis, where they were outscored 40-9 in three games.

They've lost four straight now, three at Wrigley and Monday's 4-0 loss to Colorado. The next eight games are two more with the Rockies at home, then three each in Houston and Milwaukee. Boston, the team Atlanta has to catch, is two games ahead. At 14-27 the Braves are on pace for about 52 wins, a little below even their projection.
