The Loud Season of the Braves

Atlanta Braves, 14-26 through 40 games

The Braves have hit 56 home runs in 40 games. That is a lot of trots, and it is the simplest explanation for a 14-26 team: when the ball leaves, so do the problems.

40 games, by the numbers

  • 14-26 record
  • -45 run differential (172 scored, 217 allowed)
  • 4-5 in one-run games
  • 8-10 at home
  • 6-16 on the road
  • 4.55 / 5.86 starter / reliever ERA
  • 56 home runs
  • 21 stolen bases

Through 40 games they were 14-26, scoring 172 runs and allowing 217 (-45). With 41 games in the books as of May 13 they are 14-27, 10th in the East Division, 11 games back, and 20th of 20 in MLB. By the old Pythagorean arithmetic, that is a 16-win team so far. They are 4-5 in one-run games and 1-2 in extra innings.

Their most productive hitter is Mo Vaughn (1B, 34), at .293/.354/.544 with 10 HR. Ernie Young leads the club with 11 home runs. As a team they are hitting .257/.317/.425, 9th of 20 in MLB by OPS through each club's first 40, with 56 home runs.

In the rotation, Mark Prior has been the steadiest: 3-6 with a 2.48 ERA, 69 strikeouts and 21 walks in 61.2 innings. Eric DuBose (3.19 ERA in 48.0 IP) has been the next most reliable. The staff as a whole has a 5.03 ERA, 20th of 20 in MLB; starters 4.55, relievers 5.86. Keith Foulke has 6 saves without a blown save.

The surprise might be Mo Vaughn, who arrived without a 2001 top-tier line worth the name (under 150 plate appearances) and is hitting .293/.354/.544. The weakest regular bat has been Lou Lucca, at .203/.257/.260 in 138 plate appearances. In the rotation, Jose Contreras has a 7.08 ERA over 40.2 innings.

Some details worth knowing. When they score three runs or fewer they are 2-18; when they score six or more, 8-6. Baseball is sometimes exactly as simple as it looks.

The best night was Apr 19, a 15-1 win over the Red Sox. The worst was Apr 28, a 14-2 loss to the Cardinals. Their best 10-game stretch went 5-5 (Apr 14 to Apr 23); their worst, 2-8 (Apr 1 to Apr 10).

MLB has no wild card and no consolation prize: division winners play for the title, the last-place club in each division goes down to the ABL. The Braves are 11 games behind the Marlins in the East Division. They are also last, which in this league is not a sad statistic but a trapdoor: 2 games behind the Red Sox for the right to stay up.

The next stretch is 10 games (4 at home) against the Rockies, the Astros, the Brewers, the Griffins. Those clubs are 199-211 combined, a .485 winning percentage, so it will mostly be up to them. The record matches the runs, so there is no regression bill coming and no hidden credit either. The next 122 games will mostly be about whether Mo Vaughn keeps doing this.
