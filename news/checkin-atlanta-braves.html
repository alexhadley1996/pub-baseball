The Season So Far · MLB · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Mark Prior Is 3-6 With a 2.48 ERA, Which Tells You About Atlanta

The Braves hit for more power than Alexander expected and pitch about as badly as he feared. Their 21-year-old ace is the best thing in the East's last-place clubhouse, and the relegation math is closing in.

Atlanta Braves · 14-27 · 172 runs scored, 221 allowed

On April 10 in Oakland, Mark Prior allowed one hit in 7.2 innings, struck out 10 and lost 1-0. Freddy Garcia threw eight shutout innings for the A's, and the roundup that day called it "a classic." It was a typical Prior start. He is 21. He has a 2.48 ERA, a 2.17 FIP, 69 strikeouts and three home runs allowed in 61.2 innings, worth 2.5 WAR. His record is 3-6.

Alexander's March preview said Prior would be "thrown in as an ace immediately," and he has been. The rest of the March preview has held up too. The Braves are 14-27, last in the East and two games behind Boston. The site gives them a 57.5% chance of relegation, the highest in MLB.

What March got right

Alexander's predicted standings had Atlanta at 54-97 and projected the staff to allow 838 runs, the most in MLB. The Braves have allowed 221 runs, the most in MLB, and their 5.01 ERA is last. Beyond Prior and Eric DuBose (3.19 ERA), the rotation has struggled. Jose Contreras is 1-6 with a 7.08 ERA, and Tim Harikkala has a 6.39 ERA. The bullpen ERA is 5.80. Alexander thought the back end "could be great" with Keith Foulke and Jose Valverde. Foulke has six saves, and Valverde has 13 strikeouts in 10 innings. The innings before them have gone badly. Juan Dominguez, 21, has an 18.47 ERA. The personalities report flagged "two disruptive pitchers on a staff that already projects to allow the most runs in MLB (838), which is a bad place for friction," and listed Atlanta's pitching among the league's most volatile groups. There's no public sign of trouble yet, but the losses are piling up.

What March got wrong

The offense got an F. Alexander called it "a stunningly inexperienced offense with only one reliable major leaguer (Steve Finley) and he, 37 and diminished." Finley is hitting .283/.370/.493 with a 132 OPS+, though he's been caught stealing eight times in 14 attempts. Atlanta is third in MLB in home runs. Mo Vaughn has 10 and a 137 OPS+, and one of them, on April 4 against Miami, was the 300th of his career. Ernie Young has 11 home runs in 139 plate appearances. Ben Broussard has nine, including six RBI in the 15-1 win in Boston on April 19, a game in which Geronimo Gil also homered twice. Gil was the catcher Alexander called "ok." He has a 109 OPS+ and 0.8 WAR.

The infield, Alexander's biggest March concern, has been as bad as he expected. Lou Lucca is hitting .198/.252/.254 at third. Kevin Nicholson and Esteban German both have OPS+ in the low 70s. Hideki Matsui, signed to a one-year deal in the first week, has an 87 OPS+ in left.

The season, briefly

Atlanta began with two wins in Detroit. Prior threw seven scoreless innings on opening day, then DuBose beat the Wolverines again. The Braves then lost 10 of their next 12. One of the two wins in that stretch was the strangest game of their season: a 3-2 win over the Yankees in 15 innings, ended by German's walk-off home run. Two days later they lost to New York in 14. They have had a few good series since, taking two of three in San Francisco in late April and sweeping the Dodgers at home. Those are balanced by the three games in St. Louis where they were outscored 40-9.

They've lost four straight, a sweep at Wrigley and Monday's 4-0 loss to Colorado. The next eight games are two more against the Rockies at home and then three each in Houston and Milwaukee. The Red Sox, the team Atlanta has to catch, are two games ahead. At 14-27 the Braves are on pace for about 52 wins, slightly below Alexander's 54.
