ABL · 2002-05-03 · By Frank DeLuca, New York Evening Star

Queens Wins One

The Knights, 9-21 and still wearing last October's 39-5 sweep, fell behind Pittsburgh three times and tied it three times. J.C. Romero struck out the side in the ninth, and Ty Wigginton singled home the winner in the 10th.

QUEENS, N.Y. — There’s a banner in the Knights’ clubhouse that nobody on the team hung and nobody has taken down. It says 39-5, the combined score of last October’s four-game sweep by the Yankees in what the tabloids called the King of New York series, and some clubhouse attendant with a mean streak put it up over the coffee machine in spring training. The players walk past it every day. Manager Bobby Valentine says he’s going to take it down when the Knights give him a reason to.

On Friday night they gave him one, small but real. The Knights fell behind the Pittsburgh Pirates three separate times, tied the game three separate times, and beat them 7-6 in the 10th inning on Ty Wigginton’s two-out single.

“That’s the kind of game we’ve been losing all year,” Valentine said. “Tonight we didn’t. I’m not taking the banner down yet. But I looked at it on the way in.”

Three leads, three answers

Pittsburgh hit four home runs, and every time it went ahead, the Knights came back.

Shannon Stewart and Jay Gibbons homered off Chris Holt in the first. Danny Ardoin, the catcher hitting .154, singled in two in the second to tie it. F.P. Santangelo and Stewart singled in runs in the fourth; Ramon E. Martinez homered off Tomo Ohka in the bottom half. Brian Daubach homered in the fifth; Lyle Mouton singled in two to tie it at 5. Gibbons homered again in the sixth, off Heath Bost, his second of the night and ninth of the year; Chris Singleton singled home a run to tie it at 6.

“Every time we went up, they had an answer,” said Pirates manager Lloyd McClendon. “That’s not a 9-21 team tonight. That’s a team that’s sick of being 9-21.”

Romero

The Knights’ bullpen won it from there. J.C. Romero, a 25-year-old left-hander who came in with an 8.25 ERA and some unkind nicknames from the Queens bleachers, pitched 3⅓ innings and allowed one hit. He struck out five, the last three of them in a row in the ninth: Dave Berg, Daubach and Jeff Liefer.

“I’ve been bad,” Romero said. “Everybody knows I’ve been bad. You keep pitching. Tonight I was good.”

The Knights nearly won it in the ninth. Lance Berkman singled and Carlos Guillen doubled, and the Knights had runners on second and third with nobody out against Steve Reed. Reed got a ground ball, a fly ball and another ground ball, and the game went on.

The 10th

Scott Stewart pitched around a hit batter in the top of the 10th. In the bottom half Pittsburgh brought in its closer, B.J. Ryan, who got a fly ball from Ardoin. Cristhian Presichi, 21, singled and stole second. Singleton struck out. Wigginton, the 24-year-old first baseman, singled to left, and Presichi scored standing up.

“I didn’t even see the ball,” Wigginton said. “I saw Presichi running, and I knew.”

Gibbons finished 3-for-5 with two homers for the Pirates, who are 16-15. Their 18-year-old second baseman, Jose Reyes, went 0-for-6. The Knights are 10-21, last in the ABL’s Eastern Conference, and somewhat less miserable than they were on Friday morning.
