MLB · 2002-04-05 · By Ken Albrecht, The Baseball Courier

Fourteen Innings, Two Comebacks and One Kid

Oakland led Boston 6-1 and 8-5, and Shawon Dunston, 39, finished a single short of the cycle. The Red Sox came back both times, tied it again in the 13th, and won 10-9 in the 14th on Kevin Young's single. The winning pitcher was 18 years old.

BOSTON — The winning pitcher on Friday night at Fenway Park was born in 1983. The Oakland shortstop who nearly beat him was drafted in 1982.

That was the kind of night it was. Fourteen innings, a little under five hours, 19 runs, two Boston comebacks from multi-run deficits, and an ending, Kevin Young’s bases-loaded single in the 14th, that sent most of the 32,900 home happy and the rest of New England to bed late. The Red Sox beat the Athletics 10-9.

“I’ve been doing this a long time,” Boston manager Grady Little said. “I don’t remember being this tired after a game I won.”

Randy’s rough night

It started as a Randy Johnson night, and for a while it was a bad one. Johnson struck out 11 Athletics in seven innings, which is the kind of number that usually comes with a win attached. This time it came with three home runs and six runs.

Orlando Merced homered in the second. Sal Fasano, making his first start of the season, led off the third with a homer, and later in the inning Albert Pujols, the kid the Cardinals let go, hit a three-run shot over the Monster. Shawon Dunston tripled in another run in the sixth, and Oakland led 6-1.

“He threw some great pitches tonight,” Pujols said. “I got one that wasn’t. That’s all you can ask for against him.”

Johnson was blunter. “Eleven strikeouts doesn’t mean much when you give up six,” he said. “I put us in a hole. The guys dug us out. I owe them.”

The first comeback

Freddy Garcia had cruised for Oakland, but the Red Sox started chipping in the sixth on Young’s solo homer, and in the seventh they got to him for real. Young doubled in a run, and David Bell, who’d already driven in Boston’s first run, hit a two-run homer to make it 6-5.

Then the Boston bullpen, which has heard about itself all spring, gave two back. Dan Wheeler, making his Red Sox debut, allowed a two-run double to Dunston in the eighth. 8-5.

Rivera

Here is the thing about the ninth inning: Oakland had Mariano Rivera, and in this league that is supposed to end the conversation.

It didn’t. Rivera faced five batters and retired one. Fred McGriff doubled in a run. Rivera walked Pat Burrell, and another run came home in the confusion. Art Howe went to John Franco, and Rich Aurilia lofted a fly ball to right that tied the game at 8.

“That’s on me,” Rivera said, standing at his locker without a shirt and without excuses. “I’ve had a lot of good nights. This wasn’t one. Tomorrow I’ll want the ball.”

Dunston

From the 10th through the 12th, nobody scored. Matt Belisle, 21, threw 2⅔ hitless innings for Boston. Then Dunston, who turned 39 in March and was supposed to be a bench piece for Oakland, homered with one out in the 13th off Dan Serafini.

That gave him a homer, a triple and a double. He needed a single for the cycle and didn’t get one.

“I didn’t know until somebody told me in the dugout,” Dunston said. “At my age you don’t think about the cycle. You think about your hamstrings.”

Boston answered anyway. With two outs in the bottom of the 13th, Burrell doubled off Rudy Seanez to tie it 9-9.

The kid

Edwin Jackson came in for the 14th. He’s 18, the right-hander Randy Johnson has been photographing all spring, and Little had no one else he wanted to use. Jackson walked one and got three outs.

In the bottom of the inning the Red Sox loaded the bases against Seanez with one out, and Young, who finished with three hits, four runs scored and three RBI, singled through the middle. It was Jackson’s first big-league win.

“I didn’t do much,” Jackson said. “I got three outs. Kevin did everything.”

Johnson, across the clubhouse, was already looking for his camera.

Boston is 3-3. Oakland is 2-4.
