The Season So Far · ABL · May 13, 2002 · By Site Staff

Picked 17th in the Preseason Consensus, Cleveland Is Tied for First in the East

The Spiders came into the year with a D+ offense led by Tino Martinez and Placido Polanco. Martinez is hitting .416, and Cleveland is 23-17.

Cleveland Spiders · 23-17 · 239 runs scored, 208 allowed

Nobody expected much from Cleveland. The preseason consensus ranked the Spiders 17th of 20 in the ABL, with a D+ offense led by Tino Martinez and Placido Polanco and a C for a rotation that was old and not too exciting. The projections were a little kinder, at 80-71.

Six weeks in, Cleveland is 23-17 and tied with Montreal for first in the Eastern Conference, with the second-best record in the league. The site gives the Spiders a 79.3% chance of making the playoffs and a 39.8% chance of a first-round bye.

Tino

Most of that is Martinez. He's 34 and hitting .416 with 12 home runs, 40 RBI and a 185 OPS+, worth 2.2 WAR already. His OPS was around 1.200 by April 21 and hasn't come down much since. He's driven in six runs in a game twice, at Nashville on April 4 and in a 17-4 rout of San Juan on May 5, when he went 5-for-6, and on Sunday he hit two more homers in a 9-8 walk-off win over New York. Polanco, the other half of the pair, has had a rough start, hitting .269 with a 54 OPS+.

The rest of the plan depended on young hitters panning out in a big way, namely Josh Phelps, Cody Ross and Eric Byrnes. Byrnes has been exactly average, at a 100 OPS+. Phelps is at 79, but he hit one of the biggest home runs of the season. On April 3 in Nashville the Spiders were down three with two outs in the ninth when he hit a grand slam, the last four runs of a 7-6 win. Ross has a 37 OPS+ in 46 plate appearances.

The young hitter who has broken through is Jason Alfaro, 24, who is hitting .453 in 87 plate appearances at third base. He's gotten the job because Pedro Feliz went down on April 16 with a 28-day injury. Feliz was hitting .444 at the time, and on April 12 he hit a three-run walk-off homer to finish a comeback from five runs down against San Diego. Between them, Cleveland's third basemen have been the best thing on the roster after Martinez, and the offense ranks fourth in the ABL in runs per game.

Schmidt, Rhodes and the close ones

The pitching has been about what was expected: Jason Schmidt and Arthur Rhodes, and not much else. Schmidt is 5-2 with 61 strikeouts in 56 innings, and on May 2 at Southside he struck out 10 in eight innings of a 2-1 win. The rotation as a whole has a 5.31 ERA. R.A. Dickey is at 7.19, though he also threw 8.1 shutout innings at Austin on April 15, and Steve Trachsel is at 6.75. Rhodes has seven saves in nine chances. Kazuhisa Ishii, a crafty lefty out of the bullpen but nothing to write home about, is 4-1 with four blown saves.

Somehow it works in close games. Cleveland is 8-3 in one-run games and has five walk-off wins. The one time the bullpen really collapsed was May 1, when the Spiders blew a six-run lead at Southside and lost 9-7.

Lately

The season started with two home losses to Mexico City, and a 3-7 stretch in late April looked like the regression everyone was waiting for. Instead the Spiders went 8-2 from April 27 to May 6, including a three-game sweep of San Juan in which they outscored the Coquis 36-10. They've won the last two over New York. Next come three in Minnesota, three at home against Pittsburgh and then Cincinnati.
