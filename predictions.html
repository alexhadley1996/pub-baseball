The MVP race in the MLB is a one-man show; Barry Bonds remains the greatest hitter in the game. His astounding 73 HR season last year along with his incredible patience at the plate make him the most fearsome hitter on the planet, and Yankee stadium's short right field porch will only make things easier. Carlos Delgado for the Wolverines looks like a potential high value catcher and Alex Rodriguez (Houston Astros), protected by a still potent Sammy Sosa is an intriguing young candidate, but this award is certainly Bonds' to lose. An array of power-hitting first-baseman types, from Pujols to Giambi to Helton could all make a run at the award if either their team plays well or they have a career year.

San Diego' and Baltimore's pitching are good in a league of bad pitching; the cumulative pitching ability of this league is about on par with the FBL, but the offenses are excellent. It is striking how desperately close this league is, particularly the Eastern Conference where it looks like 6-7 teams will legitimately be fighting for the title. The offensive envirorments of this league are off the charts; Salt lake, Mexico City, and Cincinnati are power ball parks and, weirdly, the steals are also massively up in the ABL. Cincinnati, Tampa, and Minnesota are all former MLB teams in danger of slipping even further, and Portland looks just as weak as expected, particularly offenseively (in this league). the Black Sox could set a record for steals in a season. Interestingly, the Cleveland Spiders split opinion based on my power rankings and their projections. A solid staff in the projections leads them to promotion potential. San Juan Coquis also seem tobefuddle the predictions.
